About Calcora

Maintainer: Abdul Qadir · Contact: Contact Calcora · Updated: September 30, 2026

Calcora is an independent calculator project built around a straightforward principle: a useful result should be understandable, testable and presented with the assumptions that matter.

Editorial promise: We aim to make calculations easier to check, not simply easier to click. Time-sensitive facts are identified separately from mathematical formulas, and reported errors are reviewed rather than ignored.

What Calcora is

Calcora provides free online calculators for finance, mathematics, science, solar planning, agriculture and other practical tasks. The project is maintained as an independent web resource and is not a bank, government department, university or financial institution.

Who maintains it

Calcora is maintained by Abdul Qadir. The website is published from the project's public GitHub repository, so its implementation can be inspected and updated through the project's source code.

View the Calcora GitHub repository

Abdul Qadir maintains the website, calculator code, testing, explanations and content updates. The project does not claim professional licences or institutional affiliations that it does not hold.

What users can expect

Calculator pages aim to identify the required inputs, explain the method, provide useful examples and make important limitations visible. Results remain estimates when real-world conditions extend beyond the formula.

How we build a calculator

We begin with the calculation problem a user is trying to solve. We define inputs and units, select or derive the formula, implement the calculation, and test it against hand calculations or known values. The page then explains enough of the method for a user to understand what the result represents.

  1. Define: identify the question, inputs, units and expected output.
  2. Calculate: implement the stated mathematical method without hiding key assumptions.
  3. Test: check ordinary values, boundary cases and representative worked examples.
  4. Explain: document formulas, assumptions, limitations and relevant sources.
  5. Maintain: review time-sensitive information and update the page when the underlying rules or assumptions change.

How we handle changing information

A mathematical formula may remain stable while the number used with it changes. Tax slabs, interest rates, market figures, electricity tariffs, equipment specifications and similar facts can become outdated. We therefore separate sourced external information from the calculation itself and identify the relevant date or tax year when the external fact changes over time.

Sources and editorial review

For factual information that affects a calculation, we prefer primary or authoritative sources such as government and regulatory bodies, official institutions, manufacturers, universities and established technical references. A source supports the external fact; it does not replace testing of the calculator's mathematical implementation.

Examples are meant to be checked

Worked examples show how a formula behaves with specific inputs. They are not presented as personalized financial, tax, engineering or investment advice. When an example depends on a current rule, the page should identify the relevant tax year, rate or source context.

Editorial accountability

Each published calculator should have a clear purpose, identifiable inputs and a calculation path that can be inspected. When a page uses a current external rule or figure, the page should distinguish that fact from the underlying arithmetic. This helps readers understand what can be independently checked and what may need a fresh source check.

How to read a Calcora result

Start with the inputs and units, then review the displayed formula or method. Check the worked example when one is provided, and read any stated assumptions before applying the number to a real-world decision. If your situation includes rules, deductions, rates or measurements not represented by the calculator, treat the output as an estimate rather than a complete assessment.

Corrections and updates

If you find an incorrect result, unit, formula, link or outdated factual claim, please contact Calcora with the calculator name, inputs, result and a short explanation. Reported issues are reviewed against the calculation method and available evidence, and corrections may be applied to the relevant page.

Finance and other decision-support tools

Some Calcora tools concern taxes, loans, investments, agriculture, electricity and other decisions where real-world circumstances matter. These calculators are intended to support understanding and estimation. They do not replace current official documents, contracts, site-specific measurements or qualified professional advice.

Privacy and responsible use

EDITORIAL STANDARD

How Calcora keeps calculator pages useful

Calcora focuses on practical calculators with clear inputs, formulas, examples and assumptions. The goal is simple: answer the calculation first, then explain enough of the method for the result to be checked.

For Pakistan-focused tools, local terms and assumptions are stated where they matter. Rules that change over time should be checked against the relevant official source before a result is used for filing or payment.

Guides are written around real calculation questions rather than repeating keywords, with related calculators linked where they solve the next step.

How Calcora is organized

Calcora is a connected calculator platform. The homepage introduces the platform, category hubs group related calculation tasks, and individual calculator pages answer specific problems with formulas, inputs, units, examples and assumptions.
