Price Per Square Foot Calculator

Calculate price per square foot from a property price and area, or estimate total price from area and a unit price.

Price per square foot is a comparison metric; it does not account for property condition, location, taxes, financing or other costs.

Price per square foot: formula and property comparison guide

Price per square foot divides a property’s total asking or purchase price by its measured area in square feet. It gives a unit-price figure that can help compare properties of different sizes, but it does not prove that one property is better value than another.

Formula

Price per square foot = total price ÷ area in square feet. To estimate a price from a unit rate, reverse the calculation: estimated total price = price per square foot × area.

Worked example

If a property costs 250,000 and has 2,000 square feet of area, its unit price is 250,000 ÷ 2,000 = 125 currency units per square foot. If another property is 1,500 square feet at the same unit price, the implied price is 187,500. These figures are examples only, not current market values.

How to use it

  1. Enter the total property price in the same currency as your comparison.
  2. Enter the relevant area in square feet, using a consistent measurement basis.
  3. Select Calculate Price per Sq Ft.
  4. Compare similar properties and note the source and date of each price.

Interpretation and limitations

Compare like with like: location, property type, condition, floor level, plot versus covered area, amenities and sale date can materially affect the unit price. Confirm whether the area is carpet area, covered area, gross internal area or another local measurement. A larger unit price does not automatically mean a property is overpriced, and a lower one does not guarantee a bargain. The calculation excludes taxes, transfer charges, maintenance, financing, repairs and other ownership costs. It is a comparison aid, not a valuation or investment recommendation.

Frequently asked questions

Is price per square foot the same as total property value?

No. It is a unit-price metric. Multiply it by the relevant area to estimate a total price under that unit rate.

Can I compare a plot with an apartment?

Use caution. Land area and covered floor area are different bases, and plot price per square foot is not directly equivalent to apartment covered-area pricing.

Does a lower price per square foot always mean better value?

No. Location, condition, title, access, amenities, building quality and ongoing costs matter. Compare similar properties and verify details independently.
