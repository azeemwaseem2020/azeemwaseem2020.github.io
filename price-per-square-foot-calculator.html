Price per square foot: formula and property comparison guide

Price per square foot divides a property’s total asking or purchase price by its measured area in square feet. It gives a unit-price figure that can help compare properties of different sizes, but it does not prove that one property is better value than another.

Formula

Price per square foot = total price ÷ area in square feet. To estimate a price from a unit rate, reverse the calculation: estimated total price = price per square foot × area.

Worked example

If a property costs 250,000 and has 2,000 square feet of area, its unit price is 250,000 ÷ 2,000 = 125 currency units per square foot. If another property is 1,500 square feet at the same unit price, the implied price is 187,500. These figures are examples only, not current market values.

How to use it

  1. Enter the total property price in the same currency as your comparison.
  2. Enter the relevant area in square feet, using a consistent measurement basis.
  3. Select Calculate Price per Sq Ft.
  4. Compare similar properties and note the source and date of each price.

Interpretation and limitations

Compare like with like: location, property type, condition, floor level, plot versus covered area, amenities and sale date can materially affect the unit price. Confirm whether the area is carpet area, covered area, gross internal area or another local measurement. A larger unit price does not automatically mean a property is overpriced, and a lower one does not guarantee a bargain. The calculation excludes taxes, transfer charges, maintenance, financing, repairs and other ownership costs. It is a comparison aid, not a valuation or investment recommendation.

Frequently asked questions

Is price per square foot the same as total property value?

No. It is a unit-price metric. Multiply it by the relevant area to estimate a total price under that unit rate.

Can I compare a plot with an apartment?

Use caution. Land area and covered floor area are different bases, and plot price per square foot is not directly equivalent to apartment covered-area pricing.

Does a lower price per square foot always mean better value?

No. Location, condition, title, access, amenities, building quality and ongoing costs matter. Compare similar properties and verify details independently.

Price Per Square Foot Calculator: Compare Property and Material Costs

Price per square foot expresses a total price relative to floor or surface area. It is a convenient comparison measure for property listings, flooring, paintable surfaces and other projects, but it does not by itself prove that two options have equal value. Location, condition, quality, finishing and included services can change the overall comparison.

Formula

Price per square foot = total price ÷ area in square feet. To estimate a total cost from a unit price, multiply price per square foot by the area. If the area is given in square metres, convert it first: 1 m² is approximately 10.7639 ft². Use the same currency and confirm whether the price includes taxes, labour, installation or other charges.

Worked example

If a room renovation quote is PKR 240,000 for 800 ft², the unit cost is 240,000 ÷ 800 = PKR 300 per ft². A 1,000 ft² area at the same rate would have an estimated base cost of PKR 300,000. The estimate assumes the rate scales evenly and that the scope and material specification remain the same.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the full price or quote amount.
  2. Enter the matching area in square feet, or convert the measurement before entering it.
  3. Calculate the unit price and check that the area is positive.
  4. Compare options with the same scope, currency, measurement method and inclusion of taxes or labour.
  5. For a project budget, add separately quoted items that are not included in the unit rate.

Features and real-world applications

Limitations and common errors

Do not compare a covered-area price with a plot-area price as though the denominators were equivalent. Shared spaces, balconies, parking, finishing quality and legal status may be treated differently across listings. For materials, waste, cuts, breakage, labour, transport and minimum order sizes can make actual costs higher than a simple area multiplication. If a quote includes fixed fees, the average rate may fall as the project size increases.

This calculator is an arithmetic aid, not a property valuation or contractor quote. Measure the actual area, request itemised pricing and verify what is included before making a purchase or signing an agreement.
