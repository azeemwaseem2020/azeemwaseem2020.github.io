Percentage Calculator Guide: Percent of a Number, Change, Difference & Reverse Percentage

“What percent is this?”, “What is 20% of 500?” and “A price increased by 20%—what was it before?” are different percentage problems. The correct formula depends on which quantity is known and which quantity is unknown.

Percent of a number

Part = Whole × (Percent ÷ 100)

For 15% of 800, calculate 800 × 0.15 = 120. The percentage is a ratio, so 15% means 15 parts per 100.

Percentage increase and decrease

Percentage change = ((New − Old) ÷ Old) × 100

An increase from 200 to 240 is 20%; a decrease from 200 to 160 is −20%. For a stated increase, multiply the old value by 1 + rate; for a decrease, multiply by 1 − rate.

Reverse percentage

If a final price is known after a 20% increase, do not subtract 20% from the final value. If Final = Original × 1.20, then Original = Final ÷ 1.20.

Percentage points versus percent

Moving from 30% to 40% is a 10-percentage-point increase. Relative to the original 30%, it is a 33.33% increase. These are different measurements.

Discounts, markups and successive changes

A 25% discount leaves 75% of the original price. A 25% markup makes the result 125% of the cost base. Successive changes are multiplicative: a 10% increase followed by a 10% decrease gives 1.10 × 0.90 = 0.99, or 1% below the starting value.

Practical checking method

  1. Identify the reference whole.
  2. Identify the known part or final value.
  3. Convert percentage to a decimal when multiplying.
  4. Choose the formula that matches the unknown.
  5. Reverse-check the result in the original relationship.

FAQ

What is 20% of 500?

100, because 500 × 0.20 = 100.

How do I find the original price after a discount?

Divide the final price by the percentage remaining. After a 20% discount, divide by 0.80.

Is a 10-point increase the same as a 10% increase?

No. Percentage points describe an absolute percentage difference; percent increase compares the change with the original value.
