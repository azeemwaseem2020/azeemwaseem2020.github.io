Arc length formulas

For a circle, arc length is the fraction of the circumference represented by the central angle. With angle θ in radians, s = rθ. With degrees, s = (θ/360°) × 2πr.

Worked example

For radius 10 and a 90° central angle, the arc length is one quarter of the circumference: (90/360) × 2π × 10 = 5π units.

Arc length FAQ

Why does the angle unit matter?

The formula s = rθ expects θ in radians. If degrees are used, convert them or use the degree-based formula.

What units does arc length use?

The result uses the same length unit as the radius.

Arc length formulas

For a central angle θ in radians, s = rθ. For an angle in degrees, s = (θ/360) × 2πr.

Worked example

With radius 10 cm and a 90° angle, the arc is one quarter of the circumference: (90/360) × 2π × 10 = 5π ≈ 15.71 cm.

Units and angle conversion

The arc length uses the same length unit as the radius. The radians formula requires the angle in radians; degrees must be converted or handled with the degree formula.

FAQ

What if the angle is 360°?

The result is the full circumference, 2πr.

Can radius be zero?

A zero radius gives an arc length of zero.
