Arc length calculation context

Arc length measures the distance along a curve. For a circular arc, the result depends on the radius and central angle, with the angle converted to the unit required by the formula. This makes arc length a geometry calculation rather than a general distance measurement.

Arc length formula and the angle unit

For a circle, arc length is the fraction of the circumference covered by the central angle. In radians, use L = rθ, where r is the radius and θ is the angle in radians. In degrees, an equivalent formula is L = 2πr × (θ/360). These formulas describe the same geometry; the angle unit determines which form is convenient. If the angle is supplied in degrees, convert it to radians before using L = rθ.

Worked comparison: arc and chord

For a circle with radius 5 cm and a 60° central angle, the arc length is 5π/3, approximately 5.24 cm. The straight chord joining the same endpoints is shorter: its length is 2r sin(θ/2), or 5 cm in this example. This shows why a curved path and a straight-line distance should not be treated as interchangeable.

Limits and practical measurement

The radius must be a positive length, and the central angle should describe the arc being measured. For a full circle, 360° corresponds to circumference 2πr; for a semicircle, 180° corresponds to πr. Keep the output in the same length unit as the radius. For physical construction, cutting materials or surveying, allow for measurement tolerances and confirm whether the drawing specifies an inside, outside or centre-line radius.

Worked example

For a radius of 5 cm and a central angle of 60°, convert the angle to radians: 60 × π/180 = π/3. The arc length is L = 5 × π/3 ≈ 5.24 cm.

Degrees and radians

The formula L = rθ requires θ in radians. If the calculator accepts degrees, the conversion is θ = degrees × π/180. Keeping the angle unit explicit prevents a common factor-of-57.3 error.

When to use arc length

Arc length is useful for circular tracks, curved edges, sectors and geometry problems where the distance follows the curve rather than the straight chord between two points.

Common checks

  • Use the radius, not the diameter.
  • Confirm the angle is the central angle.
  • Keep length units consistent.

Arc length versus chord length

The arc follows the curved circumference, while the chord is the straight line between the arc endpoints. They are different measurements, so use the arc-length formula when the distance follows the curve.

Arc length formula and calculation method

Arc length is the distance measured along the curved circumference between two points. With radius r and central angle θ in radians, the formula is L = rθ. If the angle is supplied in degrees, use L = 2πr × (θ/360), or convert it with θ(rad) = θ(deg) × π/180 before using L = rθ.

Worked example: a 120° arc

For a circle of radius 6 cm and central angle 120°, the angle in radians is 120 × π/180 = 2π/3. Therefore L = 6 × 2π/3 = 4π cm ≈ 12.57 cm. The result stays in centimetres because the radius was entered in centimetres.

Arc length versus chord length

The arc follows the curve; the chord is the straight line joining the same endpoints. The chord formula is c = 2r sin(θ/2) when the angle is in radians. For the 6 cm, 120° example, the chord is about 10.39 cm, shorter than the 12.57 cm arc. Use the measurement that matches the real task.

Practical checks and FAQs

  • Use radius, not diameter; if only diameter is known, divide it by two.
  • Make sure the angle is the central angle and that degrees/radians are handled correctly.
  • For a full circle, 360° gives L = 2πr; for a semicircle, 180° gives L = πr.
Can an arc angle be greater than 180°?

Yes. A major arc can have a central angle greater than 180° and up to 360° for one full turn.

Does the answer change if I use metres instead of centimetres?

The physical length is the same, but the number changes with the unit. A radius of 0.06 m and the same angle gives the answer in metres.

Can I use arc length to cut a curved material?

It can provide a geometric estimate, but verify whether the design specifies an inside, outside or centre-line radius and allow for measurement and manufacturing tolerances.
