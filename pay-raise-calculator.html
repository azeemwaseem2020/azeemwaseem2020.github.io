Pay Raise Calculator

Calculate your new salary, hourly wage and increase amount after a percentage pay raise.

Pay raise formula

Raise amount = current salary × raise percentage ÷ 100. New salary = current salary + raise amount. This calculator treats the entered salary as annual gross pay and shows an annual and monthly equivalent; it does not calculate hourly pay or take-home salary.

Worked example

If annual gross salary is 600,000 and the raise is 8%, the increase is 600,000 × 0.08 = 48,000. New annual gross salary is 648,000, and the simple monthly equivalent rises from 50,000 to 54,000. Use the same currency for the old and new amounts; the tool does not add a currency symbol.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter current annual gross salary as a non-negative amount.
  2. Enter the proposed raise percentage, such as 5 for five percent.
  3. Calculate and compare the increase and updated annual/monthly equivalents.

Gross raise versus take-home pay

A percentage raise usually applies to gross salary, not the amount deposited into your account. Income tax, pension contributions, insurance and other payroll deductions can change net pay. A bonus or allowance may also be treated differently by an employer's payroll rules. Confirm the written offer and applicable tax rules before budgeting around the increase.

Common mistakes

  • Entering 0.05 when you mean 5%. Enter 5 for a five-percent raise.
  • Comparing monthly salary with annual salary without converting both to the same period.
  • Assuming a gross raise percentage guarantees the same percentage increase in take-home pay.
  • Ignoring whether the raise applies to base salary only or includes allowances and bonuses.

How to calculate a pay raise

A pay raise compares your old compensation with the proposed new compensation. The percentage increase is the difference between the new and old pay divided by the old pay, multiplied by 100.

Example

If monthly pay rises from PKR 60,000 to PKR 66,000, the increase is PKR 6,000 and the raise rate is 10%. The new amount is the figure to compare against future monthly or annual budgeting.

Annual impact

Convert both amounts to the same pay period before comparing them. A monthly raise can be multiplied by 12 for a simple annual gross comparison, while bonuses, allowances, tax and deductions may change the actual take-home difference.

Pay-raise FAQ

Is a 10% raise the same as 10% more take-home pay?

Not necessarily. Taxes, pension or other payroll deductions can mean the change in net pay differs from the gross raise percentage.

How do I calculate a raise percentage?

Divide the increase by the original salary and multiply by 100. For example, an increase of 6,000 on an original salary of 60,000 is 10%.

Does this calculator include income tax?

No. It estimates gross pay only. Use an appropriate tax or paycheck calculator to estimate deductions.

How is monthly pay calculated?

The displayed monthly equivalent is annual salary divided by 12. It is a simple comparison and may not match every payroll arrangement.
