Dividend income in practice

Dividend income is driven by the number of eligible shares and the dividend declared per share for a particular payment period. The simple multiplication in this calculator is useful for checking a declared dividend, comparing portfolio income scenarios or understanding how yield relates to a share price. It does not determine whether you are entitled to a payment; eligibility depends on the company's announced terms and relevant dates.

Dividend yield is a ratio rather than a guaranteed return. If a share price falls while the dividend per share stays unchanged, the calculated yield rises. If the market price rises, the yield falls. This is why yield should be read alongside the dividend amount, the period covered, and the price used in the calculation.

For Pakistan or PSX holdings, use the dividend per share and share price that match the security and period you are analysing. Keep brokerage, withholding tax, currency conversion and other portfolio costs separate unless you deliberately want a net-income calculation.

Dividend formulas

Estimated dividend income = shares × dividend per share. Dividend yield = dividend per share ÷ share price × 100. These formulas assume the inputs represent the same dividend period and currency.

Dividend example

If you own 100 shares, the dividend is $5 per share, and the share price is $100, estimated dividend income is $500 and dividend yield is 5%.

What dividend yield means

Dividend yield expresses a dividend as a percentage of the share price. A yield can change when the share price changes even if the company does not change its dividend.

Important limitations

This is an estimate based on user-entered values. It does not forecast future dividends, taxes, withholding, brokerage costs, currency changes, or dividend growth. Actual payments depend on the company and applicable rules.

FAQ

How is dividend income calculated? Multiply the number of shares by the dividend per share for the chosen period.

How is dividend yield calculated? Divide dividend per share by share price and multiply by 100.

Can dividend yield change without a dividend change? Yes. Market price changes can change the yield percentage.
