Logarithms: formula, base and example

A logarithm answers the question: “What exponent produces this number?” In logb(x) = y, the equivalent exponential statement is by = x. The base must be positive and not equal to 1, and the argument x must be positive for ordinary real-valued logarithms.

Worked example

For log10(1000), ask which power of 10 equals 1000. Because 10³ = 1000, the answer is 3. For natural logarithms, the base is e and the notation is ln(x).

Practical applications

Logs appear in growth models, compound processes, signal measurements, chemistry and finance. When solving for time in a compound-growth equation, logarithms can isolate the unknown exponent.

Common mistakes

Do not confuse the base with the argument, and do not enter zero or a negative argument when a real-valued result is required. Check whether your problem specifies common log, natural log or another base.
