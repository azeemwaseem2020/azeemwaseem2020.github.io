Standard conversion used here: 1 Pakistani tola = 11.6638 grams. Gold Nisab 87.48 g (7.5 tola); silver Nisab 612.36 g (52.5 tola). Some references use different gram conversions; confirm the convention you follow.

The field is prefilled with PKR 503,529 as a historical reference for bank-account deductions in 1446–47 AH (2026). This is not a confirmed threshold for a later Zakat year or a universal personal Nisab. Verify the amount appropriate to your valuation date and method, and edit it if needed. See the official State Bank of Pakistan notification reference before using a historical figure.

How this Zakat calculator works

Enter the values of assets you believe should be included under the Zakat method you follow, then enter any liabilities that method permits you to deduct. The calculator adds the listed assets, subtracts the entered liabilities (without allowing a negative net total), compares the result with the Nisab value you entered, and estimates 2.5% when the net amount reaches that threshold.

Formula: Net listed assets = cash + bank balances + gold value + silver value + included investments + business inventory + eligible receivables − deductible liabilities. Estimated Zakat = 2.5% of net listed assets when the net amount meets or exceeds the entered Nisab; otherwise the estimate is zero.

The formula is arithmetic only. The calculator does not decide whether a particular asset is zakatable, whether a debt is deductible, which valuation date applies, or whether other conditions are met in your circumstances.

Hanafi and Hanbali selection: read before using

The madhhab selector is a transparency aid, not an automated fatwa. The Hanafi approach commonly compares combined cash, gold, silver and trade wealth against the silver Nisab. Hanbali rulings and contemporary applications may differ by asset type and scholarly guidance; in particular, the treatment of personal jewelry can differ. The Nisab basis can be selected separately, so confirm the correct basis with a qualified scholar rather than assuming one dropdown settles every case.

Important: understand the Nisab reference

The published PKR 503,529 amount is a historical Pakistan government reference for the Zakat year 1446–47 AH (2026), notified for deduction at source from specified bank, profit-and-loss-sharing and similar accounts. As of 13 October 2026, the official references linked on this page still identify that prior-year notification; do not treat it as a confirmed threshold for a later Zakat year. The State Bank of Pakistan circular page links to the notification, and the Punjab Zakat & Ushr Department FAQ displays the same amount. You can also read the original government notification (PDF).

This government bank-deduction threshold should not be treated as a universal personal Zakat ruling. Personal Nisab assessment may depend on the valuation basis (including the method followed for gold or silver), the relevant date and qualified religious guidance. The value of precious metals changes, and the official bank-deduction figure is tied to a particular Zakat year and specified account categories. Check the appropriate threshold for your own calculation date and method, and edit the field above rather than assuming the default is always applicable.

Current Nisab and valuation-date check

Before calculating, identify the date on which you are assessing your assets and confirm the relevant Nisab using a current official notification where applicable, plus the valuation basis and religious guidance you follow. The bank-deduction threshold is a specific administrative figure for certain accounts; it is not automatically the personal threshold for every person, asset mix or scholarly method. If you cannot verify a current threshold, enter one only after checking a reliable current source rather than reusing the prior-year amount by default.

Which assets and liabilities can I enter?

The calculator has separate fields for cash in hand, bank balances, gold, silver, investments or shares, business inventory or trade assets, and eligible receivables. Enter values consistently for the valuation date you are using. For gold or silver, use a value calculated according to the method you follow; this tool does not fetch live metal prices or infer value from weight.

The liability field is only an arithmetic deduction. Not every debt, future expense or liability is necessarily deductible under every method. Similarly, the treatment of shares and other investments can depend on the nature of the holding and the scholarly approach followed. If you are unsure whether an asset or liability belongs in the calculation, confirm its treatment with a trusted qualified scholar before relying on the result.

Worked example

Suppose the assets you have identified total PKR 900,000 and the liabilities you are permitted to deduct total PKR 100,000. The arithmetic net is PKR 800,000. If the Nisab entered is PKR 503,529, the net amount is above that threshold; 2.5% of PKR 800,000 is PKR 20,000. This example demonstrates the calculation only. It does not determine whether the chosen assets, valuation or deductions are religiously applicable to you.

Steps to prepare a more reliable estimate

  1. Choose the relevant valuation date and the Zakat method or guidance you follow.
  2. List potentially eligible assets and value them on a consistent basis.
  3. Confirm which liabilities, if any, may be deducted under that method.
  4. Use a Nisab value appropriate to your date, valuation basis and circumstances.
  5. Review the result and ask a qualified authority about any asset-specific or complex question before payment.

Frequently asked questions

Does this calculator check the lunar-year (hawl) condition?

No. The calculator checks only the entered asset total, liabilities and Nisab threshold. It does not track ownership duration, the relevant lunar-year conditions, or other personal circumstances that may affect a Zakat ruling. Confirm these requirements with the qualified authority or method you follow.

Does this calculator give a fatwa?

No. It performs arithmetic from the values you enter and does not issue a religious ruling or determine personal eligibility.

Can I change the Nisab amount?

Yes. The field is editable because the default is a dated reference for specified bank-account deductions and may not be the appropriate personal threshold for every method or valuation date.

Does the calculator decide whether my debts are deductible?

No. It subtracts the liability amount entered. You must establish whether and to what extent that liability is deductible under the guidance you follow.

Are live gold and silver prices included?

No. Enter the monetary values you have determined for your chosen valuation date and method. The calculator does not retrieve live metal prices.

Can I use this outside Pakistan?

The arithmetic can be used with values in another currency, but you must supply a suitable Nisab value and follow the guidance applicable to your location and circumstances. The official reference described on this page is specific to Pakistan's 2026 bank-deduction notification.
