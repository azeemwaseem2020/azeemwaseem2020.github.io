How the Zakat estimate works

  1. Enter the Nisab threshold applicable to your calculation.
  2. List the assets you consider zakatable under the method you follow.
  3. Enter eligible liabilities only where your applicable rules allow a deduction.
  4. The calculator subtracts entered liabilities from listed assets, without allowing a negative net value.
  5. If the resulting net amount reaches the entered Nisab, the tool applies 2.5% to produce an estimate.

What is Nisab?

Nisab is the minimum threshold used in Zakat calculations. Its value can change with the valuation method, precious-metal prices, date and applicable authority or scholarly guidance. For that reason, Calcora makes the Nisab field editable rather than treating one number as universally correct.

Pakistan reference for 1446–47 AH (2026): The Punjab Zakat & Ushr Department states a Nisab of PKR 503,529 for that year. This is a dated government reference, not a permanent threshold. Official Zakat & Ushr Department FAQ.

Important: Zakat rules can depend on asset type, valuation date, ownership, debts and the scholarly or official method you follow. This calculator is an educational arithmetic tool, not a religious ruling. Verify the current applicable Nisab and guidance before payment.

Assets included in this calculator

The calculator provides fields for cash, bank balances, gold, silver, selected investments or shares, business inventory and eligible receivables. Whether a particular asset is included, how it is valued and which liabilities can be deducted can vary by methodology. Do not assume every field applies to every personal situation.

Why gold and silver need careful valuation

Enter the value you are actually using for your chosen calculation date and method. If you are working from weight rather than a monetary value, first determine the appropriate valuation under the guidance you follow. This page intentionally does not hard-code a metal price.

Simple example

If eligible net assets after permitted deductions are PKR 800,000 and the entered Nisab is PKR 503,529, the net amount reaches the threshold. At a 2.5% arithmetic rate, the estimate is PKR 20,000. This example demonstrates the calculation only; it does not decide which assets or deductions are religiously applicable.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using an outdated Nisab value without checking the applicable date.
  • Mixing different valuation dates for cash, metals and other assets.
  • Assuming every debt or liability is deductible under every method.
  • Confusing investment market value with the Zakat treatment required by the method you follow.
  • Treating a calculator result as a substitute for qualified religious guidance in a complex case.

FAQ

Is the 2.5% rate always the correct rule for my situation?

This calculator uses 2.5% as the stated arithmetic estimate when the entered net assets meet the entered Nisab. Individual Zakat rulings can involve additional conditions, so verify the method you follow.

Can I change the Nisab?

Yes. The Nisab field is editable because applicable thresholds can vary by date, valuation basis and authority.

Does the calculator give a religious fatwa?

No. It performs the arithmetic from your inputs. For questions about asset eligibility, debts, ownership or special circumstances, consult a trusted qualified authority.

Can I use it for Pakistan?

Yes, the page is written for Pakistan-focused use, but you should still verify the current official or scholarly guidance applicable to you rather than relying on a fixed default.

Using the result responsibly

The calculator provides an arithmetic estimate from the assets, liabilities and Nisab value you enter. Nisab treatment and eligible assets can involve religious and factual questions that should be verified with a trusted qualified scholar or authoritative source for your circumstances. How to prepare your Zakat calculation

Start by identifying the valuation date and the method you follow. Then list the assets that are potentially subject to Zakat, value them consistently, identify any liabilities that are deductible under that method, and compare the resulting amount with the applicable Nisab.

Why the Nisab field is editable

Nisab is not a permanently fixed PKR number. A threshold can change with precious-metal prices, valuation date and the method or authority being followed. Enter the value appropriate to your calculation instead of assuming that the default value applies to every person and date.

Cash, bank balances and investments

Cash and bank balances are straightforward inputs for an arithmetic estimate. Investments and shares require more care because the appropriate treatment can depend on the nature of the holding and the method followed. The calculator therefore describes the investment field as an input rather than deciding the religious treatment of every asset.

Worked example

If the listed eligible assets total PKR 900,000 and permitted deductions entered in the calculator are PKR 100,000, the arithmetic net amount is PKR 800,000. If the entered Nisab is PKR 503,529, the net amount is above that threshold and 2.5% of PKR 800,000 gives an arithmetic estimate of PKR 20,000. Whether every asset and deduction belongs in the calculation must be checked separately.

Zakat calculation FAQ

Can I use a different Nisab value?

Yes. Enter the threshold applicable to your chosen date, valuation basis and guidance.

Does this calculator decide which debts are deductible?

No. It only subtracts the liability amount you enter. Whether a debt is deductible depends on the method and circumstances you follow.

Should shares be included at market value?

The appropriate treatment can depend on the type of investment and the scholarly method being followed. Verify the treatment applicable to your holdings rather than assuming one universal rule.

Can I use this outside Pakistan?

The arithmetic can be used with another currency, but the applicable Nisab, valuation date and guidance should be changed to match your circumstances.
