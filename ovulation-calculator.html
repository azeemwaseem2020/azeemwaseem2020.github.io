Ovulation Calculator

Estimate an approximate ovulation date and fertile window from the first day of the last menstrual period and cycle length.

How the estimate works

The calculator estimates ovulation about 14 days before the next period and uses the cycle length you enter. The fertile-window result is intentionally approximate because ovulation does not occur on exactly the same day in every cycle.

Important health note

This calendar estimate is not a diagnostic test and should not be relied on as a contraceptive method. Cycle timing can vary, including with irregular periods. If you are trying to conceive, having difficulty conceiving, or need reliable contraception, a qualified healthcare professional can provide advice suited to your circumstances.

Ovulation Calculator: Cycle Timing, Example and Limitations

An ovulation calculator estimates a possible fertile window from menstrual-cycle dates and an assumed cycle length. It is a planning aid, not a test that confirms ovulation, pregnancy or infertility. Ovulation often occurs roughly 14 days before the next period, but timing varies between people and can change from cycle to cycle. Calendar estimates are less dependable when cycles are irregular or affected by illness, stress, breastfeeding or hormonal contraception.

Estimation method

A common estimate is ovulation date ≈ expected next period − 14 days. Estimate the next period by adding the usual cycle length to the first day of the last menstrual period (LMP). The fertile window is often described as the five days before ovulation and the day of ovulation. This is approximate: sperm may survive for several days, while the egg remains viable for a limited time after release.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the first day of your most recent period, not the final day of bleeding.
  2. Enter your usual cycle length, counted from the first day of one period to the first day of the next.
  3. Review the estimated ovulation date and fertile-window range.
  4. Compare the estimate with several months of personal cycle records rather than relying on one result.

Worked example

If a cycle usually lasts 28 days and the last period began on 1 March, the next period is estimated around 29 March. Counting back about 14 days gives an estimated ovulation date around 15 March. A rough fertile window would be 10–15 March. If the cycle lasts 31 days, the estimate shifts later; actual ovulation can still occur earlier or later than the calendar suggests.

Use the estimate responsibly

Changes in cervical mucus, urinary LH tests and basal body temperature may provide additional clues, but each has limitations and should be interpreted according to its instructions. Calendar calculations should not be relied on as contraception. If you are trying to conceive, have very irregular or absent periods, or have fertility concerns, a qualified healthcare professional can help assess your circumstances. The calculator cannot diagnose a condition or confirm that an egg was released.

Frequently asked questions

Does this confirm ovulation?

No. It estimates dates from cycle inputs and cannot confirm that ovulation occurred.

Is the fertile window identical every month?

No. Ovulation timing can shift even when cycles appear fairly regular.

Can I use this estimate to avoid pregnancy?

Do not rely on a calendar estimate alone for contraception; seek reliable contraceptive guidance.
