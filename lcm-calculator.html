Find the least common multiple of two positive integers quickly, with the standard GCF-based method and a worked example.

Formula: LCM(a,b) = (a × b) ÷ GCF(a,b).

What is the LCM?

The least common multiple is the smallest positive whole number that is divisible by both input numbers. It is useful for comparing repeating cycles, matching schedules, and solving fraction problems with different denominators.

LCM example

For 12 and 18, the greatest common factor is 6. Therefore, LCM = (12 × 18) ÷ 6 = 36.

When to use an LCM calculator

Use it when you need the first shared multiple of two integers or a common denominator for fractions. The result is a whole number when the inputs are positive integers.

What the least common multiple means

The least common multiple (LCM) is the smallest positive integer that is a multiple of every input number. It is useful for repeating schedules, common denominators and problems where separate cycles need to line up.

Worked example

For 12 and 18, the multiples of 12 include 12, 24, 36, 48... and the multiples of 18 include 18, 36, 54... The first shared positive multiple is therefore 36. Using prime factors gives the same result: 12 = 2² × 3 and 18 = 2 × 3², so LCM = 2² × 3² = 36.

A practical Pakistan example

Imagine two maintenance tasks at a workshop in Faisalabad repeat every 6 days and 8 days. If both are completed today, they line up again after LCM(6, 8) = 24 days. This helps with scheduling when the cycles repeat consistently.

LCM and GCF are different

GCF identifies the largest shared divisor; LCM identifies the smallest shared multiple. For positive integers a and b, the relationship LCM(a,b) × GCF(a,b) = a × b provides a useful check on a two-number calculation.

Common mistakes

LCM is normally the smallest positive common multiple, not simply the product of the inputs. Multiplying the inputs gives the LCM only when the numbers have no common factor greater than 1.

LCM FAQ

Can LCM be smaller than an input? No, for positive integers it is at least as large as every input.

Can LCM handle more than two numbers? Yes. Calculate the LCM progressively across the inputs.

Is LCM useful for fractions? Yes. LCM of denominators is commonly used to find a common denominator.

What happens if one input is zero? LCM conventions vary when zero is included, so the calculator should follow its stated input rules rather than assuming the ordinary positive-integer definition.

LCM in practical calculations

The least common multiple is the smallest positive integer divisible by each supplied positive integer. For 12 and 18, the multiples overlap at 36, so LCM(12,18) = 36. A useful identity for two positive integers is LCM(a,b) × GCF(a,b) = a × b.
