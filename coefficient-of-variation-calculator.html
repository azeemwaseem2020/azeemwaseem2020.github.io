Coefficient of variation explained

The coefficient of variation (CV) measures standard deviation relative to the mean. A common formula is CV = standard deviation ÷ mean, often reported as a percentage by multiplying by 100.

Worked example

If the mean is 50 and the standard deviation is 5, CV = 5 ÷ 50 = 0.10, or 10%.

When CV is useful

CV can help compare relative variability across datasets measured on different scales when the mean is meaningful and positive. It is not appropriate for every type of data.

CV FAQ

Can CV be used when the mean is zero?

No. Division by zero makes the standard CV undefined.

Is a lower CV always better?

A lower CV means lower variability relative to the mean, but whether that is desirable depends on the application and measurement objective.
