Coefficient of Variation Calculator

Calculate the coefficient of variation (CV) to express a standard deviation relative to a positive mean, usually as a percentage.

Enter a non-negative standard deviation and positive mean.

Formula and calculation method

The coefficient of variation is calculated as CV = (standard deviation ÷ mean) × 100%. The units cancel when both inputs describe the same variable in the same units, so the result is a percentage rather than a measurement in the original units.

Practical worked example

If the mean is 50 and the standard deviation is 5, CV = (5 ÷ 50) × 100% = 10%. This means the standard deviation is one-tenth of the mean's magnitude; it does not mean every observation lies within 10% of the mean.

How to use this calculator

  1. Enter the standard deviation calculated from your dataset.
  2. Enter the mean from the same dataset.
  3. Calculate CV and keep the statistical convention consistent if comparing datasets.

When is CV useful?

CV is a relative measure of spread. It can help compare variability between measurements on different scales when the variable has a meaningful, positive zero and the means are appropriate for comparison. For example, it may be useful when reviewing consistency of repeated laboratory measurements or production processes, provided the underlying data and measurement methods are comparable.

Limitations and common mistakes

CV is undefined when the mean is zero and can become unstable when the mean is close to zero. Conventional CV interpretation is generally unsuitable for negative means or variables whose zero point is arbitrary, such as many temperature scales. This calculator uses the standard deviation you enter; it does not calculate it from raw observations. Make sure mean and standard deviation describe the same sample or population, and use either sample or population standard deviation consistently when comparing results.

Frequently asked questions

Is coefficient of variation the same as standard deviation?

No. Standard deviation measures spread in the variable's original units. CV expresses that spread relative to the mean, usually as a percentage.

Can I calculate CV when the mean is zero?

No. The formula divides by the mean, so a zero mean makes CV undefined. A mean close to zero can also make the percentage unstable.

Does a lower CV always mean better results?

No. A lower CV means less spread relative to the mean, but whether that is desirable depends on the context, quality target and data-generating process.
