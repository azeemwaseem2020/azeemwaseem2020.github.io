Understanding the coefficient of variation

The coefficient of variation (CV) measures relative variability by comparing standard deviation with the mean. A common formula is CV = standard deviation ÷ mean × 100%. Because it is dimensionless, CV can help compare relative spread across data sets measured in the same type of quantity but at different scales.

Worked example

If a data set has a mean of 50 and a standard deviation of 5, CV = 5 ÷ 50 × 100 = 10%. If another process has a mean of 100 and a standard deviation of 8, its CV is 8%, even though its absolute standard deviation is larger.

A practical Pakistan example

A dairy operation in Pakistan could compare variation in daily milk yield across two groups of animals. If one group averages 20 litres with a standard deviation of 2 litres, its CV is 10%. A second group averaging 30 litres with a standard deviation of 2.4 litres has an 8% CV. The relative spread is therefore different even though both groups have measurable day-to-day variation.

When CV is useful

CV is most informative when the mean is positive and meaningfully represents the scale of the data. It is commonly used to compare relative variability in measurements such as costs, yields and laboratory results. It can be misleading when the mean is near zero, negative, or when the measurement scale has no meaningful ratio interpretation.

Common mistakes

Check whether the standard deviation is a sample or population estimate, and keep that choice consistent with the analysis. Do not compare CVs blindly when the underlying variables or measurement scales have fundamentally different meanings.

CV FAQ

Can CV be calculated when the mean is zero? The standard formula is undefined because it requires division by the mean.

Is a lower CV always better? No. Whether lower variability is desirable depends on the process and objective.

Does CV have units? No. Standard deviation and mean have the same units, so they cancel.

Can CV compare different scales? It can compare relative variability when the variables have a meaningful ratio scale and the means are suitable for the calculation.
