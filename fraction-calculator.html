Fraction Calculator Guide

Use this tool to add, subtract, multiply or divide two fractions and reduce the result to its simplest form.

How the operations work

For addition or subtraction, convert the fractions to a common denominator. Multiplication uses numerator × numerator and denominator × denominator. Division is multiplication by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

Worked example

For 1/2 + 1/3, a common denominator is 6: 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6.

Important check

A denominator cannot be zero, and division by a fraction with a zero numerator is undefined. Keep negative signs with the correct part of the fraction when checking the result.

Formula & assumptions

For a/b ± c/d use a common denominator. Multiplication is ac/bd and division is ad/bc. The result is reduced by the greatest common divisor.

How fraction calculations work

Fractions represent a numerator divided by a non-zero denominator. Addition and subtraction require a common denominator; multiplication multiplies numerators and denominators; division by a fraction means multiplying by its reciprocal. Keeping the fraction form until the final step can make exact results easier to verify.

Worked example

For 2/3 + 1/4, the least common denominator is 12. Convert the fractions to 8/12 and 3/12, then add to get 11/12. For multiplication, 2/3 × 3/5 = 6/15 = 2/5 after simplifying.

A practical Pakistan example

Fractions are useful in recipe scaling, construction measurements and classroom mathematics. If a recipe uses 3/4 kg of flour and you make half the recipe, the required amount is 1/2 × 3/4 = 3/8 kg, or 375 grams.

Improper and mixed fractions

An improper fraction has a numerator whose absolute value is at least the denominator, such as 7/4. It can be written as the mixed number 1 3/4. Converting between forms does not change the value; it only changes how the value is represented.

Common mistakes

Do not add denominators directly when adding fractions. Also check signs, simplify the final result and never divide by a zero denominator. For negative fractions, keep the sign placement consistent.

Fraction FAQ

Why do fractions need a common denominator for addition? The numerator units must represent the same-sized parts before they can be combined.

Can a denominator be zero? No. Division by zero is undefined.

Should fractions always be simplified? Simplifying is useful because it gives an equivalent fraction in lowest terms.

When should I use decimal form? Decimals can be convenient for measurement, while fractions often preserve exact values.
