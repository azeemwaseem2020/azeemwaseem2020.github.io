Rectangular prism volume

The volume of a rectangular prism is length × width × height. All three dimensions must use compatible units. The result is expressed in cubic units, such as cm³, m³ or ft³.

Worked example

A storage box 2 m long, 1.5 m wide and 1 m high has volume 2 × 1.5 × 1 = 3 m³. If you need litres, remember that 1 m³ equals 1,000 litres.

Practical example

This formula can estimate room volume, tanks, boxes, concrete forms and storage capacity. For real-world containers, usable capacity may be smaller than geometric volume because of walls, fittings or required free space.

Rectangular Prism Volume: Formula and Example

The volume of a rectangular prism is V = L × W × H, where length, width and height are measured in the same unit. The result is a cubic unit: centimetres produce cm³, metres produce m³, and feet produce ft³. Keeping units consistent is essential because volume scales with all three dimensions.

For example, a storage box measuring 2 m long, 1.5 m wide and 0.8 m high has a volume of 2 × 1.5 × 0.8 = 2.4 m³. If you need the capacity in litres, use 1 m³ = 1,000 litres, giving 2,400 litres. This conversion is useful for tanks, rooms, containers and water-storage planning.

In Pakistan, the same calculation can help estimate the internal volume of a rectangular water tank before comparing capacity with household demand. If the tank is 2.5 m × 1.5 m × 1.2 m, its theoretical volume is 4.5 m³, or 4,500 litres. Actual usable capacity may be lower because of freeboard, fittings, sediment space or a non-rectangular interior.

Measurement notes

Measure internal dimensions when calculating usable container capacity. For construction material estimates, external dimensions may be more appropriate depending on the purpose. Do not mix centimetres and metres without conversion. For a box with variable or sloped sides, the simple rectangular-prism formula may not represent the actual volume.

FAQs

Can I enter inches? Yes, as long as all three dimensions use the same unit.

How do I convert cubic metres to litres? Multiply by 1,000.

Is volume the same as surface area? No. Volume measures three-dimensional capacity; surface area measures exposed area.
