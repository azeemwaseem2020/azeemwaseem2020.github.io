Rectangular prism volume and surface area: complete guide

A rectangular prism is a three-dimensional solid with six rectangular faces, such as a box, brick or cuboid. This calculator uses length, width and height to find its enclosed volume and total surface area. Volume measures interior space; surface area measures the sum of all outside faces.

Formulas

Volume = length × width × height.
Surface area = 2 × (length × width + length × height + width × height).

For dimensions in metres, volume is in m³ and surface area in m². For centimetres, results are cm³ and cm². Keep all three dimensions in the same unit.

How to use the calculator

  1. Measure the object’s length, width and height.
  2. Enter the three dimensions in their matching fields.
  3. Select Calculate to display volume and surface area.
  4. Check units and round based on measurement precision.

Worked example

A box is 4 cm long, 3 cm wide and 2 cm high. Volume = 4 × 3 × 2 = 24 cm³. Surface area = 2 × (12 + 8 + 6) = 52 cm². The first result describes the space inside an ideal solid box; the second is the total area of its six outer faces.

Applications and common mistakes

Use volume for approximate capacity or material calculations and surface area for coating or wrapping estimates. A real container’s usable capacity may be lower because of wall thickness, a lid, rounded corners or internal fittings. Do not confuse surface area with the area of just one face, and do not mix centimetres with metres. If a shape has sloped sides or is not rectangular, use a formula for its actual geometry. This calculator assumes a rectangular prism and does not include material thickness or waste.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find the volume of a box?

Multiply its internal length by internal width and internal height when estimating usable capacity. For a solid object, external dimensions give the object’s geometric volume. Choose the measurements that match the question you are trying to answer.

How is surface area different from volume?

Surface area adds the areas of all six rectangular faces and uses square units. Volume multiplies three dimensions and uses cubic units. Surface area can help estimate wrapping or coating, while volume describes space occupied.

Why might the actual capacity be lower?

Walls, lids, rounded corners and internal supports reduce usable space. This calculator models a perfect rectangular prism, so use internal dimensions and allow for real-world features when estimating a container’s capacity.

Volume versus usable capacity

The formula length × width × height gives the geometric volume of an ideal rectangular prism. A box measuring 40 cm × 30 cm × 20 cm has a volume of 24,000 cm³, equivalent to 24 litres because 1,000 cm³ equals 1 litre. A real container may hold less because dimensions can be external, walls take up space, and the container may not be filled to the rim.

Choosing the right dimensions

  • Use internal dimensions when estimating liquid or storage capacity.
  • Use external dimensions when checking whether a box will fit through a doorway or into a vehicle.
  • Use surface area when estimating the outside material needed to wrap, coat or paint all six faces.
  • For an open-top box, the relevant material area differs from the total six-face surface area.

All three dimensions must use the same unit. This calculator models a cuboid; cylinders, irregular packages and objects with sloping sides need different geometry.
