Effective annual rate formula

For a nominal annual rate compounded a fixed number of times per year, the effective annual rate (EAR) is EAR = (1 + r/m)m − 1, where r is the nominal annual rate as a decimal and m is the number of compounding periods per year. Multiply the decimal result by 100 to express it as a percentage.

Worked example

At a nominal annual rate of 12% compounded monthly, the periodic rate is 12% ÷ 12 = 1%. Applying monthly compounding gives an effective annual rate of about 12.6825%. The effective rate is higher than the nominal rate because interest compounds during the year.

How to use this calculator

  1. Enter the nominal annual interest rate as a percentage, such as 12 for 12%.
  2. Enter the number of compounding periods per year: 12 for monthly, 4 for quarterly, or 1 for annual compounding.
  3. Calculate and compare the effective annual rate with the quoted nominal rate.

Rate comparison and limitations

This calculation accounts for compounding frequency, but it does not automatically include account fees, loan origination charges, insurance, taxes or a changing rate. For borrowing products, the legally disclosed annual percentage rate may follow rules that include certain fees and differ from this mathematical EAR. How to interpret the effective annual rate

The effective annual rate (EAR) expresses the effect of compounding over a full year. When a nominal annual rate is compounded m times per year, the standard formula is EAR = (1 + r/m)m − 1, where r is the nominal annual rate written as a decimal and m is the number of compounding periods. Convert the final decimal to a percentage by multiplying by 100.

Worked example: monthly compounding

Suppose a nominal annual rate is 12% and interest compounds monthly. Use r = 0.12 and m = 12: EAR = (1 + 0.12/12)12 − 1 ≈ 0.1268, or about 12.68%. The effective rate is higher than 12% because interest earned in earlier months can itself earn interest later in the year.

Compare offers on the same basis

Before comparing savings accounts or borrowing offers, check that both rates cover the same period and use the same compounding convention. A quoted nominal rate is not directly equivalent to an effective annual rate. For loans, also check whether the lender includes arrangement fees, account charges, insurance, payment timing or other costs in its disclosed APR or total-cost figure. The mathematical EAR calculated from a rate and frequency may exclude these cash-flow costs.

EAR is not the monthly payment rate

An annual effective rate should not simply be divided by 12 to obtain the equivalent monthly rate. The equivalent monthly rate for an EAR is (1 + EAR)1/12 − 1. This matters when translating an annual return into a monthly growth assumption. The correct method still depends on the product's terms and cash-flow schedule.

Effective interest rate FAQ

Why is the effective rate usually higher than the nominal rate?

With positive interest compounded more than once per year, interest can compound within the year, increasing the effective annual percentage. With annual compounding, the nominal and effective rates are equal under the standard formula.

Does EAR include fees?

Not automatically. A calculation based only on rate and compounding frequency excludes fees unless the tool explicitly asks for and incorporates those costs. Review the lender's formal APR, total repayment and fee disclosures for a real offer.

Can I compare a loan rate with a savings rate?

You can compare annualized rates as a starting point, but taxes, fees, access restrictions, risk and payment timing may differ. The highest headline rate is not necessarily the best overall product.

Effective Interest Rate Calculator

The effective interest rate shows the annual cost or return after the effect of compounding is included. It is useful when comparing nominal rates that compound at different frequencies.

Formula

Effective annual rate = (1 + r/n)n − 1, where r is the nominal annual rate as a decimal and n is the number of compounding periods per year.

Worked example

If a savings product quotes 12% nominal interest compounded monthly, use r = 0.12 and n = 12. The effective annual rate is about 12.68%, because interest is added to the balance throughout the year.

Nominal vs effective rate

A nominal rate is a stated annual rate before the compounding effect. The effective rate converts the compounding schedule into an annual equivalent, making comparisons easier when the underlying products use different compounding periods.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the effective rate always higher than the nominal rate?

For positive interest with more than one compounding period per year, the effective annual rate is higher than the nominal rate.

What if interest compounds annually?

With one compounding period per year, the effective annual rate equals the nominal annual rate.
