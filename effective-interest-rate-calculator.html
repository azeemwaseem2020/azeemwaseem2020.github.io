Effective Interest Rate Calculator

The effective interest rate shows the annual cost or return after the effect of compounding is included. It is useful when comparing nominal rates that compound at different frequencies.

Formula

Effective annual rate = (1 + r/n)n − 1, where r is the nominal annual rate as a decimal and n is the number of compounding periods per year.

Worked example

If a savings product quotes 12% nominal interest compounded monthly, use r = 0.12 and n = 12. The effective annual rate is about 12.68%, because interest is added to the balance throughout the year.

Nominal vs effective rate

A nominal rate is a stated annual rate before the compounding effect. The effective rate converts the compounding schedule into an annual equivalent, making comparisons easier when the underlying products use different compounding periods.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the effective rate always higher than the nominal rate?

For positive interest with more than one compounding period per year, the effective annual rate is higher than the nominal rate.

What if interest compounds annually?

With one compounding period per year, the effective annual rate equals the nominal annual rate.
