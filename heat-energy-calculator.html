Heat Energy Calculator Guide

Use this calculator to estimate heat transferred when a material changes temperature and its mass and specific heat capacity are known.

Formula

Q = mcΔT, where Q is heat energy in joules, m is mass in kilograms, c is specific heat capacity in J/(kg·°C), and ΔT is the temperature change.

Worked example

If 2 kg of a material has a specific heat capacity of 900 J/(kg·°C) and its temperature rises by 10°C, Q = 2 × 900 × 10 = 18,000 J.

Temperature change

The formula uses the difference between final and initial temperature. A change of 10°C has the same numerical size as a change of 10 K.

Sign and meaning

Using ΔT = final temperature − initial temperature, a positive result represents heat added in this simplified model; a negative result represents heat removed.

Limitations

The model assumes a known, approximately constant specific heat capacity. It does not include phase changes, latent heat, or heat lost to the surroundings.

How to use the heat energy calculator

  1. Enter mass in kilograms.
  2. Enter specific heat capacity in J/(kg·°C).
  3. Enter temperature change.
  4. Calculate heat energy in joules.

THERMAL MODEL CHECK

Heat transfer depends on the material and temperature change

For sensible heating or cooling under a constant specific heat approximation, Q = mcΔT. The equation describes energy transferred as heat when the relevant material and temperature range make the approximation appropriate.

Specific heat matters

Different materials require different amounts of energy for the same mass and temperature change. Use the correct specific-heat value and units.

Temperature difference

Only the temperature change enters this relationship. Celsius and kelvin have the same size increment for ΔT, provided the difference is used consistently.

Phase changes are different

Melting, boiling and other phase changes require latent heat terms rather than relying only on Q = mcΔT.
