Mechanical work and angle

For a constant force acting through a displacement, mechanical work is W = Fd cos θ. F is force, d is displacement and θ is the angle between the force direction and displacement. Work is measured in joules when force is in newtons and distance in metres.

Worked example

A 50 N force moves an object 4 m in the same direction as the motion, so θ = 0° and W = 50 × 4 × 1 = 200 J. If the force is perpendicular to the displacement, cos 90° = 0 and that force contributes zero mechanical work.

Practical interpretation

In real machines, friction, changing force and curved paths can require more detailed analysis. The calculator's constant-force model is best for a defined force and displacement. Always distinguish displacement from total path length.

FAQs

What if force opposes motion? The work can be negative. What is the unit? Joule. Does holding an object still require mechanical work on it? If there is no displacement, mechanical work on the object is zero even though the person may expend metabolic energy.

Mechanical work and force angle

For a constant force and displacement, mechanical work is W = Fd cos θ. The angle is measured between the force and displacement directions. In SI units, newtons multiplied by metres produce joules.

Worked example

A 50 N force moves an object 4 m in the same direction, so θ = 0° and work is 200 J. A force at 90° contributes zero work along that displacement because cos 90° = 0.

Practical interpretation

Changing forces, friction and curved paths may require more detailed analysis. Also distinguish displacement from total distance travelled. Negative work can occur when a force opposes motion.

FAQs

What is the unit? Joule. Can work be negative? Yes. Does holding an object still do mechanical work on it? With zero displacement, mechanical work on the object is zero.
