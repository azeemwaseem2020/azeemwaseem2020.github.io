Pakistan Bank Loan Calculator — Monthly Payment & Total Cost

A transparent way to estimate a loan payment and total repayment from the amount, rate and term shown in a lender offer. Use the same inputs when comparing two offers so the comparison is based on mathematics rather than a guessed bank rate.

How to use this for a bank offer

Select the bank or financing product you are comparing, then enter the current financed amount, rate and term from the bank's official quotation. This page does not publish guessed bank rates and is not affiliated with HBL, UBL, Bank Alfalah, MCB, Meezan Bank or Faysal Bank.

How to compare a real bank offer

First record the financed amount, annual rate or markup, repayment term and payment frequency from the lender's quotation. Then run those exact inputs here. If two offers have different fees, down payments or repayment structures, keep those differences visible instead of forcing them into one headline rate.

What this calculator can and cannot compare

  • Can compare: monthly payment, scheduled repayment and the arithmetic interest/profit-equivalent amount under the model shown above.
  • Cannot confirm: a bank's current rate, approval decision, Shariah structure, insurance/takaful, taxes, processing charges or contractual settlement terms.
  • Best practice: use the lender's official quotation and repayment schedule as the source of truth.

Conventional loan vs Islamic financing

A conventional reducing-balance loan and an Islamic financing product such as Ijarah may use different contractual structures. Do not enter a rental or profit figure into this calculator and assume it represents the legal or Shariah structure of the product. For Islamic financing, compare the provider's documented payment schedule and total obligation.

Check the rate type before entering it

Bank quotations can describe pricing in different ways. Before entering a percentage, identify whether it is a reducing-balance rate, flat-rate figure, benchmark-plus-spread, variable markup or another product-specific measure. The calculator's standard payment formula should not be used to reinterpret a contractual rate that follows a different repayment method.

A practical quotation checklist

  1. Record the financed principal after the down payment.
  2. Record the stated rate and whether it is fixed or variable.
  3. Record the repayment term and payment frequency.
  4. List processing fees, insurance or takaful, taxes and registration costs separately.
  5. Compare the calculator output with the lender's official repayment schedule before making a decision.

This approach makes the calculator useful as a checking tool without implying that Calcora knows a lender's current product pricing.

What can change the final bank payment?

Processing fees, insurance or takaful, registration, down payment, variable pricing, payment dates and contract structure can change the actual cash cost. Reconcile the result with the lender's amortization or payment schedule.

Pakistan Bank Loan Calculator: Installments and Total Cost

This calculator estimates repayments from a principal, rate and tenure. For a Pakistani bank loan, treat the result as an illustration until you compare it with the lender's written offer, fee schedule and repayment plan. Conventional reducing-balance loans, flat-rate offers and Islamic financing structures can calculate costs differently.

Formula and calculation method

For a standard fixed-rate amortizing loan, EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1), where P is principal, r is the monthly rate and n is the number of monthly payments. For a zero rate, payment = P ÷ n. Total scheduled repayment = EMI × n; financing cost before extra charges = total repayment − principal.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the principal amount in PKR.
  2. Use the annual rate from the actual offer, not an unrelated advertised rate.
  3. Enter tenure in the unit requested and confirm whether it means months or years.
  4. Compare the result with the bank's written schedule, fees and early-settlement terms.

Worked example

For an illustrative PKR 1,000,000 loan at 18% nominal annual interest over 36 months, the monthly rate is 0.18 ÷ 12 = 0.015. The standard EMI formula gives roughly PKR 36,152 monthly, with scheduled payments around PKR 1.30 million overall. This is a mathematical illustration, not a quote from a specific bank.

Interpretation, assumptions and common mistakes

The estimate may exclude processing charges, insurance or takaful, taxes, variable-rate changes and late-payment charges. Ask whether the quoted rate is fixed, how early settlement is handled and whether the repayment schedule changes when a benchmark rate changes. Islamic financing contracts may use a different structure; do not present a simple EMI as an approved financing quotation. The calculator does not confirm eligibility or current bank rates.

Decision tip: Compare offers using the same principal and tenure, and look at both the monthly installment and total repayment. A lower advertised rate does not necessarily mean the lowest overall cost if fees or compulsory add-ons differ. Ask for the payment schedule in writing and check whether a missed payment, rate reset or early settlement changes the amount owed. Keep your own budget and emergency expenses in mind before committing to any borrowing.

Frequently asked questions

Is this an official bank offer?

No. The bank's approved offer and written repayment schedule determine the actual terms.

Why may my bank's installment differ?

Fees, rate conventions, insurance, variable rates and financing structure can change the payment.

Does a longer tenure reduce cost?

It often reduces the monthly payment but can increase total cost because repayment lasts longer.
