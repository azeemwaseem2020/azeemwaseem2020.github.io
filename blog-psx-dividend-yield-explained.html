PSX Dividend Yield Explained: DPS, Price & Yield on Cost

Dividend yield turns a dividend payment into a percentage of the share price. It is useful for comparing income relative to price, but it is not the same thing as total investment return.

The basic formula

Dividend yield = annual dividend per share ÷ share price × 100. If a company pays Rs. 12 per share over a year and the relevant share price is Rs. 240, the indicated yield is 5%.

What does DPS mean?

DPS means dividend per share. When several cash dividends are paid during a year, add the eligible payments for the period before calculating an annual yield. Keep special or one-off distributions clearly separated if you are comparing companies or years.

Yield on current price vs yield on cost

If you bought at Rs. 180 and the annual DPS is Rs. 12, your yield on cost is 6.67%. If the share now trades at Rs. 240, the yield on the current price is 5%. Both calculations can be mathematically correct because they answer different questions.

Dividend yield is not total return

A share can pay a 5% dividend and still produce a negative overall return if its market price falls by more than the income received. Conversely, price appreciation can make total return higher than the dividend yield.

Costs and taxes matter for net income

A simple yield formula is a gross arithmetic measure. Brokerage, taxes, withholding and other applicable costs can change what reaches an investor. The correct treatment depends on the transaction and current rules.

Common mistakes

  • Using one quarterly dividend instead of the full annual DPS.
  • Mixing a historical dividend with an unrelated current price without explaining the period.
  • Calling dividend yield a guaranteed return.
  • Comparing yields without checking whether distributions were recurring or exceptional.
