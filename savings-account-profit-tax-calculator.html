Savings Account Profit Tax Calculator Pakistan: How to Estimate Withholding

This tool estimates tax on a profit amount from a savings account or deposit. Enter the profit subject to the rate you have verified—not the deposit balance itself. Withholding deducted by a bank and a person's final annual tax position are related but are not always interchangeable concepts.

Formula

Estimated withholding = eligible profit × applicable rate ÷ 100. Net profit after this assumed withholding = eligible profit − estimated withholding. This simple calculation does not establish which rate applies or account for any separate tax adjustment, exemption, credit or filing obligation.

How to use the calculator

  1. Read the bank statement or profit certificate and identify the relevant profit amount.
  2. Confirm the rate and treatment for your account, taxpayer category and tax year from current official guidance or the bank's documentation.
  3. Enter the profit amount and the rate as a percentage.
  4. Compare the result with the withholding shown on your statement and retain supporting records.

Worked example

If eligible profit is PKR 50,000 and a hypothetical verified rate is 15%, the arithmetic estimate is 50,000 × 15 ÷ 100 = PKR 7,500, leaving PKR 42,500 after that assumed deduction. The 15% rate here is illustrative only; it should not be treated as a claim about the current rate for every Pakistani depositor or account.

Common mistakes

Do not apply a profit-tax rate to the principal balance. Do not assume all banks, account types or taxpayers receive identical treatment. Keep the gross profit, tax withheld, net credit and any bank fees as separate line items. For tax filing, use official statements and current FBR guidance rather than relying solely on a calculator output.

Frequently asked questions

Do I enter my full account balance?

No. Enter the profit amount relevant to the tax calculation, not the deposit principal.

Is bank withholding always my final tax?

Not necessarily. Your final position depends on the rules and your circumstances.

Where should I verify the rate?

Check current FBR guidance and your bank's statement or written explanation for the relevant tax year.
