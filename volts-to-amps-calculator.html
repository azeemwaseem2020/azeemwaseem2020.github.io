Volts to Amps Calculator

Calculate electrical current from voltage when you also know power or resistance. Voltage by itself is not enough to determine current.

Volts to amps formula

For a DC or unity-power-factor relationship, current from power is I = P ÷ V. If resistance is known instead, Ohm's law gives I = V ÷ R. For AC loads with a non-unity power factor, real power also depends on power factor and the applicable single- or three-phase relationship.

Worked example

If a device uses 1,150 W at 230 V, current = 1,150 ÷ 230 = 5 A.

Why volts alone are not enough

Current depends on the load. Two devices connected to the same voltage can draw very different currents because their power demand or impedance differs.

Common uses

This conversion is useful for estimating appliance current, checking power-supply loads, and translating electrical specifications when the required power or resistance is known.

FAQ

How many amps is 230 V? There is no single answer without another quantity such as power, resistance, or impedance.

What is the formula from watts to amps? For the simple P = VI relationship, I = P/V.

Can I use this for three-phase AC? Three-phase calculations require the appropriate voltage definition, power factor, and phase relationship; this simple calculator is not a full three-phase load calculator.

Converting volts and power to current

For a simple DC or resistive calculation, current can be found from I = P ÷ V, where P is power in watts and V is voltage in volts. This relationship assumes the entered power and voltage describe the same electrical condition.

Worked example

A 240 W load at 12 V draws 240 ÷ 12 = 20 A. If resistance is known instead, Ohm's law gives I = V ÷ R, which is a different input route to current.

A practical Pakistan example

For a 12 V solar or battery-powered load, estimating current helps with selecting suitable wiring, fuses and battery capacity. Real installations require appropriate electrical design, protection and equipment ratings rather than relying on one arithmetic result.

Important assumptions

AC loads with power factor, motors and other non-resistive equipment can require additional information. Do not assume P ÷ V gives the full supply current for every AC appliance.

FAQ

How do I calculate amps from watts and volts? Divide watts by volts for the simple relationship I = P/V.

What if voltage is zero? The division is undefined.

Does this always work for AC? Not without considering power factor and the relevant electrical system.
