Volts to Amps Calculator

Calculate electrical current from voltage when you also know power or resistance. Voltage by itself is not enough to determine current.

Volts to amps formula

For a DC or unity-power-factor relationship, current from power is I = P ÷ V. If resistance is known instead, Ohm's law gives I = V ÷ R. For AC loads with a non-unity power factor, real power also depends on power factor and the applicable single- or three-phase relationship.

Worked example

If a device uses 1,150 W at 230 V, current = 1,150 ÷ 230 = 5 A.

Why volts alone are not enough

Current depends on the load. Two devices connected to the same voltage can draw very different currents because their power demand or impedance differs.

Common uses

This conversion is useful for estimating appliance current, checking power-supply loads, and translating electrical specifications when the required power or resistance is known.

FAQ

How many amps is 230 V? There is no single answer without another quantity such as power, resistance, or impedance.

What is the formula from watts to amps? For the simple P = VI relationship, I = P/V.

Choosing the right electrical model

For a simple DC load, current can be estimated with I = P / V when power and voltage are known. For a resistive load, I = V / R can be used when resistance is known. These formulas are different ways of describing the same circuit relationship, but they require different inputs.

AC loads

For single-phase AC equipment, real power can depend on power factor, so a simple watts-to-amps calculation may overestimate or underestimate current if power factor is ignored. Check the equipment nameplate and electrical specifications before sizing wiring, breakers or other protective equipment.

Calculation method: voltage needs another quantity

Voltage alone does not determine current. If power is known for a simple DC or unity-power-factor model, use I = P ÷ V. If resistance is known, Ohm's law gives I = V ÷ R. Choose the equation that matches the information you actually have.

Practical example

For a 230 V device rated at 1,150 W, the simplified current is 1,150 ÷ 230 = 5 A. If instead a resistive load has 46 Ω resistance at 230 V, I = 230 ÷ 46 = 5 A.

Why does the calculator require power?

Why voltage alone does not determine current

This calculator estimates current from power and voltage using I = P ÷ V. A 120 W load supplied at 12 V draws 10 A under the simple model. If voltage is zero, the division is undefined, so enter a valid non-zero voltage.

DC loads versus AC loads

The simple relationship is suitable for DC power calculations and idealized resistive cases. In AC circuits, real power can depend on power factor; single-phase current may require I = P ÷ (V × power factor) for the relevant model. Three-phase systems use a different relationship. Do not apply the basic result to a complex AC load without checking its specification.

Check the rating and wiring

Use the actual operating voltage and power rating, not a nominal figure that does not match the equipment. This estimate does not select a cable size, breaker, fuse or safe current limit. Electrical installations should follow applicable codes and be assessed by a qualified professional.

Unit conversion

Enter watts and volts to obtain amperes. If power is listed in kilowatts, convert it to watts first: 0.5 kW equals 500 W. A unit mismatch can make the current estimate incorrect by a factor of 1,000.
