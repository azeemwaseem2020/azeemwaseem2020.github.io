Marla to Square Feet Converter Pakistan

A Pakistan-focused marla to square feet converter that makes the measurement convention explicit instead of hiding a regional assumption.

Why marla conversion needs a convention

There is no single universal square-foot definition of a marla across every Pakistani property market. Two commonly encountered conventions are 272.25 square feet and 225 square feet. Your registry, housing scheme, developer or property documents should determine which one applies.

Formula

Square feet = marla × selected square-feet-per-marla value. A kanal is commonly 20 marla under the same local convention, but verify the standard for your transaction.

Marla to Square Feet in Pakistan: Choose the Correct Standard

A marla is a traditional land-area unit used across Pakistan, but its square-foot value is not uniform in every city, housing scheme or property record. This converter lets you select a convention so that the result reflects the standard in your documents instead of silently assuming one definition.

Formula

Area in square feet = number of marla × square feet per marla. Under the 272.25-square-foot convention, 1 marla equals 272.25 sq ft. Under the 225-square-foot convention, 1 marla equals 225 sq ft. These alternatives produce different totals, so the choice matters for plot comparisons and transaction paperwork.

How to use the converter

  1. Enter the plot size in marla.
  2. Select the square-foot convention stated by your registry, approved layout, developer or property documents.
  3. Calculate the equivalent square footage.
  4. For a sale, purchase or construction plan, verify the same convention with the relevant authority or surveyor.

Worked example

For a 5-marla plot, the 272.25 convention gives 5 × 272.25 = 1,361.25 sq ft. The 225 convention gives 5 × 225 = 1,125 sq ft. The difference is 236.25 sq ft, which can materially change how a plot is compared or described. Neither option should be selected merely because it produces the larger area.

Marla, kanal and measurement checks

Twenty marla are often treated as one kanal within a given convention, but verify the local standard before converting larger plots. Also check whether a property advertisement quotes covered area, plot area, gross area or an approximate marketing size. These are not necessarily the same measurement. This tool converts units; it does not verify a property's legal dimensions, boundaries or ownership documents.

Frequently asked questions

Which marla size should I choose?

Use the convention explicitly recorded by the relevant registry, scheme or property documents.

Is every 5-marla plot the same size?

No. The stated unit convention and the actual surveyed plot dimensions both matter.

Can this converter confirm plot boundaries?

No. It performs a unit conversion only; consult approved plans or a qualified surveyor for boundaries.
