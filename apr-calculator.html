APR versus interest rate

An interest rate describes the rate used to calculate interest, while APR can incorporate certain borrowing costs depending on the jurisdiction and disclosure rules. The exact definition and required fees vary by product and regulation.

Why APR matters when comparing loans

Two loans can advertise the same nominal rate but have different fees and therefore different overall borrowing costs. Compare the lender's disclosed APR and assumptions rather than treating every percentage as interchangeable.

APR FAQ

Is APR the same as APY?

No. APR is commonly used for borrowing costs, while APY generally describes annualized yield including compounding for deposit or investment products.

Is a calculator result an official disclosure?

No. Use the lender's official disclosure for a regulated loan or credit offer.

APR explained

APR, or annual percentage rate, is an annualized measure of borrowing cost. Depending on the product and jurisdiction, APR can incorporate certain fees in addition to the stated interest rate.

APR vs interest rate

The nominal interest rate is the rate used to calculate interest. APR is intended to express borrowing cost on an annualized basis under specified disclosure assumptions. Always compare offers using the lender's stated assumptions.

Worked example

Suppose two loans advertise the same nominal rate but one has substantially higher eligible fees. Their APRs may differ because the borrowing costs are not identical.

Important limitation

A calculator estimate is not an official regulated disclosure. Actual APR can depend on payment timing, fees, term, compounding and local rules.

FAQ

Is APR the same as APY?

No. APR is generally associated with borrowing cost, while APY expresses an effective annual yield after compounding.

Why can lender APR differ from my estimate?

Fee treatment, timing, rounding and regulatory calculation rules can differ.

APR calculation context

APR expresses borrowing cost as an annualized percentage so loan offers can be compared on a common basis. This calculator focuses on the relationship between principal, stated rate, fees and repayment assumptions; the exact APR method can vary by jurisdiction and lender.

APR and the real cost of borrowing

Annual percentage rate is intended to help compare borrowing costs by expressing an annualized cost under defined assumptions. The exact treatment of fees and compounding depends on the credit product and applicable rules.

Why APR is useful

A stated interest rate alone may not show the full cost of credit. Comparing APR alongside the loan amount, term, payment schedule and disclosed fees can give a more useful picture of competing offers.

Check the lender's terms

Do not assume every fee is included in the same way. Use the provider's disclosure for the official APR and total repayment figure, especially when a result will be used for a borrowing decision.

APR FAQ

Is APR the same as the interest rate?

No. The interest rate is one component of borrowing cost; APR is designed to express an annualized cost using defined assumptions and applicable charges.
