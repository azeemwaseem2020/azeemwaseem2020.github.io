Electric power formula

For a basic DC or simplified resistive calculation, P = V × I. P is power in watts, V is voltage in volts, and I is current in amperes. Related resistive forms are P = I²R and P = V²/R.

Worked examples

At 12 V and 2 A, power is 12 × 2 = 24 W. A 240 W load at 12 V would draw 20 A in the simplified model.

Watts, volts, and amps explained

Voltage is potential difference, current describes charge flow, and power describes the rate of energy transfer. Keep the units consistent when applying the formulas.

DC versus AC

For many AC loads, real power can depend on RMS voltage, RMS current, power factor, and system configuration. This simple P = VI calculation should not be treated as a complete mains-power analysis.

Practical uses

Use this calculator for basic electronics, battery-load estimates, introductory electrical calculations, and quick checks. For electrical installations, use appropriate engineering and safety practices.

Electric power FAQ

How do I calculate watts?

Multiply voltage in volts by current in amps for the simplified model: P = V × I.

Can I calculate current from watts?

Yes when voltage is known: I = P ÷ V. AC systems may require power factor and other information.

Is power the same as energy?

No. Power is a rate measured in watts; energy is accumulated over time and can be measured in watt-hours or joules.
