Electrical power formula

For a DC circuit or a simplified resistive calculation, electrical power is P = V × I, where P is power in watts, V is voltage in volts, and I is current in amperes.

When resistance is known, related forms are P = I²R and P = V²/R. AC systems can require power factor and other electrical-system details, so this simple calculator should not be treated as a complete AC power analysis.

Worked example

For 12 V and 2 A, P = 12 × 2 = 24 W.

Understanding watts, volts, and amps

Voltage describes electrical potential difference, current describes charge flow, and power describes the rate at which electrical energy is transferred. Keeping units consistent is essential for a correct result.

Where this calculator is useful

It can provide a quick estimate for basic electrical calculations, electronics projects, battery loads, and simple DC power checks. For mains or complex installations, use appropriate engineering methods and safety procedures.

FAQ

How do I calculate watts from volts and amps? Multiply volts by amps: watts = volts × amps.

Can I use this for AC appliances? Only for simplified cases. Real AC power may depend on power factor and system configuration.
