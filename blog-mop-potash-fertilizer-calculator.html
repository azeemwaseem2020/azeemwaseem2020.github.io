MOP Potash Fertilizer Calculator: How to Calculate 0-0-60

For MOP, the arithmetic is not the difficult part. The important checks are the K₂O basis, the field unit, the actual product grade and whether other potassium sources are already in the plan.

What does 0-0-60 mean?

A product labelled 0-0-60 contains 60% K₂O by weight and no labelled N or P₂O₅. Always confirm the guaranteed analysis on the actual product label.

Calculate K₂O supplied

K₂O supplied = MOP weight × 0.60

For example, 50 kg of 0-0-60 MOP supplies 30 kg K₂O on the labelled basis.

Calculate MOP from a K₂O target

If a trusted recommendation requires 60 kg K₂O for the selected area and the product is 60% K₂O, the mathematical MOP rate is 60 ÷ 0.60 = 100 kg for that same area basis.

Elemental K versus K₂O

Fertilizer labels commonly use K₂O, while some technical recommendations use elemental potassium. A commonly used conversion is K = K₂O × 0.83. Keep the target and product on the same basis before calculating.

Acres versus hectares

Use the same area basis as the recommendation. A rate stated in kg/ha should not be multiplied directly by an acreage figure.

Calculate bags

Total bags = total MOP kilograms ÷ actual bag net weight. If purchasing whole bags, round the purchase quantity up while keeping the calculated field application separate.

Cost per kg K₂O

For nutrient economics, compare cost per unit of K₂O rather than only the bag price. For a 60% K₂O product, product cost per kg of K₂O is approximately product cost per kg ÷ 0.60.

Where MOP fits in an NPK plan

MOP supplies potassium but does not supply labelled nitrogen or P₂O₅. If a plan also needs N or P₂O₅, those nutrients must be credited from other selected sources without double-counting.

Before you trust the number

Do one quick back-check: multiply the calculated product rate by the nutrient fraction and make sure it returns the intended nutrient rate. Then check the area conversion and the bag or volume conversion separately. Keeping those steps separate makes a wrong unit much easier to spot.

Common mistakes

  • Confusing K₂O with elemental K.
  • Using 60% when the actual product grade differs.
  • Mixing kg/acre and kg/ha.
  • Ignoring other potassium sources in the plan.
  • Assuming a potassium target without a suitable crop and soil recommendation.

FAQ

How much K₂O is in a 50 kg MOP bag?

At 60% K₂O, 50 × 0.60 = 30 kg K₂O.

How much MOP gives 50 kg K₂O?

50 ÷ 0.60 = about 83.33 kg product.

Is MOP suitable for every crop?

Product selection depends on crop, soil, salinity or chloride considerations and the nutrient recommendation. Use appropriate local agronomic guidance.

MOP / POTASH • FIELD LESSONS

Potash lessons: calculate K₂O, then check the field context

MOP is mathematically straightforward, but potassium management is not only a percentage calculation. Crop, soil, chloride sensitivity, existing potassium and application method can change the appropriate product choice.

Pakistan: potassium should follow evidence, not habit

FAO's Pakistan crop guidance notes that potassium application should be based on soil analysis in the relevant crop context. The practical lesson is to avoid adding MOP simply because a neighbouring field uses it.

FAO Pakistan crop fertilizer guidance

India: balanced nutrient management is broader than N

ICAR campaigns emphasize balanced fertilization and soil-test-based decisions, including attention to nutrients beyond nitrogen. A potash calculation therefore fits inside a broader nutrient plan rather than standing alone.

ICAR balanced fertilization campaign

Useful MOP field hacks

  • Write K₂O on the calculator sheet. Do not shorten it to “potash kg” without recording whether the number is product mass or nutrient mass.
  • Back-check every result. For 0-0-60, product kg × 0.60 should return K₂O kg.
  • Compare nutrient cost, not bag cost. A 50-kg bag tells you little until the K₂O percentage and price are known.
  • Keep chloride considerations visible. Product selection can depend on crop and soil conditions; do not assume MOP is interchangeable with every potassium source.
  • Use a calibrated container or spreader. If a manual scoop is used repeatedly, weigh it with the actual product first and re-check periodically.
Rule of thumb: the calculator can tell you how much product supplies a K₂O target; soil and crop evidence must tell you whether that target and source are appropriate.
