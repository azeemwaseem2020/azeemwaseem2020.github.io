Specific Heat Calculator

Editorial information: Written and maintained by Abdul Qadir · Last updated: October 17, 2026

Calculate specific heat capacity, heat energy, mass or temperature change.

Formula

Heat relationship: Q = mcΔT.

Worked example

Using 2 kg of water, a specific heat capacity of about 4,186 J/(kg·K), and a 1.2 K temperature increase gives Q = 2 × 4,186 × 1.2 = 10,046.4 J, assuming negligible heat loss and no phase change. The value is an illustrative calculation; specific heat varies with material and conditions.

Assumptions and units

Use consistent SI units unless the calculator explicitly says otherwise. Real physical systems can require additional effects beyond these introductory equations.

When the simple heat equation is not enough

The equation Q = mcΔT estimates sensible heat when the material’s specific heat can be treated as constant over the temperature range. It does not model energy absorbed or released during melting, boiling or another phase change. A real experiment may also need a correction for the container, heat lost to the surroundings and measurement uncertainty.

Check the units before calculating

If c is expressed in J/(kg·K), use mass in kilograms and temperature change in kelvins or degrees Celsius; a temperature interval of 1 K equals an interval of 1°C. Do not mix grams with kilograms or joules with kilojoules without converting consistently. For mixtures or changing material properties, use an appropriate value or a more complete model.

What does a negative temperature change mean?

A negative ΔT describes cooling under the chosen sign convention, so Q is negative when the mass and specific heat are positive. The magnitude indicates the energy change in the idealized model; the physical process may still require a separate account of heat transfer and losses.
