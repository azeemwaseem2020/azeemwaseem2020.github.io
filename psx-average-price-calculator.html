WEIGHT → AVERAGE → REVIEW

What the result means

The result is a weighted average purchase cost. It is not the simple average of two prices because the number of shares in each purchase matters.

A lower average does not automatically make a stock a better investment. Treat averaging as a scenario to analyse, not a recommendation.

PSX weighted average price formula

New average = [(existing shares × existing average) + (new shares × new price)] ÷ total shares. This method gives each purchase weight according to its share quantity.

Worked PSX averaging example

If you hold 1,000 shares at PKR 100 and buy 500 more at PKR 80, total purchase value becomes PKR 140,000 across 1,500 shares. The weighted average is PKR 93.33 per share before additional charges.

FAQ

What if I buy at a higher price?

The weighted average normally moves upward when the new purchase price is above the existing average.

Can I use this for several PSX purchases?

Yes. Treat the current weighted average and total shares as the existing position, then add the next purchase. Repeating the process produces the updated weighted average.

EXPERT VALIDATION LAYER

Corporate-action boundary

Weighted average arithmetic covers entered purchases. Rights issues, bonus shares, splits, fees or broker-specific cost-basis treatment can change the account record and should be reconciled separately.

What the calculator proves

The calculator should be used as a transparent arithmetic check, not as a claim about the broker's official cost basis.

What must be verified externally

WEIGHTED COST BASIS

Why the average-price result is auditable

Weighted average cost is a transparent arithmetic calculation: total purchase cost divided by total shares. The important distinction is between the mathematical average and the real account cost after fees or corporate actions.

Purchase 1

Quantity × purchase price produces the first purchase cost.

Purchase 2

Quantity × purchase price produces the additional purchase cost.

Combined basis

Add both costs and divide by combined shares to obtain the weighted average.

Account reconciliation

Compare the result with your broker's recorded average cost when fees, rights, bonuses or adjustments affect the account.

How weighted average price works

The weighted average cost of your PSX holdings is based on the total purchase cost divided by the total number of shares. A larger purchase at one price therefore has more influence on the average than a smaller purchase at another price.

Important PSX checks

Keep brokerage, taxes, fees and other transaction costs separate unless you deliberately include them in the inputs. Market prices can change after the calculation, and the result is a cost-basis estimate rather than a prediction of future performance.
