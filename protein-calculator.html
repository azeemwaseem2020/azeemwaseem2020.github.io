How the estimate is calculated

The calculator multiplies body weight in kilograms by the protein target in grams per kilogram. For example, 70 kg at 1.2 g/kg gives an estimated 84 g of protein per day.

Important note

This is a planning estimate rather than individualized medical or dietary advice. Protein needs can vary with age, activity, goals, pregnancy, illness and other circumstances. Use an appropriate target for your situation and consult a qualified professional when needed.

Daily protein estimate: formula and guide

This calculator estimates a daily protein amount from body weight and a selected protein target in grams per kilogram. It lets you test different assumptions; it does not decide which target is appropriate for every person.

Formula

Daily protein (g) = body weight (kg) × target (g/kg). For example, a 70 kg person using a target of 1.2 g/kg gets 70 × 1.2 = 84 g per day. The output is an estimate based on the target entered, not a laboratory measurement of nutritional need.

How to use it

  1. Enter body weight in kilograms.
  2. Choose a grams-per-kilogram target that is appropriate to your circumstances.
  3. Select Calculate Protein and review the estimated daily total.
  4. Use the estimate as a planning reference and consider protein across meals and food sources.

Choosing a target carefully

Protein needs vary with age, activity level, training goals, total energy intake, pregnancy, recovery from illness and other factors. Some medical conditions require individualized protein guidance, and more protein is not automatically better. Food quality, overall dietary pattern and adequate energy intake also matter. This tool does not prescribe a diet or assess kidney function, malnutrition or other health conditions.

Frequently asked questions

Does this calculate protein from food?

No. It estimates a daily target. To total protein from meals, use reliable nutrition labels or a food-tracking resource and add the amounts consumed.

What does g/kg mean?

It means grams of protein per kilogram of body weight. Multiplying body weight in kilograms by this rate gives grams per day.

Should everyone use the same target?

No. Requirements depend on individual circumstances. Seek qualified advice if you have a medical condition, are pregnant, are planning a restrictive diet or are unsure which target is appropriate.

Protein Calculator: Daily Target, Formula and Food Planning

A protein calculator estimates a daily target from body weight and a selected grams-per-kilogram guideline. Protein supports tissue maintenance and repair, but the appropriate target depends on age, activity, overall diet and health. The result is a planning estimate, not a diagnosis or a substitute for individualized advice when medical conditions affect nutrition.

Formula and calculation method

Daily protein (g) = body weight (kg) × target (g/kg/day). For example, a selected target of 1.2 g/kg/day for a 70 kg adult gives 70 × 1.2 = 84 g/day. The multiplier matters: general adult references, athletic goals and clinical diets may use different recommendations.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter body weight in kilograms, converting from pounds if needed (1 kg ≈ 2.20462 lb).
  2. Choose a grams-per-kilogram target suitable for your situation.
  3. Calculate and remember that the result is total protein from all foods and drinks.
  4. Plan meals with varied protein sources while considering overall nutritional balance.

Worked example

For a 70 kg adult using an illustrative target of 1.2 g/kg/day, the estimate is 84 g daily. This amount may be distributed across meals. Actual foods differ in protein content, so package labels or a reliable food database help with tracking. The example explains arithmetic; it does not recommend the same target for everyone.

Interpretation, assumptions and common mistakes

Protein grams are not the same as grams of food. Eggs, dairy, beans, lentils, fish, meat, soy and nuts provide different amounts and also contribute other nutrients. A calculator cannot assess total energy intake, dietary quality or medical suitability. People with kidney disease or other conditions requiring dietary management should follow advice from their clinician or registered dietitian.

Planning tip: Use the estimate to review a typical day of eating rather than trying to hit a number perfectly at every meal. Nutrition labels and serving sizes can differ, and total intake varies from day to day. If you track intake, use consistent portions and consider the broader pattern over time. If a prescribed diet conflicts with this general estimate, follow the individualized plan from your healthcare professional rather than the calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Does the result mean grams of meat or beans?

No. It means grams of protein, not the total weight of the food.

Should everyone use the same target?

No. Needs vary with age, activity, health and other circumstances.

Can more protein replace a balanced diet?

No. Overall energy, fiber, vitamins, minerals and food variety still matter.
