How calculator work is checked

The review process focuses on the actual calculation: define the inputs and units, implement the formula, test representative examples, check boundary cases, compare the output with an independent calculation, and document assumptions that can change outside the calculator. For pages involving tax, finance, agriculture, markets or equipment sizing, changing external facts are treated separately from the arithmetic.

Examples of documented software checks

For transparency, the repository includes automated runtime checks for selected finance calculators. The EMI tests compare a PKR 100,000 loan at 10% over five years with a reducing-balance result of PKR 2,124.70 monthly and PKR 127,482.27 total scheduled repayment; they also check a zero-rate case and invalid numeric inputs. The Zakat tests check a worked arithmetic result of PKR 800,000 net listed assets and PKR 20,000 at 2.5%, plus missing-Nisab and numeric-overflow handling. These are examples of software verification, not proof that every possible input or external rule has been independently audited.

What users can expect from Calcora pages

My goal as maintainer is to keep each calculator focused on a real calculation rather than filling pages with generic search-oriented text. Where a formula can be shown, the page should make the inputs, units and method understandable. Where a result depends on an assumption, the assumption should be stated instead of hidden behind a single number.

For practical calculators, examples are used to make the method easier to verify. For pages that depend on changing rules or published information, such as tax rates, market-related calculations or agricultural guidance, the relevant external information should be identified separately from the underlying arithmetic. This helps users distinguish a calculation from a claim about current policy, prices or conditions.
