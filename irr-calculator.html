IRR formula

IRR is the discount rate r that makes net present value equal to zero: 0 = Σ CFₜ/(1+r)ᵗ. Because the equation is solved numerically, some cash-flow patterns can produce multiple IRRs or no conventional solution.

Worked example

For an initial outflow of −10,000 followed by 6,000 and 6,000 in the next two periods, IRR is the rate that makes the discounted inflows equal the 10,000 initial investment.

How to use

  1. List cash flows in chronological order.
  2. Use negative values for investments/outflows and positive values for inflows.
  3. Calculate IRR and review whether the cash-flow pattern has a meaningful solution.
