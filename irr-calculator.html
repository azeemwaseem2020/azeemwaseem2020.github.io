IRR formula

IRR is the discount rate r that makes net present value equal to zero: 0 = Σ CFₜ/(1+r)ᵗ. Because the equation is solved numerically, some cash-flow patterns can produce multiple IRRs or no conventional solution.

Worked example

For an initial outflow of −10,000 followed by 6,000 and 6,000 in the next two periods, IRR is the rate that makes the discounted inflows equal the 10,000 initial investment.

How to use

  1. List cash flows in chronological order.
  2. Use negative values for investments/outflows and positive values for inflows.
  3. Calculate IRR and review whether the cash-flow pattern has a meaningful solution.

What IRR represents

Internal rate of return is the discount rate that makes the net present value of a series of cash flows equal to zero.

Worked example

For an investment of Rs. 100,000 followed by Rs. 60,000 and Rs. 60,000 in two later periods, IRR is the rate solving NPV = 0; numerical methods are normally used.

A practical Pakistan example

A Pakistani business can use IRR to compare project cash-flow patterns, but the timing and reliability of each cash flow matter as much as the resulting percentage.

Important assumptions

IRR can have multiple or no solutions for unusual cash-flow patterns, so it should be checked against NPV and the project's assumptions.

FAQ

Can the result be treated as exact? Only when the inputs, assumptions and method are appropriate.

Why can another source show a different result? It may use different definitions, rates, rounding or assumptions.

How to interpret IRR

Internal rate of return is the discount rate that makes the net present value of a series of cash flows equal to zero. It is useful for comparing investment cash-flow patterns, but it can be ambiguous when cash flows change sign more than once. A calculated IRR should therefore be checked against the actual timing and scale of the cash flows.

For example, an initial investment followed by regular positive annual cash flows may produce one economically meaningful IRR. If a project has later investment outflows, multiple mathematical IRRs can occur. In that situation, NPV at a chosen discount rate can provide a clearer decision framework. Always enter the cash flows in chronological order and keep the period spacing consistent.
