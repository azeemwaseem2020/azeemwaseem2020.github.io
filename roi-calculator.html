Where this calculator fits

ROI connects investment cost, return, profit and percentage return. It is a return-measurement calculation and should remain distinct from future-value investment growth calculations.

Understanding return on investment

ROI compares a gain or loss with the amount invested. It is useful for a simple project or investment comparison, but it does not automatically account for time. Two projects can have the same ROI while one takes months and the other takes years. For time-sensitive comparisons, pair ROI with a time-based measure.

Formula

ROI = (final value − initial investment) ÷ initial investment × 100. If the investment loses value, ROI becomes negative. Costs should be included in the initial investment or deducted from the return consistently.

Worked example

An investment of Rs 200,000 grows to Rs 230,000. Gain = Rs 30,000. ROI = 30,000 ÷ 200,000 × 100 = 15%. If Rs 10,000 of additional costs are ignored, the reported ROI can overstate the economic result.

Pakistan-specific example

A small business owner in Pakistan can use ROI to compare a machine purchase, marketing campaign or project after defining all relevant costs. For shares, brokerage, taxes and other transaction costs can materially change the net result.

Common questions

Does ROI include time? Not by itself.

Can ROI be negative? Yes, when the final value is below the investment.

Should fees be included? For a meaningful net comparison, relevant fees should be included.

Is ROI the same as annual return? No. Annualized return accounts for the time period.
