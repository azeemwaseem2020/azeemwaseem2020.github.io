Triangle calculations and consistency

A triangle has three sides and three angles, but the available information determines which calculation is possible. A right triangle can use the Pythagorean theorem, while a general triangle may require the law of sines, law of cosines or a suitable area formula. A good calculator should also check that the supplied dimensions can actually form a triangle.

Formula

For a right triangle, c² = a² + b². For a general triangle, the cosine rule is c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos(C). Area can be found from ½ab sin(C) when two sides and their included angle are known.

Worked example

For a right triangle with legs 3 m and 4 m, c = √(3² + 4²) = √25 = 5 m. The perimeter is 3 + 4 + 5 = 12 m and the area is ½ × 3 × 4 = 6 m².

Pakistan-specific example

A mason or student checking a right-angle layout for a small construction project can use a 3-4-5 relationship as a practical verification. Real construction measurements still need a suitable tape, level and site check because rounding can accumulate over larger dimensions.

Common questions

Can any three positive lengths form a triangle? No. Each pair must sum to more than the third side.

What is the hypotenuse? In a right triangle it is the side opposite the 90° angle and is the longest side.

Does this replace a site measurement? No. It is a mathematical calculation.
