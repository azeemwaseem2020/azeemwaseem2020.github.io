Triangle area and perimeter: complete guide

A triangle is a polygon with three sides. This calculator uses all three side lengths to find perimeter and area, and checks that the sides can form a real triangle. The result uses the same length unit as the inputs for perimeter and the square of that unit for area.

Formulas

Perimeter = a + b + c. The semiperimeter is s = (a + b + c) ÷ 2. Heron’s formula gives area: Area = √[s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)].

How to use it

  1. Measure or enter side a, side b and side c in the same unit.
  2. Select Calculate.
  3. Review the perimeter, area and semiperimeter.
  4. If the calculator rejects the values, recheck measurements and units.

Worked example

For sides 3, 4 and 5, perimeter = 3 + 4 + 5 = 12 units, and s = 6. Area = √[6 × 3 × 2 × 1] = 6 square units. These values describe a 3-4-5 right triangle.

Triangle inequality and limitations

The sum of any two side lengths must be greater than the third: a + b > c, a + c > b and b + c > a. All sides must be positive. If equality holds, the shape collapses into a straight line and has no positive area. Measure with consistent precision; rounded or inaccurate measurements can affect the calculated area. Heron’s formula assumes a flat Euclidean triangle and does not calculate angles, height or a missing side. Use a specialised tool if you need those values.

Frequently asked questions

Can any three side lengths make a triangle?

No. The sum of any two sides must be greater than the third side. If the two shorter sides add up to the longest side or less, no ordinary triangle can be formed from those lengths.

What is Heron’s formula?

Heron’s formula calculates area from all three side lengths. First find the semiperimeter s = (a + b + c) ÷ 2, then area = √[s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)]. Use the same unit for all sides; the resulting area is in square units.

Why might a result differ slightly from a hand calculation?

Rounding side measurements or intermediate values can change the final area. Keep full precision during the calculation and round only the displayed result. For field measurements, the result is only as accurate as the measurements entered.
