Annual percentage yield

Understanding the result

APY is useful when you want to compare rates that compound more than once during a year. The result reflects the mathematical effect of compounding; it does not automatically include account fees, taxes, minimum-balance rules or promotional conditions.

Formula & assumptions

Results are estimates based on the inputs and standard formulas shown or described on this page. Real-world rates, taxes, fees and eligibility rules can vary by country and provider.

Frequently asked questions

What is APY?

APY, or annual percentage yield, expresses an annual interest return while accounting for compounding during the year.

How is APY different from the nominal rate?

A nominal annual rate does not by itself show the effect of intra-year compounding, while APY incorporates that compounding effect.

Does APY include account fees?

The mathematical APY calculation does not automatically include account fees or taxes.

How APY includes compounding

Annual percentage yield expresses an annualized return after accounting for the effect of compounding. For a nominal annual rate r compounded n times per year, the standard formula is APY = (1 + r/n)ⁿ − 1.

Worked example

If a nominal annual rate is 12% and compounds monthly, use r = 0.12 and n = 12. The resulting APY is slightly higher than 12% because interest is added during the year and can itself earn interest.

APY FAQ

Is APY the same as interest rate?

No. A quoted rate may be nominal, while APY incorporates the specified compounding effect.

Does more frequent compounding always increase APY?

For the same positive nominal rate and comparable assumptions, more frequent compounding increases the annualized effective yield.

APY formula and example

For nominal annual rate r compounded n times per year, APY = (1 + r/n)n − 1. Enter the rate as a decimal in the formula.

Example: a 12% nominal rate compounded monthly uses r = 0.12 and n = 12. The resulting APY is approximately 12.68%.

Why compounding matters

APY incorporates interest earned on previously credited interest. More frequent compounding changes the effective annual result for the same positive nominal rate.

FAQ

Does APY include fees or taxes?

No. The standard mathematical APY formula does not automatically include account fees or taxes.

Is APY the same as APR?

No. APY is commonly used for deposit or investment yield; APR is commonly used for borrowing cost.
