What half-life means

Half-life is the time required for a quantity undergoing exponential decay to fall to half its previous amount. It does not mean the material disappears after two half-lives; after each additional half-life, another half of what remains is removed. The same mathematical structure is used in radioactive decay and in other first-order decay processes, although the physical interpretation depends on the application.

Formula

Remaining quantity N = N₀(1/2)^(t/t½), where N₀ is the initial quantity, t is elapsed time and t½ is the half-life. The number of half-lives is t ÷ t½. After n half-lives, the remaining fraction is (1/2)^n.

Worked example

If a substance starts at 80 units and has a half-life of 5 days, after 5 days 40 units remain, after 10 days 20 units remain and after 15 days 10 units remain. For non-integer numbers of half-lives, the exponential formula gives the intermediate value.

Pakistan-specific example

A university physics or chemistry student in Pakistan can use the model to check textbook decay questions, provided the initial quantity and half-life are stated in compatible units. Real laboratory measurements may show statistical variation around the ideal exponential model.

Common questions

Does half-life depend on the starting amount? For an ideal first-order decay law, the half-life remains constant.

Is half the material gone after one half-life? Yes, half remains.

Can half-life be used for every decay process? The concept is broad, but the exponential formula assumes the relevant first-order model.

What if time is not a whole number of half-lives? Use the exponential expression rather than repeated halving.
