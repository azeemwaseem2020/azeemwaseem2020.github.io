Half-Life Calculator

Calculate radioactive decay, remaining quantity or elapsed time.

Formula

N = N₀(1/2)^(t/t₁/₂)

Worked example

If the initial amount is 100 units and the half-life is 5 years, after 10 years two half-lives have passed: 100 × (1/2)² = 25 units remain.

Half-life and exponential decay

Half-life is the time required for an exponentially decaying quantity to fall to half its previous amount. Repeated half-lives reduce the remaining quantity by another factor of two each time.

Worked example

If a sample starts at 80 units and has a half-life of 5 years, about 40 units remain after 5 years and 20 units after 10 years under the ideal decay model.

Interpretation

Half-life is a statistical property of radioactive decay and does not mean each individual atom waits exactly that long before changing.

Half-life FAQ

Does half-life depend on the starting amount?

For the same exponential decay process under stable conditions, the half-life does not depend on the initial amount.
