Ellipse Area Calculator

Calculate the area of an ellipse from its semi-major axis and semi-minor axis using the standard geometry formula.

Ellipse area formula

The area of an ellipse is A = πab, where a is the semi-major axis and b is the semi-minor axis. Both measurements must use the same length unit, so the resulting area is expressed in the corresponding square unit.

Worked example

If a = 5 cm and b = 3 cm, then A = π × 5 × 3 ≈ 47.12 cm².

Semi-axis vs full axis

The formula uses the semi-axes, which are half of the full major and minor axis lengths. If you are given full axis lengths, divide each by 2 before applying A = πab.

Where ellipse area is useful

Ellipse area calculations appear in geometry, engineering, design, astronomy, architecture, and measurements involving elliptical shapes.

FAQ

What is the area formula for an ellipse? A = πab.

Can a and b have different units? No. Convert them to the same length unit first.

What if I know the full major and minor axes? Divide each full axis by two to obtain the semi-axes.

Worked example

If the semi-major axis is 8 m and the semi-minor axis is 5 m, then A = π × 8 × 5 ≈ 125.66 m². The result is square units because two length measurements are multiplied.

Check the dimensions

The formula uses semi-axis lengths. If the full major axis is 16 m and the full minor axis is 10 m, divide both by 2 before calculating. Mixing a full axis with a semi-axis doubles the intended area.

Ellipse versus circle

A circle is a special ellipse where both semi-axes are equal. In that case A = πr², while an ellipse uses two potentially different semi-axis lengths.

Ellipse area: practical measurement guide

The area of an ellipse is A = πab, where a and b are the semi-major and semi-minor axes. These are distances from the centre to the edge, not the full widths across the ellipse. The area is measured in square units: centimetres produce cm², metres produce m², and inches produce in².

Worked application: a garden bed

If an elliptical garden bed measures 4 m across its longest direction and 2.5 m across its shortest direction, the semi-axes are 2 m and 1.25 m. Its approximate area is π × 2 × 1.25 ≈ 7.85 m². If seed, mulch or soil is sold by area coverage, use this result as the base area and then account separately for product depth, package coverage and waste.

How measurement errors affect area

Area changes in direct proportion to each semi-axis. If one semi-axis is doubled while the other stays fixed, the area doubles. If both are doubled, the area becomes four times as large. This is why it is important to measure both full axis lengths through the centre and divide each by two before using the formula.

Ellipse area versus perimeter

Area measures the surface enclosed by the ellipse, while perimeter measures the distance around its boundary. The area formula is exact for an ideal ellipse, but its perimeter does not have a simple elementary exact formula; common perimeter formulas are approximations. Do not use an area result when estimating edging or fencing around the boundary.

Ellipse area FAQ

Does it matter which axis is called a?

No. Multiplication is commutative, so swapping the semi-major and semi-minor values gives the same area. Both values must still be positive lengths in the same unit.

Can I use the full width and height directly?

Not in A = πab if those values are full diameters. Divide each full axis by two first, or use A = π × (major axis/2) × (minor axis/2).

Can this estimate paint or soil quantity?

It provides the two-dimensional area only. Material quantity depends on coverage per package, application depth, surface condition and waste, so calculate those separately.

Use scaling as a quick reasonableness check

Because ellipse area is A = πab, area changes in direct proportion to each semi-axis. If the semi-major axis doubles while the semi-minor axis stays fixed, the area doubles. If both semi-axes double, the area becomes four times as large. This is a useful way to catch unit-entry mistakes before using the result for a drawing, layout or material estimate.

For example, changing semi-axes from 5 m and 3 m to 10 m and 6 m increases area from about 47.12 m² to about 188.50 m². The dimensions and final area must retain consistent units.
