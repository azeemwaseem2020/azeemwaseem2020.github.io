Ellipse Area Calculator

Calculate the area of an ellipse from its semi-major axis and semi-minor axis using the standard geometry formula.

Ellipse area formula

The area of an ellipse is A = πab, where a is the semi-major axis and b is the semi-minor axis. Both measurements must use the same length unit, so the resulting area is expressed in the corresponding square unit.

Worked example

If a = 5 cm and b = 3 cm, then A = π × 5 × 3 ≈ 47.12 cm².

Semi-axis vs full axis

The formula uses the semi-axes, which are half of the full major and minor axis lengths. If you are given full axis lengths, divide each by 2 before applying A = πab.

Where ellipse area is useful

Ellipse area calculations appear in geometry, engineering, design, astronomy, architecture, and measurements involving elliptical shapes.

FAQ

What is the area formula for an ellipse? A = πab.

Can a and b have different units? No. Convert them to the same length unit first.

What if I know the full major and minor axes? Divide each full axis by two to obtain the semi-axes.

Area of an ellipse

Ellipse area is A = πab, where a is the semi-major axis and b is the semi-minor axis.

Worked example

If a = 5 m and b = 3 m, area = 15π ≈ 47.12 m².

A practical Pakistan example

This can estimate the geometric area of an elliptical garden, design feature or plot before accounting for irregular boundaries.

Important assumptions

Use semi-axis lengths, not full major and minor diameters.

FAQ

What inputs matter most? Use the values and units required by the stated formula.

Can the result be treated as exact? Only when the inputs, assumptions and method are appropriate.

Why can another source show a different result? It may use different definitions, rates, rounding or assumptions.

Practical interpretation

For an ellipse with semi-major axis a and semi-minor axis b, the area is πab. The axes are half of the full major and minor diameters, so entering a diameter where a radius is required doubles the dimension and changes the result. For example, semi-axes 6 cm and 4 cm give 24π ≈ 75.40 cm². This relationship is useful for elliptical plots, design drawings and cross-sectional estimates. Keep both axes in the same unit and report area in square units. If measurements come from a physical object, rounding the axes before calculating can introduce avoidable error.
