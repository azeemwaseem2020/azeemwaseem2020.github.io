Solar Battery Backup Calculator: kWh, Ah & Backup Hours

Battery sizing becomes much clearer when you separate the appliances that must stay on from the home's total connected load.

Basic relationship: required usable battery energy ≈ backup load (kW) × backup hours. Nominal battery capacity must then account for usable depth of discharge and conversion losses.

How to estimate battery energy

Start with the average power of the essential loads and the number of hours you want them to run. For example, 600 W for 5 hours requires about 3 kWh of delivered energy before allowing for inverter losses and battery limits.

Worked battery backup example

Suppose your essential backup load averages 600 W and you need 5 hours of backup. The simple energy requirement is 0.6 kW × 5 h = 3 kWh. If a battery is planned around 80% usable capacity, a rough nominal requirement would be 3 ÷ 0.8 = 3.75 kWh, before additional conversion losses and manufacturer constraints.

12V, 24V and 48V battery calculations

For a simplified energy-to-Ah conversion, Ah ≈ kWh × 1,000 ÷ volts. A 3.75 kWh nominal requirement is about 313 Ah at 12 V, 156 Ah at 24 V, or 78 Ah at 48 V. These are arithmetic estimates, not a recommendation for a particular battery bank.

Do not size the battery from connected load alone

A home may have a 5 kW connected load but only need lights, fans, a refrigerator and internet during an outage. Entering the entire connected load can make the battery unnecessarily large. Define the essential backup circuit or backup wattage first.

How many batteries do you need?

The number of physical batteries depends on each battery's voltage, usable energy, Ah rating, series/parallel arrangement and the inverter's supported configuration. Do not select a battery count from Ah alone without checking the complete electrical system.

Peak load also matters

Battery energy determines how long a system can run, but inverter power determines whether the system can handle the instantaneous load. Refrigerators, pumps and other motors can draw a higher starting current than their normal running power. Check the inverter's continuous and surge ratings separately.

Battery chemistry and real-world limits

Lithium and lead-acid batteries differ in usable capacity, charge limits, cycle behavior, temperature requirements and cost. Inverter efficiency, cable losses, temperature and manufacturer limits can also affect runtime. Treat the calculator as a planning estimate rather than an engineering design or warranty.

Solar battery backup in Pakistan

For a Pakistani home, first identify which loads need to operate during load-shedding or another outage and for how many hours. A battery sized for fans, lights, refrigerator and internet can be very different from one intended to run air-conditioning, pumps or electric heating. Equipment availability, local installation practice and the selected inverter also affect the final configuration.

Quick questions

How do I calculate battery backup time?

A rough estimate is usable battery energy in kWh divided by average backup load in kW. Real runtime can be lower because of conversion losses, battery limits and changing load.

What is the difference between Ah and kWh?

Ah measures charge capacity at a stated voltage, while kWh expresses energy. Voltage is needed to relate the two.

Should I use the whole house load?

Only if the whole house is intended to remain powered. For backup planning, essential-load sizing is usually more useful.
