Watts to Amps Calculator

Calculate electrical current from power in watts and voltage in volts.

Watts to amps formula

For the simple relationship P = V × I, current is I = P ÷ V. P is power in watts, V is voltage in volts, and I is current in amperes.

Worked example

For a 1,000 W load at 230 V, I = 1,000 ÷ 230 ≈ 4.348 A. This is a simplified DC or unity-power-factor example.

AC power factor

For a single-phase AC load where real power and power factor are known, a common relationship is I = P ÷ (V × PF). Three-phase systems require the appropriate phase and voltage relationship. Check equipment ratings and electrical standards before using a result for installation.

Why voltage matters

The same wattage can draw different current at different voltages. Higher voltage generally means lower current for the same real power under the same simplified assumptions.

FAQ

How many amps is 1,000 watts at 230 V? About 4.348 A for a unity-power-factor or DC calculation.

Can watts be converted to amps without voltage? No. Voltage is required, and AC loads may also require power factor and phase information.

Is this a full appliance current calculator? No. Starting current, changing loads, power factor and system configuration can affect real equipment current.
