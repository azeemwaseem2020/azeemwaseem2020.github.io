How debt-to-income ratio works

Debt-to-income ratio (DTI) compares recurring monthly debt obligations with gross monthly income. A basic formula is DTI = monthly debt payments ÷ gross monthly income × 100.

Worked example

If qualifying monthly debt payments total $1,500 and gross monthly income is $5,000, DTI is 1,500 ÷ 5,000 × 100 = 30%.

Why lender definitions differ

Different lenders and products can define qualifying debt, income and allowable adjustments differently. A personal estimate therefore may not match an underwriting calculation.

DTI FAQ

Should I use take-home pay?

Many DTI definitions use gross income rather than take-home income. Use the definition specified by the lender or calculator.

Does a lower DTI guarantee approval?

No. Credit history, income stability, collateral, loan type and other underwriting factors can also matter.
