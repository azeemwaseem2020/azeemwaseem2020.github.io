Factorial Calculator

Calculate the factorial of a non-negative integer and review the standard n! formula, zero-factorial rule, and a simple example.

Formula: n! = n × (n − 1) × (n − 2) × ... × 2 × 1.

What is a factorial?

The factorial of a non-negative integer n is the product of every positive integer from 1 through n. Factorials are commonly used in permutations, combinations, probability, counting, and other areas of mathematics.

Factorial example

For 5, the calculation is 5! = 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 120.

Why is 0! equal to 1?

By definition, 0! = 1. This convention keeps standard counting and combinatorics formulas consistent and makes the factorial recurrence n! = n × (n − 1)! work at the boundary.

Input limit and precision

The calculator accepts 0 through 170. Very large factorials grow extremely quickly and exceed ordinary JavaScript number precision, so results near the upper limit should be treated with appropriate numerical caution.

FAQ

Can factorials be negative? This calculator is for non-negative integers; ordinary integer factorial notation is not defined for negative integers.

Where are factorials used? They appear in permutations, combinations, probability, series, and discrete mathematics.
