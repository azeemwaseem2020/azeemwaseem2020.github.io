What is a factorial?

The factorial of a non-negative integer n is the product of all positive integers from 1 through n. It is written n! and is widely used in permutations, combinations, probability, counting, series, and discrete mathematics.

Factorial formula

n! = n × (n − 1) × (n − 2) × ... × 2 × 1. The recurrence form is n! = n × (n − 1)! for n ≥ 1.

Worked examples

5! = 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 120.

3! = 3 × 2 × 1 = 6.

0! = 1 by definition, which keeps standard counting formulas consistent.

Factorials in combinations

Factorials are used in the combination formula C(n,r) = n! / [r!(n−r)!] and the permutation formula P(n,r) = n! / (n−r)!, when the inputs satisfy the relevant conditions.

Input limits and numerical precision

This calculator accepts integers from 0 through 170. Factorials grow extremely quickly; values near the upper limit can exceed ordinary JavaScript number precision even when the magnitude is representable. Use arbitrary-precision software when exact very-large results are required.

Factorial FAQ

Can factorials be negative?

Ordinary integer factorial notation is defined for non-negative integers. This calculator therefore rejects negative inputs.

Why is 0! equal to 1?

It is the standard mathematical convention that makes recurrence and counting formulas work consistently.

Where are factorials used?

Common applications include permutations, combinations, probability, counting problems, power series, and discrete mathematics.

Using factorials correctly

The factorial of a non-negative integer n is written n! and means the product of every positive integer from 1 through n: n! = n × (n−1) × ... × 2 × 1. By definition, 0! = 1. Factorials grow extremely quickly, so even moderate inputs can produce very large integers.

Worked example

For 5!, calculate 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 120. For 7!, the result is 5,040. Notice that 7! is 7 × 6!, which is a useful way to check calculations.

A practical Pakistan example

A statistics or mathematics student in Pakistan may use factorials when studying permutations and combinations. For example, arranging five different books on a shelf gives 5! = 120 possible orders when every book must be used once and order matters.

Factorials in combinations

Factorials also appear in the combination formula C(n,r) = n! / [r!(n−r)!]. Because factorials become large quickly, a calculator or software implementation should avoid unnecessary intermediate values when inputs are large.

Common mistakes

Factorial is normally defined for non-negative integers in elementary combinatorics. Do not interpret 5! as 5 × 1 or as a decimal operation. Also remember that 0! is 1, not 0.

Factorial FAQ

Why is 0! equal to 1? It preserves the factorial recurrence and makes combinatorial formulas work consistently.

Can factorial be negative? The ordinary integer factorial is not defined for negative integers.

Why do factorials become huge? Each new factorial multiplies the previous result by the next integer.

Where are factorials used? Common uses include permutations, combinations, probability and series expansions.
