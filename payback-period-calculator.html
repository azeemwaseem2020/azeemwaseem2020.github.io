Payback period calculation

Payback period estimates how long it takes for cumulative cash inflows to recover an initial investment. With equal periodic cash flows, the simple formula is payback period = initial investment ÷ periodic cash inflow. When cash flows vary, cumulative totals must be tracked period by period.

Worked example

An investment of PKR 500,000 producing PKR 100,000 of annual net cash flow has a simple payback period of 5 years. If the fifth year's cash flow is only partly needed, the fractional year can be estimated from the unrecovered balance divided by that year's cash flow.

Business example

For a solar system, machine or small-business project in Pakistan, use net cash savings rather than gross sales where appropriate. Electricity tariffs, maintenance, downtime and changing output can affect real cash flows.

Limitations

Simple payback ignores the time value of money and cash flows after recovery. Discounted payback or NPV may be more informative for long-term investment decisions.

FAQs

Does payback equal profitability? No. Does it include future cash after payback? Simple payback does not. Can payback be fractional? Yes, when cash flows are measured by period.
