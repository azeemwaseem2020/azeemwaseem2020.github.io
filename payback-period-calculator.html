Payback period calculation

Payback period estimates how long it takes for cumulative cash inflows to recover an initial investment. With equal periodic cash flows, the simple formula is payback period = initial investment ÷ periodic cash inflow. When cash flows vary, cumulative totals must be tracked period by period.

Worked example

An investment of PKR 500,000 producing PKR 100,000 of annual net cash flow has a simple payback period of 5 years. If the fifth year's cash flow is only partly needed, the fractional year can be estimated from the unrecovered balance divided by that year's cash flow.

Business example

For a solar system, machine or small-business project in Pakistan, use net cash savings rather than gross sales where appropriate. Electricity tariffs, maintenance, downtime and changing output can affect real cash flows.

Limitations

Simple payback ignores the time value of money and cash flows after recovery. Discounted payback or NPV may be more informative for long-term investment decisions.

FAQs

Does payback equal profitability? No. Does it include future cash after payback? Simple payback does not. Can payback be fractional? Yes, when cash flows are measured by period.

Interpret payback before choosing a project

Payback is a recovery-time measure, not a complete measure of investment value. Two projects can recover their costs in the same number of years but produce very different cash flows after that point. A short payback can be useful when liquidity or uncertainty matters, but it should be considered alongside total expected cash flow, operating costs, useful life and risk.

Uneven cash flows

When yearly cash flows differ, add each period's net cash flow to the cumulative total until the original investment is recovered. If PKR 500,000 is invested and net cash inflows are PKR 120,000, PKR 140,000, PKR 160,000 and PKR 180,000 over successive years, the unrecovered amount after year three is PKR 80,000. If the fourth year's inflow is expected evenly through the year, a simple estimate is 3 + (80,000 ÷ 180,000) ≈ 3.44 years. This fractional-year method assumes cash flow arrives evenly during that year.

Simple versus discounted payback

Simple payback adds nominal cash flows. Discounted payback first converts future cash flows to present values using an explicit discount rate, so recovery may take longer. Neither method guarantees a project's outcome; use consistent timing, realistic net cash-flow estimates and a rate that matches the decision being evaluated.
