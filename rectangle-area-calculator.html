Rectangle area and perimeter: full guide

A rectangle has four right angles and opposite sides of equal length. Area measures the surface inside the shape, while perimeter measures the distance around its boundary. These measurements are useful for geometry, room planning, flooring, gardens and estimating borders.

Formulas

Area = length × width. Perimeter = 2 × (length + width). Area uses square units (such as m² or ft²); perimeter uses linear units (m or ft). For example, a rectangle 5 m long and 3 m wide has area 15 m² and perimeter 16 m.

How to use it

  1. Measure length and width using the same unit.
  2. Enter length in L and width in W.
  3. Select Calculate and review both outputs.
  4. Keep the correct unit with each answer.

Practical guidance

Use area to estimate surface coverage and perimeter for the boundary. Convert mixed units before calculating. Do not use a diagonal as a side length. For an irregular room or plot, divide it into non-overlapping rectangles and add their areas. Material orders may require extra allowance for cuts, waste, openings and packaging. This calculator performs basic geometry and does not account for obstacles, sloped surfaces or construction tolerances.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between area and perimeter?

Area measures the two-dimensional space inside a rectangle and is written in square units. Perimeter measures the total distance around its four sides and is written in linear units. They answer different questions, so do not compare their numerical values without considering the units.

Can I calculate a room’s flooring area?

Yes, if the floor is rectangular and you measure its inside length and width. For an L-shaped room, split the floor into non-overlapping rectangles, calculate each area and add the results. Deduct permanent floor openings only when they should not be covered.

How much extra material should I buy?

The geometric area is a starting estimate, not a complete purchase quantity. Flooring, paint and edging may require allowances for cuts, patterns, breakage or waste. Follow the material supplier’s guidance and check package coverage before ordering.

Planning with area and perimeter

For a rectangular room, area is the useful starting point for estimating floor covering, while perimeter can help estimate skirting, edging or border length. These are different quantities: a 5 m by 3 m room has 15 m² of floor area and 16 m of perimeter. If you are estimating a material, check whether its coverage is stated per square metre, per linear metre or per pack.

Measurement checklist

  • Measure wall-to-wall in the same unit and record the dimensions before rounding.
  • Measure each separate rectangular section of an L-shaped room and add the areas without overlap.
  • For wall covering, calculate each wall separately and account for doors and windows only when the project estimate requires it.
  • Keep a separate allowance for cuts, breakage and waste rather than changing the measured area.

This calculator assumes a flat rectangle. It does not estimate sloped surfaces, material thickness, labour or a supplier-specific waste allowance.
