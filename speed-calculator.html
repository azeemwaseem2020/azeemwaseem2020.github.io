Speed Calculator — Distance, Time & Average Speed

Calculate speed, distance or time using d = vt. Useful for introductory physics and everyday distance-and-time problems.

Formula

v = d ÷ t, with distance in metres and time in seconds for m/s.

Worked example

If an object travels 100 m in 10 s, its average speed is 100 ÷ 10 = 10 m/s.

Important distinction

Speed is a scalar quantity. Velocity includes direction and is handled by the Velocity Calculator.

Common mistakes

  • Using minutes or hours without converting when the result is required in m/s.
  • Confusing distance with displacement.
  • Dividing by zero time.

Speed calculation context

Speed connects distance travelled with elapsed time. It belongs to the motion cluster alongside velocity and acceleration; speed uses distance, while velocity uses displacement and direction.

Average speed in real journeys: choose the right distance and time

The basic relationship is average speed = total distance ÷ total elapsed time. Use the full journey time when the question asks for average speed over a trip, including stops if the clock continues to run. Do not average two speed readings by simply adding them and dividing by two unless the time spent at each speed is equal.

Worked example with a stop

A vehicle travels 60 km in 1.5 hours, stops for 30 minutes, then travels another 40 km in 1 hour. The total distance is 100 km. If the stated journey time includes the stop, total elapsed time is 3 hours, so average speed is 100 ÷ 3 ≈ 33.3 km/h. If the question asks only for moving speed, the 30-minute stop is excluded and the answer is 100 ÷ 2.5 = 40 km/h. Always state which time definition you use.

Convert units before comparing results

To convert metres per second to kilometres per hour, multiply by 3.6. To convert km/h to m/s, divide by 3.6. For example, 72 km/h = 20 m/s. Keep distance and time units consistent: metres with seconds gives m/s; kilometres with hours gives km/h.

Speed versus velocity

Speed is a scalar: it describes how fast distance is covered and has no direction. Velocity uses displacement per time and includes direction. On a return journey, an object can travel a substantial distance while ending where it started; its average speed can be positive even though its average velocity is zero. Use the Velocity Calculator when displacement and direction matter.

Speed calculator FAQ

How do I calculate average speed for a trip with stops?

Divide the total distance by the total elapsed time if the stop time is included in the journey. If you want moving speed only, exclude stopped time and clearly label the result.

Can I average two speed readings directly?

Not in every situation. The arithmetic mean is appropriate only under specific conditions, such as equal time spent at each speed. For a whole trip, use total distance divided by total time.

What is 10 metres per second in kilometres per hour?

Multiply by 3.6: 10 m/s × 3.6 = 36 km/h.

Can average speed be zero?

For a positive journey distance and positive elapsed time, average speed is positive. A stationary object covers zero distance over a positive interval, so its average speed is zero.

Average speed for a complete journey

Average speed is total distance divided by total elapsed time. If a trip covers 150 km in 2.5 hours including a stop, the average speed is 150 ÷ 2.5 = 60 km/h. If you exclude the stop, label the answer as moving speed because it answers a different question.

Use compatible units

Convert distance and time to a matching unit before dividing. A common conversion is 1 m/s = 3.6 km/h, so 15 m/s equals 54 km/h. Mixing kilometres with seconds without conversion produces a unit mismatch.

Speed is not velocity

Speed describes how fast distance is covered and is non-negative. Velocity uses displacement and direction. For a round trip returning to the starting point, average velocity can be zero even though average speed is greater than zero.
