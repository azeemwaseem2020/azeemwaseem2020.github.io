Surface area calculator: complete guide

Surface area is the total area covering the outside of a three-dimensional object. This calculator supports a rectangular box, cube or cylinder. Surface area is useful for estimating paint, wrapping, coatings and exposed material, but the real amount needed may be higher because of overlaps, waste and surface texture.

Formulas used

Rectangular box: SA = 2(lw + lh + wh). Cube: SA = 6s². Cylinder: SA = 2πr(r + h), including both circular ends and the curved side. Here l, w and h are length, width and height; s is cube side length; r is cylinder radius.

How to use it

  1. Choose the shape that matches your object.
  2. Enter its dimensions in the same unit.
  3. For a cylinder, use radius rather than diameter.
  4. Calculate and report the answer in square units.

Example

A box measuring 4 cm × 3 cm × 2 cm has SA = 2(12 + 8 + 6) = 52 cm². A cube with side 3 cm has SA = 6 × 3² = 54 cm². For a cylinder with radius 2 cm and height 5 cm, SA = 2π × 2 × (2 + 5) = 28π ≈ 87.96 cm².

Common mistakes and limits

Surface area is not volume: area uses square units, while volume uses cubic units. Do not enter a cylinder’s diameter as its radius. Keep all dimensions consistent and round to match measurement accuracy. Hollow objects, open containers, overlapping panels, lids and wall thickness may require a different calculation depending on which surfaces are exposed. This calculator models ideal shapes and does not estimate paint absorption, seams, waste or material thickness.

Frequently asked questions

What is surface area used for?

Surface area estimates the outside area of a three-dimensional object. It can help with paint, coating, wrapping or material coverage, although real projects also require allowances for overlap, waste, openings and surface texture.

Is surface area the same as volume?

No. Surface area measures the boundary and uses square units; volume measures three-dimensional space and uses cubic units. For example, m² is an area unit while m³ is a volume unit.

Which formula should I use for a different shape?

Each solid has its own surface-area formula. A sphere, cone, cylinder and rectangular prism have different surfaces and may require slant height or other dimensions. Confirm the object’s geometry and use a calculator designed for that shape.
