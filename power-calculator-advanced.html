When to use the advanced power calculator

Use this page when a power problem involves more than a basic work-and-time calculation, such as comparing energy, elapsed time or efficiency. Keep the physical quantity and units consistent throughout the calculation.

Core power relationship

Power describes the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred: P = W/t. For a simple calculation, use the Power Calculator.

Power, work and time

Mechanical power describes how quickly work is performed. The basic relationship is P = W/t, where P is power, W is work or energy transferred, and t is elapsed time. One watt equals one joule per second. If efficiency is included, distinguish useful output power from input power.

Worked example

If a machine performs 18,000 J of useful work in 30 s, its average useful power is 18,000 ÷ 30 = 600 W. If the machine is 75% efficient, the required input power for that useful output would be approximately 600 ÷ 0.75 = 800 W under a steady-state model.

Practical use

The relationship helps compare motors, pumps, lifting equipment and electrical devices. For rotating machinery or changing loads, instantaneous power may vary with time, so an average value should be labeled as such.

FAQs

Is power the same as energy? No. Energy is an amount; power is the rate of transfer. Can time be zero? No. Does higher power always mean more energy? Not necessarily; energy also depends on how long the system operates.
