Core power relationship

Power describes the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred: P = W/t. For a simple calculation, use the Power Calculator.

Power as a rate of energy transfer

Power is the rate at which energy is transferred or work is performed. The appropriate equation depends on whether the system involves mechanical work, electrical energy or another energy-transfer process.

Average versus instantaneous power

Average power uses total energy transfer over a time interval. Instantaneous power describes the rate at a particular moment and may vary throughout the process.

Advanced Power: Work, Energy, Time and Efficiency

Power measures how quickly work is done or energy is transferred. Two systems can perform the same amount of work but have different power ratings if one completes the task in less time. This page uses average power from work and elapsed time, a common starting point for mechanics and energy calculations.

Formula and units

P = W/t = ΔE/t, where P is average power in watts, W is work in joules, ΔE is transferred energy in joules and t is elapsed time in seconds. One watt equals one joule per second. Rearranged forms are W = Pt and t = W/P, provided power is non-zero when solving for time.

Worked example

A motor transfers 18,000 J of useful energy in 30 seconds. Average output power is P = 18,000 ÷ 30 = 600 W. If a system instead delivers 600 W steadily for 2 minutes, energy transferred is 600 × 120 = 72,000 J. Convert minutes to seconds before using the SI formula.

Features and applications

The calculator helps compare motors, lifting tasks, appliances, mechanical work and energy-transfer examples. It makes the relationship between work and time explicit and supports back-calculating energy from a known power rating. A kilowatt is 1,000 watts; electrical energy billed in kilowatt-hours is energy, not power. For example, a 1 kW device running for 3 hours consumes 3 kWh of energy if it operates at that power throughout.

Average power, efficiency and limitations

This formula returns average power over the selected interval. Instantaneous power can vary over time, and a device's nameplate rating may represent a maximum, nominal or input value rather than continuous useful output. Efficiency is useful output energy divided by input energy, often multiplied by 100%; it cannot exceed 100% for a passive real-world energy conversion when definitions and boundaries are consistent. If efficiency is known, useful output power is input power multiplied by efficiency as a decimal.

Keep mechanical and electrical contexts distinct. Electrical power may use P = VI for direct current or a more detailed AC relationship involving RMS values and power factor. Do not assume the work-time formula alone estimates electrical demand, losses or running cost. Use measured values and the correct system boundary for engineering decisions.

Frequently asked questions

What is the SI unit of power?

The watt (W), equal to one joule transferred per second.

Is kWh a unit of power?

No. Kilowatt-hour is a unit of energy; kilowatt is a unit of power.

Does higher power always mean more energy use?

Not by itself. Energy depends on power and how long the system operates.
