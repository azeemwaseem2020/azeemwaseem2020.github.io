Solar Battery Backup: kWh, Ah and Backup Hours Explained

Battery sizing starts with the energy you want to store, not the battery's Ah label alone. Voltage, usable capacity and system losses all matter.

Convert the load into energy

If a 500 W load runs for 4 hours, it needs 2,000 Wh, or 2 kWh. Multiple loads should be added by their actual expected runtime.

From kWh to battery capacity

A simplified usable-energy model is required nominal storage = required load energy ÷ (usable depth fraction × inverter/system efficiency). For example, 2 kWh of required AC energy with 80% usable depth and 90% efficiency needs about 2.78 kWh of nominal storage.

Where Ah fits

Battery energy is approximately Wh = V × Ah. A 48 V, 100 Ah battery is about 4.8 kWh nominal before usable-depth and efficiency limits. The actual specification and chemistry determine how much of that energy can be used.

Backup hours depend on load

A battery does not provide a fixed number of hours. A 5 kWh battery may run a 500 W load for roughly 10 hours in an idealized calculation, but real usable time will be lower after system losses and operating limits.

Do not confuse battery energy with inverter power

Battery kWh answers how much energy is stored. Inverter kW answers how much power can be delivered at a time. Motors and compressors can also create starting surges.
