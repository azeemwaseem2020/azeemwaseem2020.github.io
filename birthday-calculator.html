How birthday age is calculated

Calendar age is based on completed years, months and days between the birth date and the calculation date. Month lengths and leap years matter, so a simple division by 365 is only an approximation. The calculator compares the calendar components rather than treating every year as exactly the same number of days.

Worked example

A person born on June 15, 1995 reaches age 30 on June 15, 2025. On June 14, 2025, that person has completed 29 years. After the birthday, the completed-year count increases by one. The months and days shown for dates between birthdays depend on the exact calendar positions of the two dates.

Next birthday planning

The weekday of a birthday is determined by the actual month, day and year in the calendar. For planning a celebration, the next birthday is the next occurrence of the same month and day after the calculation date. A birthday that has already occurred this year moves to the following year.

Leap years and February 29

Leap years add February 29 to the calendar and can affect date comparisons. A person born on February 29 has a birthday date that does not occur in every calendar year. Different legal, administrative or personal conventions may be used for non-leap years, so this calculator should be treated as a calendar tool rather than a legal age determination.

Choosing the right date

Enter the birth date exactly as recorded. A one-day input error can change the completed age, weekday and birthday timing. For official forms, identification records, school or employment documents, use the date shown on the relevant official record.

FAQ

Does a leap year affect age? Calendar calculations account for leap-year dates and month lengths, so the result can differ from an estimate based only on 365 days.

Is this the same as a date-difference calculator? It uses calendar dates to present age and birthday information, while a date-difference tool may focus only on elapsed time between two dates.

Can I use it for a legal age decision? It can help with ordinary calendar calculations, but legal age rules can depend on the jurisdiction and the purpose of the calculation. For an official decision, check the applicable rule or record.

Calendar example: before and after a birthday

If a birth date is June 15, 1995 and the calculation date is June 15, 2025, the completed age is 30 years. If the calculation date is June 14, 2025, it is still 29 completed years. This illustrates why calendar comparison is more useful for age than simply dividing elapsed days by 365.

Weekday and birthday timing

The weekday belongs to the specific calendar date, not simply to the month and day. When planning a future birthday, the relevant year should be included because the weekday can change from one year to the next. Leap-day birthdays also require an explicit convention when the next occurrence falls in a non-leap year.

Privacy note

A birthday calculation normally needs only dates. Avoid entering names, identity numbers or other personal information that the calculation does not require.
