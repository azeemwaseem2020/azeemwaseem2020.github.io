Snell's Law Calculator: Refraction and Critical Conditions

Snell's law describes how a light ray changes direction when it crosses a boundary between two transparent media. The refracted angle depends on both refractive indices and the angle of incidence. Angles in this calculator are measured from the normal to the boundary, not from the surface itself.

Formula

n₁ sin(θ₁) = n₂ sin(θ₂), where n₁ and n₂ are the refractive indices of the first and second media. To solve for the refracted angle, calculate sin(θ₂) = n₁ sin(θ₁) ÷ n₂, then take the inverse sine. The calculator converts degrees to radians internally for the trigonometric calculation.

Worked example

For light travelling from air (approximately n₁ = 1.00) into a material with n₂ = 1.50 at an incident angle of 30°, sin(θ₂) = 1.00 × sin(30°) ÷ 1.50 = 0.3333. The refracted angle is approximately 19.47°. The ray bends toward the normal as it enters the higher-index medium.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the refractive index of the incident medium.
  2. Enter the incident angle from the normal, between 0° and 90° for this interface.
  3. Enter the refractive index of the second medium.
  4. Calculate and compare the result with a ray diagram.

Total internal reflection

When light travels from a higher-index medium toward a lower-index medium, the expression for sin(θ₂) can exceed 1 at sufficiently large incident angles. No real refracted angle then exists; the physical condition is total internal reflection. The critical angle satisfies sin(θc) = n₂ ÷ n₁ when n₁ is greater than n₂. Real materials may have wavelength-dependent refractive indices, and the simple model assumes a clean boundary and ideal ray optics.

Frequently asked questions

Are angles measured from the surface?

No. Snell's law uses angles measured from the normal, perpendicular to the boundary.

Why might there be no refracted angle?

Total internal reflection can occur when light travels from a higher to a lower refractive index.

Is refractive index measured in units?

No. It is a dimensionless ratio of light speeds in vacuum and the medium.
