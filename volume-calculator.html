How volume is calculated

Volume measures the three-dimensional space occupied by an object. The correct formula depends on its shape. For a rectangular box, V = length × width × height. For a cylinder, V = πr²h; for a sphere, V = 4πr³/3. Always use the formula that matches the selected shape.

Worked example

A rectangular tank is 2 m long, 1.5 m wide and 1 m high. Its volume is 2 × 1.5 × 1 = 3 m³. Since 1 m³ equals 1,000 litres, the same volume is 3,000 litres.

A practical Pakistan example

For a household water tank, converting dimensions to metres before calculating volume makes the result easy to interpret in cubic metres. If a tank is 2 m × 1.5 m × 1 m, its geometric capacity is about 3,000 litres, although usable capacity may be lower because of freeboard, fittings and the tank's actual internal dimensions.

Units and conversions

Volume units are cubic units: cm³, m³, ft³ and so on. Length conversion must happen before cubing. For example, converting every dimension from centimetres to metres first avoids mixing units inside the formula. For water, litres are especially convenient because 1 litre = 0.001 m³.

Common mistakes

Do not use external dimensions when internal capacity is required, and do not confuse radius with diameter. If the diameter is given, radius is half the diameter before using formulas such as πr²h.

FAQ

Is volume the same as capacity? They are closely related, but capacity usually refers to how much a container can hold.

How many litres are in 1 m³? 1,000 litres.

What if a container is not a standard shape? Use the appropriate geometric approximation or a measured displacement method when suitable.

Why do cubic units matter? Volume has three dimensions, so changing a length unit affects the result cubically.
