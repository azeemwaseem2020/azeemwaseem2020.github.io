Worked volume examples

A rectangular box that is 10 cm long, 5 cm wide and 4 cm high has a volume of 10 × 5 × 4 = 200 cm³. A cube with a side length of 3 m has a volume of 3³ = 27 m³. A cylinder with radius 2 cm and height 6 cm has a volume of π × 2² × 6 = 24π, approximately 75.40 cm³. The calculator returns cubic units because each dimension contributes a length unit.

Volume and capacity are related but different labels

Volume describes three-dimensional space and is expressed in cubic units such as cm³ or m³. Capacity often describes how much liquid or material a container can hold and may be expressed in litres. One litre is equal to 1,000 cubic centimetres, and one cubic metre is equal to 1,000 litres. Convert only after calculating with compatible dimensions, and remember that a container's usable capacity can be lower than its geometric volume because of wall thickness, headspace or its shape.

Choose dimensions carefully

For a cylinder, enter the radius rather than the diameter. If you know the diameter, divide it by two first. For a rectangular box, length, width and height must refer to perpendicular dimensions of the same shape. This calculator covers rectangular boxes, cubes and cylinders; it does not directly calculate irregular objects, cones or spheres. For irregular shapes, use an appropriate geometric model or a measurement method suited to the object.

Common volume unit conversions

Because volume is three-dimensional, conversion factors are cubed. One metre is 100 centimetres, but one cubic metre is 1,000,000 cubic centimetres. For practical capacity comparisons, 1,000 cm³ equals 1 litre and 1 m³ equals 1,000 litres. Do not convert cubic units using the linear factor alone. The calculator reports a numeric result in cubic units based on the dimensions you enter; it does not automatically label the answer as litres or gallons. Convert the result separately when needed and state the unit clearly. For containers, tanks or packaging, use measured internal dimensions if the goal is usable capacity, and allow for rounded dimensions and real-world tolerances.
