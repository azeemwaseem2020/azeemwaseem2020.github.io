CALCULATION INTEGRITY

How to verify a PSX result

For a reproducible trade calculation, keep the arithmetic separate from changing market, broker and tax inputs. Calcora calculates from the figures you enter; it does not supply live prices or decide which current rule applies.

1. Reconcile the trade

Match buy price, sell price, quantity and actual charges to the broker contract note before treating the result as a completed trade calculation.

2. Check the cost layer

Brokerage, other charges and applicable levies should be entered from the current tariff or transaction record rather than copied from an old example.

3. Check tax separately

CGT and dividend withholding are assumptions in this tool. Confirm the treatment applicable to the relevant transaction, investor status and tax period before filing or reporting.

4. Re-run edge cases

Test both profit and loss scenarios, zero-cost cases, high charges and corporate-action scenarios when reviewing a calculation or building an audit trail.

PSX Calculator FAQs

What does a PSX profit calculation include?

A basic result can compare purchase cost with sale value and may also account for dividend income and the transaction assumptions entered by the user.

Does the calculator automatically guarantee the exact amount I will receive?

No. Actual settlement values can differ because brokerage, taxes, levies, charges and transaction-specific rules may apply.

Can I use this calculator for dividend income?

Yes. Enter the relevant dividend amount and share quantity where supported, then review the result alongside your other trading assumptions.

How to use a PSX profit calculator

For a simple Pakistan Stock Exchange trade estimate, start with the number of shares, purchase price and selling price. Then include the transaction costs that apply to your scenario. The useful result is not only the gross price difference: you also want to see the cash invested, selling proceeds, costs, net profit or loss, return percentage and break-even level where supported by the inputs.

Gross profit is not the same as net profit

If a share rises from your purchase price, the price movement creates a gross gain before costs. Brokerage, commissions, taxes, levies and other transaction charges can reduce the amount you actually keep. Enter documented costs when you have them instead of assuming that one universal fee applies to every broker, account or transaction.

Average price after buying more shares

When you add shares at another price, the average purchase price changes because the total cost is spread across the new total share count. This is useful for scenario planning, but an average price does not remove the market risk of the position. Use the dedicated PSX average-price calculator when you want to model multiple purchases.

Dividend and total-return thinking

A share can produce a price gain or loss and may also pay dividends. Keep price return and dividend income visible as separate components before combining them into a total-return estimate. Dividend treatment, taxes and eligibility depend on the transaction and applicable rules.

Important limitation

This calculator does not provide a live PSX quote or a recommendation to buy or sell a security. It works from the figures you enter. For a current market price, company disclosure or applicable tax rule, verify the information from the relevant official or professional source before acting.

PSX PROFIT ANALYSIS

Separate trade profit, costs, dividend and return

A PSX trade result can contain several different measures. Keeping them separate makes the calculation easier to audit and prevents a gross price difference from being mistaken for the final result.

Gross price movement

Compare the buy and sell values before transaction costs. This is the starting point, not necessarily the final profit.

Trading costs

Include the applicable brokerage and other entered charges when calculating the net result.

Capital gain or loss

Compare the net sale proceeds with the relevant acquisition cost and entered adjustments.

Dividend income

Dividend cash flow is a separate component from the share-price movement and should be analyzed separately when appropriate.

Tax, brokerage and other market rules can change. Verify current rates and charges with the relevant authoritative source before using a result for a real transaction.
