Weighted Average Calculator

Calculate a weighted mean when different values contribute unequally to the final result.

Weighted average formula

The weighted mean is Σ(wx) ÷ Σw, where x is a value and w is its corresponding weight. The calculation depends on the relative size of the weights, not on whether they add to 100.

Worked example

For values 80, 90 and 70 with weights 20, 30 and 50:

(80×20 + 90×30 + 70×50) ÷ (20+30+50) = 7700 ÷ 100 = 77.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the values in order, separated by commas.
  2. Enter the corresponding weights in the same order.
  3. Make sure both lists contain the same number of entries.
  4. Use non-negative weights and a total weight greater than zero.

How weights work

Weights can be percentages, proportions, counts or other relative measures when the weighting system is appropriate. For example, weights of 2 and 8 have the same effect as 20 and 80 because their proportions are identical.

Where weighted averages are useful

Weighted means are common in course grades, portfolio allocations, survey summaries, performance metrics and price calculations where observations do not contribute equally.

Choosing the right weighting method

Use weights that reflect the contribution each observation should make to the result. In a course grade, an exam worth 40% should influence the final mark more than a quiz worth 10%. In a portfolio, market values or portfolio proportions may be appropriate, depending on the question. Do not mix incompatible units or use weights that describe something other than relative contribution. If every observation should count equally, use an ordinary arithmetic mean instead.

Input quality and interpretation

Before relying on the result, confirm that each weight is paired with the intended value and that the weighting basis is consistent. A weighted average summarizes the inputs supplied; it does not correct biased samples, missing observations or an unsuitable weighting scheme.

Common mistakes

  • Pair each value with the correct weight.
  • Do not calculate an ordinary average when the observations have unequal importance.
  • Do not use a negative weight in a standard weighted mean.
  • Check that the total weight is greater than zero.

FAQ

Do weights have to total 100?

No. Relative weights are sufficient. If the weights are intended as percentages, they should normally represent the intended percentage allocation.

Can a weight be zero?

Yes. A zero weight contributes nothing to the weighted sum, provided the total weight remains positive.

What is the difference between mean and weighted mean?

An ordinary mean gives every observation equal weight. A weighted mean gives each observation a specified contribution through its weight.
