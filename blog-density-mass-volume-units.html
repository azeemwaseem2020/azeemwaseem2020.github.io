Density, Mass and Volume: Formula, Units and Worked Examples

Density describes how much mass is contained in a given volume. The formula is simple, but unit consistency and measurement quality determine whether the result is useful.

The density formula

ρ = m ÷ V. If a sample has mass 200 g and volume 50 cm³, its density is 4 g/cm³.

Rearranging the formula

Mass is m = ρV, while volume is V = m/ρ. A calculator should make the direction of the calculation explicit so a user does not accidentally divide the wrong quantities.

Unit conversion matters

One of the easiest mistakes is combining grams with cubic metres or kilograms with cubic centimetres without conversion. Choose a consistent unit system before calculating, then convert the final result if needed.

Density is not the same as specific gravity

Density has physical units such as kg/m³. Specific gravity is a ratio relative to a reference substance and is dimensionless. The two concepts are related but should not be reported as interchangeable quantities.

Measurement uncertainty

If both mass and volume are measured, their uncertainty affects the density. Small errors in volume can be important, especially when the volume is obtained from a difference between measurements.
