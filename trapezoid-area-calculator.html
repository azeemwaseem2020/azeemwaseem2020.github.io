Trapezoid area: formula and guide

A trapezoid is a four-sided shape with one pair of parallel sides. The area formula uses the lengths of the two parallel sides and the perpendicular distance between them. Depending on regional convention, this shape may also be called a trapezium.

Formula

Area = (a + b) × h ÷ 2. The values a and b are the parallel base lengths, and h is the perpendicular height between them. The height is not the sloping side length unless that side happens to be perpendicular to the bases.

Worked example

Suppose the parallel sides are 8 cm and 12 cm, and the perpendicular height is 5 cm. Area = (8 + 12) × 5 ÷ 2 = 50 cm². The average of the two parallel sides is 10 cm, and multiplying that average by the height gives the same area.

How to use the calculator

  1. Measure the two parallel sides and enter them as A and B.
  2. Measure the perpendicular distance between the parallel sides and enter it as H.
  3. Use the same unit for all three measurements.
  4. Select Calculate and express the result in square units.

Common mistakes and practical uses

Do not enter the sloping edge as height; doing so usually overstates or understates the area. If dimensions are in metres, the result is m²; if dimensions are in centimetres, it is cm². The formula can help estimate the area of a trapezoid-shaped panel, garden section or plot, but it assumes a flat, straight-edged shape. For construction or land measurement, verify field dimensions and applicable surveying requirements.

Frequently asked questions

Do both parallel sides need to be equal?

No. The two parallel sides may have different lengths. The formula uses their sum divided by two, then multiplies by the perpendicular height.

Can I use the sloping side as the height?

Only if it is perpendicular to the parallel bases. In most trapezoids, the sloping edge is longer than the perpendicular height and cannot be substituted.

What units does the result use?

The result is in square units based on the input measurements, such as m² or ft². Convert all input lengths to one unit before calculating.
