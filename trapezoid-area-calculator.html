Trapezoid area formula

The area of a trapezoid is A = ½(a+b)h, where a and b are the lengths of the parallel sides and h is the perpendicular height. The height is not the sloping side unless the geometry makes them equal.

Worked example

For parallel sides of 8 cm and 12 cm with a perpendicular height of 5 cm, area = ½(8+12)×5 = 50 cm². Keep all dimensions in compatible units.

Practical use

The formula is useful for land sections, floor layouts and engineering drawings. For an irregular real-world shape, divide it into simple shapes and combine the areas rather than forcing a trapezoid approximation.

FAQs

Does height mean slanted length? No. Can the parallel sides be swapped? Yes. What are the units? Square units.

Measure a trapezoid's area

The area of a trapezoid is the average of its two parallel sides multiplied by the perpendicular height: A = (a + b) × h ÷ 2. The height is the shortest perpendicular distance between the parallel sides, not the slanted side length.

Worked example

If the parallel sides are 8 m and 14 m and the perpendicular height is 5 m, the area is (8 + 14) × 5 ÷ 2 = 55 m². All three measurements must use the same length unit.

Common measurement errors

  • Do not use a sloping edge as the height unless it is perpendicular to both parallel sides.
  • Convert mixed units before calculating the area.
  • For a real plot or roof, verify the shape and dimensions on site; irregular boundaries may need to be split into multiple simpler shapes.
  • Use square units for the area, not linear units.

Frequently asked questions

Does it matter which parallel side is called a or b? No. Their sum is unchanged if the labels are swapped.

Can the two parallel sides be equal? Yes. In that case, the formula gives the area of a parallelogram with that base and height.

Can this formula be used for any four-sided shape? No. It requires a trapezoid with a pair of parallel sides.
