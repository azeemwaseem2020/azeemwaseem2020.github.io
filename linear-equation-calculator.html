Linear equation formula

For ax + b = c, when a is not zero, x = (c − b) ÷ a.

How to solve a linear equation

  1. Subtract b from both sides.
  2. Divide both sides by a.
  3. Check the value of x in the original equation.

Worked example

For 2x + 4 = 10, subtract 4 to get 2x = 6, then divide by 2. Therefore x = 3.

Common mistakes

Keep the sign of b when subtracting it from both sides, and do not divide by a before moving the constant term. A quick substitution back into the original equation helps verify the result.

When to use this calculator

This tool is designed for one-variable linear equations in the form ax + b = c. It is useful for algebra practice, homework checks and quick verification of a known equation.

Important limitation

This tool solves the one-variable form ax + b = c. If a equals zero, the equation becomes a constant statement and may have no solution or infinitely many solutions depending on b and c.

Common questions

How should I check the answer?

Substitute the calculated value back into the original equation. The two sides should agree within normal rounding.

What if the inputs do not define one solution?

The solver reports special cases such as no solution or infinitely many solutions instead of forcing a misleading numeric answer.
