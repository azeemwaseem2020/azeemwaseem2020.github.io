Markup versus selling price

Markup measures the amount added to cost to determine a selling price. It is not the same as profit margin. A markup percentage is normally calculated relative to cost, while margin is calculated relative to selling price. Confusing the two can produce a large difference in pricing decisions.

Formula

Markup amount = cost × markup rate. Selling price = cost + markup amount. Therefore selling price = cost × (1 + markup rate). If cost is Rs 1,000 and markup is 20%, the selling price is Rs 1,200.

Worked example

A product costing Rs 2,500 with a 30% markup has markup of Rs 750. Selling price = Rs 3,250. The markup is 750 ÷ 2,500 = 30%. The resulting margin is 750 ÷ 3,250 ≈ 23.08%, showing why markup and margin should not be treated as interchangeable.

Pakistan-specific example

A small retailer in Pakistan can use the calculation when setting a base price for an item after purchase cost. Freight, packaging, marketplace fees, taxes and discounts may also affect the final commercial price, so the calculator should be one part of the pricing worksheet.

Common questions

Is 20% markup a 20% margin? No. They use different denominators.

Does markup include tax? Not automatically. Add only the charges relevant to the pricing model.

Can markup be negative? A negative percentage represents a price below cost, which is a different business decision.

Why does my invoice differ? Discounts, tax, freight and other charges may be applied after the base price.
