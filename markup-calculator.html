Markup versus profit margin

Markup percentage measures the increase over cost, while profit margin measures profit as a share of the selling price. For example, a PKR 800 cost sold for PKR 1,000 has PKR 200 gross profit, a 25% markup on cost and a 20% gross margin on selling price.

Pricing assumptions

This calculation treats the entered cost as the relevant cost basis. It does not automatically include delivery, tax, platform fees, discounts, returns or overhead; add those costs before setting a real selling price.

How markup is calculated

Markup measures how much a selling price is increased above cost. Markup percentage is calculated from the increase relative to cost, while margin percentage uses the selling price as its base.

Example

If an item costs PKR 800 and is sold for PKR 1,000, the markup is PKR 200 and the markup percentage is 25%. The profit margin on the same sale is 20%, so markup and margin should not be treated as interchangeable.

Pricing decisions

A markup calculation does not automatically account for operating expenses, taxes, discounts, returns or payment fees. Include those costs when setting a sustainable selling price.

Markup FAQ

Is a 25% markup a 25% margin?

No. A 25% markup on cost corresponds to a 20% margin on the final selling price.

Calculate a selling price from a target markup

If an item costs PKR 800 and the desired markup is 25%, multiply cost by 1.25 to get a selling price of PKR 1,000 before other expenses. If the target is instead a 25% gross margin, divide 800 by 0.75 to get approximately PKR 1,066.67. The two methods give different prices because markup is based on cost and margin is based on selling price.

Check the full cost base

For sustainable pricing, decide whether the cost input includes purchase cost only or also packaging, freight, platform fees and other direct costs. Discounts and returns can reduce realized revenue after the initial price is set. This calculator illustrates a simple markup; it does not predict demand, competitor pricing or the net profit that remains after overhead and taxes.
