Ratios, Proportions and Scaling

A ratio compares quantities by division. A ratio of 3:5 means three parts of one quantity for every five parts of another. To simplify a ratio, divide both terms by their greatest common factor. For example, 18:30 simplifies to 3:5 because both terms divide by 6.

Ratios are useful for scaling recipes, mixtures, drawings and financial comparisons. If a concrete mix is represented as 1:2:4 for cement, sand and aggregate, the numbers describe relative parts; they do not by themselves specify the actual kilograms required. The final quantities depend on the total batch size and the material specification.

In Pakistan agriculture, a fertilizer recommendation might be expressed as a rate per acre. If a field is 3 acres and the recommended rate is 50 kg per acre, the total is 150 kg. The calculation is a proportion: 50/1 = 150/3. Always confirm whether a rate is stated per acre, hectare, kanal or another unit.

Cross multiplication

For a/b = c/d, cross multiplication gives ad = bc, provided denominators are non-zero. This is useful for solving an unknown value, but the quantities must represent comparable units. Converting units first often prevents a correct-looking but incorrect answer.

FAQs

Can ratios contain decimals? Yes, although multiplying all terms by a common factor can often produce whole numbers.

Is 2:3 the same as 3:2? No. Reversing a ratio changes the comparison.

Can ratios compare different units? Only after a meaningful unit conversion or when the ratio is explicitly defined that way.
