Core PSX calculations explained

Gross P/L is sale value minus purchase value. Net P/L adjusts that result for entered costs and tax assumptions. Weighted average price is total purchase cost divided by total shares. Dividend income is dividend per share multiplied by shares held. Dividend yield is dividend per share divided by the reference share price, expressed as a percentage.

PSX EVIDENCE FRAMEWORK

From a calculator result to a verifiable PSX workflow

A useful PSX result has three separate layers: the arithmetic, the transaction data behind the arithmetic, and the current rules that determine which charges or tax assumptions apply. Keeping these layers separate makes the tools easier to audit and reduces the risk of treating an illustrative number as a live market fact.

Layer 1 — Arithmetic

Check the formula using the quantities, prices and assumptions entered into the calculator. The result should be reproducible from those inputs.

Layer 2 — Evidence

For an actual holding or trade, reconcile the inputs with your broker statement, trade confirmation and other relevant records.

Layer 3 — Current rules

Brokerage, regulatory levies and statutory taxes can change. PSX says broker tariff schedules include commissions and applicable levies, so current rates should be verified before applying them. PSX Investor Awareness Guide

Layer 4 — Interpretation

A calculation describes the entered scenario. It does not establish whether a security is suitable, predict a future price or replace professional investment or tax advice.

Recommended audit trail

  1. Record the calculation date.
  2. Record the price, quantity and charge assumptions.
  3. Keep the source of each changing input.
  4. Run the calculation.
  5. Reconcile the result with the broker or transaction record.
  6. Recheck any time-sensitive tax, fee or regulatory assumption.

This evidence-first structure is intended to make Calcora's PSX tools transparent and reproducible, not to present them as live market-data services.

PSX DECISION MODEL

Use the right evidence for the right calculation

A PSX calculator should answer a defined numerical question without pretending to know facts that were not supplied. Calcora therefore separates calculation inputs from external market evidence and from interpretation.

Transaction question

Example: What was the net result of this completed trade? Use actual quantity, prices and charges from the trade record.

Portfolio question

Example: What is the current value and allocation of these holdings? Use prices from the same valuation point and record their source/date.

Income question

Example: What dividend yield or reinvestment scenario follows from an entered DPS and price? Treat the result as an assumption-based calculation.

Risk question

Example: What share quantity corresponds to a defined risk limit? Verify the assumptions and remember that execution costs and price gaps can alter actual risk.

Source hierarchy

  1. Primary transaction evidence: broker contract note, statement or official company/exchange notice.
  2. Current market/regulatory source: PSX and relevant official regulatory or tax sources.
  3. Calculator inputs: only the values actually supplied by the user.
  4. Interpretation: keep calculated facts separate from investment opinion or prediction.

PSX itself advises investors to verify registered brokers, understand transaction charges and levies, and review trade confirmations containing prices, commissions and applicable charges. PSX Investor Awareness Guide

This framework makes the calculation reproducible while keeping current market facts and investment decisions outside the calculator's unsupported assumptions.

PSX VERIFICATION WORKFLOW

Check a PSX calculation before relying on the result

PSX calculations are only as reliable as the transaction and market assumptions entered. Keep the mathematical result separate from changing exchange, broker, tax and corporate-action information.

  1. Confirm transaction inputs: verify quantity, buy/sell prices and the relevant charges.
  2. Check the corporate-action basis: for averaging, rights or bonus adjustments, use the actual quantities and prices recorded for the holding.
  3. Separate return from cash income: a capital gain, dividend and total return answer different questions.
  4. Review portfolio scope: make sure holdings, current prices and allocation percentages refer to the same valuation point.
  5. Verify changing rules: check current PSX, broker and tax-authority information before applying any rate, tax or settlement assumption.

Calcora performs the calculations from the inputs supplied. It does not provide a buy, sell or investment recommendation.
