Core PSX calculations explained

Gross P/L is sale value minus purchase value. Net P/L adjusts that result for entered costs and tax assumptions. Weighted average price is total purchase cost divided by total shares. Dividend income is dividend per share multiplied by shares held. Dividend yield is dividend per share divided by the reference share price, expressed as a percentage.

PSX CALCULATION GUIDE

How to calculate PSX profit, return, cost and risk

The Pakistan Stock Exchange (PSX) is Pakistan’s organised securities market. A share investment can produce a price gain or loss, cash dividends, or both; the outcome depends on the price paid, price received, transaction costs, corporate actions and the time the investment is held. Calcora’s PSX calculators use the figures you enter. They do not fetch live prices, verify a company’s dividend, or recommend a security.

1. Net trade profit or loss

For a completed buy-and-sell trade, calculate the purchase cost and sale proceeds first. A practical estimate is: net trading result = sale proceeds − purchase cost − buy-side charges − sell-side charges − any separately entered applicable tax. Use actual transaction records and avoid counting a charge twice if it is already included in a price or amount.

Illustrative example: buying 100 shares at PKR 50 costs PKR 5,000 before charges. Selling those shares at PKR 56 produces PKR 5,600 before charges. The gross price gain is PKR 600, or 12% of the original PKR 5,000 purchase value. The final net result will be lower after actual brokerage, levies and any applicable tax. These figures are an arithmetic example, not current market data or a tax calculation.

Use the PSX Profit Calculator for trade-level profit/loss and break-even, then the Brokerage Commission Calculator to estimate costs using your broker’s own tariff. For a running record across multiple buys and sells, use the PSX Trade Journal.

2. Weighted average share price

When you buy the same share at different prices, average cost is quantity-weighted: average price = total purchase value ÷ total shares acquired. For example, 100 shares at PKR 50 and 50 shares at PKR 60 have a combined purchase value of PKR 8,000 across 150 shares, giving a weighted average of PKR 53.33 per share before applicable charges. A simple average of PKR 50 and PKR 60 would be misleading because the quantities differ.

Use the Average Price Calculator for additional purchases. Bonus shares, rights issues, splits and other corporate actions can change the correct cost-basis treatment; reconcile them with your broker records rather than assuming ordinary purchase arithmetic covers every case.

3. Dividend yield and total return are different

Dividend yield = cash dividend per share ÷ the chosen share-price basis × 100. State whether the price is the current price, purchase price or another reference point. A yield based on purchase price is not the same as a yield based on today’s price, and a past dividend is not a promise of future income.

Price return measures the change in share price. Total return considers price movement and cash dividends, with the result depending on whether dividends are included and whether they are assumed to be reinvested. Bonus shares, rights issues, fees, taxes and the chosen measurement dates may also affect a fair comparison. Use the Dividend Yield Calculator, Dividend Reinvestment Calculator and PSX total-return guide for their distinct purposes.

4. Portfolio return, concentration and position sizing

A portfolio’s unrealised profit/loss is an estimate based on the entered reference prices; it is not cash profit from a completed sale. Portfolio percentage return and money-weighted annualised return (XIRR) answer different questions: XIRR also considers when cash entered or left the portfolio. Keep valuation dates consistent when comparing holdings.

Concentration measures help describe how much of a portfolio sits in a small number of holdings, but they do not capture every risk. Two companies in different sectors may still move together, and a portfolio can be difficult to sell at an assumed price. The PSX Portfolio Calculator supports holding-level analysis, while the Stock Comparison Calculator compares entered price and dividend scenarios. The Position Size Calculator translates a user-defined risk limit and stop level into an estimated share quantity; a stop price cannot guarantee execution at that price.

5. CGT, charges and changing rules

A calculator can perform arithmetic on a tax rate, but it cannot establish that the rate is legally applicable to a particular person, security, acquisition date or tax year. Tax treatment can depend on the relevant law and transition rules. Brokerage commissions and other charges may also differ between brokers and change over time. Verify current information before using a rate in a real transaction or tax return.

For market definitions and investor-protection guidance, consult the official PSX Guide to Investors, the PSX Investor Awareness Guide and the official PSX Data Portal screener. Use your broker’s current tariff and relevant official tax guidance for transaction-specific rates. These sources are references to verify, not a claim that Calcora displays live PSX data.

PSX terms in plain English

Brokerage / transaction charges
Costs associated with executing a trade. The applicable amount depends on the broker tariff and relevant charges; enter verified figures rather than a guessed universal rate.
Capital gain or loss
The difference between disposal proceeds and the relevant acquisition cost, adjusted for the costs and rules that apply to the transaction.
Dividend per share (DPS)
The cash dividend attributable to one share for the specified distribution. Confirm the amount and eligibility dates from an official company or exchange notice.
Dividend yield
A dividend amount expressed as a percentage of a stated share-price basis. Always identify which price and dividend period are used.
Unrealised P/L
The estimated difference between the entered value of shares still held and their recorded cost. It can change with market price and is not a completed cash result.
Realised P/L
The estimated result from shares sold, calculated using the cost method and transaction charges applied by the tool. It may differ from official tax or broker reporting.
XIRR
A money-weighted annualised return estimate that uses the dates and amounts of cash flows plus an ending value. It is sensitive to correct dates and signs for deposits, withdrawals and valuation.
Position size
The number of shares implied by a chosen budget or risk amount and the difference between an entry price and a planned stop level. It is a planning estimate, not a guarantee of loss limited to that amount.

PSX calculator: frequently asked questions

How do I calculate PSX share profit in Pakistan?

Multiply the sale price by shares sold to estimate sale proceeds, compare that with the relevant purchase cost, then subtract buy-side and sell-side charges and any applicable tax that you have independently verified. Use actual trade records for the final reconciliation.

Does Calcora show live PSX prices?

No. The calculators on this page use prices and assumptions entered by the user. Check current quotes and company announcements through official PSX resources or your broker before making a decision.

Is dividend yield the same as total return?

No. Dividend yield compares a cash dividend with a stated share-price basis. Total return considers price movement and dividends over a defined period and must state whether reinvestment, fees and taxes are included.

Can I use a Calcora CGT estimate for my tax return?

Do not treat a calculator estimate as an official tax determination. Confirm the relevant tax year, acquisition details, security category and applicable rules using current official tax guidance or a qualified tax professional.

Why is my portfolio return different from XIRR?

A simple portfolio return compares entered value and cost, while XIRR incorporates the dates and amounts of cash flows. Deposits, withdrawals and timing can therefore produce a different annualised result.

Are the calculations investment advice or a guaranteed result?

No. Results are estimates based on user-entered data and assumptions. They do not predict future prices, guarantee execution, establish suitability or replace independent financial, broker or tax guidance.

A reliable PSX investing workflow: from trade entry to net return

A useful result is only as reliable as the records behind it. For a Pakistani stock holding, keep the original contract note, the quantity and price actually executed, each charge shown by the broker, dividend announcements and payment records, and any corporate-action notice. Use the same ticker and date range throughout a comparison. If a figure is estimated rather than taken from a statement, label it as an estimate.

Use the right measure for the question

  • Weighted average cost: answers the average acquisition price for the units represented by the inputs. It is not automatically the final break-even price after selling costs.
  • Realised profit or loss: concerns units sold and depends on the cost-allocation method and costs included. Reconcile it with the broker's statement; personal tracking methods may differ from tax-lot treatment.
  • Unrealised profit or loss: compares the recorded cost of open holdings with an entered reference price. It can change without a sale and is not cash received.
  • Dividend yield: relates a stated dividend amount to a stated share-price basis. It does not, by itself, measure price movement or payout sustainability.
  • Money-weighted return (XIRR): uses dated contributions, withdrawals and ending value. Missing dates or incorrect cash-flow signs can materially change the result.
  • Portfolio concentration: describes how much exposure sits in a small number of holdings. A list of many tickers can still be concentrated if a few dominate the value.

Worked example: reconcile one hypothetical trade

Suppose an investor buys 100 shares at PKR 50 each. The purchase value is PKR 5,000 before charges. If the contract note shows PKR 40 of buy-side charges, the recorded outlay is PKR 5,040, or PKR 50.40 per share under this simplified cost-basis example. If 40 shares are later sold at PKR 60, gross proceeds are PKR 2,400 before sale-side costs. Do not call the PKR 400 difference between the sale price and the original PKR 50 price the final net profit: allocate the correct cost to the units sold and subtract actual sale charges and any relevant tax separately. This example is illustrative, not a statement of any broker's tariff or tax rule.

Monthly reconciliation checklist

  1. Match each buy and sell to its broker contract note and settlement record.
  2. Investigate negative holdings, duplicate imported rows, missing charges, and transactions entered in the wrong order.
  3. Record dividends separately from share-price changes and verify eligibility/payment details from the company or PSX announcement.
  4. Review bonus shares, rights issues, splits, transfers and other corporate actions before trusting the per-share cost.
  5. Keep a dated backup of your journal and compare the exported totals with broker statements.
  6. Verify current tax treatment using official guidance; do not reuse an old rate simply because it appears in a historical article or calculator.
