Voltage Drop Calculator

Estimate voltage drop across a known circuit resistance from current and resistance.

Voltage drop formula

When the total circuit resistance is known, voltage drop is Vdrop = I × R. Here I is current in amperes and R is the resistance in ohms.

Worked example

If current is 10 A and circuit resistance is 0.5 Ω, the voltage drop is 10 × 0.5 = 5 V. With a 230 V supply, the remaining voltage is approximately 225 V.

Voltage-drop percentage

If the supply voltage is known, percentage drop can be calculated as (Vdrop ÷ Vsupply) × 100. Keeping this percentage within an appropriate design limit depends on the installation, equipment, standards, and local electrical requirements.

Cable calculations need more inputs

A detailed cable voltage-drop calculation may require conductor material, cross-sectional area, one-way or circuit length, phase configuration, temperature, power factor, and installation assumptions. This calculator instead works from the total resistance you provide.

FAQ

What causes voltage drop? Current flowing through resistance produces a voltage loss along the circuit.

Is voltage drop always bad? Some drop is inherent in real circuits; whether it is acceptable depends on the equipment and applicable design requirements.

Does cable length affect voltage drop? Yes. Longer conductors generally add resistance, but the exact relationship depends on conductor and circuit details.
