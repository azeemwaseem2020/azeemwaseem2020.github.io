Acceleration formula

a = (v − u) ÷ t = Δv ÷ Δt, where u is initial velocity, v is final velocity and t is elapsed time. With velocity in m/s and time in seconds, acceleration is measured in m/s². For the force method, Newton’s second law gives a = F ÷ m when F is the net force.

How to calculate acceleration

  1. Enter the initial velocity.
  2. Enter the final velocity.
  3. Enter elapsed time greater than zero.
  4. Subtract initial velocity from final velocity.
  5. Divide the change in velocity by elapsed time.

Worked example

If velocity changes from 5 m/s to 25 m/s in 4 seconds: a = (25 − 5) ÷ 4 = 5 m/s².

Average acceleration, deceleration and g-force

Average acceleration describes the change in velocity over a time interval. A negative value can represent braking or motion opposite the chosen positive direction; it should not automatically be treated as a separate physical quantity called “deceleration.” Standard gravity is approximately 9.80665 m/s², so a result can also be compared with g when the context calls for it.

What the result means

A positive result means velocity increased according to the chosen sign convention. A negative result means velocity decreased. A zero result means there was no change in velocity over the interval. This calculator reports average acceleration for the selected interval.

Acceleration vs velocity

Velocity describes how position changes over time, while acceleration describes how velocity changes over time. An object can have velocity without accelerating, or acceleration while its velocity is changing direction.

Unit consistency

Use compatible units before calculating. If velocities are given in km/h but the result is needed in m/s², convert the velocity values to m/s first.

Common mistakes

  • Reversing final and initial velocity.
  • Using zero or negative elapsed time.
  • Mixing velocity units such as km/h and m/s.
  • Confusing average acceleration with instantaneous acceleration.

When to use this calculator

Use it for introductory physics, mechanics, classroom exercises, braking and speeding-up examples, and basic kinematics when the initial velocity, final velocity and elapsed time are known. Common acceleration problems

For a vehicle going from 0 to 20 m/s in 4 seconds, average acceleration is 5 m/s². For braking from 20 m/s to 0 in 4 seconds, the signed result is −5 m/s². For a net force of 300 N acting on a 50 kg mass, a = 300 ÷ 50 = 6 m/s².

Important limitation

This tool uses the standard average-acceleration formula. It does not model changing acceleration within the interval, forces, friction or detailed motion paths.
