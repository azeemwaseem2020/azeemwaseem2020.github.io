Ohm's law calculation context

Ohm's law connects voltage, current and resistance through V = IR. The three quantities can be solved from any two known values, making this a core electrical-calculation relationship.

Ohm's law in practical circuits

Ohm's law relates voltage, current and resistance for an ohmic component under the relevant operating conditions. It is most useful when the component behaves approximately linearly.

Worked example

With 12 V across a 6 Ω resistor, the ideal current is 2 A. The same relationship can be rearranged when current or resistance is the unknown.

Where the simple model needs care

Incandescent lamps, diodes and many electronic components are not constant-resistance devices. Their operating point can change as voltage, current or temperature changes.

Ohm's law FAQ

Does Ohm's law apply to every component?

No. It is a model for ohmic behavior rather than a universal rule for every electrical device.
