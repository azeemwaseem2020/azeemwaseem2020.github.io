Ohm's Law Calculator Guide

Ohm's law relates voltage, current and resistance through V = IR. Choose the quantity you want to find and enter the other two values.

Worked example

For 12 V across a 4 Ω resistance, current is I = V/R = 12/4 = 3 A. The same relationship can be rearranged to V = IR or R = V/I.

Units and practical limits

Use volts, amps and ohms consistently. The simple relationship is appropriate for components that behave approximately as ohmic devices under the stated conditions; some components change resistance with voltage, current or temperature.

ELECTRICITY MODEL CHECK

Ohm's law applies to an idealized voltage-current-resistance relationship

Ohm's law is V = IR. For an ohmic component, resistance is treated as constant over the relevant operating range. Real components can change resistance with temperature, voltage, current or operating conditions.

Three equivalent forms

Use V = IR, I = V/R or R = V/I depending on the known quantities, with compatible units.

Component behavior

A measured V/I ratio does not prove that every device is ohmic across all conditions. Check the component's operating characteristics when precision matters.

Power connection

Electrical power can be checked with P = VI, P = I²R or P = V²/R when the same resistance model applies.
