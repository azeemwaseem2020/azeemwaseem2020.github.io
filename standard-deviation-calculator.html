Standard Deviation Calculator

Calculate the mean, variance, and standard deviation of a dataset using either the population or sample method.

Standard deviation formula

Population standard deviation is σ = √[Σ(x − μ)² ÷ N]. Sample standard deviation is s = √[Σ(x − x̄)² ÷ (n − 1)]. The correct choice depends on whether your values are the whole population or a sample.

Worked example

For 10, 12, 15, 18, 20, the mean is 15. The population variance is 14.8, so the population standard deviation is approximately 3.847. A sample calculation uses n − 1 and produces a larger estimate.

What standard deviation means

Standard deviation describes the typical scale of variation around the mean, in the same units as the original data. A larger standard deviation indicates more spread under the same measurement context.

Population vs sample standard deviation

Population SD describes the complete set. Sample SD is commonly used to estimate population variability from a sample and uses n − 1 in the denominator.

FAQ

Can standard deviation be negative? No. Standard deviation is the non-negative square root of variance.

Does standard deviation use the same units as the data? Yes.

Why are population and sample results different? They use different denominators because they answer different statistical questions.

What standard deviation tells you

Standard deviation measures how spread out observations are around their mean. A smaller standard deviation indicates that values are more tightly clustered around the average, while a larger value indicates greater dispersion. The result has the same unit as the original observations.

Worked example

Consider measurements 10, 12, 12, 14 and 17. Their mean is 13. The deviations from the mean are -3, -1, -1, 1 and 4. Squaring and combining those deviations gives the variance basis used by the selected population or sample formula. The calculator then takes the square root to return the standard deviation.

Population versus sample

For a complete population, population standard deviation divides the squared deviations by N. For a sample used to estimate a wider population, sample standard deviation commonly divides by N − 1. Selecting the wrong definition can materially change the result, especially with small samples.

A practical Pakistan example

A quality team at a factory in Pakistan could record the diameter of several manufactured parts. The mean describes the central size, while standard deviation helps describe consistency. A low spread does not by itself prove that production meets specifications; the measurements must also be compared with the required tolerance limits.

Common mistakes

Do not mix population and sample calculations. Keep units consistent and check for data-entry errors or outliers. Standard deviation describes dispersion; it does not tell you whether the mean is correct, whether a process is acceptable, or whether two groups are statistically different.

FAQ

Can standard deviation be zero? Yes. It is zero when every observation is identical.

Does a larger standard deviation always mean worse data? No. The appropriate amount of variation depends on the purpose and acceptable range.

Should I use sample or population standard deviation? Use population when your data are the entire population of interest; use sample when they are intended to estimate a larger population.

Does standard deviation have units? Yes. It uses the same measurement unit as the underlying observations.
