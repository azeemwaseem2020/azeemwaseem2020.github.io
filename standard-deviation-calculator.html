Standard Deviation Calculator

Editorial information: Written and maintained by Abdul Qadir · Last updated: October 17, 2026

Calculate the mean, variance, and standard deviation of a dataset using either the population or sample method.

Standard deviation formula

Population standard deviation is σ = √[Σ(x − μ)² ÷ N]. Sample standard deviation is s = √[Σ(x − x̄)² ÷ (n − 1)]. The correct choice depends on whether your values are the whole population or a sample.

Worked example

For 10, 12, 15, 18, 20, the mean is 15. The population variance is 14.8, so the population standard deviation is approximately 3.847. A sample calculation uses n − 1 and produces a larger estimate.

What standard deviation means

Standard deviation describes the typical scale of variation around the mean, in the same units as the original data. A larger standard deviation indicates more spread under the same measurement context.

Population vs sample standard deviation

Population SD describes the complete set. Sample SD is commonly used to estimate population variability from a sample and uses n − 1 in the denominator.

Interpret spread, not performance

Standard deviation describes variation in the same units as the observations; it does not tell you whether a result is good or bad. Two datasets can have the same mean but different spread. For 8, 10 and 12, the mean is 10 and the population standard deviation is about 1.633. For 4, 10 and 16, the mean is also 10, but the population standard deviation is about 4.899 because the values sit farther from the mean.

Compare standard deviations only when the data units, measurement process and context make the comparison meaningful. A large value may reflect natural variation, mixed groups, measurement errors or outliers; inspect the data rather than treating one threshold as universally abnormal.

Formula and calculation method

The calculator first finds the mean, then sums each observation's squared distance from that mean. Population variance divides that sum by N; sample variance commonly divides by n − 1. Standard deviation is the square root of the selected variance, returning the spread to the original data units.

Practical example

For values 2, 4 and 6, the mean is 4 and the squared deviations sum to 8. Population variance is 8 ÷ 3 ≈ 2.6667 and population standard deviation is approximately 1.633. Sample variance is 8 ÷ 2 = 4 and sample standard deviation is 2.

Frequently asked questions

When should I choose sample standard deviation?

Use it when your observations are a sample used to estimate variability in a wider population; it uses the n − 1 denominator.

Can standard deviation be negative?

No. It is zero or positive because it is the square root of a non-negative variance.

Why do the two methods give different results?

They divide squared deviations by different denominators. The sample method applies a degrees-of-freedom adjustment.
