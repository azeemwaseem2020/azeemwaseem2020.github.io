Potential Energy Calculator — Gravitational PE

Solve for gravitational potential energy, mass, height or gravitational acceleration using PE = mgh.

Calculate

Formula

PE = mgh. Mass is in kilograms, height in metres and g in m/s², giving energy in joules.

Worked example

A 10 kg object 5 m above a reference level has PE = 10 × 9.80665 × 5 = 490.33 J.

Reference level matters

Gravitational potential energy is defined relative to a chosen reference height. A negative height can therefore be meaningful when the reference is above the object.

Common mistakes

  • Using grams without converting to kilograms.
  • Mixing height units such as cm and m.
  • Using a different gravity value without stating the location or assumption.
  • Forgetting that potential energy is relative to a reference level.

Common gravitational potential-energy questions

Use PE = mgh when you are modelling gravitational potential energy near a surface where g can be treated as approximately constant. The same relationship can be rearranged to find mass, height or gravity when the other quantities are known.

Find potential energy

Use PE = mgh with mass, gravity and height.

Find mass

Rearrange to m = PE ÷ (gh).

Find height

Rearrange to h = PE ÷ (mg).

Find gravity

Rearrange to g = PE ÷ (mh) when the reference height is non-zero.

Potential Energy Calculator Guide

Use this calculator for gravitational potential-energy problems near a surface where gravitational acceleration can be treated as approximately constant.

Formula

PE = mgh, where PE is energy in joules, m is mass in kilograms, g is gravitational acceleration in m/s², and h is height relative to the chosen reference level.

Worked example

A 10 kg object 5 m above the chosen reference level, using g = 9.80665 m/s², has approximately 490.33 J of gravitational potential energy.

Choosing the reference level

Potential energy is relative. The zero-height level is selected for the problem, so a negative height can be meaningful when the object is below that reference.

Solving for a missing value

The same relationship can be rearranged to find mass, height, or gravitational acceleration when the other quantities are known. Keep the units consistent before calculating.

Common mistakes

  • Using grams instead of kilograms.
  • Mixing centimetres and metres.
  • Using a gravity value without checking the stated assumption.
  • Forgetting that the result depends on the chosen reference height.

When this formula is not enough

The mgh model is a near-surface approximation with approximately constant g. Orbital and large-distance gravitational problems require a different model, such as gravitational potential energy based on distance from the attracting body's centre.

Choose a reference height

Potential energy depends on the chosen reference level. A change in height matters physically; the absolute zero level is a convention selected for the problem.

How this calculation is checked

ENERGY MODEL CHECK

Gravitational potential energy depends on the chosen reference level

Near Earth's surface, gravitational potential energy is commonly written as PE = mgh. The zero level for h is a reference choice, so the physically useful quantity in many problems is the change in potential energy between two positions.

Reference level

State what height is being treated as zero. Changing the reference changes the absolute PE value but not the energy difference between the same two positions.

Near-Earth model

The mgh relationship assumes approximately constant gravitational acceleration over the height range being considered.

Energy sign

A negative value can be meaningful when the chosen reference puts the object below zero height. Do not treat the sign alone as an error.
