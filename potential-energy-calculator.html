Potential Energy Calculator — Gravitational PE

Solve for gravitational potential energy, mass, height or gravitational acceleration using PE = mgh.

Calculate

Formula

PE = mgh. Mass is in kilograms, height in metres and g in m/s², giving energy in joules.

Worked example

A 10 kg object 5 m above a reference level has PE = 10 × 9.80665 × 5 = 490.33 J.

Reference level matters

Gravitational potential energy is defined relative to a chosen reference height. A negative height can therefore be meaningful when the reference is above the object.

Common mistakes

  • Using grams without converting to kilograms.
  • Mixing height units such as cm and m.
  • Using a different gravity value without stating the location or assumption.
  • Forgetting that potential energy is relative to a reference level.

Common gravitational potential-energy questions

Use PE = mgh when you are modelling gravitational potential energy near a surface where g can be treated as approximately constant. The same relationship can be rearranged to find mass, height or gravity when the other quantities are known.

Find potential energy

Use PE = mgh with mass, gravity and height.

Find mass

Rearrange to m = PE ÷ (gh).

Find height

Rearrange to h = PE ÷ (mg).

Find gravity

Rearrange to g = PE ÷ (mh) when the reference height is non-zero.

Gravitational potential energy formula

Near Earth's surface, gravitational potential energy can be calculated with PE = mgh, where mass is in kilograms, gravitational acceleration is in m/s² and height is in metres.

Choose a reference height

Potential energy depends on the chosen reference level. A change in height matters physically; the absolute zero level is a convention selected for the problem.
