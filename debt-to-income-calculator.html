How to calculate DTI

Enter total recurring monthly debt payments and gross monthly income. The calculator divides monthly debt by gross monthly income and expresses the result as a percentage.

What the result means

A higher percentage means a larger share of gross monthly income is represented by the debt amount entered. Lenders can use different definitions of debt and different thresholds, so treat this as a calculation aid rather than a lending decision.

DTI formula

DTI = (monthly debt payments ÷ gross monthly income) × 100. Use the same time period for both inputs and enter gross income consistently.

Debt-to-Income Ratio Calculator

Debt-to-income ratio, or DTI, compares recurring monthly debt payments with gross monthly income. Lenders may use DTI as one part of an affordability or credit assessment.

Formula

DTI = Total monthly debt payments ÷ Gross monthly income × 100.

Worked example

If monthly debt payments are $1,800 and gross monthly income is $6,000, the DTI is 30%. A different lender may calculate qualifying debt differently, so use the lender's stated definitions when making a borrowing decision.

What can be included?

Common recurring obligations may include mortgage or rent-related debt, auto loans, student loans, credit-card minimums, and other required payments. Exact treatment varies by lender and loan program.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is DTI based on gross or net income?

Many lending calculations use gross monthly income, before taxes and other deductions.

Does a lower DTI guarantee approval?

No. Income stability, credit history, assets, loan rules, and other underwriting factors can also affect a lender's decision.
