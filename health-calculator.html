WHAT THE NUMBERS MEAN

BMI and BMR answer different questions

BMI is a height-to-weight screening measure. BMR is an estimate of the energy your body uses at rest based on the selected equation and inputs.

Do not use either result alone to diagnose a condition or prescribe a diet. Age, pregnancy, muscle mass, medical conditions, medications and many other factors can affect health decisions.

BMI formula

For metric inputs, BMI is calculated as weight in kilograms ÷ height in metres². For imperial inputs, the calculator converts the measurements to metric before applying the same relationship.

BMR formula

This page uses the Mifflin–St Jeor equation. For men: BMR = 10W + 6.25H − 5A + 5. For women: BMR = 10W + 6.25H − 5A − 161, where W is weight in kilograms, H is height in centimetres and A is age in years.

Understanding BMI

BMI is a population-level screening measure and does not directly measure body fat. The same BMI can occur in people with different body compositions. Children, adolescents, pregnancy and some athletic or clinical situations require different interpretation.

Understanding BMR

BMR is an estimate of resting energy expenditure, not a complete daily calorie requirement. Daily energy needs also depend on activity, body composition and other factors. Do not treat the BMR result as a personalized medical or nutrition prescription.

Frequently asked questions

Is BMI a diagnosis?

No. BMI is a screening measure. A healthcare professional can interpret it with other relevant information.

Does BMR equal the calories I should eat each day?

No. BMR estimates resting energy expenditure. Daily energy needs are generally higher and vary by activity and individual circumstances.

Can this calculator replace medical advice?

No. If you have symptoms, a medical condition, are pregnant, or need individualized nutrition or treatment advice, use guidance from a qualified professional.
