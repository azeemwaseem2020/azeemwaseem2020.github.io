PSX Total Return: Price Gain, Dividends, Costs and the Break-Even Point

A share's price change is only one part of an investment result. For a more complete calculation, separate price gain or loss, cash dividends, transaction costs and the amount originally invested.

Price return

Price gain = (Sell price − Buy price) × Shares

If 500 shares are bought at Rs 100 and sold at Rs 115, the gross price gain is Rs 7,500. That is a price result before commissions, taxes or other costs.

Add dividends separately

If the position received Rs 3 per share in cash dividends while those 500 shares were held, dividend income is Rs 1,500. A simple gross cash return before costs would therefore be Rs 9,000.

Total return percentage

Total return % = (Net gain ÷ Initial investment) × 100

With an initial investment of Rs 50,000, the Rs 9,000 gross gain above corresponds to 18%. If total purchase and sale costs are Rs 1,000, net gain becomes Rs 8,000 and the net return becomes 16%.

Why average price matters

When shares are purchased in multiple lots, a simple average of trade prices can be misleading if quantities differ. A quantity-weighted average cost is:

Weighted average price = Total purchase value ÷ Total shares

This gives the correct acquisition-price basis for a position before considering additional fees or adjustments that belong in the investor's cost basis.

Break-even selling price

Break-even depends on what you include. In a simplified model, the required sale proceeds equal the invested amount plus included costs minus any cash income you choose to credit against the position. Real-world settlement charges and taxes should follow the applicable broker statement and tax rules.

Do not mix gross and net numbers

A common mistake is to compare a gross selling value with a net purchase cost, or to add dividends while forgetting that they may have their own tax treatment. Keep a transaction ledger with date, quantity, price, charges, dividend and tax fields. Then calculate from the same basis throughout.

Example with multiple purchases

Suppose 200 shares are bought at Rs 80 and 300 shares at Rs 120. Total cost is Rs 16,000 + Rs 36,000 = Rs 52,000 for 500 shares, giving a weighted average of Rs 104 per share. If the shares later sell at Rs 115, the gross price gain is Rs 5,500 before transaction costs and taxes.

What the calculator does not know automatically

A calculator cannot know your broker's current fee schedule, settlement charges, tax treatment or corporate-action adjustments unless you enter them. Treat calculated return as a transparent model and reconcile it with the official trade and account records.

FAQ

Does stock price return include dividends?

Not by itself. A price-return calculation tracks the price change; total-return analysis can add cash dividends and account for relevant costs.

Why use weighted average price?

Because different purchase quantities should contribute proportionally to the position's average acquisition cost.

Is a calculated PSX return a tax statement?

No. Tax treatment depends on the applicable rules and your transaction records. Use official statements and current tax guidance for filing decisions.
