EMI Amortization Schedule: How to Read Your Loan Payment

The monthly EMI is only the starting point. An amortization schedule shows how each payment reduces principal, covers interest and changes the remaining balance over time.

What an amortization schedule shows

A standard reducing-balance schedule lists the payment number, payment amount, interest for that period, principal repaid and remaining balance. Early payments can contain a larger interest component because interest is calculated on a higher outstanding balance. As the balance falls, the interest component normally falls too.

The reducing-balance EMI formula

For a regular monthly-payment loan, the standard formula is EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n / ((1 + r)n − 1), where P is the amount financed, r is the monthly rate and n is the number of monthly payments. The annual quoted rate must be converted to the periodic rate in a way that matches the lender's calculation convention.

Worked example

Consider PKR 100,000 at a 10% annual reducing-balance rate for 5 years. Using the standard monthly formula gives an EMI of approximately PKR 2,125 and scheduled repayment of about PKR 127,482 over 60 payments. The exact lender schedule can differ because of rounding, payment dates, fees, insurance or a different rate convention.

Why the first payments can feel interest-heavy

Interest is calculated from the outstanding balance. At the start of the loan, the balance is close to the original amount, so the interest portion is larger. After principal has been paid down, the same nominal rate is applied to a smaller balance and the interest portion becomes smaller.

EMI versus total loan cost

A lower monthly payment is not automatically the cheaper loan. Extending the term can reduce the monthly amount while increasing the number of interest-bearing periods. When comparing offers, check EMI, total scheduled repayment, interest, fees, insurance and any early-settlement conditions together.

Why the official repayment schedule matters

For a Pakistan bank or DFI facility, use the lender-issued repayment schedule when the actual contract matters. State Bank of Pakistan guidance on lending-product documentation calls for an amortization schedule showing the principal and mark-up components over the life of the financing or until the next repricing date for floating-rate products.

Official reference: State Bank of Pakistan — Pricing of Lending Products and Loan Documentation.

Reducing-balance versus flat-rate offers

A flat-rate quotation calculates interest differently from a reducing-balance loan. Two products can display the same headline percentage but have materially different total costs. Calcora's EMI calculator provides a reducing-balance estimate and a comparison view; always compare it with the lender's official schedule.

What can change a real lender schedule?

  • Variable or benchmark-linked rates.
  • Processing fees and other charges.
  • Insurance or financed add-ons.
  • Different payment dates or daily-interest conventions.
  • Rounding rules.
  • Early settlement, partial prepayment or late-payment charges.

How to use an amortization schedule

  1. Confirm the original amount financed.
  2. Check the quoted rate and whether it is fixed, variable, reducing-balance or flat-rate.
  3. Confirm the number and frequency of payments.
  4. Review the principal and interest columns rather than EMI alone.
  5. Compare total repayment and fees before accepting an offer.

Questions to ask a lender

Ask for the official repayment schedule, the method used to calculate interest, the effective cost of the loan, all compulsory fees and the rules for early settlement or partial prepayment. For variable-rate products, ask how and when the rate can change.
