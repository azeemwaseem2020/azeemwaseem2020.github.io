Photon Energy Calculator Guide — Frequency, Wavelength & Energy

Use this calculator to find the energy of a single photon from its frequency or wavelength. It is useful for introductory electromagnetic and quantum-physics calculations.

Formula

The photon-energy relationship is E = hf = hc/λ. Here, E is photon energy in joules, h is Planck's constant, f is frequency in hertz, c is the speed of light in vacuum, and λ is wavelength in metres.

Worked example

For a frequency of 5 × 1014 Hz, the photon energy is approximately 3.31 × 10−19 J. A higher frequency means higher energy per photon, while a longer wavelength means lower energy.

Frequency and wavelength must agree

For electromagnetic radiation in vacuum, frequency and wavelength are related by c = fλ. If both inputs are supplied, this calculator checks that they are reasonably consistent before calculating the energy.

Useful unit conversions

The calculator returns joules per photon. In spectroscopy and chemistry, photon energies are also commonly expressed in electronvolts (eV). Use consistent SI units when applying the formula, then convert the final result if another unit is required.

What the result means

The result is the energy of one photon, not the total energy in a light beam. Total beam energy depends on the number of photons involved.

Limits

This tool uses the ideal photon relationship for electromagnetic radiation. It does not calculate material-specific absorption, emission spectra, intensity, or quantum transition probabilities.

Photon energy depends on frequency, or inversely on wavelength

Photon energy follows E = hf and, using c = fλ, can also be written E = hc/λ. Higher-frequency photons have greater energy, while longer-wavelength photons have lower energy.

Keep wavelength units consistent

The SI form uses metres for wavelength. Convert nanometres or other units before applying constants expressed in SI units.

Frequency and wavelength

In vacuum, c = fλ. In a material, wave speed can differ from c, so the medium and wavelength definition should be clear.

Energy is per photon

The result from E = hf is the energy of one photon. A beam's total energy also depends on how many photons are involved.

Photon energy and wavelength

Photon energy is related to frequency and inversely related to wavelength. Shorter-wavelength electromagnetic radiation corresponds to higher photon energy.

Worked example

For light with a wavelength of 500 nm, the photon energy is about 3.98 × 10⁻¹⁹ J, or roughly 2.48 eV.

Unit awareness

Photon energies are often expressed in joules or electronvolts. Make sure the wavelength unit is converted consistently before applying the relationship.

Photon-energy FAQ

Does brighter light mean each photon has more energy?

Not necessarily. For a fixed wavelength, brightness can increase because more photons arrive, while the energy of each photon remains the same.

Photon Energy Formula: Frequency, Wavelength and Examples

Photon energy is the energy carried by one quantum of electromagnetic radiation. It depends on frequency: higher-frequency radiation has higher-energy photons. Wavelength gives the same information when the radiation travels through vacuum and the wavelength is expressed in metres.

Formula and constants

E = hf = hc/λ, where E is energy in joules, h is Planck’s constant (approximately 6.62607015 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s), f is frequency in hertz, c is the speed of light in vacuum (approximately 2.99792458 × 10⁸ m/s), and λ is wavelength in metres. Frequency and wavelength are related by c = fλ in vacuum.

Worked examples

For frequency f = 5.0 × 10¹⁴ Hz, E = hf ≈ 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ × 5.0 × 10¹⁴ = 3.31 × 10⁻¹⁹ J per photon. For wavelength λ = 600 nm, first convert to metres: 600 nm = 6.00 × 10⁻⁷ m. Then E = hc/λ ≈ 3.31 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. Small differences in the final digits can result from rounding constants and intermediate values.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter a positive frequency in hertz or a positive wavelength in metres, according to the field you use.
  2. Convert prefixes first: nanometres to metres, kilohertz to hertz, and so on.
  3. Calculate and inspect the units alongside the scientific-notation result.
  4. Check the result order of magnitude; visible-light photons typically have energies of a few electron-volts.

Features and applications

Energy may also be expressed in electron-volts: 1 eV ≈ 1.602176634 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. To convert joules to electron-volts, divide by this value.

Interpretation and limitations

Do not confuse the energy of one photon with the total energy in a beam. Total beam energy also depends on the number of photons or optical power and exposure duration. In a material, wavelength changes with refractive index; use the vacuum relation carefully and distinguish vacuum wavelength from wavelength inside a medium. The formula describes photon energy, not directly the biological effect, brightness or safety of radiation.

Keep significant figures consistent with the input precision. Scientific notation is expected for joule-scale photon energies and should not be mistaken for an error.

Frequently asked questions

Does shorter wavelength mean higher photon energy?

Yes. Since E = hc/λ, energy rises as wavelength decreases when the other constants are fixed.

How do I convert photon energy to electron-volts?

Divide energy in joules by approximately 1.602176634 × 10⁻¹⁹ joules per electron-volt.

Does a brighter beam mean each photon has more energy?

Not necessarily. Brightness can increase because there are more photons; individual photon energy depends on frequency.
