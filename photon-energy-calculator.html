Photon Energy Calculator Guide — Frequency, Wavelength & Energy

Use this calculator to find the energy of a single photon from its frequency or wavelength. It is useful for introductory electromagnetic and quantum-physics calculations.

Formula

The photon-energy relationship is E = hf = hc/λ. Here, E is photon energy in joules, h is Planck's constant, f is frequency in hertz, c is the speed of light in vacuum, and λ is wavelength in metres.

Worked example

For a frequency of 5 × 1014 Hz, the photon energy is approximately 3.31 × 10−19 J. A higher frequency means higher energy per photon, while a longer wavelength means lower energy.

Frequency and wavelength must agree

For electromagnetic radiation in vacuum, frequency and wavelength are related by c = fλ. If both inputs are supplied, this calculator checks that they are reasonably consistent before calculating the energy.

Useful unit conversions

The calculator returns joules per photon. In spectroscopy and chemistry, photon energies are also commonly expressed in electronvolts (eV). Use consistent SI units when applying the formula, then convert the final result if another unit is required.

What the result means

The result is the energy of one photon, not the total energy in a light beam. Total beam energy depends on the number of photons involved.

Limits

This tool uses the ideal photon relationship for electromagnetic radiation. It does not calculate material-specific absorption, emission spectra, intensity, or quantum transition probabilities.

MODERN PHYSICS MODEL CHECK

Photon energy depends on frequency, or inversely on wavelength

Photon energy follows E = hf and, using c = fλ, can also be written E = hc/λ. Higher-frequency photons have greater energy, while longer-wavelength photons have lower energy.

Keep wavelength units consistent

The SI form uses metres for wavelength. Convert nanometres or other units before applying constants expressed in SI units.

Frequency and wavelength

In vacuum, c = fλ. In a material, wave speed can differ from c, so the medium and wavelength definition should be clear.

Energy is per photon

The result from E = hf is the energy of one photon. A beam's total energy also depends on how many photons are involved.
