Voltage Divider Calculator

Calculate the output voltage of a simple two-resistor voltage divider.

Voltage divider formula

For an unloaded two-resistor divider, Vout = Vin × R2 ÷ (R1 + R2). R1 is connected toward the input and R2 toward the reference/ground side.

Worked example

With Vin = 12 V, R1 = 1,000 Ω and R2 = 1,000 Ω, Vout = 12 × 1000 ÷ 2000 = 6 V.

Loading matters

The simple formula assumes the output is unloaded. Connecting a load in parallel with R2 changes the effective lower resistance and therefore changes Vout. For circuit design, include the load when its resistance is significant.

Common uses

Voltage dividers are used for signal scaling, sensor interfaces, reference levels, and simple analog circuits. They are not automatically suitable as power supplies because the output voltage changes with load current.

FAQ

Which resistor goes in the numerator? R2, the resistor on the output side of the simple divider.

Can R1 or R2 be zero? R2 may be zero mathematically; R1 + R2 must be greater than zero for the formula to be defined.

Why is my measured voltage different? Load resistance, resistor tolerance, supply variation, wiring resistance, and measurement effects can change the real result.

How a voltage divider works

A resistor voltage divider uses series resistors to produce a fraction of an input voltage. The ideal unloaded result depends on the resistor ratio.

Worked example

With a 12 V input and two resistors of 2 kΩ and 4 kΩ, the voltage across the 4 kΩ resistor is 8 V in the ideal unloaded model.

Loading matters

Connecting a load changes the effective resistance and can change the output voltage. For practical design, the load should be included rather than assuming the divider is unloaded.

Voltage-divider FAQ

Can a divider supply unlimited current?

No. A voltage divider is not a substitute for a regulated power supply and its current capability is limited by the circuit.
