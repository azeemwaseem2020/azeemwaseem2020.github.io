Conception Date Calculator: Estimate Timing and Understand the Limits

A conception-date estimate approximates when fertilisation may have occurred. It is not usually possible to identify the exact conception day from a due date alone because ovulation, sperm survival and cycle timing vary. Use the result as a broad planning estimate, not proof of paternity or a precise medical record.

Common dating relationship

In a typical pregnancy, the estimated due date is about 266 days (38 weeks) after conception. To estimate conception from an expected due date, subtract 266 days. By contrast, pregnancy gestational age is conventionally counted from the first day of the last menstrual period, roughly two weeks before conception in a typical 28-day cycle. These are different clocks and should not be confused.

Worked example

If an estimated due date is 1 November, subtracting 266 days gives an approximate conception date around 9 February in the same calendar-year cycle context. The exact calendar result depends on the year and leap day. This estimate assumes a typical timeline; later ovulation or uncertain due-date dating can move the likely window.

How to use the calculator

  1. Check whether the tool asks for a due date, last menstrual period or another reference date.
  2. Enter the date with the correct year and use the same dating method as your source.
  3. Calculate the estimated date and treat it as approximate rather than exact.
  4. If dates matter for medical decisions, discuss them with a qualified clinician.

Features and practical uses

The estimate can help people understand the relationship between conception timing, gestational age and the expected delivery date. It can support personal timeline planning and prepare questions for a prenatal appointment. If comparing two calculators, verify whether they count 266 days from conception or use 280 days from the last menstrual period. A difference of around two weeks can be normal when tools use different starting points.

Limitations and common misunderstandings

Ovulation does not always happen on cycle day 14. Sperm can remain capable of fertilisation for several days, while the egg has a shorter fertilisation window after ovulation. Therefore, intercourse date, ovulation date and conception date are related but not interchangeable. An ultrasound-based due date can also change the estimate, especially when the original period date is uncertain.

A calculator cannot determine the exact date of intercourse, confirm paternity, or establish whether a pregnancy is progressing normally. Do not use a date estimate as the sole basis for a medical or legal decision. If you have irregular cycles, fertility treatment, uncertain dates or concerns about pregnancy, seek advice from a healthcare professional who can interpret the full history.

Frequently asked questions

Is conception always 14 days after the period starts?

No. That is a simplified assumption for a typical 28-day cycle; ovulation timing varies.

Can a due date prove the exact conception date?

No. It provides only an estimate, and the likely window can span multiple days.

Why is gestational age about two weeks more than time since conception?

Gestational age is conventionally counted from the last menstrual period, before conception usually occurs.
