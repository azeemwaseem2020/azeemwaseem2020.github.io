Sphere volume and surface area: complete guide

A sphere is a three-dimensional shape whose surface points are equally distant from its centre. This calculator uses radius to calculate volume—the space enclosed—and surface area, the outside surface. These measures are used in geometry, model-making, packaging estimates and calculations involving approximately spherical objects.

Formulas

Volume = (4/3) × π × r³.
Surface area = 4 × π × r².

Here r is the radius and π is approximately 3.14159265. Volume uses cubic units such as cm³ or m³; surface area uses square units such as cm² or m². The radius is cubed for volume and squared for surface area, so entering an incorrect radius can substantially change the answer.

How to use it

  1. Measure the radius and note its unit.
  2. Enter the radius in R. If you know the diameter, divide it by two first.
  3. Select Calculate to see volume and surface area.
  4. Keep squared and cubed units distinct, and round according to measurement precision.

Worked example

For radius 3 cm, volume = (4/3) × π × 3³ = 36π ≈ 113.097 cm³. Surface area = 4 × π × 3² = 36π ≈ 113.097 cm². The numerical values happen to match, but the units and physical meanings differ.

Radius vs. diameter and common mistakes

Diameter crosses the sphere through its centre and equals twice the radius: d = 2r. For a ball 10 cm across, enter radius 5 cm. Entering 10 cm as the radius makes volume eight times too large and surface area four times too large. Use consistent length units and do not confuse volume with surface area.

Practical limits

For liquid capacity, the internal radius matters rather than the outside radius. Real objects may have seams, openings, wall thickness or an imperfect spherical shape. A hollow shell, hemisphere or spherical cap needs a formula for that specific geometry. This tool assumes an ideal solid sphere and does not account for deformation, wall thickness or measurement uncertainty.

Frequently asked questions

Should I enter the radius or diameter?

Enter the radius, which is half the diameter. If a sphere measures 12 cm across through its centre, its radius is 6 cm. Confirm which measurement you have before calculating.

Why are the volume and surface-area numbers different in meaning?

Volume describes three-dimensional space and uses cubic units, while surface area describes the outer two-dimensional boundary and uses square units. Even when their numerical values happen to match for a particular radius, they are not interchangeable.

Can I use this for a hollow ball or tank?

The standard formula gives the volume of an ideal solid sphere. For a hollow object, subtract the inner sphere’s volume from the outer sphere’s volume to estimate shell material. For liquid capacity, use the internal dimensions and account for the actual shape and fill level.
