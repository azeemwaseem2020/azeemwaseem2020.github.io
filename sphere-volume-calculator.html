Volume of a sphere

The volume of a sphere is V = 4/3 × πr³, where r is the radius. Because the radius is cubed, small changes in radius can create relatively large changes in volume.

Worked example

For a sphere with radius 3 cm, V = 4/3 × π × 27 = 36π ≈ 113.10 cm³. If the diameter is given instead, divide it by 2 to obtain the radius.

Practical example

The formula can estimate spherical tanks, balls or idealized storage shapes. Real tanks may not be perfect spheres, and usable capacity can be smaller than geometric volume because of fittings or operating limits.

FAQs

Can I enter diameter? Convert it to radius if the calculator asks for radius. What are the units? Cubic units. Does doubling radius double volume? No; it increases volume by a factor of eight.
