Inverse trigonometric functions

Inverse trigonometric functions return an angle from a trigonometric ratio. For x in the allowed domain, asin(x) gives the angle whose sine is x, acos(x) gives the angle whose cosine is x, and atan(x) gives the angle whose tangent is x.

Domains and ranges

For asin and acos, the input must be between −1 and 1. Their principal ranges are −90° to 90° for asin and 0° to 180° for acos. Atan accepts any real input and its principal range is between −90° and 90°, excluding the endpoints.

Worked example

For x = 0.5, asin(0.5) = 30° and acos(0.5) = 60°. Atan(0.5) is approximately 26.565°.

Degrees vs radians

Many programming languages and scientific libraries return inverse-trigonometric results in radians. This calculator converts the result to degrees for easier everyday interpretation.

FAQ

What is the difference between inverse trig and reciprocal trig? Inverse functions such as asin are not the same as reciprocals such as 1/sin(x).

Why is asin limited to −1 through 1? A real sine value cannot be greater than 1 or less than −1.

Can I use atan with values outside −1 to 1? Yes. Atan accepts any real input.

Inverse trigonometric functions

Inverse trigonometric functions return an angle from a known trigonometric ratio. The principal functions are arcsin, arccos and arctan. For example, if sin(θ) = 0.5, arcsin(0.5) returns a principal angle of 30°. Always check whether the calculator is using degrees or radians before interpreting the result.

Worked example

If opposite = 3 and hypotenuse = 5, then sin(θ) = 3/5 = 0.6. The inverse sine gives θ ≈ 36.87° in degree mode. A calculator in radian mode gives the same angle expressed in radians instead.

Common uses

Inverse trigonometry is useful in surveying, engineering, physics and construction when a side ratio is known and an angle must be recovered. For field measurements in Pakistan, keep all lengths in compatible units before forming the ratio.

Limitations

Arcsin and arccos accept inputs from −1 to 1. Arctan can accept any real input, but the principal angle alone may not identify the correct quadrant when working from signed coordinates.

FAQs

Does arcsin give every possible angle? No, it returns a principal value. Why are degree and radian results different? They are different units for the same angle. Can inverse trig solve a full triangle? It can supply an angle, but other sides or angles may be needed.
