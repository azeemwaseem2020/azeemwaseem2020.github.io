TMR Feed Calculator Guide: Dry Matter, As-Fed Weight, Cost & Batch Planning

By: Abdul Qadir · Updated: October 8, 2026

TMR means Total Mixed Ration. A good calculator should do more than multiply percentages: it should make the difference between dry matter and as-fed weight clear, scale a known formulation to the required batch, and help a farmer check quantity and cost without pretending to replace professional ration formulation.

Core formula: ingredient quantity = total batch weight × ingredient percentage ÷ 100. For dry-matter conversion, as-fed quantity = required dry-matter quantity ÷ dry-matter fraction.

What modern TMR tools should help you answer

Current TMR and dairy calculator tools commonly focus on animal context, feed ingredients, dry matter, batch quantity and economics. Some also provide nutrient analysis or herd profitability. Calcora follows the useful parts of that workflow while keeping the calculation transparent and avoiding copied recommendations.

How the TMR batch calculation works

Choose the batch size, enter the number of animals and planned daily as-fed amount, then enter the known ingredient percentages. Each percentage is converted to kilograms and the page checks whether the formulation totals 100%.

Worked example: a 1,000 kg batch with an ingredient at 25% contains 250 kg. If 20 animals each receive 20 kg/day, group demand is 400 kg/day and the arithmetic batch coverage is 2.5 days.

500 kg, 1,000 kg and 2,000 kg TMR batches

The percentage formula scales directly. At 25%, a 500 kg batch contains 125 kg, a 1,000 kg batch contains 250 kg and a 2,000 kg batch contains 500 kg. This makes the calculator useful for different mixer sizes and farm routines without changing the underlying formulation.

Why the 100% check matters

If ingredient percentages total 92% or 108%, the entries do not describe a complete batch. A professional workflow should stop and correct the formulation rather than silently treating the numbers as a complete ration.

Dry matter vs as-fed TMR

Dairy rations are commonly formulated on a dry-matter basis while feeds are physically loaded on an as-fed basis. Moisture changes the physical kilograms required to deliver a dry-matter target. University of Minnesota Extension specifically recommends checking dry matter of ensiled forages and wet feeds because moisture changes can alter the amount that should be mixed.

Example: if the target is 10 kg dry matter and a feed is 40% DM, the simple conversion is 10 ÷ 0.40 = 25 kg as-fed. Use current feed analysis for real formulation rather than relying on a fixed assumed DM value.

Why forage moisture deserves attention

Silage and other wet ingredients can change in dry matter over time. A calculator can perform the conversion, but the quality of the result depends on the quality of the input data. Extension guidance recommends regularly checking moisture/DM for ensiled forages and wet feeds.

TMR ingredient quantity: what the calculator does not decide

The tool can calculate the kilograms implied by a known formulation, but it should not invent the percentage of maize silage, wheat straw, concentrate, mineral mix or another ingredient simply because a user entered a cow weight. A real formulation depends on the animal group and the nutritional value of the actual feeds.

Dairy cow and buffalo planning

Animal type, body weight, milk yield, lactation stage, forage quality and environmental conditions can change feeding decisions. Competitor tools increasingly expose these as inputs, while Pakistani-focused tools also distinguish cow and buffalo contexts. Calcora includes context fields for planning but deliberately does not turn them into a generic prescription.

Feed cost: from batch cost to cost per animal

If each ingredient has a current price per kilogram, batch cost can be estimated as the sum of ingredient kilograms × price/kg. From there, a simple daily feed cost is possible when the daily quantity is known. More advanced dairy tools extend this into feed cost per litre and income-over-feed-cost analysis.

TMR consistency and group management

A TMR is intended to provide a consistent mixture, but the result also depends on actual ingredient quality, mixing equipment, weighing accuracy and the animal group. Extension guidance notes that cows within a TMR group should be reasonably similar in production and body weight and that daily intake can change with weather and other conditions.

Practical TMR checklist

  • Use current feed or forage analysis where possible.
  • Record dry matter for wet feeds and update it when conditions change.
  • Keep DM-basis and as-fed quantities clearly separated.
  • Confirm ingredient percentages total 100% before batching.
  • Check mixer capacity and weighing-scale accuracy.
  • Compare planned feed with actual intake and refusals.
  • Group animals appropriately rather than assuming one ration fits every cow.
  • Use qualified nutrition advice for nutrient balancing or health-sensitive decisions.

FAQ

How do I calculate TMR ingredient quantity?

Multiply the total batch weight by the ingredient percentage and divide by 100. For example, 30% of 1,000 kg is 300 kg.

How do I convert dry matter to as-fed weight?

Divide the required dry-matter weight by the feed's DM fraction. A 40% DM feed has a 0.40 DM fraction.

Can this calculate a complete cow ration?

No. It can calculate quantities from a known formulation and support planning. Complete nutrient formulation requires appropriate animal requirements and actual feed analysis.

Can I use it for buffalo?

Yes for quantity planning from a known buffalo formulation. It does not prescribe a buffalo-specific diet.

Can I calculate feed cost?

Yes, when current ingredient prices are entered. The result is an arithmetic estimate and does not include every farm overhead.

Can I use a generic online TMR recipe?

Do not treat a generic recipe as a universal diet. Feed moisture, nutrient content, animal group and production level can change the appropriate formulation.

How to choose the right TMR tool

If you already know the ingredient percentages, use the Calcora TMR calculator for batch scaling, dry-matter arithmetic and cost. If you need a nutrient-balanced ration, the required workflow is different: it needs reliable animal requirements and current feed analysis, and should be reviewed by a qualified nutrition professional. Advanced ration tools may calculate nutrients such as crude protein, fibre and energy, but those outputs are only as good as the input data and model used.

That distinction is intentional: a transparent calculator should not present a simple percentage calculation as a professional nutrient prescription.

Important limitation

Calcora is a planning calculator, not a veterinary or nutrition prescription service. It does not diagnose animals, analyse forage in a laboratory or guarantee milk production. For a production ration, use current feed analysis and appropriate professional advice.
