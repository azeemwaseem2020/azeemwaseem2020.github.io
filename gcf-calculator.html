Greatest Common Factor Calculator

A clear, focused calculator with validation and a short explanation of the method.

Formula/method: Euclid's algorithm repeatedly replaces (a, b) with (b, a mod b) until the remainder is zero.

Worked example

For 48 and 18: 48 mod 18 = 12, 18 mod 12 = 6, and 12 mod 6 = 0, so the GCF is 6.

Greatest common factor explained

The greatest common factor, or GCF, is the largest positive integer that divides each of the given whole numbers without a remainder.

Worked example

For 18 and 24, common factors include 1, 2, 3 and 6. The greatest is 6, so the GCF is 6.

Why GCF matters

GCF is useful for simplifying fractions, factoring algebraic expressions and solving problems where quantities need to be divided into equal groups without leftovers.

GCF FAQ

Is GCF the same as greatest common divisor?

Yes. GCF and greatest common divisor (GCD) are commonly used names for the same concept.
