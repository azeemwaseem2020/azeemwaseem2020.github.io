Credit Card Payoff Calculator

Estimate payoff time, total payments and interest from a balance, APR and fixed monthly payment.

How it works

The calculator applies monthly interest from the APR, then subtracts the fixed payment. If the payment does not exceed the first month's interest, the balance will not be paid off.

Important note

This is a mathematical estimate. Credit cards may use daily interest, fees, changing rates and different payment rules, so compare the result with your card issuer's statement.

Before you use the result

The result depends on the inputs, units and assumptions shown on this page. Check those details before comparing the result with another source or applying it to a real-world decision.

How credit-card payoff estimates work

A payoff calculator estimates how long a balance may take to repay from the balance, interest rate, payment amount and other inputs. Credit-card terms can include changing rates, minimum-payment rules, fees and new purchases, so an estimate is not a lender statement.

Why minimum payments can extend repayment

When interest and fees consume a large share of a payment, only a smaller portion reduces principal. A higher fixed payment generally reduces the balance faster under otherwise unchanged assumptions.

Worked example

If a balance is $5,000 and the assumed monthly payment is $250, the calculator applies the stated interest assumptions period by period. The exact payoff date depends on the rate, payment timing, fees and whether new charges are added.

Credit-card payoff FAQ

Should I keep using the card during the payoff period?

New purchases can change the balance and invalidate a simple payoff estimate.

Why does the lender's result differ?

Daily interest, statement dates, minimum-payment rules, fees and rate changes can produce different results.
