Time and a half: overtime pay guide

“Time and a half” means an overtime rate equal to 1.5 times the regular hourly rate. This calculator estimates regular pay, overtime pay and combined gross pay from the hourly wage and hours entered. It does not determine whether a particular worker is legally entitled to overtime or which rate applies under local law or an employment contract.

Formulas

Overtime rate = regular hourly rate × 1.5.
Regular pay = hourly rate × regular hours.
Overtime pay = overtime rate × overtime hours.
Total estimated gross pay = regular pay + overtime pay.

Worked example

At a rate of 20 currency units per hour, with 40 regular hours and 5 overtime hours, regular pay is 20 × 40 = 800. The time-and-a-half rate is 20 × 1.5 = 30 per hour, so overtime pay is 30 × 5 = 150. Estimated gross pay is 950 before tax, deductions or other adjustments.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the base hourly rate before overtime.
  2. Enter regular hours and overtime hours separately.
  3. Select Calculate Overtime.
  4. Review the regular pay, overtime rate, overtime pay and total.

Important payroll considerations

This simple model assumes all entered overtime hours receive exactly 1.5 times the entered base rate. Actual payroll may treat hours, shifts, holidays, bonuses, breaks and rounding differently. Overtime rules vary by country, region, worker classification and contract; some systems use a different threshold or multiplier. The result is gross pay, not take-home pay, and excludes income tax, social contributions, benefits, unpaid breaks and other deductions. Check official local guidance or payroll documentation before relying on an estimate for a pay dispute or compliance decision.

Frequently asked questions

What does 1.5 times pay mean?

It means each eligible overtime hour is paid at 150% of the base hourly rate. For a base rate of 20, the time-and-a-half rate is 30 per hour.

Does this calculate take-home pay?

No. It calculates estimated gross earnings from the supplied hours and rate. Taxes and other payroll deductions are not included.

Does every extra hour qualify for time and a half?

Not necessarily. Eligibility and overtime thresholds depend on applicable law and employment terms. Confirm the rules that apply to your situation.
