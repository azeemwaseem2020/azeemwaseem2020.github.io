Roof pitch calculation

Roof pitch describes the slope of a roof. A common representation is rise over run, such as 6:12, meaning 6 units of vertical rise for every 12 units of horizontal run. The slope angle can be found from angle = arctan(rise ÷ run).

Worked example

A roof that rises 4 ft over a 12 ft horizontal run has pitch 4:12. Its angle is arctan(4/12) ≈ 18.43°. Keep rise and run in the same units.

Practical example

Roofing, solar-panel planning and drainage design may use different conventions. In Pakistan, confirm the local drawing or contractor's definition before ordering materials. The calculator does not determine structural safety or required load capacity.

Roof Pitch: Formula and Practical Meaning

Roof pitch describes how much a roof rises for a given horizontal run. A common expression is rise/run, often written as a ratio such as 4:12, meaning the roof rises 4 inches for every 12 inches of horizontal run. The roof angle can be found from θ = arctan(rise/run).

For example, a roof with 6 inches of rise over 12 inches of run has a pitch of 6:12. The angle is arctan(0.5), about 26.57°. If the run is 5 m and the rise is 2 m, the slope is 0.4 and the angle is about 21.80°. The actual roof geometry should be measured rather than estimated from appearance.

In Pakistan, roof design must also account for local rainfall, drainage, materials, structural requirements and the building's architectural design. A steeper pitch may shed water differently, but pitch alone does not determine whether a roof is structurally suitable.

Why pitch matters

Pitch can affect roofing material quantities because the sloped surface is longer than its horizontal projection. The slope length for a right triangle is √(rise² + run²). Multiply the slope length by the roof width to estimate one roof plane, then account for overlaps and waste.

FAQs

What does 4:12 mean? Four units of rise for every twelve units of horizontal run.

Can pitch be expressed as an angle? Yes. The calculator can relate rise/run to the corresponding angle.

Is pitch the same as roof area? No. Pitch describes slope; roof area depends on all roof dimensions.
