Roof pitch calculator: complete guide

Roof pitch describes how much a roof rises vertically for a given horizontal run. This calculator returns pitch as a percentage and the corresponding angle in degrees. Roof pitch is important for communicating roof steepness, drainage design and planning materials, but the result alone does not confirm that a roof design meets local building requirements.

Formulas

Pitch (%) = (rise ÷ run) × 100
Angle = arctan(rise ÷ run) × 180 ÷ π

Rise and run must use the same unit. The run is the horizontal distance, not the sloped length of the roof. The angle is measured from the horizontal.

How to use the calculator

  1. Measure the vertical rise over a known horizontal run.
  2. Enter rise and run in matching units.
  3. Select Calculate to view pitch percentage and angle.
  4. Check that the measurements describe the same roof section and are not taken along the slope.

Worked example

If a roof rises 4 units over a horizontal run of 12 units, pitch = 4 ÷ 12 × 100 = 33.33%. The angle is arctan(4/12) ≈ 18.43°. In traditional roof notation, a rise of 4 inches over 12 inches is often written as 4:12; it is not the same as a 33.33-degree angle.

Common mistakes and safety

Do not confuse percentage pitch, angle in degrees and rise-to-run ratio. For example, 100% pitch means rise equals run and the angle is 45°, not 100°. A sloped roof length differs from the horizontal run. Measurements taken on a roof can be hazardous; use safe ground-based plans or qualified professionals rather than climbing without appropriate equipment. Actual roof design also depends on span, structural loads, drainage, roofing material and local code. This calculator provides geometry only and is not a substitute for professional design or inspection.

Frequently asked questions

What does a 6:12 roof pitch mean?

It means the roof rises 6 inches for every 12 inches of horizontal run. The rise-to-run ratio is 0.5, equivalent to a 50% slope and an angle of about 26.6 degrees. The ratio, percentage and angle are different ways of describing the same slope.

Should I measure the roof surface or horizontal run?

Pitch is based on vertical rise divided by horizontal run, not the sloping rafter length. If you measure along the roof surface instead, convert the measurement or use a suitable geometry method before calculating pitch.

Can this calculator tell me whether my roof is safe?

No. Pitch alone does not establish structural capacity, drainage performance, weather resistance or code compliance. Roof design also depends on span, materials, loads, connections and local requirements. Use a qualified professional for construction decisions and follow fall-protection practices when measuring.
