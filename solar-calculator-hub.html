Planning a solar system

Most solar jobs start with the same basic questions: how much power is being used, how much panel capacity is needed, how much backup is required, and what inverter can handle the load. These calculators break those jobs into smaller steps so you can check each number before moving on.

A simple way to plan: start with electricity use or appliance load → estimate PV capacity → check panel count and roof area → size backup storage → check inverter capacity → review site and equipment constraints.

The complete solar workflow

  1. Load: list essential appliances, watts and expected daily hours.
  2. Energy: estimate daily kWh rather than relying only on peak wattage.
  3. PV: use peak sun hours and system efficiency to estimate panel capacity.
  4. Storage: choose required backup hours and usable battery capacity.
  5. Inverter: check continuous load plus starting/surge demand.
  6. Reality check: allow for shading, heat, dust, wiring, inverter losses and seasonal solar variation.
  7. Final design: confirm equipment specifications and installation requirements with a qualified installer.

Key formulas

Daily energy ≈ load in kW × operating hours. Estimated PV capacity ≈ daily kWh ÷ (peak sun hours × system efficiency). Battery planning starts with required load energy and then accounts for usable depth of discharge and efficiency. Final equipment sizing should follow manufacturer specifications.

Pakistan-specific considerations

Solar output varies with season, weather, shading, panel orientation, temperature and cleanliness. Battery performance also depends on chemistry, temperature, age and discharge limits. A calculator gives a planning estimate; it cannot inspect the actual roof, wiring, grid conditions or appliance surge behavior.

Common mistakes

  • Using appliance nameplate watts without considering actual runtime.
  • Ignoring motor/compressor starting current.
  • Assuming peak sun hours are constant every day.
  • Using 100% battery capacity as routinely usable.
  • Choosing an inverter only from the average load.
  • Ignoring future loads and seasonal demand.

What Calcora does not provide

These calculators do not guarantee energy production, backup duration or financial savings. They do not replace an electrical inspection, structural assessment or professional system design.
