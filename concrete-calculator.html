Concrete Calculator

Estimate concrete volume for a rectangular slab, footing or pad in cubic feet and cubic yards.

How to use the concrete calculator

  1. Enter length and width in feet.
  2. Enter slab thickness in inches.
  3. Choose a waste allowance when estimating an order.
  4. Review both cubic feet and cubic yards.

Formula

Volume in cubic feet = length × width × thickness in feet. Cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27. The waste allowance is applied after the base volume.

Worked example

For 20 ft × 10 ft × 4 in: 20 × 10 × (4 ÷ 12) = 66.67 ft³, or about 2.47 yd³ before waste.

Important planning note

This estimates volume only. Mix design, reinforcement, site conditions and ordering practices can change the amount required. Confirm quantities with a qualified contractor or supplier.

How to estimate a concrete pour

Choose the shape that matches the project, then measure the finished dimensions rather than relying only on nominal plans. For a slab, record length, width and thickness; for a footing or column, use the dimensions of the actual section. Keep all measurements in one unit system before calculating volume.

Volume and ordering allowance

Rectangular volume = length × width × thickness. The result is the geometric volume only. If you choose an allowance, calculate it from the base volume: a 5% allowance means multiplying by 1.05, not adding five cubic units. The appropriate allowance depends on formwork, subgrade, spillage, access and supplier minimum order sizes.

Check dimensions before ordering

Convert inches to feet by dividing by 12, and convert centimetres to metres by dividing by 100 before multiplying dimensions. Confirm whether the displayed result is in cubic feet, cubic metres or cubic yards. One cubic yard equals 27 cubic feet; one cubic metre is approximately 1.308 cubic yards.

Important project limits

This estimator calculates volume; it does not design slab thickness, reinforcement, foundations or a concrete mix. Structural requirements, soil conditions, loading, local building rules and the specified concrete strength must be assessed separately.

Worked example: slab volume and a 5% allowance

Suppose a slab is 10 ft long, 8 ft wide and 6 inches thick. Convert the thickness to feet: 6 ÷ 12 = 0.5 ft. The base volume is 10 × 8 × 0.5 = 40 cubic feet. In cubic yards, 40 ÷ 27 ≈ 1.48 yd³. With an illustrative 5% allowance, the estimate becomes 40 × 1.05 = 42 cubic feet, or about 1.56 yd³. The percentage is an example, not a universal recommendation; the site and supplier should determine the suitable allowance.

Why measured volume and delivered volume can differ

Excavations may be uneven, subgrades may not be perfectly level, and some concrete can remain in the chute or handling equipment. Conversely, ordering far more than needed can create disposal costs and placement problems. Recheck measurements, use the same units throughout, and confirm how the supplier rounds orders and whether delivery minimums apply.

Slabs, footings and columns

For a rectangular footing, use length × width × depth. For a round column, the geometric volume is π × radius² × height. For a slab with several separate sections, calculate each section and add the volumes; avoid counting overlapping areas twice. Complex shapes, sloped surfaces and steps may need to be split into simpler geometric sections.

Concrete mix and strength are separate

Volume does not tell you the cement, sand, aggregate or water quantities, nor does it specify the required compressive strength. Follow the approved mix design and supplier specification. Adding water at the site can change workability and strength, so do not use this volume estimate as a mix-design instruction.

Concrete calculator FAQ

Should I always add 5% extra?

No. Five percent is only an example used to demonstrate the arithmetic. The right allowance depends on project geometry, ground conditions, placement method and supplier advice.

Can this calculate reinforcement?

No. Rebar size, spacing, cover and structural adequacy require a design appropriate to the load, soil and applicable building code.

Does the result guarantee the exact order quantity?

No. It is an estimate based on the dimensions and options entered. Verify field measurements and confirm ordering units with the ready-mix supplier before placing an order.

Calculation context

Concrete quantity estimates connect dimensions and shape with volume and an optional waste allowance. Material requirements should be checked against the actual design and site measurements.
