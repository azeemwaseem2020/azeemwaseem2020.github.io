Concrete Calculator

Estimate concrete volume for a rectangular slab, footing or pad in cubic feet and cubic yards.

How to use the concrete calculator

  1. Enter length and width in feet.
  2. Enter slab thickness in inches.
  3. Choose a waste allowance when estimating an order.
  4. Review both cubic feet and cubic yards.

Formula

Volume in cubic feet = length × width × thickness in feet. Cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27. The waste allowance is applied after the base volume.

Worked example

For 20 ft × 10 ft × 4 in: 20 × 10 × (4 ÷ 12) = 66.67 ft³, or about 2.47 yd³ before waste.

Important planning note

This estimates volume only. Mix design, reinforcement, site conditions and ordering practices can change the amount required. Confirm quantities with a qualified contractor or supplier.

Quick guide: how to use this calculator

Select the shape of the pour, enter its dimensions and include the waste factor if the calculator provides one.

Formula or method

Concrete quantity is based on the geometric volume of the selected shape, with any stated waste allowance added afterward.

Useful note

Measure dimensions carefully and keep feet/metres or other units consistent before ordering material.

Before you use the result

The result depends on the inputs, units and assumptions shown on this page. Check those details before using the result for a real-world decision or purchase.

Estimating concrete volume

For a simple rectangular slab, concrete volume is length × width × thickness. Convert all dimensions to the same unit before multiplying, then convert the result to the unit used for ordering material.

Worked example

A slab 10 ft long, 8 ft wide and 0.5 ft thick has a volume of 40 cubic feet. Actual material ordering usually includes an appropriate allowance for waste, uneven ground and placement conditions.

Concrete mix is a separate question

Volume tells you how much concrete is needed, but it does not determine cement, aggregate and water proportions. Mix design depends on strength, materials, exposure and applicable construction requirements.

Concrete calculator FAQ

Should I add extra concrete?

Usually an ordering allowance is considered, but the appropriate amount depends on site conditions and project tolerances.

Can this calculate reinforcement?

No. Rebar sizing and placement require structural design and are separate from volume estimation.

Calculation context

Concrete quantity estimates connect dimensions and shape with volume and an optional waste allowance. Material requirements should be checked against the actual design and site measurements.
