Frequency Calculator

Calculate frequency or period from cycles and time.

Formula

Frequency: f = cycles ÷ time; period T = 1/f.

Worked example

If 20 cycles occur in 4 seconds, frequency is 20 ÷ 4 = 5 Hz. The period is the time per cycle, so T = 1 ÷ 5 = 0.2 seconds.

Assumptions and units

Use consistent SI units unless the calculator explicitly says otherwise. Real physical systems can require additional effects beyond these introductory equations.

Understanding frequency

Frequency describes how many complete cycles or events occur per unit time. The SI unit is hertz, where one hertz represents one cycle per second.

Worked example: timing 20 cycles

If 20 complete cycles occur in 5 seconds, the frequency is 4 Hz. The corresponding period is 0.25 seconds per cycle.

Frequency and period

For a repeating process, frequency and period are reciprocals. A higher frequency means a shorter time for each cycle, assuming the same definition of one cycle.

Frequency FAQ

Can frequency be zero?

Yes. A frequency of zero represents no repeating cycles or events during the measured time under the chosen definition.

Measure a complete time interval

Count complete cycles over a clearly defined interval. Frequency is cycles divided by elapsed time, so counting errors or an inaccurate timer directly affect the result. For very slow processes, measure several cycles rather than timing only one when practical; dividing the total time by the number of cycles can reduce the effect of reaction-time error.

Hertz and period units

Frequency is measured in hertz (Hz), equivalent to cycles per second. A frequency of 5 Hz means five complete cycles each second, with a period of 0.2 seconds per cycle. If time is recorded in milliseconds, convert it to seconds before using this calculator, or convert the final units carefully.

Know when this model applies

This simple relationship is appropriate for a repeating process with a meaningful cycle count. Noisy, irregular or changing signals may need an average frequency or a more specialised measurement method. Frequency alone does not determine wave speed; wave speed also depends on wavelength and the medium or system involved.

Frequency formula and how to interpret the result

Frequency is the number of complete cycles divided by the elapsed time: f = N/t. If the process repeats regularly, period is the time for one cycle and T = 1/f. Frequency is measured in hertz (Hz), where 1 Hz equals one cycle per second; period is measured in seconds.

Worked example with a longer measurement interval

If 75 complete cycles occur in 15 seconds, f = 75/15 = 5 Hz and T = 1/5 = 0.2 s. Measuring many cycles and dividing by the total time can reduce the relative effect of start/stop timing error compared with timing a single cycle.

Frequency, wavelength and wave speed

Frequency alone does not determine wave speed. For a travelling wave, speed, frequency and wavelength are related by v = fλ. If frequency is 5 Hz and wavelength is 2 m, wave speed is 10 m/s under that model. The medium and wave type can affect speed, so do not infer wavelength or speed without the required information.

Measurement limitations and FAQs

This calculator assumes the counted events are complete cycles over a clearly defined interval. Irregular pulses, changing frequency, missed cycles or a noisy signal may require repeated measurements or specialised analysis. Convert milliseconds to seconds before using the formula if the input expects seconds.

Can frequency be greater than one?

Yes. A frequency of 50 Hz means 50 cycles each second.

What happens if elapsed time is zero?

Frequency cannot be calculated by dividing by zero. A meaningful measurement needs a positive elapsed time.

Is frequency the same as loudness?

No. Frequency describes how often a periodic event repeats; loudness is a perception related to sound intensity and hearing conditions.
