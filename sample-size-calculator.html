Sample-size planning

Sample size depends on the study design and assumptions. For a proportion estimate, a common approximation uses the expected proportion, confidence level and margin of error. For means, variability and the desired precision are also important. A calculator gives a mathematical planning result, not a guarantee of study quality.

Worked example

For a large population with an assumed proportion of 50%, 95% confidence and a 5 percentage-point margin of error, the familiar simple-random-sample approximation is about 385 observations before finite-population correction, nonresponse or design effects.

Research practice

Surveys in Pakistan may need adjustments for cluster sampling, stratification, nonresponse and the actual target population. If a study compares groups, sample size should be planned around the primary comparison rather than only an overall proportion.

FAQs

Does larger confidence require more observations? Usually yes. Does a smaller margin of error require more? Yes. Does sample size remove bias? No; a large biased sample can still be biased.
