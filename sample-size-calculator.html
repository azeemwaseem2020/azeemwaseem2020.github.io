Understanding sample size

Sample size is the number of observations included in a study or survey. A larger sample can improve precision, but the required size depends on the study design, confidence level, acceptable margin of error, population assumptions and expected variability.

Margin of error and confidence

A smaller margin of error generally requires more observations. A higher confidence level also increases the required sample size when other assumptions stay the same.

Practical checks

Before using a calculated sample size, confirm the target population, sampling method and expected response rate. If non-response is likely, the number invited may need to be higher than the number of completed responses required.

Sample-size FAQ

Does a larger sample remove bias?

No. A large but poorly selected sample can still be biased. Sampling quality matters as much as numerical size.
