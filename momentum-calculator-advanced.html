Momentum and conservation

Linear momentum is p = mv, where m is mass and v is velocity. Momentum is a vector, so direction matters. In an isolated system, total momentum is conserved when the net external impulse is negligible.

Worked example

A 1,000 kg vehicle moving at 20 m/s has momentum 20,000 kg·m/s in its direction of travel. If the same vehicle reverses direction at 20 m/s, its momentum has the same magnitude but the opposite sign in a one-dimensional coordinate system.

Practical use

Momentum analysis is useful in collisions, recoil, vehicle safety and physics experiments. A collision can conserve total momentum even when kinetic energy is not conserved, such as in an inelastic collision.

FAQs

Is momentum scalar? No, it has direction. Can momentum be negative? Yes, relative to a chosen coordinate direction. Is momentum the same as kinetic energy? No; they are different quantities with different units and relationships.
