Quadratic formula

For ax² + bx + c = 0, the roots are x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) ÷ 2a. The discriminant b² − 4ac tells you whether the equation has two real roots, one repeated real root, or complex roots.

Check the coefficients

The coefficient a must not be zero. Pay particular attention to negative signs when entering b and c, because a sign error changes the equation and therefore its roots.

Common questions

What does the discriminant tell me?

A positive discriminant gives two distinct real roots, zero gives a repeated real root, and a negative discriminant gives a complex conjugate pair.

Why might the displayed decimals be rounded?

Roots are displayed to practical precision. Use the original coefficients and the stated formula when exact symbolic form is required.

Quadratic Roots Calculator: Solving and Checking Solutions

The roots of a quadratic are values of x that make ax² + bx + c equal zero. They are also called zeros or solutions. Depending on the coefficients, a quadratic can have two different real roots, one repeated real root, or two complex roots. Understanding the case helps you interpret the output rather than merely copying a number.

Formula and calculation method

For a ≠ 0, x = (−b ± √Δ)/(2a), where Δ = b² − 4ac. If Δ > 0 there are two distinct real roots; if Δ = 0 there is one repeated real root; if Δ < 0 there are two complex roots and no real roots. The sum of roots is −b/a and their product is c/a.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the equation in standard form ax² + bx + c = 0.
  2. Make sure a is not zero and preserve each coefficient's sign.
  3. Calculate and note whether roots are real, repeated or complex.
  4. Check by substitution or compare the sum and product with −b/a and c/a.

Worked example

For x² − 7x + 12 = 0, factor it as (x − 3)(x − 4) = 0, so roots are 3 and 4; their sum is 7 and product is 12. For x² + 2x + 1 = 0, the repeated root is −1. For x² + 4 = 0, roots are 2i and −2i, so there are no real x-intercepts.

Interpretation, assumptions and common mistakes

Roots need not be positive. Complex roots can exist even when a graph does not cross the real x-axis. Do not confuse a root with the parabola's vertex. If the discriminant is zero, the two formula branches give the same value. Rounding may leave a small residual when substituting the displayed answer. If a = 0, the equation is not quadratic.

Frequently asked questions

Are roots the same as x-intercepts?

Real roots correspond to x-intercepts; complex roots are not real-axis crossings.

Can both roots be negative?

Yes. Their signs depend on the coefficients and discriminant.

How do I verify roots?

Substitute each into the original equation or check their sum and product.
