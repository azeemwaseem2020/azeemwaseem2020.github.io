Loan EMI Calculator Guide: How to Calculate Your Monthly Loan Payment

If you are comparing loans, the monthly EMI is useful—but it should never be the only number you look at.

Quick answer: loan EMI depends on the principal, interest rate and loan tenure. Compare the EMI, total interest and total repayment together before choosing a loan.

What is a loan EMI?

EMI means Equated Monthly Instalment. It is the regular amount paid toward a loan, normally combining part of the principal with interest or markup. The exact payment depends on the lender's calculation method and the terms of the loan.

How is EMI calculated?

For a standard reducing-balance loan, the common formula is:

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

Here, P is the loan principal, r is the monthly interest rate, and n is the number of monthly payments. A calculator makes it easier to compare different rates and tenures.

Worked example

Suppose you borrow 1,000,000 at an annual rate of 12% for 5 years, using a monthly reducing-balance calculation. The monthly rate is 12% ÷ 12 = 1% and the number of payments is 60. The resulting EMI is about 22,244 per month. Across 60 payments, the total scheduled repayment is about 1,334,630, so the calculated interest is about 334,630. Actual lender figures can differ because of fees, rounding and the lender's specific method.

Why loan tenure changes the total cost

A longer loan term usually lowers the monthly payment but gives interest more time to accumulate. A shorter tenure normally means a higher EMI but fewer months of interest. That is why two loans with similar monthly payments can have very different total costs.

Three numbers to compare

Monthly EMI

Useful for checking whether the payment fits your monthly budget.

Total interest

Shows how much interest or markup is added over the scheduled repayment period.

Total repayment

Principal plus calculated interest across the full loan tenure.

What happens when you change the rate or tenure?

Try the same principal with two different rates, then compare a shorter and longer tenure. A lower rate generally reduces the calculated EMI and total interest. Extending the tenure can reduce the monthly payment while increasing the number of months over which interest is charged. Looking at both scenarios is more useful than choosing a loan from the EMI alone.

What an EMI calculator may leave out

A basic EMI estimate may not include processing fees, insurance, taxes, late charges, variable-rate changes, early settlement costs or other lender-specific charges. Check the lender's actual repayment schedule before signing an agreement.

What changes the EMI in real life?

The headline EMI is only one part of a loan. Processing charges, insurance, variable rates, late-payment charges and early-settlement rules can change the actual cost. When comparing offers, calculate the scheduled repayment separately from fees and other charges rather than hiding everything inside one percentage.

Frequently asked questions

Are calculator results a professional recommendation?

No. The calculator applies the inputs and calculation method you provide. Field, financial or equipment decisions should be checked against current official information and the relevant professional guidance.

Why do units and assumptions matter?

A mathematically correct formula can still produce a misleading result when units, rate conventions, product analysis, efficiency or other inputs are inconsistent.

What should I verify before using the result?

Verify the input values, units, formula assumptions and any fees, product specifications or local conditions that can materially change the result.
