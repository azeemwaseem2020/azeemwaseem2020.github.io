Hydroponic Nutrient Calculator for Multi-Fertilizer PPM

Use this hydroponic nutrient calculator to estimate how much nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), potassium (K), calcium (Ca), magnesium (Mg) and sulfur (S) your entered fertilizer products contribute to a finished hydroponic solution. Enter the actual guaranteed analysis from each label and the grams you plan to use. The checker totals estimated ppm and compares it with targets you supply—it does not invent a crop recipe.

Prepared by Abdul Qadir · Reviewed 10 October 2026 · Calculation estimates depend on accurate label and water data.

Important: This is a transparent arithmetic and cross-check tool, not an agronomic prescription. Use crop- and growth-stage-specific targets from a reliable source. It cannot account for every chemical form, solubility, precipitation, chelation or water interaction. Confirm the final mix with appropriate water analysis and calibrated pH/EC measurements.

Worked example: label percentage to nutrient ppm

Suppose a product label reports 15.5% nitrogen (N), and you add 50 g to a final reservoir volume of 100 L. The estimated nitrogen contribution is 50 × 15.5 × 10 ÷ 100 = 77.5 mg/L, approximately 77.5 ppm. If the source water already contains 20 ppm nitrogen, the estimated total becomes 97.5 ppm. This is an arithmetic example, not a recommended crop target or feeding recipe.

For oxide-based labels, the checker converts P₂O₅ to elemental phosphorus using 0.4364 and K₂O to elemental potassium using 0.8301 when those bases are selected. Confirm that each percentage and basis matches the actual guaranteed analysis on the product label before relying on the estimate.

How to use this hydroponic nutrient calculator

  1. Enter the final diluted reservoir volume in litres.
  2. For each product used, enter its name, dose in grams and nutrient percentages exactly as shown on the label.
  3. Choose the correct P and K label basis for each product; do not treat P₂O₅ as elemental P or K₂O as elemental K.
  4. Enter measured source-water nutrient values only when available, then add target values from a crop- and growth-stage-specific reference.
  5. Calculate and review both total ppm and each product's contribution. Check compatibility, pH and EC before preparing a solution.

How the estimate works

For each product and nutrient, the calculator multiplies the entered product dose by the nutrient percentage and converts the resulting nutrient mass into milligrams per litre. For dilute water solutions, ppm is commonly approximated as mg/L. It adds the source-water contribution you entered and sums the products. P₂O₅ is converted to elemental P using approximately 0.4364; K₂O is converted to elemental K using approximately 0.8301.

Formula for one nutrient contribution: product dose (g) × label nutrient (%) × 10 ÷ finished solution volume (L), with the appropriate oxide conversion factor where selected.

Read the result responsibly

  • A positive difference from your target means the estimated total is above the target you entered; it is a review flag, not proof of toxicity.
  • A negative difference means the entered products and water are below your entered target; it is not an instruction to add more fertilizer.
  • Undisclosed nutrients, impurities, nutrient availability, alkalinity, pH, precipitation and interactions are not inferred from the label.
  • EC reflects the combined conductivity of ions and cannot identify each nutrient's individual concentration.
  • Never mix concentrated calcium products directly with concentrated phosphate or sulfate products unless the product instructions explicitly allow it. Follow product labels and crop-specific technical advice.

Frequently asked questions

Does this tool generate a complete A/B nutrient recipe automatically?

No. You enter the product doses and analyses; it estimates their nutrient contributions and compares them with your own targets. It does not solve or certify a recipe.

Can I enter NPK values directly?

Enter each nutrient's guaranteed label percentage into its matching field. In many fertilizer labels, the middle number is P₂O₅ and the third is K₂O, not elemental P and K. Choose the corresponding basis above.

Can EC replace a nutrient analysis?

No. EC can help monitor a solution but cannot tell you the concentration of N, P, K or another nutrient individually. Use a suitable water test and an evidence-based nutrient plan.

Which target ppm should I use?

There is no universal target for every crop, growth stage, water source and hydroponic system. Use a current, crop-specific recommendation or qualified agronomic guidance; this calculator intentionally does not supply generic targets.
