Rounding Calculator

Round a number to a chosen number of decimal places for cleaner calculations, reports, measurements, and everyday math.

How rounding works

Choose the place you want to keep, then look at the next digit. In the common decimal rounding rule, a next digit from 5 through 9 increases the kept digit by one; a next digit from 0 through 4 leaves it unchanged.

Rounding example

To round 12.34567 to two decimal places, keep 12.34 and inspect the third decimal digit, 5. The rounded result is 12.35.

Why round numbers?

Rounding makes long decimal results easier to read and communicate. In scientific, financial, construction, and measurement work, keep enough precision for the purpose and avoid rounding intermediate values too early when accuracy matters.

FAQ

How many decimal places can I select? This calculator accepts 0 through 15 decimal places.

Does rounding change the original value? No. Rounding creates an approximation for display or reporting; the unrounded value can retain more precision.

How numerical rounding works

Rounding replaces a number with a nearby value at a chosen precision. In ordinary decimal rounding, inspect the first digit that will be removed: a digit below 5 leaves the retained digit unchanged, while 5 or greater normally increases it by one.

Worked example

Round 18.746 to two decimal places. Keep 18.74 and inspect the third decimal digit, 6. Because it is at least 5, the result is 18.75.

A practical Pakistan example

A shop may display a calculated price as Rs. 1,249.50 even when an internal calculation produces more decimal places. Display precision should match the business or reporting rule rather than create false accuracy.

Common mistakes

Repeatedly rounding intermediate values can introduce avoidable error. When precision matters, retain extra digits internally and round the final reported result.

FAQ

What does rounding to two decimals mean? Keep two digits after the decimal point and use the next digit to decide whether to increase the second digit.

Is 5 always rounded up? Many common conventions do, but some software and statistical workflows use other tie-breaking rules.

Should every intermediate value be rounded? Usually not when precision matters.
