Formula

Slope = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁). If both x-values are equal, the line is vertical and its slope is undefined.

Slope from two points

The slope of a line through two points is m = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁). It describes the change in y for each unit of change in x. A positive slope rises from left to right; a negative slope falls.

Worked example

For points (2, 3) and (6, 11), the slope is (11 − 3) ÷ (6 − 2) = 8 ÷ 4 = 2. That means y increases by 2 units for each one-unit increase in x.

Special cases

If x₂ − x₁ = 0, the line is vertical and the slope is undefined. A horizontal line has slope 0. Keep coordinate units consistent if the points represent physical measurements.

FAQs

Does point order matter? No, if both differences use the same order. Can slope be a fraction? Yes. What does a steep slope mean? A larger absolute slope means a greater change in y per unit x.
