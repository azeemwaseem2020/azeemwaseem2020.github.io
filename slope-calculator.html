Slope from two points

The slope of a line through two points is m = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁). It describes the change in y for each unit of change in x. A positive slope rises from left to right; a negative slope falls.

Worked example

For points (2, 3) and (6, 11), the slope is (11 − 3) ÷ (6 − 2) = 8 ÷ 4 = 2. That means y increases by 2 units for each one-unit increase in x.

Special cases

If x₂ − x₁ = 0, the line is vertical and the slope is undefined. A horizontal line has slope 0. Keep coordinate units consistent if the points represent physical measurements.

FAQs

Does point order matter? No, if both differences use the same order. Can slope be a fraction? Yes. What does a steep slope mean? A larger absolute slope means a greater change in y per unit x.

Slope Between Two Points

The slope of a line through two points is m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁). It measures vertical change per unit horizontal change. If the result is positive, y increases as x increases; if negative, y decreases. A zero slope represents a horizontal line.

For points (3, 4) and (7, 12), the slope is (12 − 4)/(7 − 3) = 8/4 = 2. That means y increases by 2 units for each 1 unit increase in x. If the x-values are equal, the line is vertical and the slope is undefined because division by zero is not allowed.

This concept also appears in practical rates. If a road rises 2 metres over a horizontal run of 50 metres, the slope is 2/50 = 0.04, or 4%. In engineering and construction, slope percentage, ratio and angle are related but are not interchangeable without conversion.

Checking the result

Always subtract coordinates in the same order in numerator and denominator. Reversing both points gives the same slope; reversing only one difference changes the sign incorrectly. Keep units consistent when interpreting a physical slope.

FAQs

What is a negative slope? It means y falls as x increases.

What is a vertical slope? It is undefined because the horizontal change is zero.

Can slope be a percentage? Yes. Multiply the decimal slope by 100 to express it as a percent.
