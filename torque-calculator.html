Calculate torque from force, perpendicular lever-arm distance and angle between the lever arm and force.

Torque calculation context

Torque measures the turning effect of a force and depends on force, lever-arm distance and the angle between them. It belongs to rotational mechanics rather than ordinary linear-force calculation.

Torque and rotational effect

Torque measures the rotational effect of a force about a chosen axis. The perpendicular component of the force and its lever arm determine the torque magnitude in the basic model.

Worked example

A 40 N force applied 0.25 m from an axis at 90° produces 10 N·m of torque. If the force is applied at another angle, only the perpendicular component contributes to the simple torque magnitude.

Sign and direction

In rotational problems, clockwise and counterclockwise directions should be assigned consistently. Net torque is the signed sum of torques about the selected axis.

Using the lever arm correctly

Torque magnitude is τ = rF sin θ, where r is the distance from the chosen axis to the point where force is applied, F is force in newtons, and θ is the angle between the lever-arm vector and force. The result is measured in newton-metres (N·m). Although that unit resembles joules dimensionally, torque is not energy.

Worked example at an angle

A 60 N force is applied 0.4 m from a pivot at 30° to the lever arm. The torque magnitude is 0.4 × 60 × sin(30°) = 12 N·m. A force directed along the lever arm produces zero torque because its perpendicular component is zero.

Choose the axis before calculating

Torque depends on the axis or pivot selected. For a system with multiple forces, calculate each torque about the same axis and apply a consistent sign convention before finding the net torque. For real fasteners or machinery, follow manufacturer torque specifications and use a suitable calibrated tool.

Torque FAQ

Does a larger handle always increase torque?

For the same force and angle, a longer lever arm increases torque in direct proportion to its length.

Why does the angle matter?

Only the component of force perpendicular to the lever arm contributes to torque magnitude, represented by the sine of the angle.

Is torque the same as work?

No. Torque describes rotational effect about an axis; work describes energy transferred when a force acts through displacement.
