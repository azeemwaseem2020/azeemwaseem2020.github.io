Return on ad spend

ROAS measures revenue attributed to advertising relative to advertising cost. The basic formula is ROAS = attributed revenue ÷ advertising spend. A result of 4 means PKR 4 of attributed revenue for every PKR 1 of ad spend; it does not mean PKR 4 of profit.

Worked example

If a campaign costs PKR 50,000 and generates PKR 250,000 of attributed sales, ROAS is 250,000 ÷ 50,000 = 5.0, or 500% when expressed as a percentage.

Business context

For an online business in Pakistan, include the same attribution window and revenue definition when comparing campaigns. Gross revenue may need adjustment for refunds, discounts, taxes and product costs before profitability can be assessed.

FAQs

Is ROAS profit? No. Can ROAS be below 1? Yes. Should campaigns be compared using the same attribution method? Yes, otherwise the comparison can be misleading.

ROAS: Return on Advertising Spend

Return on advertising spend, or ROAS, measures revenue generated relative to advertising cost. The formula is ROAS = Revenue Attributed to Ads ÷ Advertising Spend. If Rs 200,000 of attributed sales came from Rs 40,000 of advertising, ROAS is 5.0, meaning Rs 5 of attributed revenue for each Rs 1 spent on ads.

ROAS is not the same as profit. If a business has a 30% gross margin, Rs 200,000 of revenue contributes about Rs 60,000 before other costs, while the advertising spend is Rs 40,000. Shipping, returns, salaries, platform fees and taxes can further change the actual result.

For a Pakistan e-commerce example, a campaign may spend Rs 25,000 and generate Rs 100,000 in tracked sales. ROAS is 4.0. Before comparing campaigns, make sure the attribution window, sales definition and refund treatment are consistent. A campaign with a lower ROAS can still be useful if it brings higher-margin products or valuable repeat customers.

Attribution matters

ROAS is only as reliable as the revenue attribution method. Platform-reported sales can differ from analytics or accounting records. Compare like-for-like periods and account for cancellations and refunds where possible.

FAQs

What is a good ROAS? There is no universal number; required ROAS depends on margins and operating costs.

Is 4 ROAS equal to 400%? A ROAS of 4 corresponds to 400% when expressed as a ratio percentage, but the ratio itself is usually reported as 4×.

Does ROAS measure profit? No. It measures attributed revenue relative to ad spend.
