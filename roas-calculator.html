ROAS calculator: complete guide

Return on ad spend (ROAS) compares revenue attributed to advertising with the amount spent on those ads. Marketers use it to compare campaigns, channels and time periods. ROAS is a revenue-to-cost ratio, not the same as profit or return on investment; product costs, fulfilment, agency fees and other expenses may still make a campaign unprofitable.

Formula

ROAS = advertising-attributed revenue ÷ advertising spend. The result is commonly expressed as a ratio such as 4×, meaning four units of attributed revenue for each unit spent on advertising. Multiply the ratio by 100 to express it as a revenue-to-ad-spend percentage.

How to use it

  1. Enter revenue attributed to the campaign in the revenue field.
  2. Enter the advertising spend for the same campaign and period.
  3. Select Calculate and read the ROAS ratio.
  4. Ensure revenue attribution and spend use the same currency and time window.

Worked example

If a campaign generates 12,000 in attributed revenue and costs 3,000 in ad spend, ROAS = 12,000 ÷ 3,000 = 4×, or 400% revenue relative to ad spend. This does not mean a 400% profit margin. If goods cost 5,000 and other expenses are 2,000, those costs must also be considered when assessing profit.

Interpretation and common mistakes

A higher ROAS may indicate stronger revenue efficiency, but comparison is meaningful only when attribution windows, channels, currencies and reporting periods are consistent. Do not count all business revenue as ad-attributed revenue unless the measurement method supports that attribution. Spend must be greater than zero for this ratio to be defined. ROAS does not measure incremental sales by itself and can be affected by repeat customers, brand searches, discounts and attribution settings. Use contribution margin, customer acquisition cost and profit metrics alongside ROAS for budget decisions.

How to interpret ROAS

ROAS = advertising-attributed revenue ÷ advertising spend. A ROAS of 4× means four units of attributed revenue for every one unit spent on ads. It does not mean four units of profit: product cost, shipping, refunds, payment fees and overhead still affect profitability.

Worked example

If a campaign generates 80,000 in attributed revenue and costs 20,000 in ad spend, ROAS = 80,000 ÷ 20,000 = 4×, or 400% when expressed as revenue divided by spend times 100. Keep revenue and spend in the same currency and measurement period.

Improve the quality of the comparison

  • Use a consistent attribution window and platform reporting method.
  • Separate new-customer and returning-customer results when they serve different goals.
  • Account for returns, cancellations and delayed conversions where possible.
  • Compare campaigns with similar margins and objectives rather than relying on ROAS alone.

A profitable break-even ROAS depends on gross margin and other variable costs. This calculator reports the revenue-to-ad-spend ratio; it does not predict campaign results or calculate net profit automatically.
