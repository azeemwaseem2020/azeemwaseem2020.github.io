Momentum Calculator — p = mv

Calculate linear momentum using p = mv and solve the relationship in reverse for mass or velocity.

Calculate

Formula

p = mv. Momentum is a vector quantity, so the sign or direction of velocity matters when solving a one-dimensional problem.

Worked example

A 10 kg object moving at 5 m/s has momentum p = 10 × 5 = 50 kg·m/s in the direction of its velocity.

Momentum is not kinetic energy

Momentum changes linearly with velocity, while classical kinetic energy depends on velocity squared. They answer different physics questions.

Common mistakes

  • Using speed when direction/sign matters.
  • Mixing grams and kilograms without conversion.
  • Confusing momentum with force or kinetic energy.
  • Ignoring the system boundary when analysing total momentum.

Momentum formula

Linear momentum is p = m × v, where mass is measured in kilograms and velocity in metres per second for the SI result.

Momentum and direction

Momentum is a vector quantity, so velocity direction matters. A negative or opposite direction can change the sign of the momentum rather than simply its magnitude.

Momentum Calculator Guide

Use this calculator to find linear momentum from mass and velocity, or rearrange the relationship to solve for a missing quantity.

Formula

p = mv, where p is momentum in kg·m/s, m is mass in kilograms, and v is velocity in metres per second.

Worked example

A 5 kg object moving at 8 m/s has momentum of 40 kg·m/s in the direction of its velocity.

Momentum is a vector

Momentum has direction as well as magnitude. When objects move in different directions, add momentum using signed or vector components rather than adding magnitudes blindly.

Why mass and velocity matter

Doubling mass doubles momentum at the same velocity. Reversing velocity reverses the momentum direction.

Limits

This is the classical linear-momentum relationship. Relativistic momentum is required for speeds approaching the speed of light, and collisions may require conservation-of-momentum analysis.

How this calculation is checked

Keep units consistent, review the formula shown above, and check the result against the physical assumptions of the problem.

MOMENTUM MODEL CHECK

Momentum combines mass with velocity, so direction matters

Linear momentum is p = mv. Unlike kinetic energy, momentum is a vector quantity: reversing velocity reverses momentum while leaving kinetic energy unchanged for the same speed.

Sign and direction

Choose a positive direction before combining momentum values. Opposite directions should be represented with opposite signs.

Conservation boundary

Total momentum is conserved for an isolated system when external impulse is negligible over the interaction interval.

Momentum vs energy

Momentum and kinetic energy describe different properties. A collision can conserve total momentum while kinetic energy changes.
