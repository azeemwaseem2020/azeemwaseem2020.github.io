Momentum Calculator — p = mv

Calculate linear momentum using p = mv and solve the relationship in reverse for mass or velocity.

Calculate

Formula

p = mv. Momentum is a vector quantity, so the sign or direction of velocity matters when solving a one-dimensional problem.

Worked example

A 10 kg object moving at 5 m/s has momentum p = 10 × 5 = 50 kg·m/s in the direction of its velocity.

Momentum is not kinetic energy

Momentum changes linearly with velocity, while classical kinetic energy depends on velocity squared. They answer different physics questions.

Common mistakes

  • Using speed when direction/sign matters.
  • Mixing grams and kilograms without conversion.
  • Confusing momentum with force or kinetic energy.
  • Ignoring the system boundary when analysing total momentum.

Momentum formula

Linear momentum is p = m × v, where mass is measured in kilograms and velocity in metres per second for the SI result.

Momentum and direction

Momentum is a vector quantity, so velocity direction matters. A negative or opposite direction can change the sign of the momentum rather than simply its magnitude.

Momentum Calculator Guide

Formula

p = mv, where p is momentum in kg·m/s, m is mass in kilograms, and v is velocity in metres per second.

Worked example

A 5 kg object moving at 8 m/s has momentum of 40 kg·m/s in the direction of its velocity.

Momentum is a vector

Momentum has direction as well as magnitude. When objects move in different directions, add momentum using signed or vector components rather than adding magnitudes blindly.

Why mass and velocity matter

Doubling mass doubles momentum at the same velocity. Reversing velocity reverses the momentum direction.

Limits

This is the classical linear-momentum relationship. Relativistic momentum is required for speeds approaching the speed of light, and collisions may require conservation-of-momentum analysis.

Understanding momentum

Linear momentum depends on an object's mass and velocity. Because velocity has direction, momentum is a vector quantity and direction matters when combining momenta.

Worked example

A 5 kg object moving at 4 m/s has momentum of 20 kg·m/s in the direction of motion. Reversing the velocity reverses the momentum direction.

Momentum in collisions

In an isolated system, total momentum is conserved. Real collision problems may require the momentum of every relevant object and careful attention to signs or directions.

Momentum FAQ

Does a stationary object have momentum?

No. When velocity is zero, linear momentum is zero regardless of the object's mass.

Momentum calculator guide: direction, impulse and collisions

Linear momentum is p = mv, where mass m is measured in kilograms and velocity v in metres per second. Momentum is a vector, so the direction or sign of velocity matters. In a one-dimensional problem, choose a positive direction first and use signed velocities consistently; in two or three dimensions, resolve momentum into vector components.

Worked example: two objects moving in opposite directions

Take motion to the right as positive. A 2 kg cart moving right at 3 m/s has momentum +6 kg·m/s. A 1 kg cart moving left at 4 m/s has momentum −4 kg·m/s. Their combined momentum is +2 kg·m/s, not 10 kg·m/s, because the momenta point in opposite directions. This sum describes the selected two-object system before considering any external impulse.

Connect momentum to force through impulse

For a system with a net external force, momentum changes. The impulse–momentum relationship is J = Δp; for a constant net force over a time interval, J = FΔt. Impulse is measured in N·s, which is dimensionally equivalent to kg·m/s. A collision problem often needs the momentum of every object before and after impact, plus a clear statement about external forces.

Conservation requires a system boundary

Total momentum is conserved for an isolated system when the net external impulse is negligible. Do not assume the momentum of one object remains constant during a collision: it may transfer momentum to another object. In a real impact, energy may also become sound, heat or deformation, so momentum conservation does not mean kinetic energy is always conserved.

Momentum calculator FAQ

Can momentum be negative?

The signed component of momentum can be negative when the object moves opposite to the chosen positive direction. The momentum vector itself has direction; a negative sign is a bookkeeping convention for one-dimensional calculations.

Is momentum conserved in every collision?

Total momentum is conserved when the system is sufficiently isolated or external impulse is negligible. The momentum of an individual object usually changes during an interaction. Kinetic energy is conserved only in an ideal elastic collision, not in every collision.

What is the difference between momentum and kinetic energy?

Momentum depends on mass and velocity, p = mv, and includes direction. Classical kinetic energy is KE = ½mv² and is a scalar. They describe different properties and should not be substituted for each other.

When does p = mv need a different model?

The formula is the classical approximation for ordinary speeds. For objects moving at speeds approaching the speed of light, relativistic momentum is needed.
