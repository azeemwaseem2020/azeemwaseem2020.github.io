Voltage Calculator

Calculate electrical voltage from current and resistance using Ohm's law.

Voltage formula

Ohm's law gives V = I × R, where V is voltage in volts, I is current in amperes, and R is resistance in ohms.

Worked example

If current is 2 A and resistance is 6 Ω, voltage = 2 × 6 = 12 V.

Units and assumptions

Use amperes and ohms for the standard SI form. The simple V = IR relationship is appropriate when the stated resistance model applies. Real components can vary with temperature, frequency, operating point, and circuit configuration.

Related formulas

Current can be found from I = V/R, while resistance can be found from R = V/I when the other quantities are known.

Voltage from current and resistance

When current and resistance are known, Ohm's law gives V = I × R. Keep current in amperes and resistance in ohms to obtain volts. The relationship assumes the component behaves according to the model being used.

Example

If a 3 A current passes through a 4 Ω resistor, the calculated voltage is 12 V. The associated electrical power would be 36 W, so a real resistor would also need an appropriate power rating.

When the simple formula is not enough

Semiconductors, batteries and other nonlinear or variable sources may not follow a constant-resistance model. Use the device's electrical characteristics when the circuit cannot reasonably be represented by a fixed resistance.

Voltage calculation: formula, units and worked method

Ohm’s law gives V = I × R for a component whose voltage-current relationship can be modelled by a resistance. Multiply current in amperes by resistance in ohms to get voltage in volts. The equation can be rearranged to I = V/R or R = V/I when the other two values are known.

Worked example: checking a resistor circuit

Suppose a resistor carries 0.25 A and has a resistance of 48 Ω. The voltage across it is V = 0.25 × 48 = 12 V. As a check, 12 V ÷ 48 Ω returns 0.25 A. This reverse check is useful for spotting unit or arithmetic mistakes.

When this model is not enough

Ohm’s law is appropriate for an ohmic component under the stated operating conditions. A diode, lamp filament, battery or other non-linear device may not have a fixed resistance; its voltage and current relationship can change with temperature or operating point. In AC circuits, impedance and phase can matter, so a simple resistance-only calculation may not describe the whole circuit.

Voltage calculator FAQs

What does a negative voltage result mean?

It can indicate polarity relative to the direction or reference point chosen for the circuit. It is not automatically an error if the sign convention is intentional.

Can I calculate voltage if current is zero?

The equation V = IR gives zero volts across an ideal fixed resistor when current is zero. A real circuit may still have a source voltage elsewhere, so make sure the current and resistance describe the same component.

Should I use this for mains wiring?

No. This educational calculation is not a substitute for electrical safety procedures, local codes or qualified assessment.

Use Ohm’s law with matching values

For an ohmic component under the relevant conditions, voltage is current multiplied by resistance: V = I × R. A current of 0.5 A through a 24 Ω resistor gives 12 V. The current and resistance must refer to the same component and operating point.

Do not mix amps and milliamps

Convert current to amperes before calculating. For example, 250 mA is 0.25 A; multiplying 0.25 A by 40 Ω gives 10 V. Entering 250 as if it were amperes would produce a result one thousand times too large.

Where the simple model has limits

Some components are non-ohmic, and their current-voltage relationship changes with temperature, illumination or operating state. In AC circuits, voltage, current and impedance may require phase-aware analysis. This calculator does not replace circuit analysis or a device datasheet.

Safety note

A calculated voltage is not a statement that a circuit is safe to touch. Isolate equipment and use appropriate instruments and procedures; electrical work should follow applicable standards.
