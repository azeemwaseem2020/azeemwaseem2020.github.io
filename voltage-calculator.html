Voltage Calculator

Calculate electrical voltage from current and resistance using Ohm's law.

Voltage formula

Ohm's law gives V = I × R, where V is voltage in volts, I is current in amperes, and R is resistance in ohms.

Worked example

If current is 2 A and resistance is 6 Ω, voltage = 2 × 6 = 12 V.

Units and assumptions

Use amperes and ohms for the standard SI form. The simple V = IR relationship is appropriate when the stated resistance model applies. Real components can vary with temperature, frequency, operating point, and circuit configuration.

Related formulas

Current can be found from I = V/R, while resistance can be found from R = V/I when the other quantities are known.

FAQ

What is Ohm's law? It relates voltage, current, and resistance as V = IR for an ohmic relationship.

What is voltage measured in? Voltage is measured in volts (V).

Can voltage be negative? Yes. A negative result can indicate the chosen polarity or reference direction.

Voltage relationships

Voltage can be calculated from Ohm's law as V = I × R, where current is in amperes and resistance in ohms. Electrical power can also relate voltage to current through P = V × I for the appropriate DC or real-power case. Use the relationship that matches the known quantities and circuit conditions.

For example, 2 A through 6 Ω gives 12 V. In AC systems, phase angle and power factor can matter, so a simple DC equation should not be applied blindly to apparent power.
