Ohm's law

V = IR

Voltage V equals current I multiplied by resistance R. Rearranging gives I = V/R and R = V/I. Use consistent units: volts, amperes and ohms.

Electrical power

P = VI = I²R = V²/R

These equivalent forms are useful when different quantities are known. For example, a 12 V load drawing 2 A consumes 24 W under a simple DC model.

Series resistance

For resistors in series, total resistance is the sum: Rtotal = R₁ + R₂ + … . Parallel networks use a different relationship, so do not apply the series formula to a parallel circuit.

DC versus AC

Basic Ohm's-law and power examples often assume resistive DC loads. AC systems can involve RMS values, power factor, reactive power and frequency-dependent impedance. A simple calculator should not be treated as a complete AC system model.

Safety boundary

Calculation is not a substitute for electrical design, protection selection or safe isolation procedures. Mains-voltage work should follow applicable codes and qualified professional practice.

FAQ

What is Ohm's law?

For an ohmic component under the applicable conditions, voltage equals current multiplied by resistance.

How do I calculate watts from volts and amps?

For a simple DC/resistive model, multiply voltage by current: P = VI.
