Electricity Calculators: Ohm's Law, Power & Resistance

Editorial information: Written and maintained by Abdul Qadir · Last updated: October 17, 2026

Start with the electrical quantity you know and the quantity you need to find.

Ohm’s law: voltage, current and resistance

For an ohmic component under the conditions where the relationship applies, V = I × R. Rearranging gives I = V ÷ R and R = V ÷ I. Use volts, amperes and ohms consistently. The Ohm’s Law Calculator solves for a missing quantity when the other two are known.

Electrical power is not the same as energy

For a simple DC or resistive model, P = V × I, and equivalent forms include P = I²R and P = V² ÷ R. A 12 V load drawing 2 A uses 24 W while it is operating. Energy also depends on time: running that idealized 24 W load for five hours uses 120 Wh, or 0.12 kWh. Use the Electrical Power Calculator for watts and electrical quantities.

Series resistance

For ideal resistors in series, add the resistance values: Rtotal = R1 + R2 + …. For parallel branches, the equivalent resistance follows a different formula, so do not use the series sum. Try the Series Resistance Calculator for a series network.

Frequently asked questions

What does Ohm’s law calculate?

Ohm’s law relates voltage, current and resistance for an applicable component or circuit model.

How do I calculate watts from volts and amps?

For a simple DC or resistive model, multiply voltage by current: P = VI.

Are basic electricity calculator results suitable for mains design?

No. Mains work can involve protection, power factor, reactive effects and applicable electrical codes. Use qualified professional practice for safety-critical work.

DC versus AC

Basic Ohm’s-law and power examples often assume a resistive DC load. AC systems may require RMS values and can involve power factor, reactive power and frequency-dependent impedance. A simple calculator is not a complete AC system model.

Estimate electricity energy cost

To estimate the energy portion of a bill, multiply energy use in kilowatt-hours by the per-unit tariff you enter. For example, 100 kWh at a hypothetical PKR 50 per kWh gives PKR 5,000 for energy alone. This is not a current tariff quote and does not include fixed charges, taxes, fuel adjustments or other bill components. Use the Electricity Cost Calculator with the tariff and units from your own bill.

Safety boundary

Calculation is not a substitute for electrical design, protective-device selection or safe isolation. Mains-voltage work should follow applicable codes and be handled by qualified professionals.
