What this TMR calculator actually calculates

This tool turns a ration formulation you already have into practical batch quantities. It is useful when you know the ingredient percentages and want to scale them to a group of cattle or buffalo. It can also help you review dry-matter contribution and estimate feed cost when ingredient prices are entered.

It does not decide what a cow or buffalo should eat. Animal requirements depend on body weight, milk production, stage of lactation, forage quality, nutrient analysis, health and management. Use a qualified nutritionist or veterinarian when you need a ration formulated for a specific herd or animal.

Simple worked example

Suppose a 1,000 kg TMR batch contains 40% forage, 30% silage, 20% concentrate and 10% other ingredients. The physical batch weights are 400 kg, 300 kg, 200 kg and 100 kg respectively. The percentages should total 100% before the batch is mixed.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the total batch size or the group quantity you want to prepare.
  2. Enter each ingredient's formulation percentage.
  3. Add dry-matter values when you have reliable feed analysis or label data.
  4. Add current ingredient prices if you want a batch or daily cost estimate.
  5. Check that the formulation totals 100% and review the result before loading the mixer.

Why dry matter changes the picture

Two feeds can weigh the same but supply different amounts of dry matter because their moisture levels differ. Silage and fresh forage are common examples. Comparing rations only by as-fed kilograms can therefore give a misleading picture of the amount of dry feed being supplied.

Important assumptions

  • Ingredient percentages are treated as the formulation supplied by the user.
  • Dry-matter results are only as reliable as the dry-matter percentages entered.
  • Feed cost estimates use the prices entered and do not represent a live market price.
  • The calculation does not diagnose nutritional deficiencies or create a medical treatment plan.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use it for dairy cows and buffalo?

Yes, for scaling and checking a formulation you already have. The calculator does not create a species-specific ration recommendation.

Why should the percentages add up to 100%?

A complete formulation needs a defined total. If the percentages do not add up to 100%, the ingredient weights will not represent the stated batch correctly.

Can it calculate feed cost?

Yes, when ingredient prices are entered. The result is an estimate based on those prices and the quantities calculated from your formulation.

Should I use dry-matter values from memory?

For practical ration work, use reliable feed analysis, laboratory data, supplier information or other appropriate measurements when available.

Does it replace a livestock nutritionist?

No. It is a calculation aid. Formulating a nutritionally balanced ration requires animal-specific requirements and dependable feed composition data.
