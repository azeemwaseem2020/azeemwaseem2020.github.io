TMR INPUT STANDARD

Use the right feed basis before trusting the result

The calculator performs the arithmetic for the ration you enter. The quality of the result depends on consistent input definitions, especially as-fed weight, dry matter and ingredient price.

As-fed basis

Enter ingredient mix percentages on the same basis. Do not mix as-fed percentages with dry-matter percentages.

Dry matter

Use representative dry-matter values when you need a DM check. Moisture variation can materially change delivered dry matter.

Batch scaling

Confirm the total mix reaches 100% before scaling the ration to a larger or smaller batch.

Farm verification

Compare calculated quantities with actual weighed ingredients and current feed analysis before applying the result.

This tool supports ration quantity and arithmetic checks; it does not replace animal-specific nutritional formulation by a qualified professional.

Using a TMR result on the farm

A total mixed ration calculation is only as useful as the feed analysis and animal assumptions behind it. Keep dry-matter values, ingredient weights and the target intake basis consistent when scaling a ration.

Checks before feeding

Verify actual forage and concentrate dry matter, animal class, expected intake, water availability and the nutrient specifications used in the calculation. A mathematical ration estimate should not replace advice from a qualified livestock nutrition professional when the ration is for a high-producing or medically compromised animal.

How to use a TMR calculator on a dairy farm

A total mixed ration calculator is most useful when you already have a formulation or target mix and need to scale ingredient quantities, check dry matter, estimate batch size or calculate feed cost. It should not be treated as a universal ration prescription because animal requirements and feed composition vary.

As-fed weight versus dry matter

Two feeds can have the same as-fed weight but very different dry-matter content. For example, 10 kg of a feed at 30% dry matter contains 3 kg of dry matter, while 10 kg at 60% dry matter contains 6 kg. Keeping these bases separate is essential when checking intake and scaling a ration.

Scaling a known mix

If an ingredient represents 20% of a 500 kg batch, its as-fed quantity is 100 kg. The same percentage can then be scaled to another batch size, provided the formulation basis remains unchanged. After scaling, check the total percentage and the dry-matter contribution of each ingredient.

Worked TMR example

Consider a simple 1,000 kg batch containing 40% forage, 30% silage and 30% concentrate on an as-fed basis. The corresponding ingredient weights are 400 kg, 300 kg and 300 kg. If their dry-matter values differ, calculate each ingredient's dry matter separately before assessing the ration on a DM basis.

TMR calculation checklist for farm records

  • Confirm whether each input is as-fed or dry-matter basis.
  • Use recent feed analysis where available.
  • Keep ingredient percentages consistent and verify they total 100% when required.
  • Check animal group, intake assumptions and number of animals.
  • Recheck actual delivered weights at the mixer.
  • Use professional nutrition advice for high-producing, sick or special-needs animals.

TMR calculator FAQ

Can this calculator formulate a complete dairy ration?

No. It performs the arithmetic from the formulation and inputs you provide. Complete ration formulation requires animal requirements, feed analysis and nutritional balancing.

Why is dry matter important?

Moisture changes the amount of nutrients delivered per kilogram of as-fed feed. Dry matter provides a common basis for comparing feeds with different water content.

Can I calculate feed cost?

Yes, when current ingredient prices are entered. The cost estimate is only as accurate as the quantities and prices supplied.

Can I use the calculator for buffalo?

It can scale a known buffalo ration and perform arithmetic checks. It does not automatically create a buffalo-specific nutritional prescription.

TMR CALCULATION GUIDE

How to scale a TMR ration for cattle or buffalo

A TMR calculation is most useful when you already have a ration formulation and need to scale it for a different batch size or animal count. Keep as-fed weight and dry-matter weight separate because fresh forage can contain much more water than dry ingredients.

Ration scaling

If an ingredient represents a stated percentage of the ration, multiply that percentage by the total batch weight to obtain its ingredient quantity. The same ratio can be scaled to a larger or smaller batch without changing formulation percentages.

Dry matter

Forage, silage and other feeds can have different moisture levels. A ration that looks similar by as-fed kilograms can deliver a different amount of dry matter. Use representative feed-analysis values where available, and do not treat the calculator as a universal ration prescription.

Ingredient weight

Convert a known formulation into practical kilograms for the required batch.

Dry matter

Compare as-fed quantities with dry-matter contribution when moisture differs.
