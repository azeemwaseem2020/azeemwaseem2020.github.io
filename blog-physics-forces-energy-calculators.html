CALCORA SCIENCE GUIDE

Physics Force & Energy Calculators: A Problem-First Guide

Match the wording of a mechanics problem to the right calculator and check the units before trusting the result.

Force and motion

Use Force for mass and acceleration, Weight for gravitational force, Friction for resistance between surfaces, and Centripetal Force for the inward net force required by circular motion.

Energy and work

Kinetic Energy handles energy of translational motion. Potential Energy handles gravitational height-based energy. Work connects force, displacement and angle. Power describes the rate of energy transfer.

Momentum and impulse

Momentum uses mass and velocity, while Impulse connects force over time with momentum change.

Start with a free-body diagram

For force problems, write down the forces before reaching for a formula. Weight, friction, tension and an applied force may all be present, and the net force—not necessarily one individual force—determines acceleration through F = ma.

Worked check: kinetic energy

A 2 kg object moving at 5 m/s has kinetic energy KE = ½mv² = ½ × 2 × 25 = 25 J. If a calculator returns a different order of magnitude, check the mass and speed units first; squaring the speed makes unit errors particularly costly.

Where the simple formulas stop

These tools describe idealized relationships. Rotating systems, changing forces, drag, deformation and multi-body systems can require a fuller physical model. The calculator should support the model, not replace it.

Choose the model that matches the question

Start by identifying the quantity to solve for and the physical model behind it. Use net force for acceleration, weight for gravitational force, work for energy transferred by a force, and power for the rate of energy transfer. For rotational problems, choose a pivot and keep the torque sign convention consistent.

Check the assumptions before trusting a result

Introductory formulas often assume ideal conditions such as constant mass, a known acceleration, negligible air resistance or a rigid body. Real systems may include friction, energy losses, deformation or changing forces. Keep a short record of the inputs, units and assumptions so a result can be checked against the actual problem.

Use the linked Calcora tools as separate steps in a solution, not as substitutes for a free-body diagram, conservation-law reasoning or a more complete model when the simple assumptions do not hold.

Frequently asked questions

Should I use an individual force or the net force?

For Newton's second law, acceleration is determined by the net force, which is the vector sum of the forces acting on the object.

When should I convert units?

Convert quantities to a compatible system before substituting them into a formula; for example, use kilograms, metres and seconds for common SI mechanics formulas.

Why can a real result differ from the calculator?

The real system may include drag, friction, changing forces or other effects that the idealized equation does not represent.
