Overtime Calculator: Hourly Rate, Multiplier and Extra Earnings

This calculator estimates overtime pay from an hourly base rate, the number of overtime hours and a multiplier. It separates the overtime rate from the overtime earnings so you can check each part of the arithmetic. Whether particular hours qualify as overtime, and which multiplier applies, depends on the employment terms and applicable law.

Formula

Overtime rate = regular hourly rate × overtime multiplier. Overtime earnings = overtime rate × overtime hours. For example, a 1.5 multiplier means the overtime rate is one and a half times the regular hourly rate. It does not mean the multiplier should be applied to every hour in a whole pay period.

Worked example

If a worker's regular rate is PKR 500 per hour, the verified multiplier is 1.5 and eligible overtime is 6 hours, the overtime rate is PKR 750 per hour and the estimated overtime earnings are PKR 4,500. This is an arithmetic illustration before any payroll adjustments or deductions, not a statement of the legal overtime rule for every worker in Pakistan.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the regular hourly rate from the employment agreement or payroll record.
  2. Enter only the hours that have been confirmed as eligible overtime.
  3. Use the multiplier stated by the applicable policy, contract or legal rule.
  4. Compare the result with the payslip and keep records of dates and approved hours.

Common payroll questions

Check whether the hourly rate is based on basic salary or a wider wage definition, whether different days or shifts have different treatment, and whether the employer rounds time in a specified way. Salary-based employees may need an agreed method to convert monthly pay into an hourly equivalent. A calculator cannot resolve disputes about classification, eligibility or unpaid hours; retain time sheets and ask HR or a qualified adviser where needed.

Overtime Calculator: Hourly Rate, Multiplier and Records

Overtime pay is commonly estimated by multiplying the regular hourly rate by an overtime multiplier and then by the number of overtime hours. The legally applicable calculation can depend on jurisdiction, worker classification, industry, contract and the period being assessed. The multiplier is an input—not a universal rule—so verify it before using the estimate for a payroll dispute or official claim.

Formula and example

Overtime amount = regular hourly rate × overtime hours × multiplier. At a regular rate of PKR 500 per hour, four overtime hours and a hypothetical 1.5 multiplier, the estimated overtime amount is 500 × 4 × 1.5 = PKR 3,000 before deductions. This is an arithmetic example, not a statement that 1.5× applies to every worker.

How to use it

  1. Find the regular hourly rate under the relevant payroll method.
  2. Enter the overtime hours actually worked and recorded.
  3. Use the multiplier required by the applicable law or written employment terms.
  4. Compare the estimate with the payslip and approved time records.

Clarify whether the input rate already includes allowances or shift premiums and whether overtime is calculated on basic pay or another defined wage base. Keep dates, approved hours, rosters and payslips together. If your workplace has different rates for weekends, holidays or night shifts, calculate those categories separately rather than combining them under one assumed multiplier.
