How overtime pay is calculated

Overtime calculations depend on the normal hourly rate, the number of overtime hours and the multiplier used by the employer or applicable rules. A calculator can perform the arithmetic, but it cannot determine which overtime rate legally applies to a particular job, contract or employee category. Keep the payroll policy separate from the mathematical calculation.

Formula

Overtime hourly rate = regular hourly rate × overtime multiplier. Overtime pay = overtime hours × overtime hourly rate. Total pay for the period can then combine regular pay and overtime pay.

Worked example

If the regular rate is Rs 500/hour, overtime is 6 hours and the agreed multiplier is 1.5, overtime rate = 500 × 1.5 = Rs 750/hour. Overtime pay = 6 × 750 = Rs 4,500.

Pakistan-specific example

A payroll officer in Pakistan can use the calculator after confirming the employee's contractual rate, scheduled hours and applicable overtime policy. Different employment arrangements can use different rules, so the result should be checked against the employer's payroll documentation.

Common questions

Does the calculator decide the legal overtime rate? No.

Can overtime be a fixed amount? The calculator models a rate multiplier; a contract may use another method.

Are deductions included? No. Gross overtime pay is different from net take-home pay.

Can I combine different overtime rates? Calculate each block separately when rates differ.

For payroll review, keep regular hours and overtime hours as separate quantities. If an employee works multiple overtime bands, calculate each band independently and add the results. Rounding should normally be applied according to the payroll system rather than rounding every intermediate multiplication.

Also distinguish gross overtime from net pay. Income tax, social contributions or other deductions may affect the amount received by the employee but are not part of the basic overtime-rate formula.
