Square root formula and interpretation

The square root of a non-negative number x is the principal value r that satisfies r² = x and r ≥ 0. For example, √144 = 12 because 12 × 12 = 144. When the input is not a perfect square, the calculator returns a decimal approximation; √50 is about 7.0711. Keeping extra digits during intermediate calculations reduces rounding error.

Worked example

Suppose a square has an area of 196 m². Its side length is √196 = 14 m. This reverses the area formula A = s². The same idea is useful when recovering a radius from a circle's area, although the circle formula also requires division by π.

Common mistakes

Do not confuse the square root with dividing a number by two. Also remember that the principal square root is non-negative; the equation x² = 25 has two solutions, x = 5 and x = −5, while √25 itself means 5.

FAQs

The principal square root of x is the non-negative value whose square equals x. For example, √144 = 12. For a non-perfect square such as 50, the result is approximately 7.0711. Keep extra precision until the final step when using the result in another calculation.

FAQs

Can negative inputs have real square roots? No. Can the result be checked? Yes, square the result and compare with the input. Why are some results irrational? Numbers such as 2 do not have an exact rational square root.
