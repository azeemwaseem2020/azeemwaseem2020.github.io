Due Date Calculator

Estimate an expected pregnancy due date from the first day of the last menstrual period using a 280-day calendar estimate.

How the due date estimate works

The standard calendar estimate adds 280 days, or 40 weeks, to the first day of the last menstrual period. It is a general dating method rather than a prediction of the exact day a baby will be born.

Worked example

If the first day of the last menstrual period is January 1, adding 280 days gives an estimated due date around October 8 in a non-leap-year example.

Why the estimate can differ

Cycle length, uncertainty about the last menstrual period, ovulation timing, and clinical dating can affect the estimated gestational age. A clinician may adjust pregnancy dating based on the available medical information and ultrasound assessment.

Important note

This calculator is for general information and is not a medical diagnosis or substitute for prenatal care. If you have questions about pregnancy dating or your health, discuss them with a qualified healthcare professional.

FAQ

Is the due date exact? No. It is an estimate; birth can occur before or after the estimated date.

What date should I enter? Enter the first day of the last menstrual period if that date is known.

Can a clinician change the estimated due date? Yes. Pregnancy dating may be reassessed using clinical information and ultrasound.
