Understanding logarithms

A logarithm answers the question: “What exponent produces this number from the chosen base?” For base b, log_b(x) = y means b^y = x. Logarithms are useful for exponential growth, scientific scales and solving equations where the unknown appears as an exponent.

Formula

log_b(x) = ln(x) ÷ ln(b), provided b > 0, b ≠ 1 and x > 0. Common logarithms use base 10; natural logarithms use base e.

Worked example

log₁₀(1,000) = 3 because 10³ = 1,000. For a less obvious value such as log₁₀(100), the answer is 2 because 10² = 100.

Pakistan-specific example

A student in a Pakistani mathematics or engineering course may use logarithms when solving growth equations, decibel relationships or pH calculations. Always check the base specified by the formula before entering a value.

Common questions

Can the input be zero? No. The real logarithm of zero is undefined.

Can the base be 1? No, because 1 raised to every power remains 1.

What is ln? Natural logarithm with base e.

Why is change-of-base useful? It allows a calculator that directly supports ln or log to evaluate other bases.
