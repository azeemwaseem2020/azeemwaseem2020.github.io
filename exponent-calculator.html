What is an exponent?

An exponent tells you how many times a base is multiplied by itself for whole-number powers. In an, a is the base and n is the exponent. For example, 23 = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8.

Exponent formula

The basic relationship is an. Important rules include a0 = 1 for a non-zero base, a-n = 1/an, and am × an = am+n when the expressions are defined.

Worked examples

Positive exponent: 53 = 125.

Zero exponent: 90 = 1.

Negative exponent: 2-3 = 1/8 = 0.125.

Fractional exponent: 161/2 = √16 = 4.

When to use an exponent calculator

Use it to check powers, algebraic calculations, scientific notation work, growth calculations, and other expressions involving a number raised to a power. For very large or very small results, the browser's numeric range may limit the displayed value.

Exponent FAQ

What is 0 to a positive power?

For a positive exponent, 0 raised to that power is 0.

What is a number raised to zero?

Any non-zero number raised to the zero power equals 1.

Can I use negative exponents?

Yes. A negative exponent represents a reciprocal: a-n = 1/an, provided the expression is defined.

What about fractional exponents?

Fractional exponents can represent roots and powers. The real-number result depends on the base and the denominator of the exponent.

Understanding exponents

An exponent indicates repeated multiplication of a base. For example, 2³ means 2 × 2 × 2, which equals 8.

Positive, zero and negative exponents

A positive exponent represents repeated multiplication. A non-zero number raised to the power of zero equals 1, while a negative exponent represents the reciprocal of the corresponding positive power.

Common mistakes

Be careful with negative bases and parentheses. The expressions -2² and (-2)² are different because the exponent applies differently when the base is enclosed.

Exponent FAQ

Why do parentheses matter with negative numbers?

Parentheses make the negative number part of the base. Without them, standard order of operations can apply the exponent before the negative sign.
