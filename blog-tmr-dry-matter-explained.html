TMR Dry Matter: How to Calculate DM Intake and Moisture

Dry matter removes the water component from a feed and gives a more comparable basis for evaluating ingredients and rations. It is a calculation tool, not a complete ration recommendation.

The basic dry-matter calculation

Dry matter kg = as-fed kg × DM fraction. A 20 kg batch at 40% DM contains 8 kg of dry matter and 12 kg of water.

Why as-fed weight can mislead

Two feeds can weigh the same while supplying very different amounts of nutrients because their moisture content differs. Comparing silage, fresh forage and dry ingredients by as-fed kilograms alone can therefore be misleading.

Calculating a mixed ration

For a TMR, calculate each ingredient's dry matter separately, then add the dry-matter kilograms. Divide total DM by total as-fed weight to estimate the mixed ration DM percentage.

Worked example

Suppose 10 kg forage at 30% DM and 5 kg concentrate at 90% DM are mixed. The forage contributes 3 kg DM and the concentrate contributes 4.5 kg DM, so the mixture contains 7.5 kg DM in 15 kg as-fed feed: 50% DM.

Refusals matter

If delivered feed and refusals are measured, the consumed as-fed amount can be estimated as delivered minus refused. DM intake can then be estimated using the relevant DM percentage, provided the measurements are representative.

Use dry matter to compare feeds fairly

Fresh feeds can contain very different amounts of water, so comparing them only by kilograms as-fed can be misleading. Dry matter (DM) expresses the portion remaining after water is excluded. DM intake is useful for ration accounting, but it does not by itself establish whether a ration supplies enough energy, protein, fibre, minerals or effective fibre for a particular animal.

Worked example

If a cow receives 10 kg of silage at 30% dry matter, the feed contributes 10 × 0.30 = 3 kg DM. If it also receives 4 kg of concentrate at 90% DM, that contributes 3.6 kg DM. Together these two feeds supply 6.6 kg DM before any other feed is counted. These values are illustrative; actual feed moisture should be measured or taken from a reliable analysis.

Practical checks for dairy farms

  • Use the same units for each ingredient and convert DM percentages to decimals for calculations.
  • Recheck silage moisture as the face or storage conditions change; a stale estimate can distort the ration.
  • Record refusals as well as feed offered when estimating actual intake.
  • Do not confuse as-fed weight with dry-matter weight when comparing ration cost per kg DM.
  • Ask a qualified livestock nutritionist or veterinarian to review ration balance, especially when intake, milk yield, body condition or health changes.

A DM calculator supports feed bookkeeping; it does not replace animal-specific ration formulation or diagnosis.
