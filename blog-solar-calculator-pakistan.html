PAKISTAN SOLAR GUIDE

Solar Calculator Pakistan: Load, Panels, Battery & Inverter

For a useful Pakistan estimate, start with actual electricity units and appliances, then account for solar conditions and the backup goal.

What should a Pakistan solar calculator include?

  • Daily or monthly electricity consumption in kWh.
  • Appliance wattage and expected running hours.
  • Solar production assumptions such as peak sun hours and system efficiency.
  • Panel wattage and total PV capacity.
  • Inverter continuous and surge requirements.
  • Backup load, battery voltage and required backup hours.

Worked load example

Suppose essential appliances use an average of 1.2 kW for 6 hours during the day. Their estimated daily energy use is 1.2 × 6 = 7.2 kWh. If the wider home uses another 4 kWh during the rest of the day, total daily consumption would be about 11.2 kWh. This is a planning example, not a guaranteed production target.

Bill versus units

Units (kWh) are normally a better starting point than the rupee amount of an electricity bill because bills can include fixed charges, taxes, slabs and other components. If only the bill amount is available, treat any conversion to kWh as an estimate.

Why Pakistan estimates vary

Solar output changes with location, season, temperature, shade, dust, roof orientation and equipment. A useful calculator should show its assumptions rather than presenting one production figure as guaranteed.

Solar for load shedding

Separate daytime solar sizing from battery sizing. Decide which circuits must remain on during an outage and for how many hours. For example, if essential backup demand averages 500 W for 4 hours, the simple delivered-energy requirement is about 2 kWh before battery and inverter losses.

Panel, inverter and battery are different decisions

PV panel capacity relates to energy production. Inverter capacity relates to the power the system can supply at a given moment, including starting surges. Battery capacity relates to stored energy and backup duration. Treating all three as the same number can lead to poor system sizing.

Before purchasing

Use the calculator to compare scenarios, then have the final design checked for roof structure, wiring, protection, inverter and battery specifications, utility requirements and applicable local rules. Equipment warranties and installation requirements should be confirmed with the supplier.

A practical Pakistan workflow

Start with several electricity bills and identify the monthly units used in hotter and milder periods. Next, list the appliances that matter during the day and during load-shedding. Only then estimate PV capacity, inverter demand and battery runtime. This order keeps the three sizing questions separate.

Example: why one number is not enough

A home might use around 12 kWh per day but have a 3.5 kW peak load because several appliances run together. The solar array is planned around energy production, the inverter around simultaneous power and surge, and the battery around the loads that must remain available during an outage.

Frequently asked questions

Are calculator results a professional recommendation?

No. The calculator applies the inputs and calculation method you provide. Field, financial or equipment decisions should be checked against current official information and the relevant professional guidance.

Why do units and assumptions matter?

A mathematically correct formula can still produce a misleading result when units, rate conventions, product analysis, efficiency or other inputs are inconsistent.

What should I verify before using the result?

Verify the input values, units, formula assumptions and any fees, product specifications or local conditions that can materially change the result.
