Weight-loss calorie estimate: formula and guide

This calculator converts a target weekly weight change into an approximate daily energy deficit using a simplified energy-equivalent assumption. It is a rough planning model, not a prediction of exactly how body weight will change.

Formula

Estimated daily deficit = weekly target (kg) × 7,700 kcal/kg ÷ 7 days. For a target of 0.5 kg per week, the arithmetic gives 0.5 × 7,700 ÷ 7 = about 550 kcal per day. The 7,700 kcal/kg value is an approximation; actual weight change is affected by water, body composition, metabolism and adaptation.

How to use it

  1. Enter a positive weekly target in kilograms, matching the unit shown by the calculator.
  2. Select Calculate and review the estimated daily energy deficit.
  3. Treat the result as a simplified illustration, not as a prescribed calorie intake.
  4. Consider activity, nutrition quality, sleep, sustainability and medical context rather than relying on one number.

Important safety context

Weight change is not linear and individual energy needs differ. A daily deficit estimate is not the same as a total daily calorie target; calculating intake also requires an estimate of energy expenditure. Avoid extreme restriction. Children and adolescents, people who are pregnant or breastfeeding, people with a history of eating disorders, and those with chronic illness or relevant medicines should seek individualized professional guidance before attempting weight loss. Stop and seek help if a plan causes concerning symptoms.

Frequently asked questions

Does the result tell me how many calories to eat?

No. It estimates a theoretical deficit only. It does not calculate maintenance needs or a complete daily calorie target.

Will I lose exactly the selected amount each week?

No. Real weight changes fluctuate and the energy-equivalent assumption is only approximate. Track longer-term trends and reassess with qualified support if needed.

Why is the 7,700 kcal figure only an estimate?

Body weight includes water, fat and lean tissue, and energy expenditure changes as the body adapts. The simple calculation does not capture all of these dynamics.
