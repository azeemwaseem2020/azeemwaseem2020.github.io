How block-based tiers work

  • Tier 1 rate applies to the first block of kWh.
  • Tier 2 rate applies only to the next block after Tier 1.
  • Tier 3 rate applies to usage above both block limits.
  • Fixed and other charges are added after energy charges.

Example defaults are illustrative, not current utility prices. Replace them with your tariff values.

Formula

Tier 1 kWh = the smaller of total usage and the Tier 1 block size. Tier 2 kWh = the smaller of the remaining usage and the Tier 2 block size. Tier 3 kWh = any usage left after both blocks. Each tier's energy charge is its kWh multiplied by its rate in cents and divided by 100. The estimated bill adds all three energy charges, fixed charges and other charges entered.

Worked example

For 900 kWh, with the first 500 kWh at 12 cents, the next 300 kWh at 16 cents, and the remaining 100 kWh at 20 cents, the tier charges are $60, $48 and $20. Energy charges total $128. With $15 in fixed charges and no other charges entered, the estimated total is $143.

Important tariff limitation

This calculator models block-based energy pricing only. It does not calculate time-of-use periods, demand charges, minimum bills, seasonal adjustments, export credits, taxes by jurisdiction or complex utility-specific riders. Some tariffs use different rules or separate delivery and supply charges. Check your current utility tariff and bill before relying on the estimate.

For a simpler estimate using one average energy rate, use the US Electricity Bill Calculator. A single-rate estimate is not a substitute for a tiered tariff calculation.

Frequently asked questions

Does each rate apply to all my electricity?

No. In this block-based model, each rate applies only to the kWh inside its tier. For example, the Tier 2 rate applies to the next block, not the whole bill.

What if my usage is below the first tier limit?

All usage is charged at the Tier 1 rate; Tier 2 and Tier 3 usage is zero.

Is this a time-of-use calculator?

No. Time-of-use plans price electricity by time periods, such as peak and off-peak hours. This calculator uses total monthly kWh and usage blocks.
