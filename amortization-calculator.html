Frequently asked questions

What is an amortization schedule?

An amortization schedule shows how each loan payment is divided between interest and principal over the loan term.

Does the calculator include extra fees?

No. The estimate uses the loan amount, interest rate and payment term. Add applicable fees to the financed amount if appropriate.

Why does the interest portion change over time?

With a standard amortizing loan, the outstanding balance falls after payments, so the interest calculated on that balance generally decreases over time.

What an amortization schedule shows

An amortization schedule breaks each scheduled payment into principal and interest and tracks the remaining balance. It helps you see how the balance changes over the life of a loan instead of looking only at the monthly payment.

How the balance changes

For a standard fixed-rate amortizing loan, interest for a period is based on the outstanding balance under the lender's stated method. The payment then reduces the balance by the amount left after interest.

Worked example

A loan with a fixed rate and regular monthly payments will normally show a larger interest share earlier in the schedule because the outstanding principal is highest at the beginning. As principal falls, the interest portion generally falls as well.

Amortization FAQ

Does amortization mean the loan is interest-free later?

No. Interest can remain part of every scheduled payment until the balance is repaid; the proportion may change over time.

Why can my lender's schedule differ?

Payment dates, rounding, fees, variable rates, extra payments and the lender's interest method can change the actual schedule.

Amortization formula

For a fixed-rate loan with equal monthly payments, PMT = P × r(1+r)n / ((1+r)n − 1), where P is principal, r is the monthly interest rate, and n is the number of monthly payments. When the rate is zero, payment is simply P ÷ n.

Worked example

For $100,000 at 8% annual interest over 10 years, the monthly payment is approximately $1,213.28 under the standard monthly-payment model. Actual lender figures can differ because of rounding, fees, payment dates, or interest conventions.

What changes the schedule?

A higher rate or longer term generally changes the total interest and payment structure. Extra principal payments can reduce the outstanding balance and may reduce future interest, depending on the loan terms.
