Understanding the result

The discriminant shows the type of roots: a positive value gives two real roots, zero gives one repeated real root, and a negative value gives a pair of complex conjugate roots.

Formula & assumptions

This calculator is for algebraic calculations. Check the coefficient values carefully, especially the sign of b and c. The coefficient a must be non-zero for the equation to remain quadratic.

Solving a quadratic equation

A quadratic equation has the standard form ax² + bx + c = 0 with a not equal to zero. Its solutions can be real or complex. The discriminant provides a quick way to understand the number of real roots before evaluating the formula.

Formula

x = [−b ± √(b² − 4ac)] ÷ (2a). The discriminant is Δ = b² − 4ac. If Δ is positive there are two distinct real roots; if Δ is zero there is one repeated real root; if Δ is negative the roots are complex.

Worked example

For x² − 5x + 6 = 0, a=1, b=−5 and c=6. The discriminant is 25−24=1. Therefore x = [5 ± 1]/2, giving x=3 and x=2. Substitution confirms both values make the equation zero.

Pakistan-specific example

A student preparing for an intermediate mathematics exam in Pakistan can use the calculator after first arranging the equation into ax²+bx+c=0. This prevents a common mistake where signs are copied incorrectly from an equation written in another form.

Common questions

Can a be zero? Not for a quadratic; if a=0 the equation becomes linear.

What does a negative discriminant mean? There are no real roots, although complex roots exist.

Should I round roots? Keep extra precision until the final step.

How can I check the answer? Substitute each root back into the original equation.
