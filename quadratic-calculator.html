Quadratic Calculator: Formula, Discriminant and Examples

A quadratic equation is a polynomial equation whose highest power of the variable is two. Its standard form is ax² + bx + c = 0, with a ≠ 0. A calculator can find real or complex roots depending on the discriminant and its supported features. Enter coefficients in their correct positions and preserve negative signs.

Formula and calculation method

The quadratic formula is x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac))/(2a). The discriminant is Δ = b² − 4ac. If Δ is positive there are two distinct real roots; if zero, one repeated real root; if negative, the roots are a complex conjugate pair. If a = 0, the equation is linear rather than quadratic.

How to use the calculator

  1. Rewrite the equation as ax² + bx + c = 0.
  2. Enter coefficients a, b and c, including signs.
  3. Calculate and check whether the roots are real or complex.
  4. Substitute each result into the original equation to verify it.

Worked example

For x² − 5x + 6 = 0, Δ = 25 − 24 = 1 and x = (5 ± 1)/2, giving roots 3 and 2. For x² − 4x + 4 = 0, Δ = 0 and the repeated root is 2. For x² + 1 = 0, Δ = −4 and the complex roots are i and −i.

Interpretation, assumptions and common mistakes

The entire numerator is divided by 2a. The ± symbol means evaluate both branches when roots differ. Do not omit coefficient signs. Decimal outputs may be rounded, so retain precision when checking. If a equals zero, division by 2a is invalid and a different method is needed. Mathematical roots do not automatically tell you which solution is meaningful in an applied problem.

Frequently asked questions

What does the discriminant show?

It indicates whether there are two distinct real roots, one repeated real root or complex roots.

Can a root be negative?

Yes. Root signs depend on the equation's coefficients.

What if a is zero?

The equation is not quadratic; solve it as a linear equation instead.
