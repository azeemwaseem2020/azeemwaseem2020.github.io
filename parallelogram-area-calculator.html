Area of a parallelogram

The area of a parallelogram is base × perpendicular height. The height is the shortest perpendicular distance between the two parallel bases; it is not generally the same as the slanted side length.

Worked example

With a base of 12 cm and perpendicular height of 5 cm, the area is 12 × 5 = 60 cm². If you know the slanted side but not the perpendicular height, the base and angle may be used to derive the height.

Practical example

The formula can be used for floor patterns, land sections or technical drawings. Keep measurements in the same unit before multiplication and report the result in square units.

Common mistake

Using the sloping side as the height usually overestimates or otherwise misstates the area. Only a perpendicular distance should be used as height.

FAQs

Does the angle matter? Only when it is needed to derive the perpendicular height. What unit is area? Square units. Can a rectangle be treated as a parallelogram? Yes; a rectangle is a special case.
