Area formula

The area is A = b × h, where b is the base and h is the perpendicular height. The sloping side is not automatically the height; the height must meet the chosen base at a right angle.

Worked example

If the base is 12 cm and the perpendicular height is 7 cm, the area is 12 × 7 = 84 cm².

Base versus sloping side

A parallelogram can be drawn at different angles without changing its base and perpendicular height. Using the sloping side instead of the perpendicular height can overestimate the area.

Unit check

Keep both measurements in the same length unit. The area will use the corresponding square unit, such as m² or ft².

Why the perpendicular height matters

The height is the shortest perpendicular distance between the parallel base lines. It is not necessarily the visible side length of a slanted parallelogram. If only a sloping side is known, additional angle information may be needed.

Area and perimeter are different

Area measures the surface enclosed by the shape in square units. Perimeter measures the boundary length in ordinary length units. This calculator returns area only.

Example with a slanted shape

A parallelogram may have a long sloping side, but the area still uses the perpendicular height. If the base is 10 m and the perpendicular height is 4 m, the area is 40 m² regardless of the slant angle.

Parallelogram Area Calculator: Formula and Examples

A parallelogram is a quadrilateral whose opposite sides are parallel. Its area is the surface enclosed by the shape. The essential geometry is that area depends on a base and its perpendicular height, not simply the length of a sloping side. This calculator is useful for school geometry, checking measurements and estimating simple surfaces.

Formula and calculation method

The standard formula is A = b × h, where b is the base and h is the perpendicular distance between the parallel base lines. If base b, adjacent side s and included angle θ are known, area can also be calculated as A = b × s × sin(θ). Use compatible length units; multiplying metres by metres gives square metres.

How to use the calculator

  1. Identify the base and the perpendicular height, not the sloping side.
  2. Enter both lengths in the same unit unless the calculator converts them.
  3. Calculate and check that the output uses square units.
  4. If using a side-and-angle method, make sure the angle is the included angle and the degree/radian mode is correct.

Worked example

A parallelogram with base 8 cm and perpendicular height 5 cm has area 8 × 5 = 40 cm². A base of 12 m and height 3.5 m gives 42 m². If base is 10 cm, adjacent side is 6 cm and included angle is 30°, area is 10 × 6 × sin(30°) = 30 cm².

Interpretation, assumptions and common mistakes

Using a sloping side as the height usually gives an incorrect area because height must meet the base at a right angle. Do not report square centimetres as centimetres. A rectangle is a special case where an adjacent side can serve as height. If a real surface is irregular or measurements are uncertain, the result is an estimate based on the geometric model.

Measurement tip: Sketch the shape and mark the right-angle height before entering values. When measuring a real object, take dimensions at more than one point if edges are uneven, and record whether measurements are in millimetres, centimetres or metres. A mathematically precise answer from rounded measurements is still only as reliable as those measurements. For construction or material ordering, allow for cutting, waste and any shape that departs from an ideal parallelogram.

Frequently asked questions

Is height the same as the sloping side?

Usually not. Height is the perpendicular distance between the parallel bases.

What unit does area use?

Square units such as cm², m² or ft².

Can an angle be used?

Yes, if the base, adjacent side and included angle are known and the angle unit is correct.
