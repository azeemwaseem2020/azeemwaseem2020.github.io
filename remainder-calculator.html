Understanding remainders

When an integer is divided by another non-zero integer, the remainder is what is left after taking the whole-number quotient. For positive integers, a = bq + r, where q is the quotient and r is the remainder with 0 ≤ r < b.

Worked example

29 divided by 6 gives quotient 4 and remainder 5, because 29 = 6 × 4 + 5. The remainder is the amount that cannot form another complete group of six.

A practical Pakistan example

If 29 items need to be packed into boxes of six, four complete boxes can be filled and five items remain. This is the same arithmetic used by a remainder calculator.

Common mistakes

Do not confuse the remainder with the decimal part of a division result. Division by zero is also undefined.

FAQ

What is 20 divided by 4 remainder? 0.

Can the remainder be larger than the divisor? No under the standard positive-integer definition.

Is remainder the same as modulo? They are closely related, although negative-number conventions can differ.

How the Remainder Calculator Works

The remainder is the amount left after one whole number is divided by another. For positive integers, the basic relationship is Dividend = Divisor × Quotient + Remainder, where the remainder is smaller than the divisor. For example, 47 ÷ 6 gives a quotient of 7 and a remainder of 5 because 6 × 7 + 5 = 47.

Worked Example

Suppose a shop has 125 items and wants to pack them into boxes of 12. The calculation gives 125 ÷ 12 = 10 remainder 5. That means 10 complete boxes can be filled and 5 items remain. The same idea applies to batches, inventory, scheduling, and other situations where whole groups matter.

Using It in Everyday Calculations

In Pakistan, a simple remainder calculation can help when dividing stock into equal cartons, distributing a quantity of items among workers, or converting a total number of days into complete weeks with leftover days. The result should be interpreted using the units of the original numbers: if you divide 53 days by 7, the remainder is 4 days.

Important Notes

This calculator is intended for ordinary arithmetic with a non-zero divisor. A negative divisor or negative dividend can produce a signed remainder under programming-language conventions, so interpretation may differ between systems. If you need modular arithmetic for computer science, use the result together with the divisor and the convention required by your application.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a remainder always smaller than the divisor? For standard positive-integer division, yes. Can the remainder be zero? Yes. A zero remainder means the dividend divides evenly. What does a remainder mean in real life? It represents the quantity left after making as many complete equal groups as possible.
