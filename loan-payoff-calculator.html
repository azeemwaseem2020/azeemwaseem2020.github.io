Loan Payoff Calculator — Time, Interest & Extra Payments

Estimate how long a remaining loan balance may take to repay and how much interest may accrue from the rate and monthly payment you enter.

Calculation guide maintained by Abdul Qadir · Calculation methodology

What the payoff estimate includes

Enter the principal balance that remains today, the annual nominal interest rate and the regular payment amount. This calculator models a fixed rate with one payment each month and estimates the payoff schedule under that simplified assumption. It does not fetch your lender's balance, payment due date or settlement quote.

Formula and calculation assumptions

For a positive monthly rate, the theoretical number of payment periods is n = −ln(1 − Pr/M) ÷ ln(1+r), where P is the remaining balance, r is the annual rate divided by 12, and M is the monthly payment. The payment must exceed the interest accrued in a month; otherwise the balance will not amortize. At a zero interest rate, the period estimate is simply the balance divided by the monthly payment. The calculator rounds the displayed duration up to a whole payment month and estimates interest from the monthly balance path.

Worked example

For a remaining balance of 10,000, an 8% annual rate and a monthly payment of 250, the first month's interest under a monthly-rate model is about 66.67, leaving roughly 183.33 of the payment to reduce principal. As the balance declines, the interest portion generally declines too. The last payment can be smaller than the regular payment, so multiplying the full payment by the number of months can overstate the amount actually paid.

What changes the real payoff date?

  • Payment timing: daily interest and the actual payment date can change interest compared with a simple monthly model.
  • Rate changes: a variable-rate loan may not follow one constant-rate estimate.
  • Fees and settlement costs: lender charges or accrued interest may be included in an official payoff quote but not in this estimate.
  • Extra payments: this version does not accept a separate extra-payment amount. To model one accurately, use a lender amortization schedule or recalculate the balance after each extra principal payment.

How to use the result responsibly

Compare the estimated duration and interest with the latest statement or amortization schedule. Check whether the rate is nominal or effective, whether payments are monthly, and whether fees are part of the balance. For a real settlement, request a dated payoff quote from the lender; do not rely on a calculator estimate as a final amount due.

Frequently asked questions

Why must my payment exceed monthly interest?

If the payment only covers interest, the principal does not fall. If it is lower than accrued interest, the balance can grow rather than amortize.

Does this result include lender fees?

No separate fees or settlement charges are added unless they are already included in the balance you enter. Confirm the actual amount with the lender.

Can I use this for a variable-rate loan?

Only as a scenario using one assumed rate. A changing rate or irregular payment schedule needs a schedule that models those changes.

Does this calculator model extra payments?

No. Entering a larger regular monthly payment can show a different scenario, but it is not the same as modelling irregular extra principal payments on their actual dates.
