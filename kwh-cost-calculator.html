kWh cost formula

Electricity cost = energy used (kWh) × tariff per kWh. If you know appliance power instead, energy = watts ÷ 1000 × hours.

Worked example

If an appliance uses 12 kWh and the tariff is 50 per kWh, estimated energy cost is 600 before taxes, fixed charges and other bill components.

How to use

Estimating electricity cost from kWh

Energy cost is commonly estimated as kWh consumed × applicable price per kWh. A full bill can include taxes, fixed charges, adjustments and slab rules beyond this simple calculation.

Worked example

A 300 kWh monthly usage at a hypothetical Rs. 50 per kWh energy rate gives Rs. 15,000 before other bill components.

A practical Pakistan example

For a Pakistani household, compare the calculator estimate with the actual tariff structure on the current bill because unit rates and additional charges can vary.

Important assumptions

Use the applicable tariff and billing period; a simple single-rate estimate may not reproduce the final utility bill.

FAQ

What inputs matter most? Use the values and units required by the stated formula.

Can the result be treated as exact? Only when the inputs, assumptions and method are appropriate.

Why can another source show a different result? It may use different definitions, rates, rounding or assumptions.

Reading an electricity-cost estimate

The core relationship is cost = energy used in kWh × price per kWh. If a device uses 2 kW for 5 hours, it consumes 10 kWh. At PKR 50 per kWh, the energy charge would be PKR 500 before taxes, fixed charges or other bill components. Real utility bills can contain multiple tariff slabs and charges, so a simple rate is an estimate rather than a complete bill forecast.

Pakistan example: when estimating a household appliance, use its actual operating power and realistic hours of use. Refrigerators, pumps and air conditioners cycle rather than running at rated power continuously.
