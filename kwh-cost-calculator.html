kWh cost formula

Electricity cost = energy used (kWh) × tariff per kWh. If you know appliance power instead, energy = watts ÷ 1000 × hours.

Worked example

If an appliance uses 12 kWh and the tariff is 50 per kWh, estimated energy cost is 600 before taxes, fixed charges and other bill components.

Energy charge versus total electricity bill

Electricity energy charges are commonly estimated as consumed energy in kilowatt-hours multiplied by the applicable per-unit tariff. A real bill may also include fixed charges, taxes, duties, fuel or tariff adjustments, meter-related charges, and credits. Tariffs can vary by consumer category, slab, time of use and billing period, so use the rate that actually applies to your connection.

Worked example

If an appliance or household uses 250 kWh and the assumed energy rate is 50 currency units per kWh, the energy charge is 250 × 50 = 12,500 currency units before other bill components. This example is illustrative, not a current tariff quote.

How to use

  1. Enter electricity consumption in kWh.
  2. Enter your tariff per kWh.
  3. Calculate the estimated energy charge.
  4. Compare with your actual utility bill, which may include additional charges.

Estimate appliance electricity use

If you know an appliance's rated power in watts and how long it runs, estimate energy use with kWh = watts × hours ÷ 1,000. For example, a 1,500 W heater running for 2 hours uses about 3 kWh. At a sample tariff of 50 currency units per kWh, the energy charge would be 150 before taxes and other bill items. Actual consumption can differ if the appliance cycles or operates below its rated power.

How to read the estimate

  • Enter energy in kilowatt-hours, not watts or kilowatts.
  • Enter the price for one kWh in the same currency used for your bill.
  • Multiply appliance usage by the number of days only when the entered kWh is a daily estimate.
  • Compare the result with the energy-charge line on your bill, not necessarily the final payable amount.

This tool estimates the variable energy charge only. Fixed charges, taxes, fuel adjustments, duties, subsidies, slab rates and other utility-specific items may change the final bill. For Pakistan electricity bills, use the applicable tariff and bill period rather than assuming one universal per-unit price.
