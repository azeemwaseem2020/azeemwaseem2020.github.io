Fuel Cost Calculator

Estimate how much fuel a trip needs and its cost from distance, vehicle efficiency and fuel price.

How to use the fuel cost calculator

  1. Enter the trip distance.
  2. Enter your vehicle's fuel economy.
  3. Enter the current fuel price per litre.
  4. Calculate litres used and estimated trip cost.

Worked example

For 240 km at 12 km/L and a fuel price of 300 per litre: fuel used = 240 ÷ 12 = 20 L; estimated cost = 20 × 300 = 6,000.

Formula

Fuel used = distance ÷ km/L. Trip fuel cost = fuel used × price per liter. Actual consumption can vary with traffic, load, driving style and vehicle condition.

Planning a realistic trip cost

A fuel-cost estimate is most useful when the fuel economy figure reflects the vehicle and conditions you expect. Manufacturer figures may be measured under controlled conditions, while real driving can vary with traffic, air conditioning, tyre pressure, road gradient, passenger or cargo weight and driving style. For a long trip, testing the vehicle's actual consumption over a known distance can provide a more useful input.

If fuel prices may change during the trip, calculate separate legs using the expected price for each leg rather than treating one price as certain. For round trips, you can also double the distance when the return route and conditions are similar, but do not assume this if the routes or loads differ materially.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the basic formula? Fuel used = distance ÷ efficiency; trip cost = fuel used × fuel price.

Why do units matter? Distance and efficiency must use compatible units such as km and km/L.

Calculation context

Fuel-cost planning connects trip distance, vehicle fuel economy, fuel price and estimated total fuel expense. Use this calculator for a trip-level estimate and keep distance, economy and price units consistent.

Calculation transparency

The calculator performs straightforward distance, efficiency and price arithmetic. It does not estimate traffic, vehicle condition or future fuel-price changes. Use actual fuel receipts or a measured tank-to-tank average when you need a closer record of real consumption.
