Fuel Cost Calculator

Estimate how much fuel a trip needs and its cost from distance, vehicle efficiency and fuel price.

How to use the fuel cost calculator

  1. Enter the trip distance.
  2. Enter your vehicle's fuel economy.
  3. Enter the current fuel price per litre.
  4. Calculate litres used and estimated trip cost.

Worked example

For 240 km at 12 km/L and a fuel price of 300 per litre: fuel used = 240 ÷ 12 = 20 L; estimated cost = 20 × 300 = 6,000.

Formula

Fuel used = distance ÷ km/L. Trip fuel cost = fuel used × price per liter. Actual consumption can vary with traffic, load, driving style and vehicle condition.

Planning a realistic trip cost

A fuel-cost estimate is most useful when the fuel economy figure reflects the vehicle and conditions you expect. Manufacturer figures may be measured under controlled conditions, while real driving can vary with traffic, air conditioning, tyre pressure, road gradient, passenger or cargo weight and driving style. For a long trip, testing the vehicle's actual consumption over a known distance can provide a more useful input.

If fuel prices may change during the trip, calculate separate legs using the expected price for each leg rather than treating one price as certain. For round trips, you can also double the distance when the return route and conditions are similar, but do not assume this if the routes or loads differ materially.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the basic formula? Fuel used = distance ÷ efficiency; trip cost = fuel used × fuel price.

Why do units matter? Distance and efficiency must use compatible units such as km and km/L.

Calculation context

Fuel-cost planning connects trip distance, vehicle fuel economy, fuel price and estimated total fuel expense. Use this calculator for a trip-level estimate and keep distance, economy and price units consistent.

Worked example: estimate a trip fuel budget

Suppose a car travels 240 km and averages 12 km per litre. Estimated fuel use is 240 ÷ 12 = 20 litres. If the price entered is PKR 280 per litre, the estimated fuel cost is 20 × 280 = PKR 5,600. These numbers are illustrative rather than a live fuel-price quote. Enter the price available to you and the fuel economy your vehicle is likely to achieve.

Use the right fuel-economy unit

Kilometres per litre (km/L) and litres per 100 kilometres (L/100 km) describe efficiency in opposite ways: a higher km/L figure means fewer litres are needed per kilometre, while a higher L/100 km figure means more fuel is used. Do not enter an L/100 km figure into a field expecting km/L. If your vehicle's display uses a different unit, convert it first.

Budget for the whole journey

For a return journey, estimate each leg separately when distance, traffic, elevation or vehicle load differs. Fuel cost is only one part of travel expense; tolls, parking, maintenance and meals are not included unless you add them separately. A sensible budget may include a small contingency for detours and congestion, but avoid treating that allowance as a prediction of actual consumption.

Improve the estimate with real data

When possible, record the litres purchased and distance driven over several normal trips, then calculate distance divided by litres used. A longer sample is generally more representative than one short drive. Recalculate if you change vehicle, route, load or driving conditions. This tool estimates fuel cost from the inputs; it does not access live pump prices or vehicle telemetry.

Fuel cost FAQ

Does this include tolls and maintenance?

No. It estimates fuel expense only. Add other trip costs separately to build a complete travel budget.

Why is my actual cost different?

Actual consumption can change with traffic, idling, air conditioning, tyre pressure, road gradient, cargo and driving style. Fuel prices and the route may also differ from the assumptions entered.
