Slope-intercept form

A straight line can be written as y = mx + b, where m is the slope and b is the y-intercept. The slope describes the rate of change, while the intercept is the value of y when x equals zero.

Worked example

For y = 3x + 5, the slope is 3 and the y-intercept is 5. At x = 2, y = 3(2) + 5 = 11. If two points are known, calculate the slope first and then substitute either point to solve for b.

Practical use

This form is useful for trend lines, basic engineering relationships and algebra. Make sure the variables are consistently defined and that the relationship is reasonably linear over the range being modeled.

FAQs

What if the line is vertical? It cannot be represented as y = mx + b with a finite slope. What is b? The y-intercept. Can m be negative? Yes.
