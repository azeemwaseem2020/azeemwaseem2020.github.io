How slope-intercept form works

A non-vertical straight line can be written as y = mx + b, where m is the slope and b is the y-intercept.

For two points (x₁,y₁) and (x₂,y₂), the slope is m = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁). Once m is known, the intercept is b = y₁ − mx₁.

Worked example

For (1,2) and (4,8), the slope is (8 − 2)/(4 − 1) = 6/3 = 2. Substituting (1,2) gives b = 2 − 2(1) = 0, so the equation is y = 2x.

Vertical lines need special handling

If x₁ = x₂, the denominator in the slope formula is zero. The line is vertical and has equation x = x₁; it does not have a finite slope or a slope-intercept form y = mx + b. The calculator reports this case instead of dividing by zero.

Common mistakes

  • Keep the point order consistent in both numerator and denominator.
  • Do not divide by zero when the two x-values are equal.
  • Do not confuse the y-intercept with the y-coordinate of the first point.

FAQ

Can two identical points define a unique line?

No. Infinitely many lines pass through one point, so two identical coordinate pairs do not determine a unique line.

What does a negative slope mean?

A negative slope means y decreases as x increases along the line.

What is the y-intercept?

It is the value of y where the line crosses the y-axis, corresponding to x = 0.
