Wallpaper Calculator — Wall Area & Roll Estimate

Estimate the surface area to cover and a starting roll quantity from your wall measurements. Pattern matching, roll dimensions and the usable coverage stated by the supplier can change the final amount.

Calculate wallpaper needed

For one 4 m × 2.5 m wall, gross area is 10 m². Enter supplier coverage and waste allowance to estimate rolls.

Formula used

Gross wall area = wall width × wall height × number of same-size walls. If you have different wall sizes, calculate each size separately and add their areas.

Net area = gross wall area − combined opening area. The calculator prevents the opening deduction from exceeding the total gross area. It then applies the allowance:

Planning area = net area × (1 + waste percentage ÷ 100).

If usable roll coverage is entered, the basic area-only roll estimate is the planning area divided by coverage per roll, rounded up to a whole roll. This is an approximation, not a strip-layout calculation.

Worked example

One wall measuring 4 m wide by 2.5 m high has a gross area of 4 × 2.5 = 10 m². With no opening deduction and a 15% allowance, the planning area is 10 × 1.15 = 11.5 m². If the manufacturer states 5 m² of usable coverage per roll, the area-only estimate is 11.5 ÷ 5 = 2.3, which rounds up to 3 rolls. Pattern repeat or awkward cuts may require more.

How to use the wallpaper calculator

  1. Measure wall width and finished height, using the same length unit for both.
  2. Enter how many walls share those dimensions. For differently sized walls, calculate each group separately and add the results.
  3. Optionally enter the combined area of large openings if you can reasonably account for offcuts around them.
  4. Choose a waste allowance appropriate to the room and design; patterned wallpaper often needs more careful matching than plain paper.
  5. For a roll estimate, enter the supplier's usable coverage per roll in the same area unit shown by the calculator.
  6. Check the final estimate against the manufacturer's instructions and consider buying rolls from the same batch.

Why roll count is not just wall area divided by roll area

Wallpaper is installed in strips. Ceiling height, roll width and length, pattern repeat, motif alignment, doors, windows, alcoves and trimming determine how many full drops can be cut from a roll. A large area may leave unusable offcuts, so two products with similar printed square footage may provide different practical coverage. Use a supplier's roll calculator when it accounts for pattern repeat and strip layout; treat this calculator's roll number as a first estimate only.

Measurement tips and limitations

  • Measure the finished wall height rather than assuming every room is exactly the same height.
  • For several walls, account for corners and any recesses or chimney breasts that change the surface area.
  • Subtracting every small window or door can understate the amount needed because cuts around openings may not be reusable.
  • Do not mix metres with feet, or square metres with square feet, in a single calculation.
  • Keep the product name, batch/dye lot and spare-roll information in case a repair or future purchase is needed.

This calculator does not design strip layouts, account exactly for pattern repeats, or guarantee a purchase quantity. Check package coverage and supplier guidance before ordering.

Frequently asked questions

How many rolls do I need for a 10 m² wall?

It depends on each roll's usable coverage and the amount of waste. For example, 5 m² per roll and a 15% allowance gives a planning area of 11.5 m², or an area-only estimate of three rolls. Pattern matching can change this result.

Should I subtract doors and windows?

You can enter the combined area of large openings, but small openings may not save a full strip because wallpaper must be cut around their edges. If unsure, compare estimates with and without the deduction or follow supplier advice.

What waste percentage should I use?

There is no single percentage for every room. Plain wallpaper, patterned designs, large pattern repeats, sloped walls and inexperienced cutting can have different waste levels. Use the supplier's guidance for the specific product.

Why round rolls up?

Rolls are purchased as whole units, so a fractional result cannot usually be bought. The calculator rounds up, but that still does not guarantee sufficient material if the strip layout wastes more than the allowance.

Can I calculate several walls?

Yes, if the walls have the same dimensions, enter their count. If dimensions differ, calculate each group separately and add the gross and opening areas consistently.
