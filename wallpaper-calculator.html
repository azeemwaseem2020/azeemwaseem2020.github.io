Estimate wallpaper rolls

Wallpaper planning begins with wall area and the usable coverage of each roll. Measure wall width and height, subtract major openings where appropriate, then account for pattern matching and trimming. The number of rolls is normally rounded up because partial rolls cannot always be reused efficiently.

Worked example

If a wall is 4 m wide and 2.8 m high, its area is 11.2 m². If a roll provides 5 m² of usable coverage after the manufacturer's assumptions, the mathematical minimum is 2.24 rolls, so at least 3 rolls would be needed before pattern and waste considerations.

Practical example

For a Pakistani home or office, check roll width, length and repeat pattern on the label. Large windows and doors can reduce the area, but awkward corners and pattern alignment can increase waste.

FAQs

Why round up? You need enough complete material. Does pattern repeat matter? Yes. Can one roll cover exactly its printed area? Not always after trimming and matching.

Wallpaper Quantity: Measuring a Room

Wallpaper estimates depend on wall area, roll dimensions and pattern repeat. A simple starting point is total wall area divided by the coverage area of one roll. For a rectangular wall, area is width × height. For several walls, calculate each wall separately and add the results before allowing for doors, windows and installation waste.

For example, a wall 4 m wide and 2.8 m high has an area of 11.2 m². If a roll provides 5 m² of usable coverage after considering its dimensions, 11.2 ÷ 5 = 2.24, so at least 3 rolls are needed before pattern matching and waste. Actual roll requirements can be higher when the design has a large repeat.

In Pakistan, room dimensions are often measured in feet while wallpaper may be sold by roll with metric dimensions. Convert all measurements to one unit before calculating. For a 12 ft × 10 ft wall, the area is 120 ft². If buying by square metre, divide by 10.7639 to get about 11.15 m².

Why waste matters

Wallpaper is installed in vertical drops, not as a perfect mathematical sheet. Door and window openings do reduce material needs, but offcuts may not be reusable because of pattern alignment. A practical estimate should therefore include a waste allowance rather than buying exactly the theoretical minimum.

FAQs

Should doors and windows be subtracted? Yes for a more refined estimate, but keep enough material for cuts and pattern matching.

Does pattern repeat matter? Yes. Large repeats can substantially increase waste.

Can I calculate one roll exactly? Use the manufacturer's usable coverage because roll dimensions alone do not always reflect practical coverage.
