Wallpaper calculator: complete guide

This calculator estimates the area of a wall from its width and height. That area is a starting point for wallpaper planning, but it does not directly determine the number of rolls because roll dimensions, pattern repeat, matching, doors, windows and trimming affect usable coverage.

Formula

Wall area = wall width × wall height. Use the same unit for both measurements; the result is in square units. For multiple walls, calculate each wall separately and add the areas.

How to use it

  1. Measure the wall width and height accurately.
  2. Enter both values in the same unit.
  3. Select Calculate to estimate the wall area.
  4. Repeat for additional walls and check the wallpaper roll’s stated coverage.

Worked example

A wall 4 m wide and 2.5 m high has area 4 × 2.5 = 10 m². If wallpaper’s stated usable coverage is 5 m² per roll, a simple area-only estimate is 10 ÷ 5 = 2 rolls. Pattern repeats and cutting waste may mean more than two rolls are needed, so follow the manufacturer’s calculator or guidance.

Doors, windows and pattern matching

Large openings may reduce the covered area, but some installers prefer to keep a waste allowance rather than subtract every opening. Patterned wallpaper often needs extra length to align motifs between strips. Check roll width, roll length, pattern repeat, batch or dye lot and the supplier’s usable-coverage guidance before buying. Do not mix feet with metres. This tool estimates one rectangular surface only; it does not calculate strip layout, pattern repeat or exact roll count.

Frequently asked questions

Why does wallpaper pattern repeat matter?

A repeating design may require each strip to start at a matching point in the pattern, which creates offcuts. A larger repeat can increase waste compared with plain wallpaper. Use the manufacturer’s roll coverage guidance when it accounts for pattern repeat.

Should I subtract doors and windows?

Large openings can reduce the wall area, but small offcuts around openings may not be reusable. For a practical estimate, measure each wall separately and consider how strips will be positioned. Do not automatically assume every deducted square foot saves the same amount of wallpaper.

How can I avoid buying too few rolls?

Check the roll width, length and stated coverage, then allow for pattern matching, trimming and damaged pieces. Keep a record of the batch or lot number so any additional rolls can match the original colour as closely as possible.
