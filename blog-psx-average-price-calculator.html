PSX Average Price Calculator Guide

When you buy the same stock at different prices, a weighted average gives you a clearer view of your combined purchase cost.

Weighted average formula

New average price = (existing shares × existing average price + new shares × new purchase price) ÷ total shares.

Worked example

Suppose you own 1,000 shares at PKR 100 and buy another 500 shares at PKR 80. Existing cost is PKR 100,000 and the new purchase is PKR 40,000. Total cost is PKR 140,000 across 1,500 shares, so the weighted average is PKR 93.33 per share.

Why average price matters

Average cost helps you understand the price level at which the position starts to recover its purchase cost before additional transaction costs. It is not a forecast of the stock's future market price.

Include transaction costs when needed

If brokerage and other charges are material, include them in the total cost for a more complete break-even analysis. Keep the calculation separate from any tax treatment that depends on current rules and your circumstances.

Average price after bonus shares

Bonus shares can change share count without a new cash purchase. A simplified planning calculation can divide the original total cost by the adjusted share count, but actual tax and corporate-action treatment should be verified for the specific announcement.

Common mistakes

  • Taking a simple average of two prices instead of weighting by shares.
  • Forgetting that the quantities may be different.
  • Mixing average purchase cost with the current market price.
  • Ignoring transaction costs when calculating a true break-even.
  • Assuming a lower average cost guarantees a profit.

FAQ

Is average price the same as break-even price?

Not always. Average purchase cost excludes additional costs unless you deliberately include them. A complete break-even calculation may need brokerage, taxes and other applicable charges.

Should I average down a losing stock?

A calculator cannot answer that investment decision. Review the company's fundamentals, risk, valuation and your investment plan before changing a position.

Why weighted average cost is the correct model

When quantities differ, averaging trade prices directly gives each trade equal influence even when the share counts are different. Weighted average cost instead divides total acquisition value by total shares.

Weighted average = Σ(quantity × price) ÷ Σ(quantity)

Worked example

Buy 100 shares at Rs 80 and 300 at Rs 120. Total cost is Rs 44,000 for 400 shares, so the weighted average is Rs 110. If the next purchase is 200 shares at Rs 90, the revised cost is Rs 62,000 for 600 shares, or about Rs 103.33 per share before applicable charges.

Charges change the economic break-even

The weighted purchase price is not automatically the final break-even price. Brokerage, taxes, settlement charges and other transaction costs can alter the amount required to exit without a net loss. Keep trading costs separate so the calculation remains auditable.

Corporate actions require care

Bonus shares, rights issues, splits and other corporate actions can change quantity or cost-basis treatment. Do not blindly apply the simple purchase-price formula to an account statement that includes corporate actions; reconcile with the broker's official records.

FAQ

Should I use a simple average of buy prices?

Not when the quantities differ. Use total purchase value divided by total shares.

Does average price equal break-even?

Not necessarily. Transaction costs and the applicable treatment of other cash flows can move the economic break-even point.
