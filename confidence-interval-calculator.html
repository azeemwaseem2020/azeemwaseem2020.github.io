Confidence interval formula

For the inputs on this page, the margin of error is E = z × s ÷ √n. The interval is sample mean ± E.

Worked example

With mean 50, standard deviation 10, sample size 100 and z = 1.96, E = 1.96 × 10 ÷ 10 = 1.96. The interval is 48.04 to 51.96.

Assumptions

This calculator uses a z-critical-value model. For small samples or unknown population standard deviation, a t-based interval may be more appropriate.

Formula or method

For a mean with known standard error, an interval is estimate ± critical value × standard error; the exact formula depends on the selected method.

Useful note

A confidence interval describes uncertainty in an estimate; it does not mean there is a fixed probability that the true value is inside one already-computed interval.

Before you use the result

The result depends on the inputs, units and assumptions shown on this page. Check those details before comparing the result with another source or applying it to a real-world decision.

What a confidence interval represents

A confidence interval gives a range produced by a statistical procedure for estimating an unknown population parameter. A 95% confidence procedure does not mean there is a 95% probability that a fixed parameter is inside one already-computed interval; the confidence level describes the long-run performance of the procedure under its assumptions.

What changes the interval width?

Larger samples generally reduce standard error and can produce narrower intervals. Greater variability generally widens the interval. The selected confidence level also affects the critical value and therefore the width.

Confidence interval FAQ

Does a narrower interval always mean a better study?

Not by itself. Precision is only one consideration; sampling design, bias, measurement quality and model assumptions also matter.

Can I interpret the interval without checking assumptions?

No. The appropriate formula and interpretation depend on the parameter, data structure, sampling method and assumptions behind the statistical procedure.

Calculation context

A confidence interval estimates a range around a sample statistic using the selected confidence level and statistical assumptions. The interpretation depends on the sampling method, model and interval procedure used.
