Series resistance and circuit behavior

For ideal resistors connected in series, the total resistance equals the sum of the individual resistances. The same current passes through each series resistor in the simple circuit model.

Worked example

Three resistors of 2 Ω, 5 Ω and 8 Ω in series have a total resistance of 15 Ω.

Voltage division

The supply voltage is distributed across series resistors according to their resistance values. This makes series combinations useful in simple voltage-divider designs.

Series-resistance FAQ

Does adding a series resistor increase total resistance?

Yes, for ideal resistors in series, each additional positive resistance increases the total.

Series Resistance Calculator: Add Resistors in One Path

Resistors connected in series carry the same current because there is only one path for charge flow. Their total resistance is the sum of their individual resistances. This differs from a parallel circuit, where current has multiple paths and the equivalent resistance is calculated using reciprocals.

Formula

Rtotal = R₁ + R₂ + … + Rn. All resistance values should be in ohms (Ω), or all should be converted to the same unit before adding. For example, convert kilo-ohms to ohms or keep all values in kilo-ohms consistently. The total series resistance cannot be smaller than any one non-negative resistor in the list.

Worked example

For three resistors of 10 Ω, 20 Ω and 30 Ω connected in series, total resistance is 10 + 20 + 30 = 60 Ω. If a 12 V ideal supply is connected across the chain, Ohm's law predicts current I = V ÷ R = 12 ÷ 60 = 0.2 A. The voltage drops across the resistors would be 2 V, 4 V and 6 V, adding to the 12 V supply in this ideal example.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter resistor values separated by commas.
  2. Use consistent units and remove labels such as “kΩ” unless the tool explicitly supports them.
  3. Check for missing entries, accidental negative values and misplaced decimal separators.
  4. Compare the total with a simple hand sum or the circuit diagram.

Practical electronics notes

Real resistors have tolerances and their resistance can change with temperature and power dissipation. The nominal sum is therefore an estimate of the circuit's total resistance, not a precision measurement. Make sure the physical circuit really is series-connected: a branch or parallel path changes the equivalent resistance. Disconnect power before changing a circuit and follow safe practices when working with electrical equipment.

Frequently asked questions

Does order affect the total?

No. Addition gives the same total regardless of the order of resistors.

Can resistance be negative?

Ordinary passive resistors have non-negative resistance; negative resistance requires a special active-device model.

Is series resistance the same as parallel resistance?

No. Parallel networks use a reciprocal relationship, not a direct sum.

Series Resistance Calculator: Total Resistance and Circuit Checks

For resistors connected in series, the same current flows through each component and total resistance is the sum of the individual resistances: Rtotal = R₁ + R₂ + … + Rn. This assumes a simple series path without branches between the listed components. Parallel branches require a different formula and should not be added as if they were in series.

Worked example

Three resistors of 10 Ω, 20 Ω and 30 Ω in series give Rtotal = 10 + 20 + 30 = 60 Ω. If a 12 V ideal source is connected across that total resistance, Ohm’s law estimates current as I = V ÷ R = 12 ÷ 60 = 0.2 A. Real circuit limits, source resistance and component tolerances can affect measured values.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter each resistance in ohms, separated using the format shown by the tool.
  2. Use positive resistance values for ordinary passive resistors.
  3. Check that no entry was treated as a decimal or thousands separator by mistake.
  4. Compare the total with a circuit diagram to confirm all listed resistors really are in series.

Resistance values in kilohms or megohms should be converted to ohms if the calculator expects ohms. For physical components, consider tolerance and power rating as well as resistance. A correct total resistance does not alone confirm that a circuit is safe or that each resistor can dissipate the expected power.

FAQs

Does series resistance always increase?

For ordinary positive resistors, adding another series resistor increases total resistance.

Can I use this for parallel resistors?

No. Parallel resistance uses a reciprocal relationship and needs a different calculation.

Why does a measured total differ slightly?

Component tolerance, lead resistance, temperature and meter accuracy can create small differences.
