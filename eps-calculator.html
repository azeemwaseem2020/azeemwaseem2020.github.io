EPS Calculator — Earnings Per Share

Estimate basic earnings per share from net income and shares using a simple, transparent calculation.

EPS formula

For this simplified calculator, EPS = Net income ÷ Shares. Formal basic EPS may use weighted-average shares and adjustments required by the applicable accounting framework.

Worked examples

If net income is $100,000 and 10,000 shares are used, EPS = $10.00 per share. If earnings are $2 million and 500,000 shares are used, EPS = $4.00 per share.

What does EPS mean?

Earnings per share expresses earnings on a per-share basis. It can be compared across periods and alongside measures such as P/E, but EPS alone does not describe a company's complete financial position.

Basic vs diluted EPS

This tool gives a simplified basic-EPS estimate. Reported basic EPS can use weighted-average common shares and preferred-dividend adjustments, while diluted EPS can include potentially dilutive securities.

EPS FAQ

What is EPS?

EPS means earnings per share and expresses earnings on a per-share basis.

Is higher EPS automatically better?

Not by itself. EPS should be considered with profitability, cash flow, share-count changes, valuation, and the reporting period.

Is this diluted EPS?

No. The calculator does not model potentially dilutive securities.

Calculation context

Earnings per share divides applicable earnings by the relevant share count under the selected assumptions. Reported EPS can involve accounting adjustments, diluted shares and other reporting rules.

Use the right earnings and share figures

EPS is meaningful only when the earnings period and share count refer to the same reporting period. For a quick estimate, this calculator divides the net income entered by the share count entered. Published basic EPS may instead use weighted-average ordinary shares outstanding during the period and subtract preferred dividends where required by the accounting framework.

Why weighted-average shares matter

If a company issues or buys back shares during a reporting period, the number of shares changes over time. Using only the ending share count can produce a different result from weighted-average shares. Diluted EPS may be lower when options, convertible securities or other potential ordinary shares are included under applicable accounting rules.

Compare EPS across periods carefully

Higher EPS does not automatically mean a company is a better investment. Earnings can be affected by one-off gains, changes in accounting estimates, share buybacks and the business cycle. Compare the reporting period, accounting basis, continuing operations and share-count assumptions before interpreting a change in EPS.

EPS example

If net income available to ordinary shareholders is PKR 12 million and the relevant weighted-average ordinary share count is 3 million, basic EPS is PKR 4 per share. The currency of EPS follows the currency of the earnings input; the share count is a number of shares, not a currency amount.

Important limitation

This tool is an educational estimate and does not reproduce a full financial-statement calculation. For listed-company analysis, use the reported financial statements and notes, and do not treat EPS alone as a buy or sell recommendation.
