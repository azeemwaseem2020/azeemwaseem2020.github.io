SIP Calculator

Estimate the future value of regular monthly investments using an assumed annual return and investment period.

Calculation method

The estimate compounds monthly using the monthly rate derived from the annual percentage assumption. It shows total contributions, estimated gains and future value.

Important investment note

This is a mathematical projection, not a guaranteed return. Actual investment performance, taxes, fees, inflation and market conditions can materially change results.

How to read the SIP result

The result separates the amount you contribute from the estimated growth. For example, a monthly contribution of Rs 10,000 for 10 years means 120 monthly contributions and Rs 1,200,000 of total contributions before any investment growth. The projected future value is higher only because the calculation assumes a positive return and monthly compounding.

What the inputs mean

Monthly investment is the amount added each month. Expected annual return is an assumption used to model growth, not a promise from a fund or market. Investment period determines how many monthly contributions are included.

Why actual returns can differ

Market returns are not normally a fixed monthly rate. Fund expenses, taxes, transaction costs, inflation, skipped contributions and changes in market performance can all make the actual outcome different from this projection. Compare scenarios rather than treating one result as a guaranteed target.

Useful comparison

Try the same contribution and period with several return assumptions. This shows how sensitive a long-term projection is to the assumed growth rate and helps prevent overconfidence in a single forecast.

SIP Calculator: Monthly Contributions and Estimated Growth

A systematic investment plan (SIP) means investing a set amount at regular intervals, commonly each month. This calculator estimates the future value of a stream of monthly contributions using an assumed return. It is a mathematical scenario, not a guarantee that a fund, stock or other investment will deliver that return.

Formula used

For end-of-month deposits, future value = P × (((1 + i)n − 1) ÷ i), where P is the monthly contribution, i is the assumed monthly return, and n is the total number of monthly deposits. The calculator approximates the monthly rate by dividing the entered annual rate by 12. If the monthly rate is zero, the future value equals monthly contribution × number of months.

Worked example

If you invest PKR 10,000 per month for 10 years, your own contributions total PKR 1,200,000. With a hypothetical 12% nominal annual return and monthly compounding, the estimated future value is higher because earlier contributions have more time to grow. Actual results can be lower or negative depending on market performance, timing, costs and the investment product.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the amount you can consistently invest each month.
  2. Choose a time period that matches your goal.
  3. Test several return assumptions, including a conservative scenario.
  4. Compare estimated future value with total contributions to understand the modeled gain.

What the estimate leaves out

This simplified model assumes a steady return and regular contributions. It does not model market volatility, skipped deposits, step-up contributions, fund management fees, taxes, inflation or withdrawal charges. If you are investing in Pakistan, confirm product documentation, fees, eligibility and regulatory status with the relevant provider and regulator. Avoid choosing a return just because it produces the target balance you hope to reach.

Frequently asked questions

Is SIP the same as a guaranteed deposit?

No. Investment risk depends on the underlying product; returns are not assured by this calculator.

Does the estimate account for inflation?

No. A future nominal balance may buy less than the same amount today.

Can I change my contribution later?

This basic estimate assumes a fixed monthly contribution. Changing deposits requires a step-up or cash-flow model.

SIP Calculator: Monthly Contributions and Compounding

A systematic investment plan (SIP) usually means investing a fixed amount at regular intervals. A calculator estimates how repeated contributions may grow under an assumed return, but actual investments do not earn a smooth, guaranteed rate. Market prices fluctuate, contributions may buy different numbers of units each month, and fees or taxes can reduce the final value.

What the estimate means

The result depends on the contribution amount, contribution frequency, assumed annual return and investment duration. A common planning model converts an annual rate into a monthly rate and compounds each contribution for the time it remains invested. The timing convention—beginning or end of each period—can change the result slightly.

Worked planning example

If you contribute PKR 5,000 monthly for 10 years, your contributions alone total PKR 600,000, before any growth. A projected future value above or below that amount depends on the return assumption and the calculator's compounding convention. Treat the projection as a scenario, not as a promise of profit.

How to use it responsibly

  1. Enter an amount you can realistically invest every period.
  2. Choose a duration aligned with your goal.
  3. Test several return assumptions, including a lower-return scenario.
  4. Compare the projected value with total contributions and account for fees, taxes and inflation separately.

Investment products differ in risk, liquidity and regulation. Do not choose a product solely because one forecast is larger; review the underlying investment, costs and your ability to tolerate losses.

FAQs

Is the return guaranteed?

No. It is an assumption used for illustration, and actual performance can be higher or lower.

Does a SIP eliminate market risk?

No. Regular investing changes contribution timing but does not remove investment risk.

Does the result include inflation?

Unless explicitly stated by the calculator, treat the displayed amount as nominal and adjust for inflation separately.
