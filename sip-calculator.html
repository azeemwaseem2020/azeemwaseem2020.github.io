Understanding a systematic investment plan projection

A SIP-style calculator models regular contributions into an investment over time. The result depends heavily on the assumed return and contribution frequency. Regular investing can smooth the timing of contributions, but it does not remove market risk or guarantee a profit.

Formula

For a regular periodic contribution P, periodic rate r and n periods, the future value of an ordinary annuity can be expressed as FV = P × [(1+r)^n − 1] ÷ r, with a separate starting balance if applicable.

Worked example

If Rs 10,000 is contributed each month, the final projection depends on the monthly rate and number of months. Compare the projection with a zero-return baseline: 120 contributions over ten years would total Rs 1.2 million before any investment growth.

Pakistan-specific example

A Pakistani investor using a mutual-fund or other investment plan should check the product's fees, risk level, tax treatment and contribution rules. Use the calculator to explore scenarios rather than treating the projected value as guaranteed.

Common questions

Is SIP a guaranteed return? No.

Does the calculator know market returns? No. You supply the assumption.

Can contributions change? Use a different scenario when deposits increase or decrease.

Should inflation be considered? Yes when evaluating future purchasing power.

SIP planning and assumptions

A systematic investment plan models recurring contributions rather than a single lump sum. A common future-value model assumes a constant periodic return and regular contributions, with the exact formula depending on whether deposits occur at the beginning or end of each period.

For example, investing PKR 10,000 monthly for 10 years at a modeled 10% annual return can produce a substantially larger ending value than the total PKR 1.2 million contributed, but the actual investment outcome can be higher or lower because market returns are not guaranteed. For Pakistan investments, consider fees, taxes, inflation and the specific product's return structure.
