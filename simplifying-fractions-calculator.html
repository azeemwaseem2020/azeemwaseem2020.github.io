How to simplify a fraction

A fraction is in simplest form when the numerator and denominator have no common factor greater than 1. Divide both by their greatest common factor (GCF). The value of the fraction stays unchanged because the same factor is removed from both parts.

Worked example

For 18/24, the GCF is 6. Dividing numerator and denominator by 6 gives 3/4. The decimal value is unchanged because 18 ÷ 24 and 3 ÷ 4 are equivalent.

Practical use

Simplifying fractions is useful in school mathematics, recipes, ratios and engineering calculations. Keep the sign with the numerator or denominator consistently; the overall fraction should have only one negative sign.

FAQs

Can a fraction be simplified if the numerator is prime? Yes, if it shares a factor with the denominator. What about zero? 0 divided by a nonzero denominator simplifies to 0. Can the denominator be zero? No; division by zero is undefined.

Simplifying Fractions: Method and Example

To simplify a fraction, divide the numerator and denominator by their greatest common factor (GCF). The fraction 18/24, for example, has a GCF of 6, so 18 ÷ 6 over 24 ÷ 6 becomes 3/4. The value has not changed; only the representation has been reduced to lowest terms.

A reliable manual method is to factor both numbers or use the Euclidean algorithm to find the GCF. If the numerator and denominator share no factor greater than 1, the fraction is already simplified. Keep the sign with the numerator or move it to the front of the fraction so the final form remains consistent.

For a practical example, imagine a recipe uses 24/36 cup of an ingredient. The GCF of 24 and 36 is 12, so the reduced quantity is 2/3 cup. In school or engineering calculations, simplifying fractions can make later multiplication, division and comparison easier while preserving the exact value.

Mixed numbers and improper fractions

An improper fraction such as 11/4 can first be simplified if possible and then converted to a mixed number: 11/4 = 2 3/4. A mixed number can be converted back by multiplying the whole number by the denominator and adding the numerator. Always keep the denominator unchanged during that conversion.

FAQs

Can a fraction be simplified by dividing only the numerator? No. Both numerator and denominator must be divided by the same non-zero factor.

What is lowest form? It is the form in which numerator and denominator have no common factor greater than 1.

Does simplifying change the decimal value? No. Equivalent fractions represent the same number.
