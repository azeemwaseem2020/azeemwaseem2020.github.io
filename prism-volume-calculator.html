Prism volume: complete guide

A prism is a three-dimensional solid with two matching, parallel bases joined by side faces. The bases may be triangles, rectangles or other polygons. This calculator uses the area of one base and the perpendicular distance between the bases to calculate volume.

Formula

Volume = base area × perpendicular height (V = B × h). If base area is measured in cm² and height in cm, volume is in cm³. Keep the units compatible before multiplying.

How to use the calculator

  1. Calculate the area of the prism’s base using the correct shape formula.
  2. Enter that base area in the base-area field.
  3. Enter the perpendicular height—the distance between the two bases—in the height field.
  4. Select Calculate and read the volume, then label it with cubic units.

Worked example

If a triangular base has area 12 cm² and the prism is 8 cm high, volume = 12 × 8 = 96 cm³. The base area is not the triangle’s perimeter, and the prism height is not necessarily the slanted length of a side face.

Applications and common mistakes

Prism volume can estimate the space inside a solid geometric prism or the amount of material in an ideal prism-shaped object. For an irregular cross-section, find the actual base area first. Do not mix cm² with m; convert units first. Real containers may have wall thickness, rounded corners or openings, so their usable capacity can differ from this ideal geometric result. This calculator assumes non-negative inputs and a constant cross-section along the prism.
