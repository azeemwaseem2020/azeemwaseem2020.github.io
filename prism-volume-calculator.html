Volume of a prism

The volume of a prism is base area × perpendicular length. The base can be a rectangle, triangle or another polygon. First calculate the area of that base using the appropriate shape formula, then multiply by the prism length.

Worked example

A rectangular prism 4 m long, 3 m wide and 2 m high has a base area of 4 × 3 = 12 m². Multiplying by 2 m gives a volume of 24 m³.

Practical example

This approach can estimate storage capacity, concrete formwork volume or packaging dimensions. Keep all dimensions in one unit and report the result in cubic units.

Common mistake

Do not multiply by a slanted edge when the prism length should be perpendicular to the base. For irregular bases, calculate the correct base area first.

FAQs

What if the base is triangular? Use triangle area first. What unit is volume? Cubic units. Can a cube be a prism? Yes; a cube is a special rectangular prism.

Prism volume

The volume of a prism is base area × prism length. The base may be triangular, rectangular or another constant cross-section. Calculate the correct base area first, then multiply by the perpendicular length.

Worked example

A triangular prism with base area 12 cm² and length 20 cm has volume 240 cm³. For a rectangular prism, the base area is simply length × width.

Practical use

Prism volume is useful for packaging, tanks, construction sections and geometry. Keep all dimensions in compatible units and remember that volume uses cubic units.

FAQs

Must a prism be rectangular? No. What is the base? The repeated cross-section. Can irregular solids use this formula? Only when the solid has a suitable constant cross-section.

Prism Volume: Formula and Example

The volume of any prism is V = B × h, where B is the area of the base and h is the perpendicular distance between the two parallel bases. For a rectangular prism this becomes length × width × height. The result is cubic units.

For example, a triangular prism with a triangular base of 6 cm and perpendicular height 4 cm has base area ½ × 6 × 4 = 12 cm². If the prism length is 10 cm, its volume is 12 × 10 = 120 cm³.

In construction or manufacturing, the same method can estimate the material volume of a uniform extrusion. Keep the base-area dimensions and prism length in compatible units. If the object has tapered, curved or changing cross-sections, the simple prism formula may not apply.

Unit conversion

One cubic metre equals 1,000 litres. One litre equals 1,000 cubic centimetres. Convert only after obtaining the volume so the unit relationship remains clear.

FAQs

Does a prism need a rectangular base? No. Any consistent polygonal base works.

What is the height? It is the perpendicular separation between the two parallel bases.

Can this calculate an irregular solid? Only if its cross-section is uniform and matches the prism definition.
