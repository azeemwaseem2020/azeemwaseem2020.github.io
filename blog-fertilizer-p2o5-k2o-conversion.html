P₂O₅ to P and K₂O to K: Fertilizer Conversion Guide

Fertilizer labels and technical recommendations can use different nutrient expressions. Identify the basis first, then convert only when the target and product analysis are not already expressed the same way.

P₂O₅ to elemental P

P ≈ P₂O₅ × 0.44

For example, 100 kg P₂O₅ corresponds to about 44 kg elemental P. The factor is a conventional stoichiometric conversion used for fertilizer nutrient expression.

Elemental P to P₂O₅

P₂O₅ ≈ P ÷ 0.44

For example, 44 kg elemental P corresponds to approximately 100 kg P₂O₅.

K₂O to elemental K

K ≈ K₂O × 0.83

For example, 100 kg K₂O corresponds to about 83 kg elemental K.

Elemental K to K₂O

K₂O ≈ K ÷ 0.83

Use the reverse conversion when a recommendation is expressed as elemental potassium but the fertilizer label uses K₂O.

When you should not convert

If both the recommendation and product label use P₂O₅, compare them directly. Likewise, if both use K₂O, no conversion is needed. Unnecessary conversions create extra opportunities for rounding or basis errors.

Worked fertilizer example

A fertilizer contains 46% P₂O₅. If a target is stated as 20 kg elemental P/ha, first convert the target: 20 ÷ 0.44 ≈ 45.45 kg P₂O₅/ha. The corresponding product arithmetic is 45.45 ÷ 0.46 ≈ 98.8 kg product/ha. This is a unit-conversion example, not a crop recommendation.

The quickest sanity check

After converting, reverse the calculation. If 45.45 kg P₂O₅ represents a target of 20 kg P, multiply 45.45 by 0.44 and you get about 20 kg P. A reverse check takes seconds and catches many basis mistakes.

Keep the label basis intact

For fertilizer work, the label is the starting point. If the bag says 46% P₂O₅, enter 46% P₂O₅. Do not silently reinterpret that number as elemental phosphorus. Convert the target only when the two sides of the calculation use different bases.

Common mistakes

  • Applying the 0.44 factor twice.
  • Using 0.83 when the target is already K₂O.
  • Mixing nutrient bases inside a blend calculation.
  • Confusing nutrient percentage with product quantity.
  • Rounding before the final calculation when more precision is useful.

FAQ

Is P₂O₅ the same as elemental phosphorus?

No. P₂O₅ is a conventional fertilizer-label expression, while elemental P is a different mass basis.

Is K₂O the same as elemental potassium?

No. K₂O is a conventional fertilizer-label expression, while elemental K is a different mass basis.

Do I need to convert every fertilizer calculation?

No. Convert only when the target and product analysis use different nutrient bases.
