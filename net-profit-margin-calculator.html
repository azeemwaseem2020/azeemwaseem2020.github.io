Net profit margin: complete guide

Net profit margin shows what share of revenue remains as net profit after the business accounts for its expenses under its accounting method. It is used to review profitability over time and compare businesses cautiously. Different industries have different cost structures, and accounting policies or one-off events can affect comparisons.

Formula

Net profit margin (%) = (net profit ÷ revenue) × 100. Revenue must be greater than zero for the calculation to be defined. Net profit can be negative, in which case the margin is negative and indicates a loss for the period.

How to use this calculator

  1. Enter revenue for the period.
  2. Enter net profit or loss for the same period.
  3. Select Calculate and read the percentage.
  4. Make sure both figures use the same currency, period and accounting basis.

Worked example

A business records revenue of 50,000 and net profit of 5,000. Net profit margin = 5,000 ÷ 50,000 × 100 = 10%. That means 10 units of net profit remain for each 100 units of revenue, according to the figures supplied. If net profit is −2,000 on the same revenue, the margin is −4%, reflecting a loss.

Interpretation and common mistakes

Do not divide revenue by profit; the formula is net profit divided by revenue. Do not confuse net profit margin with gross margin, which excludes some operating and other expenses, or with markup, which measures profit relative to cost. Revenue and net profit should cover the same period and accounting scope. A one-time gain can lift a period’s margin, while expansion costs or seasonal changes can lower it. This calculator performs the ratio only and does not validate financial statements, define accounting treatment or replace advice from an accountant.

Net profit margin versus other measures

Net profit margin is net profit ÷ revenue × 100. Gross profit margin uses gross profit, while markup compares profit with cost. These percentages answer different questions and should not be substituted for one another.

Worked business example

If monthly revenue is PKR 500,000 and net profit after recorded expenses is PKR 40,000, net profit margin is 40,000 ÷ 500,000 × 100 = 8%. That means PKR 8 of net profit remains for every PKR 100 of revenue in the period, based on the supplied accounts. If net profit is negative, the margin is negative and indicates a loss.

Make comparisons meaningful

  • Use revenue and net profit from the same reporting period.
  • Apply the same accounting definitions when comparing periods or businesses.
  • Separate one-off gains and unusual costs when interpreting trends.
  • Compare businesses with similar models cautiously because industries have different cost structures.

This calculator checks the ratio only; it does not audit accounts, verify tax treatment or assess whether a business is financially healthy.
