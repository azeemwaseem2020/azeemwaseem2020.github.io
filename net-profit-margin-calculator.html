Net profit margin formula

Net profit margin measures how much of revenue remains as profit after the costs included in the chosen net-income definition. The formula is net profit margin = net profit ÷ revenue × 100. Unlike gross margin, it can reflect operating expenses, financing costs and taxes when those items are included in net profit.

Worked example

If annual revenue is PKR 10 million and net profit is PKR 800,000, the net profit margin is 800,000 ÷ 10,000,000 × 100 = 8%. That means PKR 8 of net profit is generated for each PKR 100 of revenue under the stated accounting definition.

Business example

A Pakistani retailer or manufacturer can track net margin across months, but comparisons should use consistent accounting treatment. Inflation, exchange rates, financing costs and tax changes can affect the result even when sales volume is similar.

Net Profit Margin: Formula and Example

Net profit margin measures how much net profit remains from revenue. The formula is Net Profit Margin = (Net Profit ÷ Revenue) × 100. If a business earns Rs 5,000,000 in revenue and retains Rs 500,000 after all relevant expenses, the net profit margin is 10%.

Net profit is generally calculated after operating costs, interest, taxes and other applicable expenses, depending on the accounting definition used. That makes net margin different from gross margin, which focuses on revenue minus cost of goods sold. When comparing businesses, use consistent accounting periods and definitions.

For a Pakistan small-business example, suppose annual sales are Rs 12 million and net profit is Rs 1.2 million. Net margin is 10%. If sales rise to Rs 15 million but profit rises only to Rs 1.35 million, the margin falls to 9%. Revenue growth therefore does not automatically mean improved profitability.

Interpreting changes

Margins can change because of pricing, input costs, wages, rent, financing costs, taxes or product mix. A one-time expense can also distort a single year's result. For useful analysis, compare several periods and note unusual items.

FAQs

Is net margin the same as markup? No. Margin is profit relative to selling revenue; markup is profit relative to cost.

Can net margin be negative? Yes. A net loss produces a negative margin.

Is a higher margin always better? It is a financial metric, but interpretation depends on industry, growth and accounting context.
