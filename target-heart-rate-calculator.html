Target heart rate: complete guide

Target heart rate is an estimated beats-per-minute (bpm) range used to describe exercise intensity. This calculator uses the familiar 220-minus-age method to estimate maximum heart rate, then calculates moderate and vigorous ranges. It is a general planning aid, not a measurement of your actual maximum or a medical assessment.

Formulas

Estimated maximum heart rate = 220 − age.
Moderate range = 50%–70% of estimated maximum.
Vigorous range = 70%–85% of estimated maximum.

For age 30, the estimate is 220 − 30 = 190 bpm. The moderate range is about 95–133 bpm and vigorous range about 133–162 bpm after rounding. These are broad estimates; individual fitness, medicines, health conditions, heat and exercise type can change the response.

How to use it

  1. Enter age in years.
  2. Select Calculate Heart Rate.
  3. Review the estimated maximum and intensity ranges.
  4. Use the result as a guide, not a compulsory target; build intensity gradually and pay attention to how you feel.

Interpretation and limitations

BPM means beats per minute. A watch or pulse check measures current heart rate; this calculator estimates ranges from age alone. Maximum heart rate varies considerably among people of the same age, and the 220-minus-age equation can overestimate or underestimate an individual’s maximum. It is not a personalised exercise prescription.

Safety guidance

Some medicines, including beta blockers, can change heart-rate response. If you have a cardiovascular condition, are pregnant, have symptoms or have been advised to limit exercise, ask a qualified healthcare professional for individual guidance. Stop exercise and seek appropriate urgent care for concerning symptoms such as chest pain, fainting or severe unusual breathlessness. This educational calculator does not diagnose or treat a condition.

Frequently asked questions

Is the target heart-rate range exact?

No. It is a population-level estimate based on age, not a direct measurement of your personal maximum. Fitness, genetics, medication, temperature and health conditions can all affect the heart rate reached during exercise.

What if my measured heart rate is outside the range?

Do not treat the range as a pass-or-fail test. Consider exercise intensity, perceived effort and any guidance from your healthcare professional. A wearable monitor can also be inaccurate, particularly during movement.

Can this calculator tell me whether exercise is safe?

No. It does not screen for disease or replace medical advice. If you have symptoms, a known heart condition or instructions to restrict activity, get individual advice before changing your exercise routine. Stop and seek urgent help for severe or concerning symptoms.

Using heart-rate zones during exercise

Heart rate is one way to estimate exercise intensity, but it is only one signal. Breathing, perceived effort, fitness level, weather, hydration and the type of activity also matter. The same pace can produce different heart rates on different days, and wrist-based monitors can lag or misread during movement.

A sensible approach

  • Use the estimated range as a broad reference rather than a strict pass-or-fail target.
  • Warm up gradually and increase duration or intensity progressively.
  • Compare the reading with how hard the activity feels and whether you can speak comfortably.
  • Follow individual advice if you take medicine that affects heart rate or have a health condition.

The 220-minus-age equation is a rough population estimate and can be substantially different from an individual’s measured maximum.
