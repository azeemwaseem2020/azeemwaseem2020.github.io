Target heart rate: complete guide

Target heart rate is an estimated beats-per-minute (bpm) range used to describe exercise intensity. This calculator uses the familiar 220-minus-age method to estimate maximum heart rate, then calculates moderate and vigorous ranges. It is a general planning aid, not a measurement of your actual maximum or a medical assessment.

Formulas

Estimated maximum heart rate = 220 − age.
Moderate range = 50%–70% of estimated maximum.
Vigorous range = 70%–85% of estimated maximum.

For age 30, the estimate is 220 − 30 = 190 bpm. The moderate range is about 95–133 bpm and vigorous range about 133–162 bpm after rounding. These are broad estimates; individual fitness, medicines, health conditions, heat and exercise type can change the response.

How to use it

  1. Enter age in years.
  2. Select Calculate Heart Rate.
  3. Review the estimated maximum and intensity ranges.
  4. Use the result as a guide, not a compulsory target; build intensity gradually and pay attention to how you feel.

Interpretation and limitations

BPM means beats per minute. A watch or pulse check measures current heart rate; this calculator estimates ranges from age alone. Maximum heart rate varies considerably among people of the same age, and the 220-minus-age equation can overestimate or underestimate an individual’s maximum. It is not a personalised exercise prescription.

Safety guidance

Some medicines, including beta blockers, can change heart-rate response. If you have a cardiovascular condition, are pregnant, have symptoms or have been advised to limit exercise, ask a qualified healthcare professional for individual guidance. Stop exercise and seek appropriate urgent care for concerning symptoms such as chest pain, fainting or severe unusual breathlessness. This educational calculator does not diagnose or treat a condition.
