Target heart-rate estimates

Target heart-rate calculators estimate an exercise intensity range from age and sometimes resting heart rate. A common maximum-heart-rate approximation is 220 − age, while the Karvonen-style method uses heart-rate reserve: target = resting HR + intensity × (max HR − resting HR). Different formulas produce different estimates.

Worked example

For age 40, the simple maximum estimate is 180 beats per minute. At 60% intensity, that gives about 108 bpm using the simple percentage method. A heart-rate-reserve method would also use resting heart rate and can produce a different target.

Safety context

These are planning estimates, not medical limits. Medicines, heart conditions, fitness level and individual response can affect heart rate. People with medical concerns should follow guidance from a qualified healthcare professional.

Target Heart Rate: Estimation and Exercise Zones

Target-heart-rate methods estimate an exercise range from age and resting or maximum heart rate. A simple maximum-heart-rate estimate is 220 − age. A training zone can then be estimated as a percentage of maximum heart rate. For example, for age 40, the simple estimate gives 180 beats per minute, and 70% corresponds to about 126 bpm.

Another method, the Karvonen or heart-rate-reserve method, uses resting heart rate: Target HR = Resting HR + intensity × (Max HR − Resting HR). If a 40-year-old has a resting rate of 70 bpm and uses 70% intensity, the result is 70 + 0.70 × (180 − 70) = 147 bpm. Different formulas therefore produce different estimates.

These calculations are planning tools, not medical diagnoses. Fitness level, medication, heat, dehydration and individual physiology can affect heart rate. In Pakistan's hot climate, outdoor exercise can raise cardiovascular strain and perceived effort, so hydration and environmental conditions matter.

Exercise safety

People with known heart conditions or symptoms such as chest pain, fainting or unusual shortness of breath should seek professional guidance rather than relying on a calculator. A wearable device can also have measurement error, especially during movement.

FAQs

Is 220 − age exact? No. It is a population-level estimate.

Why use resting heart rate? It personalizes the heart-rate-reserve calculation.

Can I exercise above the calculated zone? The calculator does not determine what intensity is safe for an individual.
