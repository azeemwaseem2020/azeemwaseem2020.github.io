Quick guide: how to use this calculator

Enter the cash inflows and outflows for the period, then compare the resulting net cash flow with the individual components.

Formula or method

Net cash flow = total cash inflows − total cash outflows.

Useful note

Positive cash flow does not necessarily mean profit; cash timing and non-cash accounting items can produce different results.

Before you use the result

The result depends on the inputs and assumptions shown on this page. Keep units consistent and verify important real-world figures against the relevant source or document.

How to read a cash-flow calculation

Cash flow tracks money moving into and out of a business or project during a period. Positive cash flow means inflows exceed outflows for the period; negative cash flow means the opposite.

Cash flow is not the same as profit

A business can report accounting profit while cash is tied up in receivables, inventory or other assets. Likewise, financing or asset sales can increase cash without representing operating profit.

Worked example

If cash inflows during a month total $25,000 and cash outflows total $19,000, net cash flow for that period is $6,000. The interpretation depends on whether those flows are operating, investing or financing cash flows.

Cash flow FAQ

Why include a starting cash balance?

Starting cash shows how much liquidity is available before the period's net movement is added.

Can positive cash flow mean a business is still struggling?

Yes. A one-time loan, asset sale or delayed payment can create positive cash flow without indicating sustainable operating performance.
