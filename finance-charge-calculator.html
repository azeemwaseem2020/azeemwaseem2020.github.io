Finance charge formula

For a simple daily-rate estimate, Finance charge = Balance × (Annual rate ÷ 100) × Days ÷ 365. The 365-day basis is an assumption for this calculator.

Worked example

For a balance of 10,000 at 12% annually for 30 days: 10,000 × 0.12 × 30 ÷ 365 = approximately 98.63.

Why actual charges can differ

Credit cards, lenders, and other financial products may use average daily balances, different day-count conventions, daily compounding, promotional rates, transaction dates, minimum finance charges, fees, or contract-specific rules. Use the calculator as an estimate, not as a statement-level calculation unless its assumptions match your agreement.

Common uses

Use it for quick finance-charge estimates, interest planning, comparing simple borrowing costs, and checking a basic daily-rate calculation.

Finance charge FAQ

Is a finance charge the same as interest?

Not always. Interest is one type of finance charge; some products can include other charges or fees.

Why use 365 days?

This page uses a 365-day basis for a simple estimate. Your financial product may use another convention.

Can I calculate a credit-card statement exactly?

Only if the calculator's assumptions match the issuer's method, balance calculation, rates, and billing terms.

Finance charge calculation

A finance charge is the cost of borrowing or using credit. Depending on the product, it can include interest and certain fees. A simple percentage-based estimate is often calculated from the financed balance and applicable rate, but actual credit agreements can use daily balances, compounding, minimum payments or fixed fees.

Worked example

If a PKR 100,000 balance is subject to a simple 12% annual charge for one year, the modeled charge is PKR 12,000. A real credit product may produce a different amount if the balance changes during the period or if fees are added.

Pakistan context

When comparing financing, read the agreement for the annual rate, processing charges, late fees and repayment method. For Islamic finance, the contractual structure should be analyzed on its own terms rather than assuming a conventional interest formula.

FAQs

Is finance charge the same as interest? Not always; it can include other borrowing costs. Does a lower monthly payment mean lower cost? Not necessarily. Should fees be ignored? No, if the goal is total borrowing cost.
