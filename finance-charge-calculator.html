Finance charge formula

For a simple daily-rate estimate, Finance charge = Balance × (Annual rate ÷ 100) × Days ÷ 365. The 365-day basis is an assumption for this calculator.

Worked example

For a balance of 10,000 at 12% annually for 30 days: 10,000 × 0.12 × 30 ÷ 365 = approximately 98.63.

Why actual charges can differ

Credit cards, lenders, and other financial products may use average daily balances, different day-count conventions, daily compounding, promotional rates, transaction dates, minimum finance charges, fees, or contract-specific rules. Use the calculator as an estimate, not as a statement-level calculation unless its assumptions match your agreement.

Common uses

Use it for quick finance-charge estimates, interest planning, comparing simple borrowing costs, and checking a basic daily-rate calculation.

Finance charge FAQ

Is a finance charge the same as interest?

Not always. Interest is one type of finance charge; some products can include other charges or fees.

Why use 365 days?

This page uses a 365-day basis for a simple estimate. Your financial product may use another convention.

Can I calculate a credit-card statement exactly?

Only if the calculator's assumptions match the issuer's method, balance calculation, rates, and billing terms.

Understanding finance charges

A finance charge represents the cost of borrowing or using credit beyond the principal amount. Depending on the product, it may include interest, fees or other borrowing costs.

Why the charge can differ from simple interest

Actual credit costs can depend on compounding, payment timing, fees and the outstanding balance. A simple percentage applied to the original amount may therefore differ from the charge shown by a lender.

Compare the assumptions

When comparing credit offers, use the same principal, term, payment frequency and fee assumptions. Check the lender's disclosed annual rate and total repayment amount rather than relying on one calculated percentage.

Finance-charge FAQ

Is a finance charge the same as an interest rate?

No. An interest rate is a rate used to calculate interest, while a finance charge can represent the broader cost charged for credit.

Worked example: a simple finance-charge estimate

Suppose PKR 50,000 is financed for 90 days at a simple annual rate of 18%, using a 365-day year and no additional fees. A simple-interest estimate is principal × annual rate × days ÷ 365: 50,000 × 0.18 × 90 ÷ 365 ≈ PKR 2,219. The estimated amount before other charges would therefore be about PKR 52,219 if the principal remains outstanding for the full period. This example illustrates the arithmetic only; it is not a quote for any bank, card or lender.

Why the actual statement may differ

A lender may calculate charges from a daily or average daily balance, apply different rates to purchases and cash advances, compound unpaid interest, or add late-payment and service fees. Payments during the period can reduce the balance on which interest is calculated. Grace periods and transaction dates can also affect a credit-card charge. The simplified estimate should not be treated as an exact statement reconciliation unless its assumptions match the agreement.

Compare the total cost, not one number

For a meaningful comparison, record the financed amount, repayment schedule, annual rate, all mandatory fees, payment dates and total amount payable. A product with a lower headline rate can cost more if it has higher fees or a longer term. For instalment loans, compare the total repayment and schedule; for revolving credit, check how the balance and payments are handled each billing cycle.

Keep rate and time units consistent

Enter an annual rate as a percentage, not a decimal, if that is what the calculator field requests. A 18% rate is represented as 18 in a percentage field and 0.18 in a formula. A 90-day term should not be treated as a full year. This page's stated day-count assumption may differ from the convention used by a particular financial provider.

Finance charge FAQ

Does a finance charge include every fee?

Only if the relevant fees are explicitly included in the inputs or calculation method. Check the lender's fee schedule and formal disclosure for a complete cost.

Can I use this for a credit-card statement?

Use it as a rough check only unless you know the issuer's balance method, transaction rates, payment timing, day-count convention and fee rules. The issuer's statement and agreement govern the actual charge.
