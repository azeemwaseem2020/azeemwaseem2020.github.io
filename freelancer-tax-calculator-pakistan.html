Freelancer tax in Pakistan

Important distinction: a withholding amount deducted from a payment is not automatically the same thing as your final income-tax liability. The applicable treatment can depend on the income source, taxpayer status, tax year and the rules applying to that payment.

Freelancer income can involve different tax treatment depending on the nature of services, registration, withholding, source of income and applicable law. Calcora therefore makes the rate an explicit input instead of inventing one universal freelancer tax rate.

Platform income: Fiverr, YouTube and other online work

Platform income should be classified from the underlying work and applicable tax rules rather than from the platform name alone. For Fiverr, YouTube, AdSense or another marketplace, keep gross receipts, platform fees, payment-provider charges, withholding and supporting records separate before deciding which tax treatment applies. This calculator deliberately asks you to enter the rate you are evaluating instead of assuming one universal rate for every freelancer or creator.

Gross receipts, taxable income and withholding are different

Do not automatically enter every amount received from a platform as “taxable income.” First identify what the figure represents. Gross receipts describe money earned or received; taxable income is determined under the applicable tax rules; withholding is tax deducted at source. These figures can differ, so the calculator should be used only after you have established the basis of the income and the rate you are evaluating.

How this calculator works

This calculator first identifies the income route, then applies the corresponding Tax Year 2027 withholding rate published by FBR. For export services, the rate is applied to the gross export proceeds entered before platform fees, bank charges or business expenses. For social-media revenue, the current FBR rate card shows 5% for ATL persons and 10% for non-ATL persons.

Important: ATL status does not change the 154A export rates shown in FBR's current Tax Year 2027 rate card because section 154A is referenced with rule 10(ca). ATL status does matter for the separate 154B social-media rate. For 154A, whether the deduction is ultimately treated as final tax also depends on the statutory conditions and filing position.

Calcora's stronger separation: the calculator now shows the gross basis used for the withholding estimate, the estimated withholding, and an optional cash-after-costs figure separately. This prevents platform fees or bank charges from being mistaken for a change in the statutory rate.

Formula: PKR gross receipts × applicable rate ÷ 100. If income is entered in USD, Calcora first converts it using your own PKR/USD rate; it does not fetch a live exchange rate that could silently become stale.

Cash-flow planning: tax is not the same as take-home

Freelancers often confuse the amount subject to withholding with the cash that remains after platform and payment costs. The optional cash-flow section keeps those concepts separate: the tax estimate is calculated from the gross basis used by this model, while platform fees and bank/payment charges are shown only as a separate take-home planning view. This avoids quietly changing the tax base just because a platform deducted a fee.

Before using a freelancer tax estimate

  1. Identify the nature and source of the income.
  2. Keep platform statements, invoices and payment records.
  3. Separate gross receipts from platform and payment-processing fees.
  4. Check whether any withholding or advance tax has already been deducted.
  5. Verify the current FBR rules that apply to your status and income before filing.

Current-rule check

FBR currently publishes Tax Year 2027 withholding-rate material updated under Finance Act 2026. The 2026 budget material also introduced withholding provisions affecting revenues received by digital content creators and social-media influencers. That makes it especially important to distinguish payment-level withholding from the final tax position.

Check the current FBR withholding rate card and Finance Act publications before relying on a rate.

What this calculator does not assume

  • It does not treat every Fiverr, Upwork or direct-client payment as the same legal category.
  • It does not apply salary slabs to qualifying export proceeds simply because the person is a freelancer.
  • It does not subtract platform fees or business expenses before calculating the 154A withholding estimate.
  • It does not treat a bank deduction as automatically final without checking the section 154A conditions.
  • It keeps social-media platform revenue separate because section 154B has a different rate and tax treatment.

Practical checks before you rely on the result

  1. Confirm whether the receipt is an export of services or social-media/platform revenue.
  2. For IT/ITeS export income, confirm that the PSEB registration/certification requirement for the 0.25% row is actually met.
  3. Use the gross amount relevant to the applicable withholding rule, before fees and expenses where required.
  4. Keep the bank credit advice, platform statement, invoices and payment records that support the figure.
  5. Check the current FBR rate card and the underlying Income Tax Ordinance before filing.

Pakistan freelancer tax: common scenarios

Foreign software client: if the work qualifies as IT/ITeS export and the PSEB condition is met, the current FBR rate card places the proceeds in the 0.25% row.

Foreign client for another service: qualifying exported services outside that row use the 1% rate shown by FBR.

YouTube, TikTok, Facebook or Instagram revenue: platform revenue is handled separately under section 154B; the current FBR card shows 5% for ATL and 10% for non-ATL.
