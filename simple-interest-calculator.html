Simple Interest Calculator Guide

Use simple interest when interest is calculated only on the original principal rather than being added back for further interest calculations.

Formula & assumptions

Simple interest I = P × r × t, where r is the annual rate as a decimal. Total amount = P + I. It does not model compounding.

Worked example

For a principal of 10,000 at 10% per year for 2 years, interest is 10,000 × 0.10 × 2 = 2,000, so the total is 12,000.

How to use it

Enter the principal, annual rate and time in years. If your time is in months, convert it to years before calculating.

When simple interest is not appropriate

This tool does not model compound interest, fees, taxes, changing rates or payment schedules. For a real loan or savings product, use the terms supplied by the financial provider.

When simple interest is appropriate

Simple interest keeps the interest calculation tied to the original principal. That makes it useful for straightforward educational examples and some agreements where the contract explicitly specifies simple interest. It is different from compound interest, where previously accumulated interest can itself earn interest. For an actual Pakistani bank, financing company or savings product, the contract's rate, fees, payment timing and calculation method should take priority over a generic calculator.

Formula

I = P × r × t, where P is principal, r is the annual rate written as a decimal and t is time in years. Total amount A = P + I. If the rate is 10%, use r = 0.10, not 10.

Worked example

For Rs 100,000 at 10% per year for 2 years, I = 100,000 × 0.10 × 2 = Rs 20,000. The calculated amount is Rs 120,000. If the period is six months, use t = 0.5 year under a simple annual-rate model.

Pakistan-specific example

Consider a classroom example using Rs 50,000 and a stated simple annual rate of 8% for 18 months. Time is 1.5 years, so interest = 50,000 × 0.08 × 1.5 = Rs 6,000. The illustrative total is Rs 56,000. This is not a quotation for any bank product.

Common questions

Does simple interest compound? No. Interest is calculated from the original principal.

Can I enter a monthly period? Convert months to years when the annual-rate formula is being used.

Does this include fees or tax? No. Add only contractually relevant charges when evaluating a real product.

How is it different from EMI? EMI models scheduled repayments and interest on an outstanding balance; simple interest does not model an amortization schedule.
