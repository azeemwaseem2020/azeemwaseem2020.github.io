Frequently asked questions

Is the calculated result guaranteed?

No. It is an estimate from the inputs. Actual outcomes depend on the product terms and the conditions that apply to the transaction or investment.

Practical guide: compare simple-interest scenarios correctly

Simple interest is calculated on the original principal for the whole stated period. The core formula is I = P × r × t, where P is the starting principal, r is the annual rate written as a decimal, and t is the time in years. If a rate is entered as a percentage, convert it by dividing by 100 before multiplying. The total before any fees or taxes is A = P + I.

Worked example

Suppose PKR 50,000 earns simple interest at 8% per year for 2 years. Interest is 50,000 × 0.08 × 2 = PKR 8,000, so the amount before other charges is PKR 58,000. For the same principal and rate over 3 years, interest would be PKR 12,000. The increase is linear because each year’s interest is calculated on the same original principal.

Check the rate and time units

Make sure the rate and time period use compatible units. If the annual rate is 8% and the term is 6 months, use 0.5 years, not 6 years. A monthly rate should not be treated as an annual rate. When comparing offers, check whether the quoted rate is simple, flat, reducing-balance or compounded; those methods can produce very different payment and total-cost figures even when the advertised percentage looks similar.

What the estimate leaves out

This calculator models the stated simple-interest formula only. It does not automatically include processing fees, withholding tax, late charges, instalments, changing balances, inflation or early settlement terms. For a loan or deposit, compare the result with the institution’s written schedule and ask how the rate is applied. Do not treat a simple-interest estimate as a guaranteed return or as a substitute for the contract.

Quick reasonableness check: with a positive principal, positive rate and positive term, interest should be non-negative. Doubling the term doubles simple interest if all other inputs remain unchanged. If the result does not follow these checks, review the units and inputs.

Match the interest rate to the time period

Simple interest uses I = P × r × t, where r is an annual rate expressed as a decimal and t is measured in years when the rate is annual. For principal 50,000 at 8% per year for 9 months, use t = 9/12, giving interest of 50,000 × 0.08 × 0.75 = 3,000.

Simple interest versus compounding

Simple interest is calculated on the original principal throughout the term. Compound interest adds earned interest to the balance for later periods. A loan, deposit or investment may use fees, compounding, reducing balances or other terms, so this result may not match a real product quote.

Before comparing offers

Confirm whether the quoted rate is annual, monthly or for the whole term, and whether the agreement uses a 360-day or 365-day convention. Compare total payable or total earned amounts only after including fees and the product’s actual terms.
