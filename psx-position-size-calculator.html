Maintained by Abdul Qadir · Calculation methodology

Estimate a position size from the amount of capital you are willing to risk, your entry price, stop-loss price and target price.

Risk boundary: this calculator only applies the inputs you provide. It does not assess whether a trade is suitable, guarantee execution at a stop-loss price, or include every brokerage fee, tax, liquidity or market-gap risk.

Formula, example and guide

Formula

Position size is based on the amount you are willing to risk divided by the price difference between entry and stop-loss. The result is a planning quantity, not a recommendation to trade.

Worked example

If the planned risk is Rs 5,000 and the difference between entry and stop-loss is Rs 10 per share, the basic position size is 500 shares before considering fees, liquidity or account constraints.

PSX position sizing guide

This page targets PSX position size calculator, share sizing, risk-per-trade and stop-loss planning. Position size is calculated from the amount you choose to risk and the price distance to the stop-loss.

How position sizing works

The idea is simple: decide how much of your capital you are comfortable putting at risk, then work backwards from the entry and stop-loss prices. Maximum rupee risk = available capital × risk percentage. Risk per share = entry price − stop-loss price. The calculator divides those numbers and rounds down to a whole-number risk limit. The final quantity is also capped by the number of shares your entered capital can cover at the entry price. The capital check includes the buy-side cost percentage you enter. Use actual broker charges; omitted levies, taxes or fixed charges can still make the cash required higher.

Quick example

If you enter PKR 100,000 as capital, set risk at 1%, buy at PKR 100 and use PKR 95 as the stop-loss, the maximum planned loss is PKR 1,000 and the risk per share is PKR 5. That gives 200 shares before considering brokerage, taxes and other trading costs.

Risk-to-reward ratio

Target reward is estimated after entered buy/sell costs and the user-entered CGT scenario on a positive gain. Cost-adjusted risk-to-reward compares that estimated net reward with the estimated stop loss including entered costs. It is a planning metric, not a prediction or a tax determination.

Important PSX limitations

The optional cost percentages make the scenario more realistic, but fixed fees, other levies, tax rules, slippage, liquidity, price gaps and execution can still make actual results different from this arithmetic estimate. This tool does not recommend a security or tell you where to place a stop loss.

Common questions

What does position size mean?

It is the number of shares that fits a chosen risk amount and the distance between entry and stop-loss.

Can the risk-based quantity exceed my available capital?

The calculator now compares the risk-based share count with the quantity affordable from your entered capital and uses the lower number. This affordability check excludes brokerage, taxes and other transaction costs, so the final cash requirement can be higher than the displayed position value.

Does this tell me which PSX stock to buy?

No. It only does the arithmetic for the figures you enter.

Are brokerage and taxes included?

No. Keep those costs in mind when checking the final amount with your broker.

RISK ARITHMETIC

Separate planned risk from position value

Position sizing answers a risk-allocation question; it does not predict whether a trade will succeed. The planned rupee risk should be derived from the distance between entry and stop-loss and the number of shares, while the total position value remains a separate exposure measure.

Risk per share

Entry price minus stop-loss price for a long position, using the same unit throughout.

Planned shares

Risk budget divided by risk per share, subject to the investor's own execution and market constraints.

Position value

Shares multiplied by entry price. This can be much larger than the planned loss at the stop.

Re-check before execution

Prices, liquidity, charges and the actual fill can change the realised outcome. Treat the calculator as planning arithmetic, not a trading recommendation.

How PSX position sizing works

Position sizing connects the amount you are willing to risk with the distance between your entry price and planned stop price. The calculator converts those inputs into a share quantity; it does not decide whether a trade is suitable.

Worked example

If the maximum planned loss is PKR 10,000 and the difference between entry and stop is PKR 5 per share, the basic risk-based quantity is 2,000 shares before checking lot rules, liquidity, fees and total capital exposure.

Capital and risk are different limits

A risk calculation can produce a quantity that is too large for the available capital. Always compare the risk-based quantity with the quantity affordable at the entry price and use the lower practical amount when your own risk rules require it.

PSX-specific checks

Use actual broker charges and the trading rules applicable to the security. Price gaps, slippage, liquidity, taxes and execution costs can make the realised loss different from the theoretical stop-distance calculation.

Position sizing FAQ

What does position size mean?

It is the number of shares that fits a chosen risk amount and the distance between entry and stop-loss.

Can the risk-based quantity exceed my available capital?

The calculator compares the risk-based share count with the cash-affordable quantity after your entered buy-side costs and uses the lower number.

Can I include PSX brokerage and transaction costs?

Yes. Enter your own estimated total buy-side and sell-side cost percentages. These are assumptions, not broker tariffs, and should be reconciled with your contract note.

Does the CGT field calculate my legal tax liability?

No. It applies your entered percentage to a positive estimated per-share gain for scenario planning only. Actual tax treatment depends on current rules and your circumstances; verify with official guidance or a qualified tax professional.

Does this tell me which PSX stock to buy?

No. It only does arithmetic using the figures and assumptions you enter.
