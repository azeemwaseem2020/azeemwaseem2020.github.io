How this profit margin calculation works

Profit is the selling price minus the entered cost. The margin percentage divides that profit by the selling price, then multiplies by 100. This is a simple gross margin estimate based on the two inputs; it is not an investment-rate or loan calculation.

Use the same price basis

When comparing products, consistently account for discounts, returns, sales tax, shipping and direct costs. A tax-inclusive selling price may distort comparisons if tax is collected on behalf of a government and is not business revenue.

What if selling price is below cost?

The result is negative profit and a negative margin, representing a loss on the entered cost basis. A zero selling price makes margin undefined because the formula divides by selling price.

Frequently asked questions

Does gross margin include rent and salaries?

Not when the calculation uses only product cost and selling price. Operating or net margin requires the relevant additional expenses.

Profit margin explained

Profit margin expresses profit as a percentage of revenue. It answers a different question from markup: margin uses the selling price or revenue as its base, while markup measures the increase relative to cost.

Worked example

If revenue is PKR 1,000,000 and the relevant profit is PKR 150,000, the profit margin is 15%. The interpretation depends on which profit measure is being used, such as gross, operating or net profit.

Gross vs net margin

Gross margin focuses on revenue after direct cost of goods or services. Net margin goes further by accounting for broader expenses such as operating costs, financing and taxes. Use the margin type that matches the business question.

Use margin for decisions, not just reporting

Track margin alongside sales volume, costs and pricing changes. A higher revenue figure does not automatically mean a healthier business if costs are increasing faster than revenue.

Profit-margin FAQ

Is profit margin the same as markup?

No. For example, a 25% markup on cost corresponds to a 20% margin on the selling price.

Read the result correctly

This calculator uses the entered cost and selling price to calculate the difference and the percentage of selling price represented by that difference. If selling price is below cost, the profit result is negative and the margin is a loss margin. The calculation is undefined when selling price is zero.

Pricing example

If a product costs PKR 750 and sells for PKR 1,000, the difference is PKR 250 and the gross margin on that simple cost basis is 25%. To target a 25% margin, price is cost ÷ (1 − 0.25), or PKR 1,000. Applying a 25% markup instead would produce PKR 937.50, which gives a 20% margin.

Define the cost before comparing

Purchase cost alone may omit packaging, freight, payment fees, returns, staff costs and overhead. Be clear whether you are measuring gross margin, operating margin or net margin, and use the same cost definition when comparing products or periods. This tool does not determine tax treatment or forecast demand.

Useful companion metrics

Review margin alongside contribution per unit, sales volume and fixed costs. A high percentage on a low sales volume may produce less total profit than a lower percentage on a much larger volume. For business decisions, reconcile the estimate with actual records.
