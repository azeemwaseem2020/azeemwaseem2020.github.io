Profit margin formula

Profit margin expresses profit as a percentage of sales revenue. The basic formula is (selling price − cost) ÷ selling price × 100. This is different from markup, where profit is divided by cost.

Worked example

If an item costs PKR 800 and sells for PKR 1,000, profit is PKR 200 and margin is 200 ÷ 1,000 × 100 = 20%. The markup is 25%, demonstrating why the two percentages should not be confused.

Business example

For a Pakistani retailer, compare margins using consistent definitions of product cost. Shipping, discounts, returns, taxes and overhead can affect broader profitability and should not be silently mixed into a simple product-margin calculation.

FAQs

Can margin exceed 100%? Not under the ordinary positive-sales formula when cost is nonnegative. Can margin be negative? Yes. Is margin the same as net margin? No; net margin uses net profit and revenue.

Profit and margin

Profit is selling price minus cost. Profit margin is profit ÷ selling price × 100. Markup instead divides profit by cost, so the two percentages are different.

Worked example

A product costing PKR 800 and selling for PKR 1,000 has PKR 200 profit and a 20% margin. Its markup is 25%. To target a 30% margin on an PKR 800 cost, price = 800 ÷ 0.70 = PKR 1,142.86.

Business use

For Pakistan retail or online selling, keep discounts, taxes, shipping, payment fees and returns separate when deciding whether a figure is gross or net margin.

FAQs

Is markup the same as margin? No. Can margin exceed 100%? Not under the standard positive-price definition. Should tax be included? Follow and label the business's chosen pricing basis.

Use profit margin to evaluate a sale

Profit margin is profit divided by selling revenue, multiplied by 100. For a simple gross-margin calculation, profit is selling price minus the cost included in the calculation. The result depends on what you count as cost: a gross margin may exclude rent, payroll, marketing, financing, tax and other operating expenses.

Worked example

If an item sells for PKR 1,500 and its relevant cost is PKR 1,050, gross profit is PKR 450. The margin is 450 ÷ 1,500 × 100 = 30%. The markup on cost is 450 ÷ 1,050 × 100 ≈ 42.86%, which is a different measure.

Small-business checks

  • Define whether you are measuring gross, operating or net margin before comparing products.
  • Include the relevant cost basis, such as freight, packaging, wastage and payment fees where appropriate.
  • Model discounts and returns against the actual selling revenue.
  • Review the period and accounting basis when comparing margin across months or product categories.
  • A positive margin on one item does not prove that the whole business is profitable after overheads.

Frequently asked questions

Can margin be negative? Yes. If the included cost exceeds sales revenue, the result is a loss margin.

Is a 30% margin the same as a 30% markup? No. Margin uses selling price as the denominator; markup uses cost.

Does this calculate tax liability? No. It is a margin calculation, not a tax or accounting filing.
