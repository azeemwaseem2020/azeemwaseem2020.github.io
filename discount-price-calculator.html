How to calculate a discounted price

First calculate the discount amount by multiplying the original price by the discount rate. Then subtract that amount from the original price to get the final sale price.

Discount amount = Original price × (Discount % ÷ 100)

Sale price = Original price − Discount amount

Worked example

If an item costs $100 and has a 20% discount, the discount is $20 and the final price is $80. The same method works with other currencies because the calculator uses the numbers you enter.

What this calculator is useful for

Use it for shopping discounts, sale prices, invoices, promotions, retail pricing, and quick percentage calculations. A discount of 100% produces a zero sale price; discounts above 100% are not accepted.

Discount price FAQ

How do I find the sale price?

Multiply the original price by the discount percentage to find the savings, then subtract the savings from the original price.

Is the discount percentage the same as the final price percentage?

No. A 20% discount means you pay 80% of the original price.

Does this include sales tax?

No. This calculator calculates the discount only. Tax, shipping, coupons, and other charges may need to be calculated separately.

How a discounted price is calculated

The discount amount is original price × discount rate. The final price is original price − discount amount, or equivalently original price × (1 − discount rate).

Worked example

A Rs. 12,000 item with a 15% discount has a discount of Rs. 1,800. The sale price is therefore Rs. 10,200. If sales tax is added after the discount, calculate the tax on the discounted taxable price according to the applicable rules.

A practical Pakistan example

A shop in Multan advertises 20% off a Rs. 5,000 appliance. The discount is Rs. 1,000 and the advertised price becomes Rs. 4,000 before any separately stated charges or taxes.

Common mistakes

Do not confuse a percentage discount with a fixed rupee reduction, and do not assume two successive discounts simply add together. A 10% discount followed by 10% off the reduced price is a total reduction of 19%, not 20%.

FAQ

How do I calculate the discount amount? Multiply the original price by the discount percentage as a decimal.

Can a discount exceed 100%? A normal retail discount should not; validate unusual inputs.

Does tax always come after discount? Tax treatment depends on the transaction and applicable rules.
