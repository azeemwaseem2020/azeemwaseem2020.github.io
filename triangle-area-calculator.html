Triangle area formula

The area of a triangle is A = ½ × base × height. The height must be perpendicular to the selected base. It is not necessarily the same as one of the triangle's sloping sides.

Worked example

If the base is 10 cm and the perpendicular height is 6 cm, then A = ½ × 10 × 6 = 30 cm².

Units matter

If both dimensions are measured in metres, the area is in square metres (m²). If they are in centimetres, the result is in square centimetres (cm²). Convert dimensions to compatible units before calculating.

What if the height is not given?

If you know the three side lengths but not a perpendicular height, this base-and-height calculator is not enough by itself. A different method, such as Heron's formula, can calculate the area from three valid side lengths.

Common mistakes

  • Using a sloping side as the height when it is not perpendicular to the base.
  • Forgetting the factor of ½.
  • Mixing metres and centimetres without converting first.
  • Reporting an area in linear units instead of square units.

FAQ

Does the triangle have to be right-angled?

No. The formula works for any triangle when the height is the perpendicular distance to the chosen base.

Can the base be zero?

A zero base produces zero area, but it does not describe a non-degenerate triangle.

Which height should I use?

Use the perpendicular distance from the chosen base to the opposite vertex. For an obtuse triangle, that perpendicular may fall outside the side segment.

Triangle Area Calculator: Base and Perpendicular Height

The area of a triangle is half the product of a base and its corresponding perpendicular height. The height must meet the base at a right angle; it is not necessarily the length of one of the sloping sides. Once the base and height use the same length unit, the output is in square units.

Formula

A = ½ × b × h, where b is the base and h is the perpendicular height. If the base is measured in centimetres and the height in centimetres, the area is in cm². Convert units first if one measurement is in metres and the other in centimetres.

Worked example

For a base of 10 cm and a perpendicular height of 6 cm, the area is ½ × 10 × 6 = 30 cm². If the triangle's sloping side is 8 cm, that side should not be entered as the height unless it is perpendicular to the selected base. The area formula works for acute, right and obtuse triangles as long as the corresponding perpendicular height is used.

How to use the calculator

  1. Measure or identify one side to use as the base.
  2. Find the perpendicular distance from the opposite vertex to the line containing that base.
  3. Enter both lengths in the same unit and calculate.
  4. Label the result with square units and preserve appropriate measurement precision.

Alternative when only side lengths are known

If you know all three side lengths but not the height, Heron's formula can calculate area after checking the triangle inequality. With side lengths a, b and c, first calculate semiperimeter s = (a + b + c) ÷ 2, then area = √[s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)]. This is a different method and requires three valid side lengths. For survey or construction use, account for measurement uncertainty and avoid rounding intermediate values too early.

Frequently asked questions

Is the height always one of the sides?

No. It is the perpendicular distance to the chosen base and can fall outside an obtuse triangle.

Why is the formula divided by two?

Two congruent copies of a triangle can form a parallelogram with the same base and height.

Can I use different units for base and height?

Convert them to the same length unit first.

Triangle Area Calculator: Base–Height and Heron’s Formula

The standard triangle area formula is A = ½ × base × perpendicular height. The height must meet the chosen base at a right angle; a sloping side is not automatically the height. If all three side lengths are known, Heron’s formula can calculate the area without directly measuring a height, provided the side lengths form a valid triangle.

Worked example

A triangle with base 10 cm and perpendicular height 6 cm has area ½ × 10 × 6 = 30 cm². The units are squared because area measures a two-dimensional surface. If the base is entered in metres, keep the height in metres to get square metres.

Check measurements before calculating

  1. Use the perpendicular height, not an adjacent sloping side.
  2. Keep base and height in the same length unit.
  3. For a three-side method, verify that the sum of any two sides is greater than the third.
  4. Label the result in square units, such as cm² or m².

For sides a, b and c, Heron’s formula uses semiperimeter s = (a + b + c) ÷ 2 and area = √[s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)]. This is useful when height is unavailable, but measurement errors can affect results, especially for nearly degenerate triangles. For land or construction work, confirm dimensions and units before relying on the calculated area.

FAQs

Can I use a sloping side as the height?

Only if it is perpendicular to the selected base; otherwise use the perpendicular distance.

Why is the answer in square units?

Area multiplies two lengths, so the unit is squared.

Can any three side lengths form a triangle?

No. The sum of each pair must be greater than the remaining side.
