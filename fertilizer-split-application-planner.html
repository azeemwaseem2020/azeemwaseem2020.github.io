Fertilizer Split Application Planner

Convert a nitrogen target that you already have into product quantities for each planned application. Enter the field area, the nitrogen recommendation, the product's labelled nitrogen percentage and your own split percentages. This tool checks the arithmetic; it does not recommend a crop rate or application schedule.

What this planner calculates

  • Total nitrogen represented by your entered target and field area.
  • Total product kilograms needed to supply that nitrogen at the entered label percentage.
  • Product kilograms, bag-equivalents and estimated cost for each user-defined split.
  • A check that split percentages total 100%.

Example inputs are illustrative only. Confirm crop, soil-test results, target units, product grade and timing with current local agronomic guidance before field use.

Formula used

Total nitrogen (kg) = field area (acres) × nitrogen target (kg N/acre). Total product (kg) = total nitrogen ÷ (label nitrogen percentage ÷ 100). For each split, product quantity equals total product multiplied by the split percentage divided by 100. Bag-equivalents equal product kilograms divided by the bag's net weight.

Worked example: arithmetic only

Suppose a field is 5 acres and the user-entered target is 30 kg N per acre. The total target is 150 kg N. At a product grade of 46% N, the mathematical product quantity is 150 ÷ 0.46 ≈ 326.09 kg. If the user's planned shares are 40%, 40% and 20%, the product quantities are approximately 130.43 kg, 130.43 kg and 65.22 kg. For 50 kg bags, these equal 2.61, 2.61 and 1.30 bag-equivalents. Bag-equivalents are not whole-bag purchase advice; stock and leftovers must be reconciled separately.

Important limitations

This planner handles one nutrient percentage and a target supplied by the user. It does not account for nitrogen already supplied by DAP or compound fertilizers, soil mineralization, irrigation water, manure, losses, crop uptake, product compatibility, weather or application equipment. If another product contributes nitrogen, account for it in the documented nutrient plan before using a remaining nitrogen target here. Do not interpret this arithmetic as a recommendation to apply the example quantity.
