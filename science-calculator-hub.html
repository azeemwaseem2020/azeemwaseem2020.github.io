Formula map

Density: ρ = m ÷ V. Force: F = m × a. Average velocity: v = displacement ÷ time. Acceleration: a = change in velocity ÷ time. Kinetic energy: KE = ½mv². Potential energy: PE = mgh. Work: W = Fd cos θ. Power: P = W/t. Momentum: p = mv.

How to choose the right science calculator

Use Density when mass and volume describe a material. Use Force when mass and acceleration are known or need to be related. Use Velocity for displacement over elapsed time. Use Acceleration when velocity changes over time. Use Kinetic Energy when you need the energy associated with an object's translational motion.

Units are part of the calculation

A formula can be mathematically correct and still produce a misleading result if the units are mixed. Convert values into compatible units before calculating when the tool expects a consistent unit system. Always report the result with its unit.

Worked problem-solving workflow

  1. Write down the known values and units.
  2. Identify the unknown quantity.
  3. Select the formula connecting the known and unknown values.
  4. Convert units if needed.
  5. Substitute values and calculate.
  6. Check the unit, sign and approximate magnitude.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing metres with centimetres or seconds with minutes without conversion.
  • Confusing speed with velocity when direction matters.
  • Using mass in grams with a formula expecting kilograms without conversion.
  • Forgetting that kinetic energy depends on the square of speed.
  • Using the wrong force angle in a work calculation.
  • Confusing power (W) with energy (J or Wh).
  • Ignoring velocity direction when momentum is treated as a vector.
  • Reporting a number without the unit needed to interpret it.
