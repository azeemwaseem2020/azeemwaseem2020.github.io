Topsoil calculator: complete guide

Topsoil is the upper layer of soil used in lawns, raised beds and landscaping. The quantity required depends on the area and the planned depth. This calculator estimates the volume needed for a uniform layer, helping you compare delivery quantities or bulk bags. It does not determine soil quality, nutrient needs or the right soil mix for a particular plant.

Formula

Topsoil volume = area × depth. Square metres multiplied by metres produce cubic metres; square feet multiplied by feet produce cubic feet. Convert depth into the same length unit used by the area before calculating.

How to use it

  1. Measure the area to be covered.
  2. Choose a realistic depth for your project and enter it in the same unit system.
  3. Press Calculate to estimate volume.
  4. Allow for uneven ground, settling and waste, and confirm supplier quantities before ordering.

Worked example

A lawn area of 30 m² needs a 0.10 m (10 cm) topsoil layer. Volume = 30 × 0.10 = 3 m³. This is the ideal geometric volume before accounting for settlement or grading. If depth is entered as 10 while the area is in m², the result would be 300 cubic units, not 3 m³; convert 10 cm to 0.10 m first.

Practical considerations

For irregular plots, divide the space into simple shapes and add their areas. Soil delivered loose may settle after watering and compaction, while the required depth can vary across the site. Check whether the supplier quotes loose or compacted volume and ask about soil texture, screening and suitability. Avoid covering drainage points or burying tree trunks and plant crowns. This calculator estimates volume only and does not account for bulk density, moisture, contamination, nutrient content or delivery losses.

Frequently asked questions

How much topsoil do I need?

Multiply the area to cover by the planned depth to find volume. Convert the depth to the same length unit used for area, or calculate using metres to obtain cubic metres. For multiple beds, calculate each separately and add the volumes.

Should I order extra soil?

The estimate assumes a flat area and even depth. Settling, uneven ground, compaction and spillage can change the quantity needed. Check whether a supplier sells by cubic metre, cubic yard, tonne or bag, and do not convert volume to weight without a density estimate.

Can topsoil volume be converted directly to tonnes?

Not reliably without knowing moisture and bulk density. Wet and compacted soil can weigh substantially more than dry, loose soil. Use the supplier’s stated density or delivery guidance for weight-based orders.

Convert the volume into an order quantity

First calculate the geometric volume in cubic metres or cubic feet, then check how the supplier sells soil. A cubic yard is about 0.7646 cubic metres, so 1 cubic metre is about 1.308 cubic yards. Treat these as volume conversions; converting cubic volume into tonnes requires a suitable bulk-density estimate and the soil's moisture condition.

Measure uneven areas in sections

For an L-shaped lawn or several raised beds, divide the area into rectangles, calculate each volume separately using its own depth, then add the volumes. If the ground slopes, use a reasonable average depth or split it into smaller sections rather than multiplying the entire area by one depth that does not represent the site.

Ordering and soil quality

Ask whether the quoted quantity is loose or compacted volume and whether the product is screened topsoil, blended planting soil or another material. A volume estimate cannot tell you the soil's nutrient content, drainage, pH or suitability for a crop. For edible gardens or planting beds, match the soil specification to the intended use and local conditions.
