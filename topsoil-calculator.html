Topsoil volume estimate

Topsoil quantity depends on the area and desired depth. For a rectangular area, volume = length × width × depth. Convert the depth to the same unit as the length and width before calculating. For soil sold by weight, a suitable bulk density is also needed.

Worked example

A 10 m × 5 m garden covered with 0.10 m of topsoil requires 10 × 5 × 0.10 = 5 m³. Delivered volume can vary because soil settles and bulk density changes with moisture and composition.

Practical farming example

For crop beds, landscaping or orchard work in Pakistan, base the depth on the soil-improvement plan rather than simply ordering the maximum possible amount. A soil test can help determine whether imported topsoil is actually needed.

FAQs

Can I convert cubic metres to tonnes? Yes, if an appropriate bulk density is known. Does soil settle? Yes. Should depth be the final desired depth? It should represent the planned finished layer, with settling considered separately.

Topsoil volume and delivery

For a rectangular area, topsoil volume is length × width × depth. Convert depth to the same unit as the other dimensions. A 10 m × 6 m garden covered to 0.10 m requires 6 m³ before settling allowances.

Worked example

If the supplier's bulk density is 1.3 tonnes/m³, 6 m³ corresponds to about 7.8 tonnes. Density varies with moisture and material, so use the supplier's stated figure when ordering by weight.

Practical use

Gardens, landscaping and agricultural plots need a finished depth. Settling and compaction can reduce the final layer, so an appropriate allowance may be needed for the material and site.

FAQs

Can units be mixed? Convert first. Does volume equal weight? No; density is required. Should existing soil be subtracted? Yes when calculating only additional material above the finished level.
