Standard Error Calculator

Calculate the standard error of a sample mean from the sample standard deviation and sample size.

Standard error formula

For the standard error of a sample mean, the common formula is SE = s ÷ √n, where s is the sample standard deviation and n is the sample size.

Worked example

If s = 10 and n = 25, then SE = 10 ÷ √25 = 10 ÷ 5 = 2.

What standard error tells you

Standard error describes the estimated variability of a sample statistic across repeated samples under the relevant statistical assumptions. It is not the same thing as the standard deviation of individual observations.

Standard error vs standard deviation

Standard deviation describes variability within the observed data. Standard error describes uncertainty associated with estimating a population mean from a sample. Increasing sample size reduces SE when the sample standard deviation is held constant.

Important assumptions

This calculator uses the common standard error of the mean formula. Other statistics and study designs can require different standard errors, such as regression coefficients, proportions, clustered samples, or complex survey designs.

FAQ

Does a larger sample reduce standard error? Under the same variability assumptions, yes, because SE is divided by the square root of n.

Is standard error the same as standard deviation? No. They describe different aspects of statistical variation and uncertainty.

What units does standard error have? For a mean, SE has the same measurement units as the original variable.

Worked example: standard error of a mean

If a sample has standard deviation 12 units and contains 36 independent observations, the estimated standard error of the mean is 12 ÷ √36 = 2 units. If the sample size increases to 144 while the standard deviation stays at 12, the standard error becomes 12 ÷ √144 = 1 unit. The reduction comes from the square root of sample size, not from the individual observations becoming less variable.

Interpret the result in context

Standard error quantifies the sampling variability of an estimator under the method's assumptions. It is not a guarantee that the sample mean is within one standard error of the true population mean, and it does not remove selection bias or measurement error. Independence matters: repeated measures, clustered samples and complex survey designs may need a different calculation.

Use a consistent standard deviation definition and sample size from the same analysis. For confidence intervals, combine the standard error with the appropriate critical value and method; do not treat the standard error alone as a complete interval.
