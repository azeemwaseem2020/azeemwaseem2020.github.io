Turning a decimal into a fraction

A terminating decimal can be converted exactly into a fraction by using a power of ten as the denominator and then simplifying. Repeating decimals require a different algebraic method or a stated approximation. The important distinction is whether the decimal supplied represents an exact value or a rounded measurement.

Formula

For a terminating decimal with k digits after the decimal point, write it as an integer over 10ᵏ, then divide numerator and denominator by their greatest common divisor.

Worked example

0.75 has two decimal places, so 0.75 = 75/100. Divide both by 25 to obtain 3/4. For 2.125, write 2125/1000 and simplify to 17/8.

Pakistan-specific example

A student working through school or university mathematics in Pakistan may convert a decimal measurement to a fraction when a problem requires an exact symbolic form. For measurements, remember that a rounded decimal does not create an exact physical fraction.

Common questions

Can every decimal become an exact simple fraction? Every finite decimal can; repeating decimals need a different representation.

Why simplify? It gives an equivalent fraction in lowest terms.

Can negative decimals be converted? Yes; keep the sign with the fraction.

What about 0.333... It is exactly 1/3 if the repetition continues indefinitely.
