What is scientific notation?

Scientific notation writes a number as a × 10n, where a is at least 1 and less than 10 for a normalized positive value, and n is an integer exponent. It is useful for comparing the scale of very large and very small values.

How the exponent works

For 1,234,567, move the decimal point six places left to get 1.234567, so the number is 1.234567 × 106. For 0.00045, move the decimal four places right to get 4.5, giving 4.5 × 10−4.

Worked examples

1,000 = 1 × 103. 25,000 = 2.5 × 104. 0.0032 = 3.2 × 10−3.

Scientific notation and significant figures

The digits in the coefficient can communicate measurement precision. For example, 2.50 × 103 has three significant figures, while 2.5 × 103 has two.

Using scientific notation correctly

Scientific notation writes a number as a × 10ⁿ, where a is normally at least 1 and less than 10 and n is an integer. It is useful for very large or very small measurements because the exponent shows the order of magnitude without requiring a long string of zeros.

Worked examples

4,500,000 becomes 4.5 × 10⁶ because the decimal moves six places left. For a small number, 0.00072 becomes 7.2 × 10⁻⁴ because the decimal moves four places right. The sign of the exponent therefore tells you whether the original value was above or below 1.

A practical Pakistan example

A laboratory in Pakistan may record a concentration such as 0.0000035 mol/L. Scientific notation expresses this as 3.5 × 10⁻⁶ mol/L. Keeping the unit attached is important: changing the number's notation does not change the physical quantity or its unit.

Multiplication and division

For multiplication, multiply the coefficients and add the exponents. For division, divide the coefficients and subtract the exponents. If the resulting coefficient is outside the usual 1-to-10 range, move the decimal and adjust the exponent accordingly.

Significant figures

Scientific notation can make significant figures easier to see. In 3.40 × 10⁵, the digits 3, 4 and the trailing zero are significant under the usual convention. The calculator's arithmetic does not decide the appropriate measurement precision; that depends on the source data.

FAQ

Can the exponent be negative? Yes. Negative exponents represent values between zero and one.

Does scientific notation change a value? No. It is an equivalent way to represent the same number.

Why use a coefficient below 10? Normalizing the coefficient gives a consistent representation and makes magnitude comparisons easier.

Does the calculator determine significant figures? It can show the numerical conversion, but measurement precision should follow the data and reporting rules.
