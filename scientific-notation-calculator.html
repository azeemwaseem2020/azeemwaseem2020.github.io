What is scientific notation?

Scientific notation writes a number as a × 10n, where a is at least 1 and less than 10 for a normalized positive value, and n is an integer exponent. It is useful for comparing the scale of very large and very small values.

How the exponent works

For 1,234,567, move the decimal point six places left to get 1.234567, so the number is 1.234567 × 106. For 0.00045, move the decimal four places right to get 4.5, giving 4.5 × 10−4.

Worked examples

1,000 = 1 × 103. 25,000 = 2.5 × 104. 0.0032 = 3.2 × 10−3.

Scientific notation and significant figures

The digits in the coefficient can communicate measurement precision. For example, 2.50 × 103 has three significant figures, while 2.5 × 103 has two.

FAQ

Can the exponent be negative? Yes. Negative exponents represent numbers between 0 and 1.

Why use scientific notation? It makes scale, multiplication, division and significant figures easier to manage.

Scientific notation checks

Scientific notation expresses a number as a coefficient multiplied by a power of ten. It is especially useful for very large or very small quantities and helps make significant digits explicit.

Worked check

4,500,000 can be written as 4.5 × 10⁶, while 0.00045 can be written as 4.5 × 10⁻⁴. The exponent records how far the decimal point moves.

Precision

Changing the notation does not create additional measurement precision. Keep the number of significant digits consistent with the source measurement.
