What is scientific notation?

Scientific notation writes a number as a × 10n, where a is at least 1 and less than 10 for a normalized positive value, and n is an integer exponent. It is useful for comparing the scale of very large and very small values.

How the exponent works

For 1,234,567, move the decimal point six places left to get 1.234567, so the number is 1.234567 × 106. For 0.00045, move the decimal four places right to get 4.5, giving 4.5 × 10−4.

Worked examples

1,000 = 1 × 103. 25,000 = 2.5 × 104. 0.0032 = 3.2 × 10−3.

Scientific notation and significant figures

The digits in the coefficient can communicate measurement precision. For example, 2.50 × 103 has three significant figures, while 2.5 × 103 has two.

Scientific notation checks

Scientific notation expresses a number as a coefficient multiplied by a power of ten. It is especially useful for very large or very small quantities and helps make significant digits explicit.

Worked check

4,500,000 can be written as 4.5 × 10⁶, while 0.00045 can be written as 4.5 × 10⁻⁴. The exponent records how far the decimal point moves.

Precision

Changing the notation does not create additional measurement precision. Keep the number of significant digits consistent with the source measurement.

Scientific notation: exponent rules, precision and checking your answer

Scientific notation writes a number as a × 10ⁿ, where the coefficient a has an absolute value from 1 up to, but not including, 10, and n is an integer. The exponent records how far the decimal point moves; it does not by itself describe how accurately a value was measured.

How to check the decimal shift

For a large number, move the decimal point left until one non-zero digit remains before it; the number of places moved becomes a positive exponent. For a small positive number, move it right to obtain a coefficient from 1 to 10; the exponent is negative. For example, 4,500,000 = 4.5 × 10⁶, while 0.00072 = 7.2 × 10⁻⁴. Reverse the shift to check that the scientific-notation value returns to the original number.

Multiplication and division

When multiplying powers of ten, add exponents: (2 × 10³)(4 × 10²) = 8 × 10⁵. When dividing non-zero powers of ten, subtract exponents: (6 × 10⁷) ÷ (3 × 10²) = 2 × 10⁵. After any operation, normalise the coefficient again if it is 10 or greater, or less than 1 in absolute value.

Precision is not the same as display digits

A calculator may display extra digits to show the numerical conversion, but those digits are not automatically justified by the measurement. If a length was measured as 2.4 m, rewriting it as 2.4000 × 10⁰ m does not create new measurement precision. For scientific reporting, retain meaningful significant figures and follow the rounding rules required by the experiment or assignment.

Scientific notation FAQ

Can the exponent be negative?

Yes. A negative exponent represents a reciprocal power of ten; for example, 10⁻³ = 0.001.

Does scientific notation change the value?

No. It is a different representation of the same number as long as the coefficient and exponent are correct.

Why must the coefficient usually be less than 10?

The standard form uses a coefficient with absolute value at least 1 and less than 10 so each non-zero number has a consistent representation.

Does adding decimal zeros increase accuracy?

No. Extra displayed zeros do not add evidence or measurement accuracy. Significant figures should reflect the source data and the calculation method.
