Free Fall Calculator — Time, Velocity & Distance

Solve idealized vertical free-fall problems using constant gravitational acceleration. Choose the quantity you want to find.

Ideal free-fall formulas

From rest, d = ½gt², v = gt, and v² = 2gd.

Worked example

Ignoring air resistance, a 20 m drop with g = 9.80665 m/s² takes about √(2d/g) = 2.02 s.

Assumptions

This tool assumes the object starts from rest, acceleration remains constant and air resistance is ignored. Real objects may deviate from this ideal model.

Free Fall Calculator Guide — Search by Question

Use this tool when you are asking how long an object takes to fall, how fast it is moving before impact, how far it falls, or what gravity does to a falling object. It models ideal vertical motion with a chosen gravitational acceleration.

How to use the result

  1. Choose the known quantity or quantities.
  2. Use metres and seconds consistently unless the tool states otherwise.
  3. Check the assumed value of gravitational acceleration.
  4. Compare the result with the physical situation; real air resistance can make an actual fall different.

Formula and learning note

For ideal free fall from rest, distance is related to gravity and time, while final speed is related to gravity and elapsed time. For a nonzero initial velocity, use the general constant-acceleration equations.
