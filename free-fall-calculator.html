Ideal free-fall formulas

From rest, d = ½gt², v = gt, and v² = 2gd.

Worked example

Ignoring air resistance, a 20 m drop with g = 9.80665 m/s² takes about √(2d/g) = 2.02 s.

Assumptions

This tool assumes the object starts from rest, acceleration remains constant and air resistance is ignored. Real objects may deviate from this ideal model.

Free Fall Calculator Guide

This calculator models an object falling vertically from rest under a constant gravitational acceleration. It can estimate fall time, final speed, or drop distance.

Core equations

From rest, d = ½gt², v = gt, and v² = 2gd. Here d is distance, v is final speed, t is time, and g is gravitational acceleration.

Worked example

For a 20 m drop using g = 9.80665 m/s², the ideal fall time is about 2.02 s, with a final speed of about 19.81 m/s.

When the simple model is not enough

The equations assume the object starts from rest and ignore air resistance. A real falling object can be affected by drag, wind, shape and buoyancy. If the object has an initial velocity, use general constant-acceleration equations instead.

Unit check

Use metres, seconds and m/s² consistently. Convert other units before comparing the result with a measured value.

How this calculation is checked

Use compatible units, follow the formula shown on this page, and check the result against the physical assumptions of the problem.
