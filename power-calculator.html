Power Calculator — Work, Energy & Time

Calculate average power from work or energy completed over time using P = W/t.

Calculate

Formula

P = W/t. One joule per second equals one watt. This calculator reports average power over the entered time interval.

Worked example

If 1,000 J of work is completed in 10 s, average power is 1,000 ÷ 10 = 100 W.

Power versus energy

Energy describes how much work or energy is transferred; power describes the rate of transfer. A higher power can mean the same energy is transferred in less time.

Common mistakes

  • Entering minutes or hours when the time field expects seconds.
  • Confusing watts with watt-hours: watts measure power, while watt-hours measure energy.
  • Treating average power as instantaneous power in a changing system.

Power Calculator Guide

Use this calculator to relate work or energy to the time over which it is transferred. Power describes the rate of doing work or transferring energy.

Formula

P = W/t, where P is average power in watts, W is work or transferred energy in joules, and t is time in seconds.

Worked example

If 1,200 J of work is completed in 30 seconds, the average power is 40 W.

Average versus instantaneous power

The basic calculation gives average power over the stated interval. If power changes continuously, instantaneous power can vary throughout the motion or process.

Unit check

One watt equals one joule per second. Convert minutes to seconds and other energy units before using the SI formula.

Important limitation

This relationship describes the rate of energy transfer; it does not by itself determine efficiency, electrical losses, mechanical losses or the detailed behaviour of a machine.

How this calculation is checked

POWER MODEL CHECK

Power measures the rate of energy transfer or work

Average power is P = W/t or ΔE/t. The same amount of work can correspond to different power values depending on how quickly it is completed.

Energy vs power

Energy describes how much work or transfer occurred; power describes how quickly that transfer occurred. They should not be treated as interchangeable.

Average vs instantaneous

This basic relationship gives average power over an interval. Instantaneous power can vary with time and may require a more detailed model.

Unit check

One watt equals one joule per second. Keep time units consistent with the energy or work inputs before calculating.
