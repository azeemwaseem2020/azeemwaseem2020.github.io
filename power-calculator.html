Power versus energy

Energy describes how much work or energy is transferred; power describes the rate of transfer. A higher power can mean the same energy is transferred in less time.

Common mistakes

Power Calculator: Formula, Examples and Unit Conversions

Power describes the rate at which work is performed or energy is transferred. The same job can require different power depending on how quickly it is completed. The calculator uses average power from work or energy and time, so the result describes the interval entered rather than every instant of a changing process.

Formula and definitions

P = W/t, where P is power in watts (W), W is work or energy transferred in joules (J), and t is elapsed time in seconds (s). Rearranging gives W = Pt and t = W/P. One watt is one joule per second. The denominator must be positive for a normal elapsed-time calculation, and time cannot be zero when calculating P from W/t.

Worked example

If 1,000 J of work is completed in 10 seconds, P = 1,000 ÷ 10 = 100 W. If a 250 W device transfers energy at that average rate for 40 seconds, W = 250 × 40 = 10,000 J. These examples assume the stated power remains representative over the interval.

How to use the calculator

  1. Select the unknown quantity: power, work/energy or time.
  2. Enter the known quantities in joules and seconds, or convert them first.
  3. Calculate and verify the output unit.
  4. Use a reasonable number of significant figures based on the measurements.
  5. Label the result as average input or output power when that distinction matters.

Features and everyday uses

The calculator is useful for physics exercises, comparing mechanical tasks, estimating energy transfer and interpreting device ratings. It can show how doing the same work in half the time requires twice the average power. Unit conversion is essential: 1 kJ = 1,000 J, one minute = 60 seconds, and 1 kW = 1,000 W. For electrical energy, 1 kWh equals 3.6 million joules.

Interpretation and limits

For electrical circuits, the appropriate formula depends on circuit type and measured quantities. DC power may be calculated with P = VI; AC calculations can require RMS values and power factor. This work-time calculator does not estimate efficiency, current, electricity cost or a device’s safe operating limit unless those factors are modelled separately.

Frequently asked questions

Can power be negative?

In signed physical conventions, negative power can represent energy flowing out of a chosen system; this simple calculator may treat inputs as magnitudes.

How do I convert watts to kilowatts?

Divide watts by 1,000. For example, 750 W is 0.75 kW.

Does a 100 W device always use 100 Wh per hour?

Only if its actual power draw averages 100 W throughout that hour; ratings and real consumption can differ.
