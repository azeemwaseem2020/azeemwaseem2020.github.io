Pressure Calculator — Force, Area & Pressure

Calculate pressure, force or area using P = F/A with reverse solving, SI units, worked steps and practical checks.

Formula

P = F/A. Pressure is force distributed over an area. SI pressure is pascals (Pa).

Worked example

A 200 N force over 0.5 m² gives P = 200 ÷ 0.5 = 400 Pa.

Common mistakes

  • Using area in cm² without converting when a result in Pa is required.
  • Confusing force with pressure.
  • Using total surface area when the problem specifies the contact area.

Pressure Calculator Guide

Use this calculator to find pressure from force and area, or solve the basic relationship for a missing variable.

Formula

P = F/A, where P is pressure in pascals, F is the normal force in newtons, and A is area in square metres.

Worked example

A 200 N force distributed over 0.5 m² produces a pressure of 400 Pa.

Why area matters

For the same force, reducing the contact area increases pressure. This is why the same load can produce different pressures when it is distributed over different surface areas.

Unit check

Convert area to square metres before using the SI formula. Remember that changing a length unit also changes the squared area conversion.

Important limitation

The simple equation describes average pressure from a force distributed over an area. Fluid pressure, pressure varying across a surface, and stresses inside materials may require more detailed models.

How this calculation is checked

Keep units consistent, review the formula shown above, and check the result against the physical assumptions of the problem.

PRESSURE MODEL CHECK

Pressure describes force distributed over area

Pressure is P = F/A. The same force produces a larger pressure on a smaller area, while spreading that force over a larger area reduces pressure. The calculation describes the force component normal to the surface when that distinction matters.

Force direction

For contact pressure, use the force acting perpendicular to the area. A tangential force belongs to a different physical analysis.

Area matters

Convert the area consistently before dividing. A unit mistake in square centimetres versus square metres can change the result by a large factor.

Pressure vs stress

Pressure is commonly treated as an isotropic scalar in fluids, while solid mechanics may require a more detailed stress analysis.
