How to use the daily compound interest calculator

Enter the starting amount, annual interest rate, number of years, and any daily contribution. The calculator converts the years into daily compounding periods and estimates the ending balance.

Formula

For the starting balance, the basic model is A = P(1 + r/365)n, where P is the initial amount, r is the annual rate as a decimal, and n is the number of daily periods.

When you add a daily contribution, the calculator also grows those recurring deposits using the same daily rate. Contributions are treated as additions at each daily period.

Worked example

Suppose you start with $10,000, earn 8% annually, and leave the money for five years. The model uses about 1,825 daily periods. Any daily contribution entered above is added separately to the calculation.

Why your real account may differ

Banks and investment products can use different compounding frequencies, 360- or 365-day conventions, leap-year treatment, deposit timing, fees, taxes, or changing rates. Check the product's actual terms before comparing its statement with this estimate.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the rate as 0.08 instead of 8 when the field expects a percentage.
  • Confusing daily compounding with daily contributions; they are separate assumptions.
  • Ignoring fees or taxes when estimating a real account balance.
  • Assuming a calculator estimate is a guarantee of investment returns.

Before using the result

Check the amount, rate, time period, contribution, and compounding assumption. This tool is a mathematical estimate, not financial advice or a prediction of future investment performance.

Daily compounding explained

Daily compounding credits interest at a daily interval under the stated rate and day-count assumptions. For a nominal annual rate r compounded daily, a common model is A = P(1 + r/365)^(365t), although actual financial products can use different conventions.

Why the product terms matter

The quoted rate, compounding frequency, day-count convention, fees and account rules all affect the actual return. A calculator estimate should be compared with the product disclosure.

Daily compound interest FAQ

Is daily compounding always exactly 365 periods?

Not necessarily. Financial products can use different day-count conventions, leap-year handling or daily-rate definitions.

Does daily compounding create a huge difference?

For a fixed nominal rate, moving from less frequent to daily compounding changes the effective yield, but the size of the difference depends on the rate and time period.
