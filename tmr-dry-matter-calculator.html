TMR Dry Matter Calculator for Cattle & Buffalo

WHY THIS MATTERS

As-fed weight is not dry matter

A wet TMR can weigh much more than its dry matter content suggests. Tracking actual dry matter intake helps compare what animals consume with the ration that was formulated.

Formula: DMI/head/day = ((feed delivered − refusals) × DM fraction) ÷ animals.

DRY MATTER QUALITY CHECK

Use representative measurements, not assumptions

This calculator is only as useful as the measurements entered. Record delivered and refused TMR on the same as-fed basis, use a representative dry-matter result for the ration being fed, and keep the animal count consistent with the feeding period.

Same time basis

Delivered feed, refusals and animal count should describe the same feeding period.

Representative DM

Use a current sample that represents the TMR actually offered, especially when wet feeds change moisture.

Check refusals

Refusals cannot exceed delivered feed; unusual refusal levels may require investigation beyond arithmetic.

Interpret carefully

DMI is an observed calculation from supplied measurements, not a recommended intake target.

Why dry matter is worth checking

A TMR can look consistent by weight while its dry matter changes with moisture. This calculation uses the feed delivered, refusals, number of animals and measured DM percentage to estimate how much dry matter each animal actually consumed.

Why moisture changes the number

Two rations with the same as-fed weight can supply different amounts of dry matter when moisture changes. Recording delivered feed, refusals and a representative DM result gives a more useful arithmetic check.

How to use this cattle feed dry matter calculator

  1. Record the total TMR delivered to the group during the day.
  2. Record the as-fed refusals remaining before the next feeding.
  3. Count the animals that actually received the TMR.
  4. Enter a current TMR dry matter percentage from a representative sample.
  5. Calculate and compare the result with your farm's formulated plan.

Example

If 400 kg of TMR is delivered, 20 kg is refused, there are 20 animals and the TMR is 45% DM, consumed dry matter is 171 kg/day and estimated intake is 8.55 kg DM/animal/day. This is an arithmetic example, not a recommended intake.

Why moisture testing matters

Dairy rations are often formulated on a dry-matter basis but fed on an as-fed basis. Changes in silage or other wet-feed moisture can change the physical amount required to deliver the intended dry matter. Penn State Extension uses delivered feed, refusals, animal count and TMR dry matter to estimate actual DMI; University of Minnesota Extension also emphasizes monitoring feed dry matter because moisture changes affect feeding accuracy.

Important limitation

This tool calculates intake from supplied measurements. It does not determine a nutritionally appropriate DMI target, diagnose a feeding problem or prescribe a ration. Use current feed analysis and qualified nutrition guidance for formulation decisions.

DMI RECORD QUALITY

Make a dry-matter result traceable to the feeding record

A useful DMI estimate depends on consistent records. Use the same feeding period, animal group and measurement units for delivered feed, refusals, animal count and TMR dry matter. If the DM percentage comes from a laboratory or field test, record the sampling date because moisture can change over time.

Delivered

Record the actual as-fed quantity offered to the group.

Refused

Measure refusals from the same group and feeding period.

DM%

Use a representative dry-matter result rather than mixing percentages from unrelated batches.

Animals

Use the number of animals actually represented by the feed and refusal records.

As-fed vs dry-matter weight

As-fed weight includes water, while dry-matter weight excludes moisture. For example, feed at 40% DM supplies 0.40 kg of dry matter per 1 kg as-fed. Keep the basis consistent when comparing feed amounts, ration targets or farm records.

Core calculation: consumed as-fed = delivered − refusals; dry matter consumed = consumed as-fed × DM%; DMI per animal = dry matter consumed ÷ animals fed.

TMR DRY MATTER FAQ

Common dry matter and DMI questions

What is the difference between as-fed weight and dry matter?

As-fed weight includes the water in the feed. Dry matter is the portion remaining after moisture is excluded. The calculator converts consumed as-fed TMR into dry matter using the entered DM percentage.

How is dry matter intake per animal calculated?

Subtract refusals from delivered TMR, multiply the consumed as-fed amount by the DM fraction, then divide by the number of animals fed.

Why can DMI change when the TMR weight looks the same?

If TMR moisture changes, the same as-fed weight contains a different amount of dry matter. A current representative DM measurement helps detect that change.

Does this calculator give a recommended DMI?

No. It calculates observed intake from the measurements you enter. A suitable intake target depends on the animal, production stage, ration and other nutritional factors.

Dry matter calculation context

Dry-matter calculations separate the water portion of a feed from its nutrient-bearing dry portion. This makes dry matter a supporting measurement for ration comparison, intake planning and TMR formulation.
