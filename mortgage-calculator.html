Mortgage Calculator Guide

This calculator estimates a fixed-rate principal-and-interest payment from the loan amount, annual interest rate and term.

Worked example

For a 200,000 loan at 6% annual interest over 30 years, the estimated principal-and-interest payment is about 1,199.10 per month before taxes, insurance and other charges.

What is not included

Property taxes, insurance, maintenance, lender fees, changing rates and other housing costs can materially change the total cost. Compare the calculator estimate with the lender's actual terms and amortization schedule.

What a mortgage payment estimate includes

For a standard fixed-rate, fully amortizing loan with monthly payments, the payment depends on the principal, monthly interest rate and total number of monthly payments. The estimate shown here is based on those inputs; it should not be treated as a lender quote or a complete home-ownership budget.

Worked example

For illustration, a PKR 5,000,000 loan over 20 years at a nominal annual rate of 12%, with monthly compounding and payments, uses a monthly rate of 0.12 ÷ 12 and 240 payments. The resulting principal-and-interest payment is approximately PKR 55,054 per month. This is a mathematical illustration, not a current bank offer.

Costs and terms to verify

  • Check whether the quoted rate is fixed or variable and how often it can change.
  • Ask the lender about processing fees, insurance or takaful, valuation, legal costs and other charges that are not part of a basic payment formula.
  • For property finance in Pakistan, compare the actual product structure and lender documents rather than assuming every product follows a conventional mortgage model.
  • Review early settlement, late payment and repricing conditions before relying on an affordability estimate.

Frequently asked questions

Does the monthly payment include property taxes and insurance? Not unless those costs are explicitly included in the calculator inputs; the basic estimate covers scheduled loan repayment only.

Why might a lender quote a different payment? The lender may use different rate conventions, fees, payment dates, rounding or financing terms.

Can I compare variable-rate finance with this result? Use it as a scenario at the entered rate only. Future payments can change if the applicable rate changes.
