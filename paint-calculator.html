Estimate paint quantity from surface area

Paint planning starts with the surface area to cover. For a rectangular wall, area is width × height. Subtract large doors or windows when appropriate, then divide by the paint's stated coverage per litre and account for the number of coats.

Worked example

A 5 m × 3 m wall has 15 m² of area. If one litre covers 10 m² for one coat, one coat needs about 1.5 litres. Two coats would require about 3 litres before considering waste, surface absorption and the manufacturer's actual coverage.

Practical example

For a home, shop or farm building in Pakistan, check the product label because coverage varies by surface, primer, texture and application method. Keep a record of the paint type and batch if a colour needs to be matched later.

FAQs

Should windows be subtracted? Large openings can be subtracted for a closer estimate. Do two coats double the quantity? Approximately, if coverage is otherwise unchanged. Why can actual use be higher? Rough or porous surfaces and application losses reduce effective coverage.

Paint quantity estimate

Paint planning starts with surface area and the manufacturer's coverage rate. A basic estimate is area × coats ÷ coverage. Subtract major unpainted openings when appropriate and allow for textured surfaces, primer and waste.

Worked example

A 40 m² wall needing two coats at 10 m² per litre requires about 8 litres before practical waste. Rough or porous surfaces may require more.

Practical use

For a home or shop, check whether the coverage printed on the can is per coat. Keep primer and finish paint separate, and remember that application method affects actual coverage.

FAQs

Should windows be subtracted? Usually. Is coverage exact? No. Do all paints need the same number of coats? No; colour, surface and product affect coverage.
