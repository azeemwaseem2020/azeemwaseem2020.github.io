How paint quantity is estimated

A simple estimate uses paintable area ÷ coverage per litre, then accounts for the number of coats. Coverage varies by product, surface texture and application method, so the label’s stated coverage should be used when available.

Worked example

If the paintable area is 80 m², stated coverage is 10 m²/L and two coats are planned, the basic estimate is 80 ÷ 10 × 2 = 16 litres before allowing for wastage or touch-ups.

What can change the result?

  • Porous or rough surfaces can use more paint.
  • Multiple coats increase total quantity.
  • Doors and windows reduce paintable wall area.
  • Actual coverage depends on the product and application method.

Area, coverage and coats

Coverage is normally stated as an area per litre or gallon. Multiply the single-coat quantity by the number of planned coats. Subtracting doors and windows from wall area can improve an estimate, but porous surfaces may still require extra material.

Purchase planning

After calculating the theoretical quantity, compare it with available container sizes and the manufacturer’s coverage guidance. Buying whole containers may leave a small remainder for touch-ups.
