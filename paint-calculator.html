How paint quantity is estimated

A simple estimate uses paintable area ÷ coverage per litre, then accounts for the number of coats. Coverage varies by product, surface texture and application method, so the label’s stated coverage should be used when available.

Worked example

If the paintable area is 80 m², stated coverage is 10 m²/L and two coats are planned, the basic estimate is 80 ÷ 10 × 2 = 16 litres before allowing for wastage or touch-ups.

What can change the result?

  • Porous or rough surfaces can use more paint.
  • Multiple coats increase total quantity.
  • Doors and windows reduce paintable wall area.
  • Actual coverage depends on the product and application method.

Area, coverage and coats

Coverage is normally stated as an area per litre or gallon. Multiply the single-coat quantity by the number of planned coats. Subtracting doors and windows from wall area can improve an estimate, but porous surfaces may still require extra material.

Purchase planning

After calculating the theoretical quantity, compare it with available container sizes and the manufacturer’s coverage guidance. Buying whole containers may leave a small remainder for touch-ups.

Simple room example

If four walls have a combined paintable area of 60 m², coverage is 12 m² per litre and two coats are required, the theoretical quantity is 60 ÷ 12 × 2 = 10 litres. Add an appropriate allowance only after considering the surface and product instructions.

Paint Calculator: Coverage Formula, Example and Buying Tips

A paint calculator estimates how much paint is needed from the surface area, product coverage and number of coats. It supports shopping and project planning, but cannot know the exact texture, absorption, application technique or amount lost during work. Compare the estimate with the manufacturer's label and treat it as a starting point rather than a guarantee.

Formula and calculation method

Paintable area = total surface area − excluded openings. Paint quantity = paintable area × number of coats ÷ coverage per unit of paint. Keep area and coverage units consistent; for example, square metres divided by square metres per litre gives litres.

How to use the calculator

  1. Measure the walls or surfaces and enter dimensions in the units shown.
  2. Subtract large doors or windows if their sizes are known and the tool supports them.
  3. Enter the number of coats and the coverage rate stated on the paint label.
  4. Review the quantity and compare it with practical container sizes before buying.

Worked example

A room has 35 m² of wall area. If openings account for 4 m², paintable area is 31 m². With two coats and coverage of 10 m² per litre, the estimate is 31 × 2 ÷ 10 = 6.2 litres. This excludes any extra allowance for texture, touch-ups or absorption.

Interpretation, assumptions and common mistakes

Rough plaster, porous surfaces and major colour changes may need more paint than smooth, previously painted walls. Primer may need a separate estimate. Ceilings, trim and walls may require different products. Do not mix metres with feet or square metres with square feet, and do not assume a theoretical quantity maps exactly to available tin sizes.

Planning tip: Keep a record of the paint brand, colour code, finish and batch information for future touch-ups. Measure each wall separately when a room has alcoves, sloping ceilings or unusual openings, rather than forcing an irregular layout into a simple rectangle. For exterior work, also consider weather, surface preparation and the manufacturer's recommended application conditions. These checks improve the estimate without pretending that the calculator can predict every job-site condition.

Frequently asked questions

Should I include two coats?

Yes. Multiply the paintable area by the planned number of coats before dividing by coverage.

Should I subtract doors and windows?

Subtract larger openings when their dimensions are known; small details may have little effect.

Why might actual use be higher?

Texture, porosity, application method, colour changes and product-specific coverage all affect use.
