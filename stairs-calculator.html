CONSTRUCTION PLANNING

Stairs Calculator — Riser Count & Height

Estimate how many equal risers are needed to cover a floor-to-floor height. The result is a planning calculation, not a complete staircase design or building-code approval.

Calculate stair risers

For 280 cm of rise and a 17.5 cm preferred riser height, the estimate is 16 risers at 17.5 cm each.

Formula: number of risers

Divide the total vertical rise by the preferred riser height, then round up to a whole number:

Riser count = ceiling(total rise ÷ preferred riser height)

Rounding up ensures the selected count can cover the full rise without exceeding the preferred height. Calculate the equal height for the selected count with:

Actual riser height = total rise ÷ riser count

Both measurements must use the same unit. The result keeps that unit; for example, centimetres in produces centimetres out.

Worked example: 280 cm floor-to-floor rise

  • Total rise: 280 cm
  • Preferred maximum riser height: 17.5 cm
  • Count: ceiling(280 ÷ 17.5) = 16 risers
  • Equal riser height: 280 ÷ 16 = 17.5 cm

If the division is not exact, the calculator rounds the count up and divides the total rise by that whole-number count. For instance, a 275 cm rise with a 17.5 cm preferred height needs 16 risers, giving an equal height of about 17.19 cm.

How to use this calculator

  1. Measure the vertical distance between the finished lower and upper floor levels.
  2. Choose a preferred riser height based on applicable local requirements and the intended design.
  3. Select the unit, then enter both values using that same unit.
  4. Calculate and review both the estimated riser count and resulting equal riser height.
  5. Check the tread depth, stair width, headroom, landing arrangement, handrails and available horizontal run as part of the complete design.

Risers are not the same as treads

A riser is the vertical change in height between consecutive stair levels. A tread is the horizontal surface where a person places a foot. The number of treads can differ from the number of risers, especially where the upper floor acts as the final landing. This calculator estimates risers only; it does not lay out treads, landings or the full stair run.

Important measurement and safety notes

  • Measure finished levels: include planned flooring, tile, carpet or other finish thickness at both floors. Otherwise, the first or last riser may end up a different height.
  • Keep risers consistent: uneven step heights can increase trip risk. Resolve rounding through equal riser heights rather than leaving one step noticeably different.
  • Check local rules: permitted riser height, tread depth, headroom, handrails, guards and landings vary by jurisdiction and building type.
  • Check the whole layout: the number of risers alone cannot confirm that the staircase fits the available space or is structurally safe.

Do not use this estimate as a construction drawing. Have the complete layout checked against applicable building regulations by a qualified designer or building professional before construction.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the calculator round the riser count up?

Risers must be whole in number. Rounding up makes it possible to divide the full floor rise into equal steps that do not exceed the preferred maximum height.

How do I calculate the actual height of each riser?

Divide the total rise by the selected whole-number riser count. This calculator shows that equal height so you can compare it with your design target and applicable requirements.

Should I measure from the structural floor or finished floor?

For planning consistent steps, use finished floor levels and account for the finish thickness at both ends. Confirm the final dimensions on the actual project before setting out the staircase.

Does this tell me how many treads I need?

No. It calculates the number of vertical risers. Tread count and stair run depend on the layout, landing and upper-floor arrangement.

Does the result prove my stairs comply with code?

No. Building regulations depend on location and building type and cover more than riser count. Verify the complete design against the rules that apply to your project.
