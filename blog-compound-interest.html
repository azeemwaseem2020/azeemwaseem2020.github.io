Compound Interest Calculator Guide: Formula, Growth and Examples

Compounding means previously accumulated interest can become part of the balance used for later interest calculations.

Quick answer: a compound-growth result depends on the starting amount, annual rate, time period and compounding frequency. Use the same assumptions when comparing two scenarios.

What is compound interest?

With simple interest, interest is calculated on the original principal. With compound interest, accumulated interest is added to the balance, so later periods can be calculated on a larger amount.

Compound interest formula

For a standard fixed-rate calculation with no extra deposits or withdrawals, the common formula is:

A = P(1 + r/n)nt

P is the starting principal, r is the annual rate written as a decimal, n is the number of compounding periods per year, and t is time in years. The calculated interest is A − P.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the starting amount.
  2. Enter the annual interest or growth rate as a percentage.
  3. Choose the time period.
  4. Select the compounding frequency used by your scenario.
  5. Review the ending balance and estimated interest rather than looking only at the headline figure.

What changes the result?

Starting amount

A larger principal gives the calculation a larger starting base.

Rate

A higher assumed rate increases the projected future value.

Time

More years give compounding more periods to affect the balance.

Why compounding frequency matters

Monthly, quarterly and annual compounding can produce different results even when the quoted annual rate appears similar. Before comparing financial products, check whether the stated rate is nominal or effective and whether it is fixed or variable.

Worked example

Suppose the starting balance is 1,000 currency units, the annual rate is 5%, and the money remains untouched for 3 years with annual compounding. The formula gives approximately 1,157.63 as the ending balance, so the calculated interest is about 157.63. This is a mathematical example, not a promise of investment performance.

Simple interest vs compound interest

Simple interest keeps the original principal as the interest base. Compound interest allows previously accumulated interest to affect later periods. The difference becomes more noticeable as the rate, time period or compounding frequency changes.

What the estimate does not predict

Real financial outcomes can be affected by taxes, fees, inflation, withdrawals, additional deposits, changing rates and investment performance. Treat a calculator result as a scenario based on the inputs, not as a guaranteed return.

The compound-interest model

A = P(1 + r/n)nt

P is starting principal, r is the annual rate as a decimal, n is the number of compounding periods per year and t is time in years. Interest earned is A − P when no additional deposits or withdrawals occur.

Worked example

For Rs 100,000 at 10% annually compounded for 3 years, A = 100,000 × 1.10³ = Rs 133,100. The compound interest is Rs 33,100.

Compounding frequency matters

Monthly, quarterly and annual compounding can produce different future values even with the same nominal annual rate. Compare products using the same rate convention and compounding basis.

Regular contributions are a different model

If money is added periodically, the simple single-deposit formula is no longer sufficient. A recurring-contribution calculation must specify contribution timing, frequency and whether deposits occur at the beginning or end of each period.

Nominal rate versus effective annual rate

A nominal annual rate with monthly compounding is not numerically identical to its effective annual rate. For a nominal rate r compounded n times, the effective annual rate is (1 + r/n)n − 1.

FAQ

Does compound interest grow faster than simple interest?

Under the same positive rate and unchanged principal, previously earned interest can itself earn interest, producing compounding growth.

Why does compounding frequency matter?

More frequent compounding changes how often interest is added to the balance.
