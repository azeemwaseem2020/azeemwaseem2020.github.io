Split a bill fairly

A bill can be divided equally or according to each person's share. For an equal split, amount per person = total bill ÷ number of people. If a tip or service charge is added, decide whether it is calculated before or after tax and how it should be shared.

Worked example

A PKR 12,000 restaurant bill split among 4 people equally is PKR 3,000 each. If a 10% service charge is added to the bill first, the total becomes PKR 13,200 and the equal share becomes PKR 3,300.

Practical example

For group meals in Pakistan, clarify whether shared dishes, individual drinks and service charges are being divided equally. A weighted split is better when people ordered substantially different amounts.

FAQs

Can one person pay more? Yes, use a custom share. Should tax be included? Include it if the goal is the final amount due. How should rounding work? Keep enough decimal precision and adjust the final person by a small amount if necessary so shares sum exactly to the bill.

Splitting a Bill Fairly

A split-bill calculation starts with the amount that everyone agrees is shared. If a restaurant bill is Rs 8,000 and four people split it equally, each person pays Rs 2,000. If a service charge or tip is added first, calculate the adjusted total before dividing unless the group has agreed to split those items separately.

For unequal shares, assign each person a percentage or specific items. If two people cover 40% and the other two cover 10% each, the percentages total 100%. On an Rs 10,000 bill, the respective amounts are Rs 4,000, Rs 4,000, Rs 1,000 and Rs 1,000. The method avoids rounding disagreements when people contribute different amounts.

In Pakistan, restaurant receipts may include GST, service charges or discounts depending on the establishment and location. The safest approach is to use the final payable amount shown on the receipt when the goal is simply to settle the bill. If some people ordered expensive items separately, itemized splitting is usually more transparent than an equal split.

Rounding

Currency calculations can produce fractions of a rupee after percentage allocations. Round only at the final payment stage and make sure the rounded shares still add to the total. One person can absorb a one-rupee adjustment if necessary so the final amounts reconcile exactly.

FAQs

Should tax be split? Usually yes if it applies to the shared bill, unless the group agrees otherwise.

Can people pay different amounts? Yes. Use itemized amounts or percentage shares.

What if the total does not match after rounding? Add or subtract the small rounding difference from one share.
