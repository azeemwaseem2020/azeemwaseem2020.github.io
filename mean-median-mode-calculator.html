Mean, median and mode formulas

Mean = sum of all values ÷ number of values. Median is the middle value after sorting; for an even number of values, it is the average of the two middle values. Mode is the value or values occurring most frequently. Range = maximum − minimum.

Worked example

For 2, 4, 4, 7, 9, the mean is 26 ÷ 5 = 5.2, the median is 4, the mode is 4, and the range is 9 − 2 = 7.

Mean vs median

The mean uses every value and can be strongly affected by extreme observations. The median depends on the ordered middle position and can be less sensitive to outliers.

When there is no mode

If every value occurs only once, there is no mode. A dataset can also have multiple modes when several values share the highest frequency.

FAQ

How do I calculate an average? For the arithmetic mean, add all values and divide by their count.

What is the median of an even-sized dataset? Sort the values and average the two middle values.

Can a dataset have two modes? Yes. If two values tie for the highest frequency, the dataset is bimodal.

Mean, median and mode explained

The mean is the arithmetic average, the median is the middle value after sorting, and the mode is the most frequently occurring value. They describe a dataset differently, especially when the data contain outliers or repeated values.

Worked example

For 2, 3, 3, 4 and 18, the mean is 30 ÷ 5 = 6. The median is 3 because it is the middle sorted value, and the mode is 3 because it occurs most often. The large value 18 pulls the mean upward while leaving the median unchanged.

Practical example

For salaries, household expenses or farm measurements, the median can be more representative when a small number of unusually large observations exist. For classroom marks or repeated measurements, the mean is often useful when every observation should contribute equally.

Data-quality notes

Check for missing values, accidental duplicates and inconsistent units before calculating. A mode may not exist or there may be more than one mode.

FAQs

Which measure resists outliers? Usually the median. Can there be two modes? Yes. Can the mean be outside the observed range? Not for ordinary finite numeric data; it lies between the minimum and maximum.
