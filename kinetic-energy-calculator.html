Calculate kinetic energy, mass or velocity

Kinetic energy formula

KE = ½mv², where m is mass in kilograms and v is velocity in metres per second. The result is measured in joules (J).

How to calculate kinetic energy

  1. Enter the object's mass in kilograms.
  2. Enter velocity in metres per second.
  3. Square the velocity.
  4. Multiply the squared velocity by mass.
  5. Multiply the result by one-half.

Worked example

For a 10 kg object moving at 5 m/s: KE = ½ × 10 × 5² = 125 J.

Find kinetic energy

Use KE = ½mv² when mass and velocity are known.

Find mass

Rearrange to m = 2KE/v² when kinetic energy and velocity are known.

Find velocity

Use |v| = √(2KE/m) when kinetic energy and mass are known.

Check joules

With kilograms and metres per second, classical kinetic energy is reported in joules.

Why velocity matters

Kinetic energy depends on the square of velocity. If mass stays constant, doubling velocity makes kinetic energy four times larger. If velocity stays constant, kinetic energy changes directly with mass.

Kinetic energy and joules

When mass is in kilograms and velocity is in metres per second, the calculated energy is in joules. One joule is equivalent to one kilogram metre squared per second squared.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to square the velocity.
  • Using grams instead of kilograms without conversion.
  • Using km/h directly when the formula requires m/s for a result in joules.
  • Confusing kinetic energy with momentum, which uses a different formula.

When to use this calculator

Use it for introductory physics, mechanics, classroom exercises and simple energy calculations. It is especially useful when you need to answer questions such as how much kinetic energy does an object have?, what mass gives this kinetic energy?, or what velocity gives this KE?.

Important limitation

This calculator uses the classical kinetic-energy equation and is intended for speeds where relativistic effects are negligible. It does not calculate rotational kinetic energy or relativistic kinetic energy. The velocity result is a magnitude; direction is not represented.

Formula

KE = ½mv². Kinetic energy is measured in joules when mass is in kilograms and speed is in metres per second.

Worked example

A 10 kg object moving at 6 m/s has KE = ½ × 10 × 6² = 180 J.

Why speed has a large effect

Because speed is squared, doubling speed makes kinetic energy four times larger when mass stays the same. Doubling mass, by comparison, doubles kinetic energy when speed stays the same.

Unit check

Convert grams to kilograms and km/h to m/s before using the SI formula. Mixing units can produce a numerically plausible but physically incorrect result.

Important limitation

This is the classical kinetic-energy model. At speeds approaching the speed of light, relativistic energy equations are required instead.

ENERGY MODEL CHECK

Why speed matters so much in kinetic energy

Classical kinetic energy is KE = ½mv². Because velocity is squared, doubling speed makes kinetic energy four times as large for the same mass. This makes unit conversion and speed accuracy especially important.

Classical model

The formula is appropriate when classical mechanics is a valid approximation. It is not a relativistic energy calculator for speeds approaching the speed of light.

Direction is removed

Kinetic energy is a scalar, so opposite velocity directions can have the same kinetic energy when mass and speed are equal.

Unit discipline

Using kilograms and metres per second gives joules directly. Convert other mass or speed units before applying the SI formula.
