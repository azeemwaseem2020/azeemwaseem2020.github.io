Air Conditioner Electricity Cost Calculator

Estimate how much it costs to run an air conditioner per hour, day, month or cooling season. Enter the electrical watts from the unit label, or estimate input watts from cooling capacity in BTU/h and EER. Use the electricity price and currency that match your own bill.

What the estimate includes

  • Estimated electrical input and energy use in kWh.
  • Cost per active hour, typical day, 30-day month and entered cooling season.
  • An adjustable duty-cycle assumption for compressor cycling.
  • Your own tariff and currency label—no automatic exchange-rate conversion.

All example inputs are illustrative. Replace them with the unit label, measured consumption and tariff from your own situation.

Air conditioner running cost formula

When the electrical input is known, energy use (kWh) = input watts ÷ 1,000 × scheduled hours × active-power share. Estimated cost = energy use × your price per kWh. The active-power share is entered as a percentage, so 70% is treated as 0.70. Seasonal cost multiplies the daily estimate by days used per week and weeks used per year.

If you know cooling capacity but not input watts, this tool can estimate electrical input from EER: input watts ≈ cooling capacity in BTU/h ÷ EER. For example, 12,000 BTU/h ÷ EER 10 gives about 1,200 W under the rating conditions. Actual draw changes with model, compressor speed, outdoor temperature, thermostat setting, maintenance and operating conditions. If the label lists electrical input watts or measured kWh, prefer that information.

Example: estimate a cooling season

Suppose an AC has 1,200 W input, is scheduled for 8 hours daily, averages 70% active-power share, runs 7 days each week for 20 weeks, and your tariff is $0.18 per kWh. Estimated active energy is 1.2 kW × 8 × 0.70 = 6.72 kWh per day. At $0.18/kWh, that is about $1.21 per day, $36.29 over 30 days at the same schedule, and $169.34 over the 140-day season. This is an illustrative calculation, not a prediction for every AC.

What changes an air conditioner’s electricity use?

Electrical input, cooling capacity, efficiency rating, room heat gain, insulation, outdoor temperature, thermostat setting, filters, installation and runtime all matter. A larger BTU rating does not by itself tell you the exact electricity draw. Inverter models can vary their input instead of running continuously at one fixed level. For a more accurate estimate, use measured energy from a compatible energy monitor or the manufacturer’s published electrical input for the exact model.

How to use this for different countries

The formula works in the US, UK, Europe and elsewhere, but the currency label does not convert money. Enter the price per kWh in the same currency selected on this page: for example, dollars per kWh with USD, pounds per kWh with GBP, or euros per kWh with EUR. Electricity prices differ by country, supplier, plan, taxes and time of use. Use the rate from your own bill rather than assuming a national average applies to you.

Frequently asked questions

How much electricity does a 12,000 BTU air conditioner use?

BTU/h describes cooling capacity, not electrical input. If the unit has a 12,000 BTU/h capacity and EER 10, input is estimated at 1,200 W under the rating conditions. Use the unit’s electrical input label where possible and account for runtime and cycling.

How much does it cost to run an AC for 8 hours?

Multiply electrical input in kilowatts by eight hours and by your tariff per kWh. If it cycles or varies its power, adjust the active-power share or use measured energy for a better estimate.

Can I use this calculator in the UK or Europe?

Yes. Enter the tariff from your bill and choose the matching currency label. The tool does not fetch current prices or convert between currencies automatically.

Does the estimate include the whole electricity bill?

No. It estimates the air conditioner’s energy cost. Fixed standing charges, taxes, delivery charges and other household loads are not added unless included in the rate you enter.
