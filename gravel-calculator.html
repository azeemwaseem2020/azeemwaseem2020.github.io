Gravel Calculator

Estimate gravel volume in US customary units, add an optional planning allowance, and convert volume to approximate weight only when you know the material density.

Enter area, depth and an allowance to estimate gravel.

Density is optional because gravel weight varies by aggregate type, particle size, moisture and compaction. Use a supplier-provided figure; this tool does not assume one.

How to use this US gravel estimator

  1. Measure the total area in square feet; add separate non-overlapping sections for irregular shapes.
  2. Enter the planned layer depth in inches.
  3. Choose an allowance based on site conditions and supplier advice rather than assuming one percentage fits every job.
  4. Enter density only if your supplier provides a relevant value in pounds per cubic foot.
  5. Compare the volume estimate with supplier units, delivery minimums and compaction guidance.

Formula and unit conversions

Base volume (ft³) = area (ft²) × depth (in) ÷ 12. Cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27. Adjusted volume = base volume × (1 + allowance ÷ 100). If a reliable density is entered, approximate pounds = adjusted ft³ × density (lb/ft³), and US short tons = pounds ÷ 2,000.

Worked US example

A 240 ft² area covered to 3 inches deep has a base volume of 240 × 3 ÷ 12 = 60 ft³, or about 2.22 yd³. With an illustrative 10% allowance, that becomes 66 ft³ or about 2.44 yd³. The allowance is only an example; use site measurements and supplier guidance.

Important limits

This is a geometric volume estimate, not a recommendation for driveway depth, drainage design or load-bearing construction. Weight estimates are only as reliable as the density supplied and may differ with moisture and compaction.

How the gravel estimate is calculated

The basic volume calculation is area × depth. The calculator returns cubic units, so the area and depth must use compatible units. If an area is in square metres, for example, depth should be entered in metres to obtain cubic metres.

Allow for material variation

A calculated volume is a geometric estimate. Real gravel projects can need additional material because of uneven ground, compaction, settlement, irregular edges and differences in aggregate size. A supplier may also sell by weight rather than volume, so a bulk-density figure is needed to convert cubic volume into an approximate mass.

Example

An area measuring 20 m² with a planned depth of 0.08 m has a geometric volume of 1.6 m³. Before ordering, check the actual site depth and ask the supplier whether the quoted product is sold by tonne, cubic metre, bag or another unit.

Choosing the right depth

Depth depends on the intended use and the underlying surface. Driveways, paths, decorative beds and drainage layers can have different construction requirements. This calculator should therefore be used for quantity estimation rather than as a specification for a particular construction project.

Gravel Calculator: Estimate Volume Before Ordering

Gravel quantity depends on the surface area to cover and the planned compacted or loose depth. This calculator multiplies area by depth, so both inputs must use compatible units. If area is in square metres and depth is in metres, the output is cubic metres; if you use feet for both dimensions, the result is cubic feet.

Formula

Volume = area × depth. Convert depth into the same base length unit used to describe the area before multiplying. For example, a depth of 50 mm is 0.05 m. Multiplying an area in m² by 0.05 m gives volume in m³. Entering “50” while assuming the field means metres would create a result one thousand times too large.

Worked example

A rectangular driveway 8 m long and 3 m wide has an area of 24 m². At a planned gravel depth of 0.05 m, the theoretical volume is 24 × 0.05 = 1.2 m³. The order quantity may need to be adjusted for compaction, uneven ground, delivery minimums and material density. Do not confuse volume with tonnes; converting cubic metres to mass requires a reliable density for the specific aggregate and moisture condition.

How to use it

  1. Measure the total area to be covered; split irregular shapes into simpler sections and add their areas.
  2. Enter depth using the same length unit as the area measurement system.
  3. Calculate volume and check the output unit.
  4. Ask the supplier whether material is sold by volume or weight and what allowance is appropriate for compaction and waste.

Construction planning notes

Sub-base preparation, drainage, vehicle loads and local construction specifications can change the required depth and aggregate type. A calculator does not assess soil stability or drainage. For a large driveway, retaining area or load-bearing surface, confirm the layer design with a qualified professional. Order a sensible allowance only after measuring carefully; adding an arbitrary percentage can be costly and may not correct poor preparation.

Frequently asked questions

Does the result tell me how many tonnes to buy?

No. It estimates volume; a reliable material density is needed to estimate mass.

Can I enter depth in centimetres and area in square metres?

Convert centimetres to metres first so the units are consistent.

Does the calculator include compaction?

No. The result is geometric volume before any project-specific allowance.
