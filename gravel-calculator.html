Estimate gravel volume and material

A gravel estimate starts with the area to be covered and the planned depth. For a rectangular area, volume = length × width × depth. Convert the depth to the same unit as length and width before multiplying. If the supplier sells by weight, volume must then be converted using an appropriate bulk density.

Worked example

Suppose a driveway is 10 m long and 3 m wide with a 0.10 m gravel layer. The volume is 10 × 3 × 0.10 = 3 m³. If a supplier's quoted bulk density is 1.6 tonnes per cubic metre, the estimated material is about 4.8 tonnes before allowing for site-specific variation.

Pakistan project example

For a driveway, farm track or construction area in Pakistan, use the actual compacted depth rather than the loose pile depth. Ask the supplier whether the quoted density is loose or compacted. Transport and wastage should be budgeted separately from the mathematical quantity.

Accuracy notes

Gravel pieces contain voids, and moisture and compaction can change bulk density. Add a reasonable site allowance only after the base quantity is calculated; do not hide the allowance inside the depth.

FAQs

Can this estimate tonnes? Only when a suitable density is supplied. Why does delivered weight differ? Moisture and bulk density vary. Should depth be compacted depth? Yes, when estimating the finished layer.

Buying by volume or weight

If gravel is sold by cubic metre, the volume result can be ordered directly after allowing for practical waste. If it is sold by tonne, divide or multiply using the supplier's stated bulk density. Do not use a generic density when the supplier has a measured product density. For a 3 m³ requirement at 1.6 t/m³, the estimated mass is 4.8 tonnes. Moisture and compaction can change delivered weight and final layer thickness.

For driveways and farm tracks, check the finished compacted depth rather than the loose delivery depth. If several layers use different materials, calculate each layer separately so one material's depth is not accidentally applied to the whole project.
