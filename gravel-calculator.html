Gravel Calculator

Estimate gravel volume in US customary units, add an optional planning allowance, and convert volume to approximate weight only when you know the material density.

Enter area, depth and an allowance to estimate gravel.

Density is optional because gravel weight varies by aggregate type, particle size, moisture and compaction. Use a supplier-provided figure; this tool does not assume one.

How to use this US gravel estimator

  1. Measure the total area in square feet; add separate non-overlapping sections for irregular shapes.
  2. Enter the planned layer depth in inches.
  3. Choose an allowance based on site conditions and supplier advice rather than assuming one percentage fits every job.
  4. Enter density only if your supplier provides a relevant value in pounds per cubic foot.
  5. Compare the volume estimate with supplier units, delivery minimums and compaction guidance.

Formula and unit conversions

Base volume (ft³) = area (ft²) × depth (in) ÷ 12. Cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27. Adjusted volume = base volume × (1 + allowance ÷ 100). If a reliable density is entered, approximate pounds = adjusted ft³ × density (lb/ft³), and US short tons = pounds ÷ 2,000.

Worked US example

A 240 ft² area covered to 3 inches deep has a base volume of 240 × 3 ÷ 12 = 60 ft³, or about 2.22 yd³. With an illustrative 10% allowance, that becomes 66 ft³ or about 2.44 yd³. The allowance is only an example; use site measurements and supplier guidance.

Important limits

This is a geometric volume estimate, not a recommendation for driveway depth, drainage design or load-bearing construction. Weight estimates are only as reliable as the density supplied and may differ with moisture and compaction.

Gravel volume and weight: how the estimate works

This calculator uses square feet for area and inches for depth. It converts depth to feet before multiplying, so the base volume is returned in cubic feet and cubic yards. It does not convert metres or centimetres automatically.

Formula and worked example

Base volume (ft³) = area (ft²) × depth (in) ÷ 12. Cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27. For a 240 ft² driveway layer at 3 inches, the base volume is 240 × 3 ÷ 12 = 60 ft³, or about 2.22 yd³. With an illustrative 10% allowance, the planning volume is 66 ft³, or about 2.44 yd³.

When weight is needed

Gravel density varies with aggregate type, grading, moisture and compaction. If a supplier provides a suitable bulk density in pounds per cubic foot, enter it to estimate pounds and US short tons. Do not use a generic density as a guaranteed conversion; confirm the product and measurement basis with the supplier.

Choosing depth and ordering

Required layer depth depends on the application, base preparation, drainage and expected loads. This calculator estimates quantity only; it does not design a driveway base or assess soil stability. Measure irregular areas as separate non-overlapping shapes, add their areas, and confirm compaction and delivery requirements before ordering.

Frequently asked questions

Does the result tell me exactly how many tonnes to buy?

No. It estimates volume and, only when you enter supplier-provided density, an approximate mass in pounds and US short tons.

Can I enter depth in centimetres?

Not directly. Convert centimetres to inches first, or convert the full project measurements to the units accepted by the calculator.

Does the allowance account for compaction?

It applies a simple percentage to the measured volume. It does not calculate compaction; use a project-specific allowance recommended by the supplier or contractor.
