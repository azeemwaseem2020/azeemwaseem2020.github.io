Z-Score Calculator: Formula, Sign and Interpretation

A z-score expresses how far a value lies from a reference mean in units of standard deviation. It allows values from different scales to be compared when the statistical assumptions make standardization meaningful. A z-score alone does not establish whether a value is important, unusual or erroneous.

Formula

z = (x − μ) ÷ σ, where x is the observed value, μ is the mean and σ is the standard deviation. The standard deviation must be positive; if it is zero, the formula is undefined. A positive z-score means the value is above the mean, while a negative score means it is below the mean. A score of zero means the value equals the mean.

Worked example

If a test score is 85, the class mean is 70 and the standard deviation is 10, then z = (85 − 70) ÷ 10 = 1.5. The score is 1.5 standard deviations above the mean. It does not mean the student scored 1.5 percent higher, and it does not directly tell you a percentile unless you also make a distributional assumption, commonly a normal distribution.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the observation you want to standardize.
  2. Enter the mean from the relevant dataset or population.
  3. Enter a positive standard deviation calculated on a compatible basis.
  4. Read the sign and magnitude, then interpret it in context.

Interpretation cautions

For a normal distribution, a z-score can be mapped to a cumulative probability, but real datasets may be skewed, heavy-tailed or contain outliers. Also check whether the standard deviation is a population or sample estimate, and whether the observation and reference statistics came from the same measurement process. Do not label a result an outlier solely from a universal cutoff without considering sample size and subject context.

Frequently asked questions

Can a z-score be negative?

Yes. Negative values fall below the reference mean.

Does z = 2 always mean the 97.5th percentile?

Approximately under a standard normal model, but not necessarily for every dataset.

What if standard deviation is zero?

The z-score is undefined because division by zero is not valid.

Compare values on a common scale

A z-score expresses a value's distance from a reference mean in standard-deviation units. It can help compare measurements that use different scales, but only when the reference mean and standard deviation are meaningful for the value being assessed.

Example and interpretation

For a value of 85, mean of 70 and standard deviation of 10, z = (85 − 70) ÷ 10 = 1.5. The value is 1.5 standard deviations above the mean. It does not mean 1.5% above average. A percentile interpretation requires an appropriate distribution model; the familiar normal-curve interpretation is not guaranteed for skewed or unusual datasets.

Sample versus population standard deviation

Use the standard deviation that matches the method and reference dataset. A sample standard deviation estimates population variability, while a population standard deviation describes the full population under consideration. Mixing a statistic from one group with the mean of another can make the result misleading.

Limits and data quality

A large absolute z-score may flag a value for review, but it does not prove that the observation is erroneous or that it should be removed. Check measurement quality, context, sample size and distribution shape before drawing conclusions.
