What a z-score tells you

A z-score expresses how far a value is from a mean in standard-deviation units. It makes values from different normal distributions easier to compare when their means and standard deviations are known. A positive z-score is above the mean; a negative value is below it.

Formula

z = (x − μ) ÷ σ, where x is the observation, μ is the mean and σ is the standard deviation. The standard deviation must be positive.

Worked example

If a test score is 70, the mean is 60 and the standard deviation is 5, z = (70−60) ÷ 5 = 2. The score is two standard deviations above the mean.

Pakistan-specific example

A student comparing a test result with a class distribution in a Pakistani college can use a z-score when the relevant mean and standard deviation are defined. A z-score does not by itself determine a grade or merit position.

Common questions

Can standard deviation be zero? No; division would be undefined.

Does z=0 mean average? Yes, relative to the supplied mean.

Does a z-score require normal data? The calculation itself does not, but many probability interpretations assume a normal distribution.

What does z=-2 mean? The value is two standard deviations below the mean.

Interpreting a z-score

A z-score shows how many standard deviations an observation lies from a mean: z = (x − μ) ÷ σ. A positive z-score is above the mean and a negative value is below it.

For a value of 70, mean 50 and standard deviation 10, z = (70−50)/10 = 2. That observation is two standard deviations above the mean. Interpretation depends on the distribution and whether the standard deviation represents a population or sample estimate.
