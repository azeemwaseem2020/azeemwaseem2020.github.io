How to interpret a z-score

A z-score expresses how far a value is from the mean in standard-deviation units. A positive z-score is above the mean, while a negative z-score is below it. The magnitude shows how unusual the value is relative to the distribution.

Example

If a score is 80, the mean is 70 and the standard deviation is 5, the z-score is 2. The score is therefore two standard deviations above the mean.

Why the standard deviation matters

The same raw difference can have very different meanings in datasets with different variability. A z-score standardizes that difference so values can be compared on a common scale when the assumptions are appropriate.

Z-score FAQ

Does a z-score prove a value is an outlier?

No. Whether a value is considered an outlier depends on the distribution, the chosen rule and the context.
