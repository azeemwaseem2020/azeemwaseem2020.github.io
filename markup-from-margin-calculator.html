Markup and margin are different

Markup is profit divided by cost, while margin is profit divided by selling price. To convert a target margin m to markup, markup = m ÷ (1−m).

Worked example

A 20% target margin corresponds to markup 0.20 ÷ 0.80 = 25%. On Rs. 100 cost, a 25% markup gives Rs. 125 selling price and Rs. 25 profit.

A practical Pakistan example

A Pakistani retailer can use this distinction when setting prices, especially when a supplier cost is known but a desired sales margin is specified.

Important assumptions

Do not use the margin percentage directly as a markup percentage; the denominators are different.

Why markup and margin differ

If the desired profit margin is m, the selling price required from a known cost can be calculated as selling price = cost ÷ (1 − m), where m is expressed as a decimal. For a 30% margin and PKR 700 cost, the price is 700 ÷ 0.70 = PKR 1,000. Profit is then PKR 300, which is 30% of sales but about 42.86% of cost.

This distinction is important for retail pricing in Pakistan because a “30% markup” and a “30% margin” produce different selling prices. Apply discounts, taxes and delivery charges separately according to the business's pricing policy.
