Convert profit margin to markup: complete guide

Profit margin and markup both describe profitability, but they use different denominators. Margin measures profit as a share of selling price; markup measures profit as a share of cost. Because selling price includes the profit itself, the same percentage margin corresponds to a larger markup percentage. This calculator converts a target margin into the equivalent markup.

Formula

Markup (%) = Margin (%) ÷ (100 − Margin (%)) × 100

The formula assumes the margin is between 0% and 100%. A 100% margin would make the denominator zero, so it is not a valid finite conversion.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the desired profit margin as a percentage, such as 25 for 25%.
  2. Select Calculate.
  3. Use the returned markup percentage as a pricing reference, then calculate a selling price from your actual cost.

Worked example

For a target margin of 25%, markup = 25 ÷ (100 − 25) × 100 = 25 ÷ 75 × 100 = 33.33%. If an item costs 75 currency units, adding 33.33% markup gives a selling price of about 100. Profit is 25, which is 25% of the 100 selling price.

Common mistakes and practical use

Do not treat a 25% margin as a 25% markup. A 25% markup on a cost of 100 produces a price of 125 and profit of 25, which is only a 20% margin. Include relevant costs such as packaging, payment fees, shipping, returns and overhead when defining the cost basis. Taxes and discounts may also affect the final realised margin. This calculator converts percentages only; it does not decide whether a price is competitive or whether your cost data are complete.
