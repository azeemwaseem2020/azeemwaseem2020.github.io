How a grade percentage is calculated

A basic grade percentage is percentage = points earned ÷ points possible × 100. If a student earns 42 points out of 50, the percentage is 42 ÷ 50 × 100 = 84%. The percentage alone does not determine a letter grade unless the school or institution has a grading scale.

Worked example

Suppose three assessments are worth 20, 30 and 50 points. A student earns 17, 24 and 41 points respectively. Total earned = 82 and total possible = 100, so the overall score is 82%. If the assessments have different weights rather than different point totals, use a weighted-grade method instead of simply averaging the percentages.

A practical Pakistan example

A college student in Pakistan may have a 40-mark midterm and a 60-mark final. If the student scores 31 and 47, the combined result is 78 out of 100, or 78%. The institution's published rules determine whether 78% maps to a particular letter grade, GPA or division.

Weighted grades

When assignments contribute different percentages to the final result, calculate each component's contribution separately. For example, an assignment worth 20% with a score of 80% contributes 16 percentage points to the final grade. Repeat for every component and add the contributions.

Common mistakes

Do not average percentages when the assessments have different maximum marks or weights. Also check whether an institution uses rounding, minimum passing marks, practical components or separate theory and lab requirements.

Grade FAQ

Is 80% always an A? No. Grading scales vary by school, board and university.

Should I average test percentages? Only when the tests have equal weight. Otherwise use their marks or stated weights.

Can this predict GPA? Only if the institution's conversion rules are known and applied.

Why can my percentage differ from my final grade? Letter grades and GPA may use institutional thresholds, weighting and rounding rules.
