Grade Calculator

Calculate your marks percentage from the score you earned and the maximum available marks. The optional letter estimate is only a guide because grading policies differ between institutions.

Calculate marks percentage

78 out of 100 is 78%.

Formula

Percentage = (marks obtained ÷ total possible marks) × 100. The total must represent the maximum marks for that same assessment. The calculator accepts decimal marks and any positive total, not just tests marked out of 100.

Worked examples

  • 78 out of 100: 78 ÷ 100 × 100 = 78%.
  • 42 out of 60: 42 ÷ 60 × 100 = 70%.
  • 36 out of 40: 36 ÷ 40 × 100 = 90%.

These examples show why the denominator matters. A score of 42 is not automatically 42%; the percentage depends on the total possible marks.

How to use this calculator

  1. Enter the marks you earned for the assessment.
  2. Enter the maximum marks available for that same assessment.
  3. Select whether you want an illustrative letter estimate or percentage only.
  4. Calculate and check that the score is between zero and the maximum.
  5. Compare any letter grade with the official grading table from your school, board or university.

How letter grades and weighted grades differ

There is no universal conversion from percentage to letter grade. A percentage that is an A in one institution may be a B or another grade elsewhere. The example scale shown here is deliberately labelled illustrative and must not be treated as an official result, admission decision or transcript grade.

Course grades may also be weighted. If a final exam contributes 60% and coursework contributes 40%, each component contributes a share of the final mark according to its weight. Do not simply add raw scores from assessments with different maximum marks or weights. Use the institution's published weighting rules and grade boundaries for an accurate course calculation.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the number of questions instead of the maximum marks when questions have different values.
  • Using a total from a different assessment or combining marks with different weightings.
  • Assuming all schools, examination boards or universities use the same grade boundaries.
  • Rounding intermediate values too early. Keep the full percentage for calculations and round only when presenting the result.

Frequently asked questions

Can I calculate a percentage for a test out of 60 or 150?

Yes. Enter the score and the actual maximum marks. The calculator uses the same formula regardless of the total.

Is the letter grade official?

No. Any letter estimate is illustrative. Use the grading policy published by your institution for official interpretation.

Can I calculate a final course grade with weighted assignments?

This simple tool calculates one score's percentage. For a weighted course, multiply each component score percentage by its stated course weight, then add the weighted contributions. Make sure the weights use a consistent scale and total 100% when that is the institution's rule.

What if my marks are greater than the total?

Check that you entered the maximum marks for the same assessment and that bonus marks or penalties are handled according to the official policy. This calculator rejects a score above the entered total to avoid presenting a misleading percentage.
