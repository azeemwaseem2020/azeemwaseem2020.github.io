How Reducing-Balance EMI Works

An EMI is a fixed periodic payment in a common amortizing loan structure. The interest portion falls over time because the outstanding principal falls after each payment.

The monthly EMI formula

For principal P, monthly rate r and number of payments n, the standard reducing-balance payment is E = P × r(1+r)n ÷ ((1+r)n−1). If the rate is zero, the payment is simply P ÷ n.

Why annual interest must be converted

If a quoted annual rate is used as a nominal rate with monthly payments, the calculator generally converts it to a monthly rate before applying the amortization formula. The exact contract convention should always be checked.

A simple example

For a Rs. 1,000,000 loan at 12% nominal annual interest over 5 years, a standard monthly-rate calculation uses r = 0.12/12 and n = 60. The payment is then derived from the formula rather than by simply dividing principal plus a flat annual interest amount.

Reducing balance vs flat rate

With a flat-rate method, interest is often calculated against the original principal for the quoted period. With reducing balance, interest is based on the outstanding balance. That difference can make two loans with the same headline annual percentage produce very different totals.

What an EMI calculator should show

  • Monthly payment
  • Total number of payments
  • Total interest
  • Total repayment
  • An amortization schedule when appropriate

Check the contract, not just the calculator

Processing fees, insurance, late charges, variable rates, KIBOR-linked pricing and early-settlement rules can change the actual cash cost.
