Calculation context

Future-value calculations estimate how a current amount and optional recurring contributions may grow over time under an assumed return and compounding schedule. This is distinct from ROI, which measures return relative to cost, and from loan calculators, which model borrowing.

Future value formula and worked example

Future value estimates what a current amount or a stream of deposits can grow to after interest or investment returns. For a single amount with periodic compounding, the standard formula is FV = PV × (1 + r/n)^(nt), where PV is present value, r is the annual rate as a decimal, n is the number of compounding periods per year, and t is years.

Worked example

If PKR 100,000 earns 10% annually compounded monthly for two years, the calculation applies 0.10/12 to each of 24 periods. The resulting future value is about PKR 122,060. The exact figure changes if deposits are made during the period rather than invested as one amount at the start.

Pakistan savings example

For a savings or investment plan in Pakistan, keep the quoted annual rate, compounding frequency and deposit timing separate. A bank's quoted rate may not equal the effective annual return, and taxes, fees and inflation can reduce the amount you can actually keep or spend.

Limitations

This calculation assumes the stated rate remains constant and ignores taxes, fees and changes in purchasing power unless you model them separately. For recurring contributions, use a calculator designed for periodic deposits rather than treating every deposit as an initial lump sum.

FAQs

Does compounding frequency matter? Yes. More frequent compounding changes the result. Can future value be lower? Yes, when the rate is negative or when losses are modeled. Is future value the same as profit? No; profit is the increase over the amount invested.
