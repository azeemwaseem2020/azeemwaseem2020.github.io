Using the feed cost calculator

Enter the quantities and prices you are actually using. The calculator adds the ingredient costs for the batch and then works out the daily group and per-animal cost from the feeding amount you enter.

From a batch price to a daily cost

The calculator totals ingredient cost for the batch and translates it into daily group and per-animal cost from the feeding rate you enter. This is a feed-cost calculation, not a complete dairy profitability model.

What this calculator measures

For each ingredient, enter its kilograms in the batch and current price per kilogram. The calculator multiplies those values to estimate ingredient cost, then totals the batch and translates it to a daily group and per-animal figure based on the feeding rate you enter.

Why current local prices matter

Feed economics can change quickly. A useful cost calculation should use the price actually paid or a clearly dated local market estimate rather than a fixed online price. This tool therefore asks you to enter the price yourself.

Example

If a batch costs Rs 40,000 and the group consumes 400 kg/day, the batch covers 2.5 days and the arithmetic daily group cost is Rs 16,000. For 20 animals, that is Rs 800 per animal/day.

Check the cost basis before comparing rations

Compare rations on the same basis: as-fed quantity with as-fed quantity, and the same price unit and time period. A cheaper cost per kilogram of feed is not automatically a cheaper cost per unit of dry matter or a nutritionally equivalent ration.

For a practical farm comparison, keep the ingredient prices, batch size, feeding rate, animal count and measurement date together. If feed moisture changes, the as-fed quantity and dry-matter supply can change even when the price per kilogram stays the same.

What is not included

The result is feed cost only. It does not include labour, medicine, electricity, equipment depreciation, transport, wastage outside the quantities entered, milk revenue or full farm profitability.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Example: compare two TMR batches fairly

Imagine Batch A costs Rs 30,000 as fed and contains 1,000 kg of feed, while Batch B costs Rs 27,000 and contains 800 kg. Comparing only total batch cost would make B look cheaper. A better first comparison is cost per kg as fed: Rs 30/kg for A versus Rs 33.75/kg for B. If their moisture differs materially, also compare cost on a dry-matter basis before deciding which feed is economically cheaper.

Compare feed cost on a consistent basis

Two TMR batches can have different as-fed weights and still supply similar dry matter. For meaningful farm comparisons, keep the price date, unit, batch size, ingredient quantity and dry-matter basis consistent. If moisture changes, compare both the as-fed cost and the dry-matter-adjusted cost rather than treating the two figures as interchangeable.

Cost per batch vs cost per animal

Batch cost is the total ingredient cost for the ration quantity entered. Dividing that cost by the number of animals served gives an estimated cost per animal; changing ingredient prices or ration quantities changes both figures.

TMR feed cost calculation context

Feed-cost calculations connect ingredient prices with the quantities used in a ration or batch. Cost per batch, cost per unit of feed and cost per animal can be compared when the input weights, prices and feeding assumptions are consistent.

Compare feed cost on the same basis

Cost per kilogram of as-fed TMR is only one comparison. When moisture differs between rations, compare the same basis before drawing a conclusion. For a dry-matter comparison, convert both the feed quantity and its cost to a consistent dry-matter basis using representative DM measurements.

Keep the price date, unit, batch size and ingredient weights with the calculation. This makes a farm-to-farm or month-to-month comparison easier to audit and prevents an old price or moisture assumption from being mistaken for a current result.

For feed-management context, see Penn State Extension's TMR guidance and University of Minnesota Extension's TMR guidance.

Calculation transparency

This calculator estimates ration cost from the ingredient quantities and prices entered. It does not determine whether a ration meets an animal's nutritional requirements. Dry matter, nutrient composition, intake, animal stage and local feed quality can materially change a practical feeding decision.

Use current feed prices and reliable ingredient analysis when comparing rations, and seek appropriate local livestock or nutrition guidance for important feeding decisions.

How to calculate TMR cost accurately

TMR cost is easiest to audit when every ingredient uses the same unit basis. Multiply each ingredient quantity by its current price per kilogram, then add the ingredient costs. A group cost can then be compared with the number of animals and feeding days.

Worked example

If a 500 kg batch contains 200 kg of forage at PKR 20/kg and 300 kg of concentrate at PKR 80/kg, the ingredient costs are PKR 4,000 and PKR 24,000, giving a batch cost of PKR 28,000. The average cost is PKR 56/kg before any additional handling or wastage assumptions.

As-fed cost versus dry-matter cost

Cost per kilogram as-fed and cost per kilogram of dry matter answer different questions. A wetter ingredient may have a low as-fed price but a higher cost per kilogram of dry matter. Keep moisture and dry-matter assumptions explicit when comparing feeds.

Farm record checks

  • Use current supplier prices and record the price date.
  • Keep kilograms, tonnes and bag units consistent.
  • Separate purchase price from transport or processing costs when needed.
  • Recheck the actual weighed batch against the calculated formulation.

TMR cost FAQ

Does the calculator prescribe a ration?

No. It calculates cost from the quantities and prices entered. Nutritional formulation is a separate task.

Can feed prices change the result?

Yes. The output is only as current as the ingredient prices entered.
