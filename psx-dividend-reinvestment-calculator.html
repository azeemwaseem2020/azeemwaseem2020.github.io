PSX Dividend Reinvestment Calculator — Shares, Income & Growth Scenario

Model how repeated dividend reinvestment could change share count and value when the dividend per share and reinvestment price are held constant.

Dividend reinvestment method

Core calculation

For each period, dividend cash equals the current share count multiplied by dividend per share. After the entered withholding assumption, the remaining cash is divided by the assumed reinvestment price to calculate additional shares.

Additional shares = shares × dividend per share × (1 − withholding ÷ 100) ÷ reinvestment price

Worked example

Suppose an investor starts with 1,000 shares, receives PKR 5 per share and reinvests at PKR 100. The first-period dividend is PKR 5,000, which buys 50 additional shares before tax, fees or fractional-share rules. If the same assumptions repeat, the next period starts with 1,050 shares.

How to use it responsibly

Enter the holding and assumptions from your own records. Run more than one reinvestment-price scenario and compare the output with actual dividend announcements, broker statements and applicable tax rules. Treat the result as a mathematical scenario, not a promised return.

Enter your dividend reinvestment details

How to interpret a dividend reinvestment scenario

This calculator repeats the dividend per share, reinvestment price and withholding percentage you enter for each period. It isolates the arithmetic effect of reinvesting cash, but does not simulate changing company payouts, changing market prices, payment dates, brokerage charges or a broker's share-rounding rules.

Worked example after an entered withholding assumption

Suppose you hold 1,000 shares and enter a dividend of PKR 5 per share. The gross cash assumption is PKR 5,000. With a hypothetical 10% withholding assumption, PKR 4,500 remains to reinvest. At a reinvestment price of PKR 100, that buys 45 additional shares in the model, taking the holding to 1,045 shares. The 10% figure is an example input, not a statement of the current tax rate or the rate applicable to you.

Test more than one reinvestment price

At the same net dividend cash, a lower entered share price produces more additional shares and a higher price produces fewer. That arithmetic does not establish which scenario will earn a better total return: future share price, dividend policy and timing may all change. Compare scenarios with the same starting holding and period assumptions so you can identify which input caused the difference.

Fractional shares and real-world implementation

The model allows fractional shares to illustrate compounding arithmetic. A real purchase may be limited to whole shares or subject to broker-specific procedures, transaction costs and available-cash constraints. If your broker does not support fractional purchases, the actual number of shares bought may be lower and some dividend cash may remain uninvested. This tool does not calculate that residual cash.

Keep yield, cash income and total return separate

Dividend yield relates a dividend-per-share figure to a chosen share price. Reinvestment modelling asks how the entered dividend cash could change the share count. Neither measure alone captures the full investment outcome: price changes, fees, taxes, payment timing and whether the dividend is actually declared and received also matter.

What to verify before using an actual holding

  • Confirm the declared dividend per share, entitlement conditions and payment status from the company announcement and your broker or CDC records.
  • Use a clearly defined reinvestment price and period; do not assume the displayed price is a live PSX quote.
  • Enter a withholding assumption only after checking current rules relevant to your circumstances.
  • Compare the model with actual credited cash and executed transactions; do not treat an illustrative result as a broker statement or tax calculation.

This is an educational scenario calculator, not an investment recommendation or a prediction of future dividends, prices or returns.
