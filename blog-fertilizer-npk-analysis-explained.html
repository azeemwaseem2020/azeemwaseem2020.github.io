NPK Fertilizer Analysis Explained: N, P₂O₅ and K₂O

An NPK label describes nutrient percentages by mass. The fertilizer product weight and the nutrient target are different numbers, and confusing them is a common source of application errors.

What 46-0-0 means

Urea labeled 46-0-0 contains 46% nitrogen by weight under the conventional fertilizer analysis system. One 50 kg bag therefore contains about 23 kg of nitrogen before considering losses or field efficiency.

What 18-46-0 means

DAP labeled 18-46-0 is approximately 18% N and 46% P₂O₅ by mass. A 50 kg bag therefore contains about 9 kg N and 23 kg P₂O₅.

Why product rate and nutrient rate are different

If a field target is 23 kg N and you use 46% N fertilizer, the simple product requirement is 23 ÷ 0.46 = 50 kg product. The calculation must be repeated for each nutrient when a product supplies more than one nutrient.

P₂O₅ and K₂O are label conventions

Fertilizer analysis commonly reports phosphorus and potassium as oxide equivalents. Do not silently treat the number printed as P or K elemental weight. If an agronomic recommendation is expressed in elemental P or K, use the appropriate conversion.

Fertilizer calculations do not decide the agronomic target

The correct nutrient target depends on crop, soil test, expected yield, timing, irrigation and local recommendations. A calculator can translate a target into product quantities, but it should not invent the target.
