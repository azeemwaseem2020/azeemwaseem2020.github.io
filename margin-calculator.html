Profit margin calculation

Profit margin expresses profit as a percentage of revenue. The basic formula is profit margin = (revenue − cost) ÷ revenue × 100. It is different from markup, which divides profit by cost. Keeping these denominators separate prevents one of the most common pricing errors.

Worked example

If a product sells for PKR 1,000 and its cost is PKR 700, profit is PKR 300. The margin is 300 ÷ 1,000 × 100 = 30%. The markup on the same transaction is 300 ÷ 700 × 100 ≈ 42.86%.

Pakistan business example

A retailer can use margin to compare product profitability after considering purchase cost. For a fuller business view, include relevant operating costs separately rather than treating gross product margin as net profit.

Accuracy notes

Use revenue before the specific deductions defined by your accounting method. Taxes, returns, discounts, shipping and overhead can change the meaning of the figures.

FAQs

Is margin the same as markup? No. Can margin be negative? Yes, when cost exceeds revenue. Why does margin matter? It shows profit relative to sales rather than only the absolute profit amount.

Margin versus markup in pricing

Margin uses revenue as the denominator, while markup uses cost. If cost is PKR 800 and selling price is PKR 1,000, profit is PKR 200, margin is 20% and markup is 25%. A business that wants a 25% margin cannot simply add 25% to cost; the required price is cost ÷ 0.75.

For Pakistani retail or service pricing, decide whether the analysis is gross product margin or net margin after operating expenses. Taxes, discounts, returns, delivery and payment fees may need separate treatment. Consistent definitions are more important than adding extra decimal places.
