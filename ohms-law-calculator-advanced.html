Formula

Ohm's law: V = I × R. Therefore I = V ÷ R and R = V ÷ I.

How to use the advanced Ohm's Law calculator

  1. Enter any two of voltage, current and resistance.
  2. Leave the unknown field empty.
  3. Select Solve and review all three values.

Worked example

With 12 V and 6 Ω, current is 2 A because I = V ÷ R.

FAQ

How many values do I enter? Exactly two positive values.

Can Ohm's law apply to every component? No. The simple linear relationship is appropriate for ohmic conditions; some components have non-linear behavior.

Advanced electrical relationships

Use this page when an electrical problem needs more context than a single Ohm's law calculation, such as connecting voltage, current and resistance with related power relationships.

Core relationship

Ohm's law is V = IR. The correct form depends on which quantity is unknown, while the circuit and component assumptions still matter.

For a direct voltage, current or resistance calculation, use the Ohm's Law Calculator.

ADVANCED CIRCUIT MODEL CHECK

Use the resistance model only when its assumptions fit the component

The advanced Ohm's law calculation can connect voltage, current, resistance and power, but it does not replace a circuit model. Series, parallel and temperature-dependent components require their own context.

Series and parallel

Equivalent resistance depends on circuit topology. Do not add resistances directly unless the components are actually in series.

Power check

Verify component power with P = VI and compare it with the component's rated limit when designing or troubleshooting a circuit.
