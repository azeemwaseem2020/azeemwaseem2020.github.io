ANIMAL FEED DATA STANDARD

Keep feed inputs consistent before using a result

For a meaningful calculation, record whether weights are as-fed or dry matter, use current ingredient analysis where available, keep units consistent, and use current local feed prices for cost estimates. Moisture, refusals, animal class and feed intake assumptions can change the practical interpretation of a result.

  • As-fed vs dry matter: do not combine values reported on different bases.
  • Ingredient analysis: use representative laboratory or supplier data when available rather than assumed nutrient values.
  • Animal group: identify the class and production context before interpreting intake or ration figures.
  • Cost: enter the price basis clearly and update it when market prices change.

These calculators support arithmetic and data checks. They are not a substitute for animal-specific nutrition advice, veterinary assessment or a professionally formulated ration.

Choose the animal-feed calculation you need

  • Ration quantity: scale a known formulation to the required batch or animal group.
  • Dry matter: separate as-fed feed weight from dry-matter intake.
  • Feed cost: convert ingredient quantities and entered prices into batch or animal cost.
  • Ration review: inspect calculated nutrient and intake values from your own feed data.

Professional TMR workflow

  1. Identify the animal group and the documented feeding target.
  2. Use current feed analysis where available and keep as-fed and dry-matter bases separate.
  3. Scale the known ration with the TMR calculator.
  4. Check dry matter and feed cost independently.
  5. Review the final ration with appropriate livestock nutrition guidance before feeding a high-producing, sick or special-needs animal.

Why TMR calculations need clear assumptions

Feed moisture, ingredient analysis, animal class, intake assumptions and ingredient prices can change the practical meaning of a calculated ration. Calcora keeps these inputs visible rather than presenting one unexplained number as a universal feeding recommendation.
