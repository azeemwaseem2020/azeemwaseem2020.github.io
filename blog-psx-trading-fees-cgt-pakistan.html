PSX Trading Fees and Capital Gains Tax: A Practical Calculation Guide

A share trade can look profitable before costs and much less profitable after brokerage and other charges. A good calculation separates each layer instead of hiding everything inside one percentage.

Start with the gross trade result

Gross profit or loss comes from the difference between the purchase and sale values. The first calculation should stay separate from brokerage, taxes and other transaction charges so the source of each deduction remains visible.

Where trading fees enter the calculation

Brokerage and other applicable charges can reduce the amount left from a profitable trade. The exact charges depend on the transaction and current applicable terms. Use the rate or amount shown on your broker's contract note or current documentation rather than assuming one universal fee.

Net profit after fees

A transparent workflow is: gross profit or loss → subtract applicable trading costs → arrive at the pre-tax net trading result → apply any relevant tax treatment. Keeping these stages separate makes it easier to audit a result when a contract note contains several charges.

Capital gains tax is a separate layer

CGT treatment can depend on the asset, acquisition/disposal facts, tax year and current law. Calcora's share CGT calculator therefore uses the gain and an entered rate as calculation inputs; it does not invent a universal current CGT rate.

Do not confuse calculation with investment advice

A calculator can explain arithmetic and costs. It does not tell an investor what to buy or sell, and a past return does not guarantee a future return.

Build a transaction-cost checklist

Before judging a trade result, record the buy value, sell value, brokerage or other disclosed charges, and the tax assumption used in the calculation. Keeping these inputs separate makes the result auditable and avoids treating a gross gain as the final return.

Verify the inputs

Use your broker's contract note and current official tax guidance for the rates and charges that apply to the actual transaction. The calculator should be treated as an arithmetic aid, not as a live market feed or tax assessment.

Worked example: audit a trade without assuming a tax rate

Suppose an investor buys shares for PKR 100,000 and later sells them for PKR 112,000. The gross gain is PKR 12,000 before transaction costs. If the contract note shows PKR 800 in combined applicable buy-and-sell costs, the gain after those costs is PKR 11,200 before any relevant capital-gains tax. These figures are illustrative arithmetic only; PKR 800 is not a claimed PSX or broker fee schedule, and the example does not establish the tax treatment of a real trade.

The audit trail should preserve the two trade values, the source of each charge, the date of the transaction, the share quantity and the tax assumption. This makes it possible to reconcile the estimate against the broker statement rather than trying to reverse-engineer one unexplained “cost percentage.”

How to read a broker contract note

  • Match the transaction: check the symbol, quantity, trade date, buy/sell side and gross consideration.
  • Separate charges: record brokerage and each disclosed levy or fee in its own row when the note lists them separately.
  • Check the basis: confirm whether a charge is calculated on buy value, sell value, or another stated basis before combining it.
  • Reconcile rounding: small differences can arise from per-trade rounding, so retain the broker's actual figures for final reconciliation.

Keep gross return, net return and tax distinct

Gross trade gain describes the price difference. Net trading result deducts transaction costs included in the model. Tax is a further calculation whose eligibility, rate and reporting rules must be checked for the relevant tax year and circumstances. Dividends, corporate actions, multiple purchase lots and losses may require separate treatment; do not assume that a single buy/sell example covers every investor's tax position.

When this estimate is not enough

Use an actual contract note for recorded charges and current official tax guidance for the applicable rules. If your records include several acquisition lots, corporate actions, withholding, dividend income or a special tax situation, keep those items separate and seek qualified tax advice where needed.
