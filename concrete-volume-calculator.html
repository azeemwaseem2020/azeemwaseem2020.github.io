Concrete volume formula

For a rectangular slab, footing or pad, volume is length × width × thickness. Keep all dimensions in the same unit before multiplying, then convert cubic units if needed.

Worked example

A 12 ft × 10 ft slab with a thickness of 0.5 ft has a calculated volume of 60 cubic feet. Ordering quantities may require a separate allowance for waste and site conditions.

Concrete volume FAQ

Does volume equal the amount to order?

Not always. Uneven excavation, spillage, formwork and project tolerances can affect the amount required.

Does this calculate mix proportions?

No. Volume and mix design are separate calculations.

Calculation context

Concrete-volume calculations turn the dimensions of a slab, footing or other shape into an estimated volume. Keep dimensions in compatible units and include an appropriate waste allowance when planning material quantities.

Plan a concrete order carefully

Convert every measurement to one unit before multiplying. If a slab is measured in feet but its thickness is entered in inches, convert the thickness to feet first; otherwise the result will be off by a factor of twelve. For a rectangular slab, calculate length × width × thickness. For a footing or several separate pads, calculate each section independently and add the volumes.

From cubic feet to cubic yards

Concrete suppliers in the United States commonly quote ready-mix concrete by cubic yard. Divide cubic feet by 27 to convert to cubic yards. For example, 60 cubic feet ÷ 27 is about 2.22 cubic yards before any ordering allowance. Confirm the supplier's minimum order, delivery conditions and recommended rounding rather than relying on the raw mathematical result alone.

Before ordering

Measure more than one point when the base is uneven, verify formwork dimensions and ask the site professional what allowance is appropriate. This calculator estimates geometric volume; it does not design a concrete mix, specify reinforcement or replace engineering plans.
