How to calculate concrete volume

For a rectangular slab, pad or footing, multiply length by width by thickness. Enter all three measurements in the same unit. If a drawing gives thickness in inches while the other dimensions are in feet, convert inches to feet first by dividing by 12.

Worked example: 12 ft × 10 ft slab

A slab measuring 12 ft long, 10 ft wide and 6 in thick has a thickness of 0.5 ft. Its measured volume is 12 × 10 × 0.5 = 60 ft³. Divide by 27 to get approximately 2.22 yd³ before any ordering allowance.

What the ordering allowance means

The allowance is shown separately so you can compare the geometric measurement with a planning quantity. It is not a recommendation to add 5% or 10% to every pour. Excavation variation, sub-base preparation, formwork, spillage, delivery increments and the supplier's minimum order can affect the amount to request. Confirm the final quantity with your supplier or site professional.

Frequently asked questions

Can I enter thickness in inches?

Choose feet and convert inches to feet before entering the thickness. For example, 6 inches is 0.5 feet. Alternatively, choose metres and enter all dimensions in metres.

Does the result tell me exactly how much ready-mix to order?

No. It calculates geometric volume from the dimensions entered. Supplier delivery increments, site conditions and project requirements may change the order quantity.

Does this calculator design a concrete mix or foundation?

No. It does not specify cement, sand, aggregate proportions, reinforcement, foundation dimensions or structural adequacy. Use the project drawings and qualified professional advice for those decisions.
