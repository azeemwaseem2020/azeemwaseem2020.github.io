Wavelength Calculator: Formula, Units and Examples

Wavelength is the distance between equivalent points on consecutive waves, such as crest to crest. It is represented by λ (lambda) and is used for sound, radio waves, light and other periodic phenomena. The appropriate formula depends on the known quantities and the medium through which the wave travels.

Core formula

For a periodic wave, λ = v ÷ f, where λ is wavelength in metres, v is wave speed in metres per second, and f is frequency in hertz (cycles per second). The related relationship is v = fλ. Frequency and wavelength are inversely related when wave speed remains constant: doubling frequency halves wavelength. Wave speed can change in a different medium, so wavelength may change even when frequency stays the same.

How to use the calculator

  1. Identify whether the given values are wave speed and frequency, or another pair supported by the tool.
  2. Convert speed to metres per second and frequency to hertz unless the calculator explicitly handles other units.
  3. Enter the known values and calculate.
  4. Check that the output is a length and that the values describe the same wave and medium.

Worked examples

Example 1: A wave travels at 340 m/s with a frequency of 170 Hz. Wavelength is 340 ÷ 170 = 2 m. Example 2: An electromagnetic wave in vacuum travels at approximately 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s. At 100 MHz (100,000,000 Hz), its wavelength is about 3 m. Example 3: If speed is 12 m/s and frequency is 4 Hz, wavelength is 3 m. Keep scientific notation and prefixes consistent to avoid errors by factors of 1,000.

Interpretation and limitations

Do not confuse frequency with period: period is the time for one cycle and T = 1/f. If period is known, wavelength can be found from λ = vT. For light in a material, use the wave speed in that material rather than automatically using the vacuum speed of light. Sound speed depends on conditions such as temperature and medium. Real signals may contain multiple frequencies, so one wavelength may not fully describe the signal. Measurement uncertainty in speed or frequency also affects the result.

Frequently asked questions

What is the SI unit for wavelength?

The metre (m), although nanometres, centimetres and other scaled units are also common.

What happens when frequency increases?

If wave speed is constant, wavelength decreases in inverse proportion to frequency.

Can I use the speed of light for every wave?

No. Use the relevant speed for the wave type and medium; sound and waves in materials travel at different speeds.
