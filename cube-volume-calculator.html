Cube volume formula

A cube has six equal square faces and all three dimensions have the same length. Its volume is V = a³, where a is the side length.

Surface area formula

The total surface area is SA = 6a². Volume uses cubic units, while surface area uses square units.

Worked example

If the side length is 4 cm, volume = 4³ = 64 cm³ and surface area = 6 × 4² = 96 cm². If the side length doubles, volume becomes eight times larger.

Unit guidance

Use one consistent length unit. If the side is measured in centimeters, the volume is in cubic centimeters and the surface area is in square centimeters.

Cube volume FAQ

Can I enter any unit?

Yes. The numerical formulas work with any consistent length unit; the output uses the corresponding squared or cubed unit.

What is the difference between volume and surface area?

Volume measures the space inside the cube. Surface area measures the total area of its six outside faces.

What happens if the side length doubles?

Surface area becomes four times larger, while volume becomes eight times larger because the formulas use a² and a³.

Why doubling the side changes volume so much

Volume grows with the cube of the side length, while surface area grows with the square. A cube with a 2 cm edge has a volume of 8 cm³ and surface area of 24 cm². Doubling its edge to 4 cm produces 64 cm³ of volume and 96 cm² of surface area: eight times the volume and four times the surface area. This relationship is useful when comparing storage boxes, packaging or scale models.

Converting cubic units correctly

Use the same length unit for every edge. If the side is in centimeters, report volume in cm³ and surface area in cm². Remember that 1,000 cm³ equals 1 litre, so a geometric cube of 10 cm on each side has a volume of 1,000 cm³, or 1 litre, before accounting for wall thickness or unusable space in a real container. Do not apply a linear conversion factor directly to a volume.

Calculation context

A cube's volume is calculated from its side length using V = s³. Because the dimension is cubed, the output unit is a cubic version of the input unit.

Cube volume: formula, worked example and measurement checks

A cube has six equal square faces, so its length, width and height are all the same. Its volume is found by multiplying the side length by itself three times: V = s × s × s = s³. The symbol s represents the length of one edge. The result is expressed in cubic units: if the side is measured in centimetres, the volume is cubic centimetres; if it is measured in metres, the volume is cubic metres.

Worked example

Imagine a cube-shaped storage box with each internal edge measuring 0.5 m. Its volume is 0.5 × 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.125 m³. Since one cubic metre equals 1,000 litres, this is a theoretical capacity of 125 litres. If the box walls take up space or the container must not be filled to the top, its usable capacity will be lower than the geometric result.

Measure the right edge

Measure one edge straight from corner to corner and confirm the object is actually cube-shaped. A rectangular box with different length, width and height should use the rectangular-prism formula instead. For a container, internal dimensions usually estimate capacity; external dimensions describe the space the container occupies. Keep the chosen measurement type consistent with the question you are trying to answer.

Unit conversion and practical limits

Volume conversion factors are cubed. For example, 1 m equals 100 cm, but 1 m³ equals 1,000,000 cm³—not 100 cm³. To convert cubic metres to litres, multiply by 1,000. Do not mix a side length in centimetres with another side length in metres, because the resulting number will not represent a consistent unit. Convert all dimensions to the same unit before calculating.

This formula assumes sharp corners and exact dimensions. Rounded corners, thick walls, insulation, fittings and manufacturing tolerances can change usable capacity. When estimating packing space or materials, allow for the actual object shape and the practical limits of how it will be filled. Record the side length and unit alongside the result so the calculation can be checked or repeated.
