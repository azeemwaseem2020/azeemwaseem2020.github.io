Frequently Asked Questions

What is running pace? Pace is elapsed time divided by distance, usually expressed as minutes per km or mile.

Does pace equal speed? No. Speed is distance per time; pace is time per distance.

How pace and speed differ

Pace answers “how much time per kilometre?” while speed answers “how far per hour?”. They describe the same average movement from opposite directions. The distinction matters when comparing a running target, a walking route or a race estimate. This calculator uses the total elapsed time and distance you enter; it does not predict fitness, fatigue, terrain effects or a runner's physiological limit.

Formula

Average pace = total time ÷ distance. If time is entered in minutes and distance in kilometres, the result is minutes per kilometre. Average speed in km/h = distance ÷ time in hours. For a target finish time, required pace can be calculated by dividing the target time by the planned distance.

Worked example

Suppose a runner completes 5 km in 30 minutes. Pace = 30 ÷ 5 = 6 minutes/km. Convert 30 minutes to 0.5 hours: speed = 5 ÷ 0.5 = 10 km/h. The pace and speed are consistent descriptions of the same average performance.

Pakistan-specific example

For a 5 km charity run in Lahore, Multan or Islamabad, a runner might plan around a 6:30 min/km target. Over 5 km, that target corresponds to 32.5 minutes. Actual time can change with heat, humidity, road surface, elevation and traffic conditions, so treat the result as a planning calculation rather than a promise.

Common questions

Is a lower pace faster? Yes. 5:00 min/km is faster than 6:00 min/km because less time is needed for each kilometre.

Can I enter miles? Convert the distance and use a matching unit, or use a dedicated conversion tool first.

Does the calculator account for hills? No. It calculates average pace from the numbers supplied.

Why can a watch show a different pace? GPS smoothing, route measurement and moving-versus-elapsed time can produce different averages.
