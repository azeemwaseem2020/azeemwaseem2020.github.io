What are significant figures?

Significant figures, or sig figs, communicate the meaningful precision of a measured value. They are different from simply counting decimal places because zeros can be significant depending on their position.

Significant figure rules

Leading zeros normally do not count. Zeros between non-zero digits do count. Trailing zeros after a decimal point generally communicate precision. Scientific notation is useful when you need to make the intended number of significant figures explicit.

Worked example

For 0.004560 rounded to 3 significant figures, the significant digits are 4, 5, and 6; the next digit is 0, so the result is 0.00456.

Why sig figs matter

In laboratory science, engineering, chemistry, and measurements, reporting more digits than the measurement supports can imply false precision. Match the reported precision to the quality of the input data.

FAQ

Are leading zeros significant? Usually no; they position the decimal point.

Do captive zeros count? Yes. Zeros between non-zero digits are significant.

How many significant figures can I use? This calculator accepts 1 through 15.

Significant figures versus decimal places

Decimal places count digits after the decimal point; significant figures count meaningful digits starting at the first non-zero digit. For example, 0.004560 has four significant figures but six digits after the decimal point. Written as 4.560 × 10⁻³, the intended four significant figures are explicit.

Rounding depends on the operation

For multiplication and division, the reported result is commonly limited by the input with the fewest significant figures. For addition and subtraction, the result is commonly limited by the least precise decimal place. This calculator rounds a single entered value; it does not infer measurement uncertainty or automatically apply rules to a full multi-step calculation.

Exact counts and conversions

Counted quantities and defined conversion factors can be exact, so they do not necessarily limit significant figures in the same way as measured values. Record how measurements were obtained and avoid implying that calculator digits are more precise than the original data.

Rounding example

The value 12.347 rounded to four significant figures is 12.35. The next digit determines whether the last retained digit changes. Different technical fields may specify particular tie-breaking conventions, so follow the convention required by your lab, course or standard.
