What are significant figures?

Significant figures, or sig figs, communicate the meaningful precision of a measured value. They are different from simply counting decimal places because zeros can be significant depending on their position.

Significant figure rules

Leading zeros normally do not count. Zeros between non-zero digits do count. Trailing zeros after a decimal point generally communicate precision. Scientific notation is useful when you need to make the intended number of significant figures explicit.

Worked example

For 0.004560 rounded to 3 significant figures, the significant digits are 4, 5, and 6; the next digit is 0, so the result is 0.00456.

Why sig figs matter

In laboratory science, engineering, chemistry, and measurements, reporting more digits than the measurement supports can imply false precision. Match the reported precision to the quality of the input data.

FAQ

Are leading zeros significant? Usually no; they position the decimal point.

Do captive zeros count? Yes. Zeros between non-zero digits are significant.

How many significant figures can I use? This calculator accepts 1 through 15.

Significant figures and measurement precision

Significant figures communicate the meaningful precision of a measured or calculated value. Nonzero digits are significant; zeros between significant digits are significant, while leading zeros usually only locate the decimal point. Trailing zeros may be significant when a decimal point or notation makes the intended precision clear.

Worked example

The value 0.00450 has three significant figures: 4, 5 and the trailing zero. Writing 4.50 × 10−3 makes that precision explicit. By contrast, 0.0045 has two significant figures.

Calculation rules

Multiplication and division generally follow the factor with the fewest significant figures, while addition and subtraction follow decimal-place precision. Keep extra digits during intermediate steps and round the final result.

FAQs
