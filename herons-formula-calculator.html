Heron's formula

For a triangle with side lengths a, b and c, first calculate s = (a+b+c)/2, then area = √[s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)].

Worked example

For sides 5 m, 6 m and 7 m, s = 9 and area = √(9×4×3×2) = √216 ≈ 14.70 m².

A practical Pakistan example

A student or survey trainee in Pakistan can use the formula when all three sides are known but a perpendicular height is not.

Important assumptions

The three side lengths must satisfy the triangle inequality; otherwise no ordinary triangle exists.

Heron's formula for triangle area

Heron's formula finds the area of a triangle when all three side lengths are known. First calculate the semiperimeter s = (a + b + c) / 2. Then calculate Area = √[s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)]. The side lengths must form a valid triangle, meaning each side is greater than the difference between the other two and less than their sum.

Worked example

For sides 5, 6 and 7, the semiperimeter is 9. The area is √(9 × 4 × 3 × 2) = √216 ≈ 14.70 square units. Because the formula uses all three sides, it is useful when the height is not directly available.

Practical example

A surveyor or builder may know three measured boundary lengths but not the perpendicular height. Heron's formula can provide the triangular area, provided the measurements are in the same unit. For land or construction work, measurement uncertainty should be considered separately from the mathematical result.

Common mistakes

Do not mix metres and feet, and do not enter a set of lengths that cannot form a triangle. Small measurement errors can also produce a noticeably different area when the triangle is close to degenerate.

FAQs

Does Heron's formula need the height? No. Can it handle any triangle? Yes, for a valid triangle with three known sides. What unit is the result? Square units corresponding to the side-length unit.
