Heron’s formula

First calculate the semiperimeter: s = (a + b + c) ÷ 2. Then the triangle area is A = √[s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)]. The three side lengths must satisfy the triangle inequality.

Worked example

For sides 3, 4 and 5, the semiperimeter is 6. The area is √[6 × 3 × 2 × 1] = 6 square units. This matches the familiar right-triangle result of (3 × 4) ÷ 2.

Invalid side lengths

If one side is at least as long as the other two combined, a triangle cannot be formed. A calculator should reject such inputs rather than return a misleading area.

Units

All three side lengths should use the same unit. If the sides are in centimetres, the resulting area is in square centimetres.

Why Heron’s formula is useful

Heron’s formula is especially useful when the three sides are known but the perpendicular height is not. It avoids constructing an altitude and works for scalene, isosceles and equilateral triangles that satisfy the triangle inequality.

Precision

Keep the side lengths at full available precision during the calculation and round the final area to the required number of decimal places.
