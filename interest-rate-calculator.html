Interest Rate Calculator

Find an implied effective interest rate per period from a present value, future value, and number of periods using a compound-growth model.

Interest rate formula

For a single effective growth rate per period, r = (FV ÷ PV)1/n − 1. Multiply r by 100 to express the result as a percentage per period. The formula assumes positive present and future values and a consistent period length.

Worked example

If PV = $10,000, FV = $12,000, and n = 2 periods, r = (12,000 ÷ 10,000)1/2 − 1 ≈ 9.5445% per period.

What this calculator measures

This calculator finds an implied compound-growth rate from two values. It is not automatically an APR, APY, loan rate, or inflation rate unless the period definition and compounding convention match those concepts.

How to use it

Enter the starting value, ending value, and number of equal periods. Keep the time unit consistent—for example, monthly periods should be interpreted as monthly periods throughout the calculation.

FAQ

Is the result an annual interest rate? Not necessarily. It is a rate per input period. Annualizing it requires knowing the period and compounding convention.

What happens if future value is lower than present value? The implied rate can be negative, representing a decline rather than growth.

Can this calculate loan APR? Not directly. Loan APR calculations can include payment timing, fees, and other terms.

How the implied rate is calculated

The compound-growth relationship is FV = PV × (1 + r)n. Rearranging gives r = (FV ÷ PV)1/n − 1, where PV is the present value, FV is the future value, n is the number of equal periods and r is the effective rate per period. The rate is only annual when each period represents one year.

Worked interpretation

If 10,000 grows to 12,000 over two equal periods, the implied rate is about 9.5445% per period. This is not the same as dividing the total 20% increase by two, because compound growth earns returns on earlier growth as well.

What the model excludes

The calculation assumes no intermediate deposits, withdrawals, fees or taxes and uses equal-length periods. If money is added or removed during the interval, use a cash-flow return method such as IRR with the dated or periodic cash flows instead. A loan APR may include fees and payment timing, so it cannot generally be inferred from only a starting and ending balance.

Interest-rate FAQ

Can the implied rate be negative?

Yes. If the future value is below the present value, the calculated compound rate is negative, assuming both values are positive.

How do I convert a monthly rate to an effective annual rate?

For a monthly effective rate r, the effective annual rate is (1 + r)12 − 1. Convert the rate to decimal form before applying the formula.

Compare rates only when their periods match

The result is an effective rate for one input period, not automatically an annual rate. For example, a 1% effective monthly rate compounds to about 12.68% over 12 months because the monthly growth is applied repeatedly: (1.01)12 − 1 ≈ 0.1268. A simple 12% nominal annual rate and a 12% effective annual rate are not interchangeable. Before comparing a bank offer, investment return or financing quote, confirm the period, compounding method, fees and cash-flow timing.

For uneven deposits or withdrawals, a single present value and future value may not describe the full cash-flow pattern; use a method designed for the actual dated cash flows instead.
