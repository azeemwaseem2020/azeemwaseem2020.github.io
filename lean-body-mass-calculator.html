Lean body mass formula

LBM = body weight × (1 − body-fat percentage ÷ 100). This simple calculation estimates the portion of body weight that is not body fat.

Worked example

At 80 kg body weight and 20% body fat: LBM = 80 × (1 − 0.20) = 64 kg.

Lean body mass estimates

Lean body mass (LBM) is the portion of body weight that is not body fat. Depending on the method, an estimate may be derived from body weight, height, sex and sometimes body-fat percentage. LBM is not identical to skeletal muscle because it also includes water, organs, connective tissue and other fat-free components.

Worked example

If a method estimates 70 kg of body weight with 20% body fat, a simple fat-free-mass calculation is 70 × (1 − 0.20) = 56 kg. Formula-based LBM estimates can differ because different equations use different assumptions.

Practical use

Fitness planning can use LBM as one reference for protein or calorie calculations, but it should not replace measured clinical information. Keep the same measurement method when comparing changes over time because switching formulas can create an apparent change that is only methodological.

Limitations

Height-and-weight equations are population estimates. Hydration, body composition, age and athletic build can make an individual result differ substantially from the estimate.

FAQs

Is LBM the same as muscle? No. Can LBM change without weight changing? Yes, because fat and fat-free mass can change in opposite directions. Is the result diagnostic? No.
