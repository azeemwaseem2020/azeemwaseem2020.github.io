Lean body mass: formula and interpretation

Lean body mass (LBM) is an estimate of body weight excluding body fat. It includes muscle, bone, organs, body water and other non-fat tissues; it does not mean muscle mass alone. This calculator estimates LBM from total body weight and a body-fat percentage.

Formula

LBM = body weight × (1 − body-fat percentage ÷ 100). For a body weight of 80 kg and estimated body fat of 20%, LBM = 80 × (1 − 0.20) = 64 kg. The estimated fat mass would be 16 kg, and fat mass plus lean mass equals the original 80 kg.

How to use it

  1. Enter body weight in your chosen unit, such as kilograms or pounds.
  2. Enter body-fat percentage as a number from 0 to 100.
  3. Calculate and read the estimated lean body mass in the same weight unit.
  4. Use consistent measurement methods when comparing results over time.

Accuracy and limitations

The result depends on the accuracy of the body-fat percentage. Consumer scales, tape-based equations and visual estimates can differ from clinical measurement methods. Hydration, recent exercise and measurement conditions may affect some estimates. Because LBM includes water and many tissues, a change in LBM is not automatically a change in muscle. This calculator is for general education and does not diagnose a health condition or prescribe a training or nutrition plan.

Frequently asked questions

Is lean body mass the same as muscle mass?

No. Lean body mass includes muscle but also bone, organs, water and other non-fat tissue.

Can I use pounds?

Yes, as long as the body weight is entered in pounds; the estimated LBM will also be in pounds. The percentage is unitless.

What if my body-fat percentage is only an estimate?

The LBM result will also be an estimate. For meaningful comparisons, use a consistent measurement method and avoid over-interpreting small changes.
