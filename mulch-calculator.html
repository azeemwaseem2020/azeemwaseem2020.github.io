How to use the US mulch estimator

  1. Measure the bed area in square feet and add non-overlapping sections for irregular beds.
  2. Enter the target mulch depth in inches.
  3. Enter the bag volume printed on the product label in cubic feet.
  4. Choose an allowance based on uneven ground and project needs; it is not automatically right for every garden.
  5. Use the result as a purchase estimate and follow local horticultural guidance for appropriate mulch depth.

Formula and worked example

Base volume (ft³) = area (ft²) × depth (in) ÷ 12. Adjusted volume = base volume × (1 + allowance ÷ 100). Bags needed = round up (adjusted volume ÷ bag volume). For a 200 ft² bed at 3 inches, base volume is 50 ft³ or about 1.85 yd³. With an illustrative 10% allowance, it is 55 ft³; at 2 ft³ per bag, that is 28 bags after rounding up.

Planning limits

This is a volume estimate and does not account for settling, moisture, uneven ground or product packaging variation. Check bag volume on the label. Keep mulch away from plant stems and tree trunks, and follow local horticultural guidance.

Mulch calculator: complete guide

Mulch is spread over soil to help conserve moisture, moderate soil temperature and reduce weed growth. The amount needed depends mainly on the surface area to cover and the planned depth. This calculator estimates volume by multiplying area by depth; it does not estimate bags until the volume is converted to the supplier’s bag size.

Formula

Mulch volume = area × depth. Use compatible units: square metres multiplied by metres gives cubic metres; square feet multiplied by feet gives cubic feet.

How to use it

  1. Measure the area to cover, splitting irregular beds into smaller shapes if needed.
  2. Enter the total area and desired mulch depth in compatible units.
  3. Select Calculate to estimate volume.
  4. Add a practical allowance for settling, uneven ground and waste before ordering.

Worked example

A bed covers 20 m² and needs a 0.05 m (5 cm) mulch layer. Volume = 20 × 0.05 = 1 m³. If the supplier sells bags of 50 litres, and 1 m³ equals 1,000 litres, the theoretical quantity is 1,000 ÷ 50 = 20 bags; actual needs may vary with bag fill and settling.

Planning tips and limitations

Measure depth in metres when area is in square metres: 5 cm must be converted to 0.05 m. Do not multiply area in square metres by depth in centimetres and label the result cubic metres. Different mulch materials settle differently, and established beds may need less depth than bare ground. Keep mulch away from plant stems and tree trunks and follow local horticultural guidance for suitable depth. The calculator estimates geometric volume only; it does not account for compaction, moisture, material density or delivery packaging.

Frequently asked questions

How deep should mulch be?

The appropriate depth depends on the material and the planting area. A common landscape guideline is roughly 2–3 inches for many mulches, but follow local horticultural advice and the product label. Keep mulch away from plant stems and tree trunks to avoid trapping moisture against them.

How do I convert cubic feet to bags?

Divide the required volume by the volume printed on each bag, using the same units. For example, a 2-cubic-foot bag covers 2 cubic feet at the stated depth; a 12-cubic-foot requirement would need 6 bags before allowing for settling or uneven ground.

Why might my actual mulch use differ?

Uneven ground, existing vegetation, compacted material and depth variation affect the amount needed. Measure the area as accurately as possible, calculate each separate section, and add the volumes together. Treat the result as an estimate rather than a guarantee.
