Pregnancy Due Date Calculator

Estimate an expected due date from the first day of the last menstrual period (LMP). The result is a calendar estimate, not a clinical due-date assessment.

How the estimate works

A common calendar estimate uses 280 days (40 weeks) from the first day of the last menstrual period. Actual pregnancy dating can differ when the LMP is uncertain, cycles are irregular, or ovulation occurred at a different time.

Important health note

This calculator is for general information and does not diagnose pregnancy, confirm gestational age, or replace prenatal care. A healthcare professional can assess pregnancy dating using the clinical history and, when appropriate, ultrasound or other medical information.

If you are pregnant or think you may be pregnant, use this estimate only as a general reference and discuss dates or concerns with a qualified healthcare professional.

Pregnancy Due Date Calculator: Formula, Dating and Practical Guidance

A pregnancy due date is an estimate of when a baby may be born, not a guaranteed delivery date. This calculator provides a planning estimate from the date entered. Clinical dating may be adjusted using menstrual history, ultrasound findings and advice from a qualified maternity professional.

How the estimate is calculated

For a typical 28-day menstrual cycle, the traditional last menstrual period (LMP) method estimates the due date as LMP + 280 days (40 weeks). This convention assumes ovulation around day 14. If the calculator uses conception date instead, a common estimate is conception date + 266 days (38 weeks). These methods use different starting points, so confirm which date the input field requests.

Worked example

If the first day of the last menstrual period was 1 January, adding 280 days gives an estimated due date around 8 October in a non-leap-year example. Calendar arithmetic and leap years can shift the exact displayed date; enter the actual year in the calculator. The result is an estimate of gestational dating rather than a prediction of the exact birth day.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the first day of the last menstrual period (LMP); this calculator uses the LMP method.
  2. Enter the date accurately, including the year.
  3. Calculate and note the estimated due date and any gestational-age information displayed.
  4. Compare the result with your healthcare provider's dating if an ultrasound or clinical assessment is available.

Important limitations and safety

Cycle length and ovulation timing vary, and some people do not remember their LMP precisely. Irregular cycles, recent hormonal contraception, breastfeeding or bleeding mistaken for a period can make LMP dating less reliable. Ultrasound-based dating may be more appropriate in some situations, especially early in pregnancy, but a clinician should interpret the findings. A calculator cannot confirm pregnancy, assess fetal growth or diagnose a complication.

Seek prompt medical advice for concerning symptoms such as heavy bleeding, severe abdominal pain, fainting or other urgent changes. Do not delay care while checking a date online. Use this tool for general planning only and follow local maternity guidance for appointments and screening.

Frequently asked questions

Is the due date the day labour will begin?

No. It is an estimate, and birth can occur before or after that date.

Why does an ultrasound give a different date?

Menstrual dating depends on cycle and ovulation assumptions; a clinician may adjust dating using ultrasound and medical history.

Can this calculator confirm how healthy a pregnancy is?

No. It estimates dates only and cannot assess maternal or fetal health.
