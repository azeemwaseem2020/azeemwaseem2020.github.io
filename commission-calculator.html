Commission Calculator

Calculate sales commission from eligible sales and a commission rate, with optional base pay.

Commission formula

Commission = commissionable sales × commission rate. In this simple model, total gross pay = base pay + commission.

Worked example

If eligible sales are $20,000 and the rate is 5%, commission is $1,000. With $3,000 base pay, gross base-plus-commission pay is $4,000.

Tiered commission plans

Real compensation plans may use thresholds, different rates by sales band, profit-based commissions, returns, discounts or excluded products. Apply the actual agreement rather than assuming every sale qualifies at one rate.

FAQ

Is commission always based on total sales? No. The commissionable base is defined by the compensation plan.

Can commission be added to salary? Yes, when the plan includes both fixed base pay and commission.

How commission affects earnings

Commission is commonly calculated as a percentage of an eligible sales amount, although some plans use tiers, fixed amounts or different rates for different products.

Worked example

If eligible sales are PKR 500,000 and the commission rate is 5%, the basic commission is PKR 25,000 before any plan-specific adjustments.

Check the commission plan

Real compensation may depend on returns, cancellations, payment collection, targets, thresholds, taxes and whether commission is calculated on gross sales, net sales or collected revenue.

Commission FAQ

Is commission always a simple percentage?

No. Many plans use tiers, quotas or different rates, so the actual plan terms should be checked before treating a calculator result as payroll.

Flat-rate commission example

If an agreed plan pays 3% on PKR 200,000 of eligible sales, the basic commission is 200,000 × 0.03 = PKR 6,000. If the plan instead pays 3% only on collected sales and PKR 20,000 remains unpaid, eligible sales would be PKR 180,000 and the calculation would be PKR 5,400. Which base applies depends on the written commission agreement.

Tiered commission and deductions

A tiered plan may pay one rate up to a threshold and a higher rate only on sales above it, or it may apply the new rate to the entire total. Those methods produce different results. Confirm how targets, refunds, cancellations, tax withholding and payment timing are handled. This calculator provides a simple percentage estimate and should not be treated as a payroll statement unless its inputs match the actual plan.

Match the calculation to the written plan

The simple result is commissionable sales multiplied by the entered rate. If base pay is included, this page adds it to commission to show an estimated gross total before payroll deductions. It does not calculate income tax, benefits, overtime or take-home pay.

Example with an adjustment

If eligible sales are PKR 180,000 and the commission rate is 4%, commission is PKR 7,200. With PKR 35,000 base pay, the simple gross total is PKR 42,200. If refunds or cancellations reduce eligible sales, calculate the commission on the adjusted base required by the actual plan.

Flat-rate versus tiered plans

Some agreements apply one rate to all eligible sales; others pay different rates by band, product, margin or target attainment. A threshold may affect only the amount above it or may change the rate for the whole period. This calculator handles a single flat rate only, so calculate tiers separately if the agreement requires them.

Before using the estimate for payroll

Check whether commission is based on booked, invoiced, delivered or collected sales, and how discounts, tax, returns and payment timing are treated. The result is a planning estimate, not a payroll statement or a substitute for the signed compensation terms.

Verify the commission base and timing

Before relying on an estimate, check the signed plan for the eligible sales base, rate, target period and payment trigger. Commission might be based on signed orders, invoiced sales, delivered goods or cash collected; these definitions can produce different results for the same deal. Confirm how refunds, discounts, cancellations and split-credit sales are treated, and whether the rate applies to all sales or only the portion above a threshold. Add base salary only if the calculator's fields and your plan call for it. Use the result as an estimate of gross earnings, not net pay after tax and deductions.
