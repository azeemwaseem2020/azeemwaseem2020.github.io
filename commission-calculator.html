Commission Calculator

Calculate sales commission from eligible sales and a commission rate, with optional base pay.

Commission formula

Commission = commissionable sales × commission rate. In this simple model, total gross pay = base pay + commission.

Worked example

If eligible sales are $20,000 and the rate is 5%, commission is $1,000. With $3,000 base pay, gross base-plus-commission pay is $4,000.

Tiered commission plans

Real compensation plans may use thresholds, different rates by sales band, profit-based commissions, returns, discounts or excluded products. Apply the actual agreement rather than assuming every sale qualifies at one rate.

FAQ

Is commission always based on total sales? No. The commissionable base is defined by the compensation plan.

Can commission be added to salary? Yes, when the plan includes both fixed base pay and commission.

How commission is calculated

A basic commission calculation starts with Commission = eligible sales × commission rate. If the rate is 5% and eligible sales are Rs. 200,000, commission is Rs. 10,000. Real compensation plans can be more complicated because they may exclude returns, use different rates for different products or apply thresholds.

Worked example

A salesperson completes Rs. 450,000 of eligible sales at a 4% rate. Commission = 450,000 × 0.04 = Rs. 18,000. If Rs. 50,000 of those sales are later cancelled and the employer calculates commission on net eligible sales, the base becomes Rs. 400,000 and commission becomes Rs. 16,000.

A practical Pakistan example

Consider a property or vehicle sales role in Pakistan where the written agreement specifies a percentage of the eligible transaction value. Before calculating expected income, check whether the agreement uses gross sales, collected cash, net sales after refunds, or another base. The percentage alone does not define the final commission.

Tiered commission plans

Some plans use one rate up to a threshold and a higher rate above it. For example, a plan might pay 3% on the first Rs. 500,000 and 5% on additional eligible sales. That is different from paying 5% on the entire amount once sales pass Rs. 500,000. Read the contract carefully before applying a tiered model.

Common mistakes

Check whether the rate is written as a percentage or decimal, whether returns are deducted, and whether tax or other payroll deductions are separate from the commission calculation. A calculator can perform the arithmetic, but the employer's documented commission rules determine what sales qualify.

Commission FAQ

What is the basic formula? Eligible sales multiplied by the commission rate.

Does a refund reduce commission? It may, if the compensation agreement defines commission using net eligible sales.

Is tiered commission the same as a flat rate? No. Different portions of sales can be paid at different rates.

Does commission equal take-home pay? Not necessarily. Payroll taxes and other deductions may affect the amount actually received.
