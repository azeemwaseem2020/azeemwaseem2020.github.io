Zakat Calculator Guide: Nisab, Assets and the 2.5% Arithmetic

The difficult part of a Zakat calculation is often deciding which value and method applies. The arithmetic itself should remain transparent and easy to check.

What Nisab means

Nisab is the threshold used to determine whether a person has reached the minimum level relevant to Zakat. A PKR threshold should not be treated as a permanent number because official deductions and valuation references can change by Islamic year and market conditions.

Pakistan Nisab reference for 1446–47 AH

The Punjab Zakat & Ushr Department currently states that the Nisab for 1446–47 AH (2026) is PKR 503,529. This is a government reference for that stated year; it should not be copied forward as a permanent threshold for every future Zakat year.

Official government source: Punjab Zakat & Ushr Department FAQ. The department states PKR 503,529 for 1446–47 AH (2026). This government figure is a dated reference; it should not be presented as a permanent religious threshold or a substitute for the scholarly method you follow.

Separate the arithmetic from the religious question

Calcora can add the values you enter, subtract the liabilities you enter and apply the 2.5% arithmetic rate when the net amount reaches the entered Nisab. It cannot determine every religious question about ownership, asset eligibility, valuation or debt deduction. Those questions should be checked against the method and qualified guidance you follow.

Assets to review before entering numbers

For an arithmetic estimate, the calculator provides fields for cash, bank balances, gold, silver, selected investments or shares, business inventory and eligible receivables. Do not assume that every field is automatically zakatable in every circumstance. The correct treatment can depend on the asset, purpose, valuation date and applicable guidance.

Liabilities and deductions

The calculator accepts a liability amount and subtracts it from the listed assets. That is an arithmetic operation, not a decision that every debt is deductible. Before entering a deduction, determine whether it is permitted under the approach you are following.

Worked example

If the assets entered total PKR 900,000 and permitted deductions are PKR 100,000, the calculator's net amount is PKR 800,000. With an entered Nisab of PKR 503,529, the net amount is above the threshold. Applying 2.5% to PKR 800,000 gives an arithmetic estimate of PKR 20,000. The example demonstrates the mathematics only.

Why the calculator keeps Nisab editable

A fixed default can be useful for a current-year reference, but it can become misleading when prices, dates or official thresholds change. Calcora therefore displays a current reference while allowing the user to enter the threshold appropriate to the calculation being performed.

Common mistakes

  • Using a previous year's Nisab without checking the relevant Islamic year.
  • Mixing valuation dates for different assets.
  • Assuming all liabilities can be deducted in the same way.
  • Treating market value alone as the answer for every investment or business asset.
  • Using a calculator result as a religious ruling.

How to use Calcora

  1. Confirm the Nisab applicable to your calculation date and method.
  2. List the assets that your method treats as relevant.
  3. Use consistent valuation information.
  4. Enter only deductions that are actually applicable to your method.
  5. Review the arithmetic result and then verify any religious or special-case questions separately.
