Waves & Thermodynamics Calculators: Frequency, Heat and Gas Laws

These calculators cover common introductory relationships between frequency, wavelength, heat, specific heat and ideal gases.

Waves

Use Wave Speed when speed, frequency and wavelength are related. Use Frequency when period and frequency need to be converted.

Heat

Heat Calculator handles basic thermal-energy relationships, while Specific Heat Calculator focuses on material heating and calorimetry-style problems.

Ideal gases

Ideal Gas Law Calculator solves for pressure, volume, temperature or amount of gas under the ideal-gas model. Use absolute temperature and compatible units.

Keep the units visible

Wave speed, frequency and wavelength are linked by v = fλ. If frequency is entered in hertz and wavelength in metres, the calculated speed is in metres per second. For heat, check whether the quantity is mass, specific heat, temperature change or energy before substituting numbers.

One quick wave example

If a wave has a frequency of 5 Hz and a wavelength of 2 m, its speed is 5 × 2 = 10 m/s. If you instead know speed and frequency, rearrange the same relationship to find wavelength.

Temperature deserves extra care

Gas-law calculations require absolute temperature, normally kelvin. A Celsius value should not be inserted directly into PV = nRT. This is a calculation issue, not a rounding issue, so check the temperature scale before anything else.

Choose the right relationship

For a periodic wave, use v = fλ and keep speed, frequency and wavelength units compatible. For heating, distinguish energy transferred from temperature change and material mass. For ideal-gas calculations, use absolute temperature and keep pressure, volume and amount units consistent with the gas constant selected by the tool.

Common modelling limits

The wave relationship assumes the stated wave speed applies to the medium and conditions. Heat calculations may omit phase changes, heat loss or changing specific heat. The ideal gas law is an approximation and can be less accurate at high pressure or near condensation. If a real experiment disagrees with a simple estimate, check the model assumptions before blaming rounding.

Use each calculator for one defined step, keep a record of input units and compare the result with the measurement conditions or textbook model.

Frequently asked questions

Why must temperature be in kelvin for PV = nRT?

The ideal gas law uses absolute temperature; Celsius has an arbitrary zero and cannot be substituted directly.

How are wave speed, frequency and wavelength connected?

For the stated wave conditions, speed equals frequency multiplied by wavelength. Rearrange the same equation to solve for either unknown.

Why can heat estimates differ from measurements?

Real systems can lose heat to their surroundings or involve phase changes and material properties not included in the simplified calculation.
