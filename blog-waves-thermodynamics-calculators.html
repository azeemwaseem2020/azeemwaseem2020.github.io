Waves & Thermodynamics Calculators: Frequency, Heat and Gas Laws

These calculators cover common introductory relationships between frequency, wavelength, heat, specific heat and ideal gases.

Waves

Use Wave Speed when speed, frequency and wavelength are related. Use Frequency when period and frequency need to be converted.

Heat

Heat Calculator handles basic thermal-energy relationships, while Specific Heat Calculator focuses on material heating and calorimetry-style problems.

Ideal gases

Ideal Gas Law Calculator solves for pressure, volume, temperature or amount of gas under the ideal-gas model. Use absolute temperature and compatible units.

Keep the units visible

Wave speed, frequency and wavelength are linked by v = fλ. If frequency is entered in hertz and wavelength in metres, the calculated speed is in metres per second. For heat, check whether the quantity is mass, specific heat, temperature change or energy before substituting numbers.

One quick wave example

If a wave has a frequency of 5 Hz and a wavelength of 2 m, its speed is 5 × 2 = 10 m/s. If you instead know speed and frequency, rearrange the same relationship to find wavelength.

Temperature deserves extra care

Gas-law calculations require absolute temperature, normally kelvin. A Celsius value should not be inserted directly into PV = nRT. This is a calculation issue, not a rounding issue, so check the temperature scale before anything else.

Choosing the right heat relationship

For a temperature change, the basic relationship is Q = mcΔT, where Q is heat energy, m is mass, c is specific heat capacity and ΔT is the change in temperature. Use kilograms with specific heat in joules per kilogram per kelvin to obtain joules. The formula assumes the specific heat value is suitable for the material and temperature range; phase changes require additional latent-heat calculations.

Check the model before calculating

The wave equation assumes the frequency and wavelength describe the same wave in the same medium. A wave can change speed when it enters a different medium, which can change its wavelength while frequency remains set by the source. The ideal gas law is an approximation that works best when gas particles are sufficiently far apart and intermolecular effects are small. For high pressure or near-condensation conditions, a real-gas model may be more appropriate.

Frequently asked questions

What does this calculator help estimate?

It applies the stated inputs and standard formula to produce an estimate. The result should be checked against the equipment specification, local conditions and the assumptions used.

Are the results exact?

No calculation is exact when real-world inputs vary. Treat the result as a planning estimate and verify important decisions with current technical specifications.

Which inputs should I check first?

Check the units, rated values, system conditions and any losses or efficiency assumptions before relying on the final result.
