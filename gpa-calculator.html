How a weighted GPA is calculated

Grade point average is normally a weighted average: a three-credit course contributes more to the semester GPA than a one-credit course when both have different grade points. The calculator uses the grade-point values you enter, so it does not assume one universal grading scale. Universities in Pakistan may use a 4.00 scale or institution-specific rules, and official transcripts can also apply policies for repeats, withdrawals, pass/fail courses or grade replacement.

Formula

GPA = Σ(grade points × credit hours) ÷ Σ credit hours. For example, a 4.00 grade in a 3-credit course contributes 12 grade-credit points. Add the weighted contributions and divide by total credits.

Worked example

Suppose a student has three courses: 3 credits at 4.00, 3 credits at 3.00 and 2 credits at 2.50. Weighted points are 12 + 9 + 5 = 26. Total credits are 8, so GPA = 26 ÷ 8 = 3.25. The result is a calculation from the supplied grades, not an official academic decision.

Pakistan-specific example

A student at a Pakistani university can use the calculator after entering the grade points shown in the institution's handbook or transcript. If the university uses letter grades, first map each letter to the official grade-point scale. Do not copy a US or another university's scale unless it is the one your institution actually uses.

Common questions

Are all courses equally weighted? Not when credit hours differ; GPA is normally credit-weighted.

Can I convert percentage directly to GPA? Only when your institution provides a valid conversion rule.

Does this calculate CGPA? It can represent a weighted average for the entries supplied; cumulative rules depend on the institution.

Why does my university portal differ? It may apply repeats, exclusions, grade replacement or a different scale.
