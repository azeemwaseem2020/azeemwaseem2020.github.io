Down payment percentage calculator: complete guide

A down payment is the portion of a purchase price paid upfront rather than borrowed. The down payment percentage makes it easier to compare offers for homes, vehicles and other financed purchases. A higher upfront payment usually reduces the financed balance, but it also leaves less cash available for emergency savings, fees and other expenses.

Formulas

Down payment amount = purchase price × percentage ÷ 100.
Down payment percentage = down payment amount ÷ purchase price × 100.
Financed amount = purchase price − down payment amount.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the full purchase price in the price field.
  2. Enter the amount you plan to pay upfront in the down-payment field.
  3. Select Calculate to find the down payment as a percentage of the price.
  4. Use the percentage alongside the financed amount, loan term, rate and fees when comparing financing options.

Worked example

For a purchase price of 300,000 and an upfront payment of 60,000, percentage = 60,000 ÷ 300,000 × 100 = 20%. The remaining amount before fees is 240,000. Currency does not change the ratio, provided price and down payment are in the same currency.

What the percentage does—and does not—tell you

The percentage shows the share of the purchase price paid upfront; it does not calculate monthly instalments, total interest or affordability. Lenders may also consider credit history, income, collateral, loan-to-value limits, insurance and fees. A down payment can reduce interest costs by lowering the amount borrowed, but the overall benefit depends on the rate, term and product terms.

Common mistakes

  • Use the purchase price, not the loan amount, as the denominator.
  • Do not mix currencies or include fees in one value but not the other.
  • Remember that taxes, registration, insurance and closing costs may be separate from the down payment.
  • Check whether the lender defines the eligible purchase price or required upfront contribution differently.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a 10% down payment?

Multiply the purchase price by 0.10. For a price of 100,000, 10% is 10,000.

Does a higher down payment lower the loan amount?

Yes. For the same purchase price, a larger down payment leaves a smaller balance to finance, though the total decision should also account for cash reserves and all borrowing costs.

Is this a loan approval or payment estimate?

No. This calculator reports a percentage from the two amounts entered. It does not determine lender approval, required minimum contributions or monthly payments.
