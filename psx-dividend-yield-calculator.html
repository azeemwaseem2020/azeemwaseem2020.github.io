Dividend yield

Dividend yield = dividend per share ÷ share price × 100

Example: PKR 5 dividend ÷ PKR 100 share price × 100 = 5% yield.

How the PSX dividend yield calculation works

Dividend yield compares a cash dividend per share with the share price used in the calculation. The formula is dividend per share ÷ share price × 100. Estimated gross cash income for your entered holding is number of shares × dividend per share.

Worked example: yield and estimated cash income

Suppose you hold 100 shares, use a share price of PKR 100, and enter PKR 5 per share as the dividend for the selected period. The yield is 5 ÷ 100 × 100 = 5%, and the estimated gross dividend is 100 × 5 = PKR 500. These are arithmetic examples, not a forecast or a confirmation that a company will declare or pay this dividend.

Choose the dividend period carefully

Select Per declared dividend when the amount entered is for one declared payment. Select Annual total only when the dividend-per-share amount represents the total for a full year. The calculator does not annualise a quarterly or interim dividend automatically, and the yield shown is based on the period represented by your input.

Gross dividend is not necessarily take-home cash

The result is a gross estimate before any applicable withholding tax, brokerage or other charges. Actual cash received can depend on the investor’s tax treatment, eligibility, the company’s final payment and account records. This tool does not determine a personal tax rate or calculate net proceeds.

Price basis and investment risk

Keep the price basis consistent with your purpose: current market price for a current-price comparison, or your own purchase cost for a cost-yield calculation. A falling share price can make the percentage yield appear higher even when the cash dividend has not increased; it does not by itself mean the investment is safer or more profitable. Share prices and future dividends can change, and past payments do not guarantee future income.

Important PSX assumptions

This tool does not fetch live PSX prices, company announcements, eligibility dates, tax rates or brokerage charges. Before relying on an estimate, verify the company’s official dividend announcement, relevant book-closure/eligibility details, payment status and the price basis you intend to use.

Official investor education reference

The PSX Guide to Investors describes dividend yield as a measure relating cash dividend to share price. Use the company’s latest dividend announcement and your relevant share-price basis; this calculator does not fetch live prices, predict future dividends or determine your tax treatment.

PSX dividend yield calculator FAQs

How is dividend yield calculated?

Dividend yield is the annual dividend per share divided by the share price, multiplied by 100. Use values for the same share and clearly chosen period.

Does a higher dividend yield guarantee a better investment?

No. Yield is a calculation from entered dividend and price figures, not a forecast or recommendation. Share prices can fall and dividends can change or stop.

Does the result include tax and brokerage?

The basic yield calculation does not automatically determine your personal tax or all transaction costs. Check the applicable dividend tax treatment and broker records separately.

Does this calculator use live PSX prices?

No. It uses the values you enter. Verify the latest price and company dividend announcement with official market or company information before making a decision.

How to interpret PSX dividend yield

Dividend yield is meaningful only when its dividend period and share-price basis are clear. Dividing an annual dividend per share by the current market price gives a different percentage from dividing the same dividend by your purchase price. Label the basis so the result is not mistaken for a guaranteed return on the money you could receive if you sold today.

Declared dividend is not the same as cash received

Check the company or exchange announcement for the dividend amount, eligibility dates and payment details. A declared dividend may be subject to eligibility conditions, withholding or other applicable treatment. Do not assume that a prior year’s dividend will repeat, and do not treat an indicative or interim amount as a promise of future income.

Yield versus total return

Yield measures dividend income relative to a stated price. Total return considers both price movement and dividends across a defined period, with the result depending on whether reinvestment, fees and taxes are included. A high yield alone does not show whether the share price has fallen, whether the payout is sustainable or whether the investment suits your needs. Use the Dividend Reinvestment Calculator for hypothetical reinvestment and the Stock Comparison Calculator for matched, user-entered scenarios.

Calcora does not fetch live dividend announcements or quotes. Verify the amount and relevant dates from official PSX/company information, record the source, and treat the calculator output as an estimate based on your inputs—not as a recommendation.

PSX dividend income: check the period, eligibility and net cash

Dividend calculations are easy to misread when the amount, period and price basis are not stated. Before comparing yields, identify whether the figure is an annual total, a trailing 12-month amount, a declared interim/final dividend or a forward estimate. Do not annualise a one-off payment automatically unless you explicitly label it as a scenario.

Three different questions, three different numbers

  • Current-price yield: stated annual dividend per share divided by the current reference price. This describes income relative to that price, not a guaranteed future return.
  • Yield on purchase price: the relevant dividend amount divided by your original purchase price. This is a historical cost comparison and may differ from yield available to a new buyer at today's price.
  • Net cash received: the eligible dividend amount multiplied by eligible shares, less withholding or other deductions that actually apply. The tax treatment must be verified for the investor and relevant tax year.

Worked example

If a hypothetical company pays PKR 8 per share over a stated 12-month period and the reference price is PKR 160, the simple yield is 8 ÷ 160 × 100 = 5% before tax and transaction costs. If the share price changes to PKR 200 while the dividend amount remains PKR 8, the same dividend produces a 4% yield at that price. Neither example implies that the next dividend will be PKR 8.

Verify before relying on a payout

  1. Read the company's official announcement and confirm whether the amount is per share and which distribution it covers.
  2. Check eligibility/book-closure dates and whether the payout was declared, approved and paid.
  3. Use a clearly dated share price and state whether the calculation is before withholding tax or other deductions.
  4. Keep dividend income separate from price return, and avoid counting the same dividend both as cash and as reinvested shares.
  5. Verify applicable tax and any Zakat treatment independently; this calculator does not decide personal liability.
