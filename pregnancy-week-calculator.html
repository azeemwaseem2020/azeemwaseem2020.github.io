How the calculation works

The calculator compares the selected LMP date with today's calendar date, then converts the elapsed days into complete weeks and remaining days. This is a simple date-based estimate; clinical pregnancy dating can differ.

Important note

This tool is for general information and is not a diagnosis or a substitute for prenatal care. If the dates are uncertain or the estimated gestational age matters for medical care, confirm it with a qualified healthcare professional.

Common question

What does “10 weeks 3 days” mean?

It means 73 elapsed days from the selected LMP date under this calendar-based method: 10 complete weeks plus 3 days.

Estimate pregnancy weeks

Pregnancy is commonly dated from the first day of the last menstrual period rather than the conception date. Under a conventional 40-week model, the gestational age is calculated from that starting date. This means the first roughly two weeks are counted before typical ovulation and conception.

Worked example

If the first day of the last menstrual period was 8 August, then 8 September is approximately 4 weeks of gestational age under the calendar method. The exact clinical dating may differ when cycle length or ultrasound findings suggest another estimate.

Health context

Irregular cycles, uncertain dates and early ultrasound measurements can change the estimated gestational age. The calculator is useful for a calendar estimate but does not assess fetal development, viability or pregnancy health.

FAQs

Are pregnancy weeks counted from conception? Usually no; clinical gestational age is generally dated from the last menstrual period. Can the date change? Yes. Is this a medical diagnosis? No.
