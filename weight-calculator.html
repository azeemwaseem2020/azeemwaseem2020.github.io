Weight Calculator — Mass, Gravity & Weight

Use the relationship between mass and gravity to calculate an object's weight, find its mass from a known weight, or solve for gravitational acceleration.

Calculate with W = mg

A 60 kg mass at standard Earth gravity weighs about 588.4 N.

Formula: how weight is calculated

The standard near-surface relationship is W = m × g, where W is weight in newtons (N), m is mass in kilograms (kg), and g is gravitational acceleration in metres per second squared (m/s²). Because a newton equals kg·m/s², these units are consistent.

If weight is known, rearrange the equation to find mass: m = W ÷ g. To find gravitational acceleration from weight and mass, use g = W ÷ m, provided mass is not zero.

Worked example: a 60 kg object on Earth

Using standard gravitational acceleration, g ≈ 9.80665 m/s²:

  • Mass = 60 kg
  • Weight = 60 × 9.80665 = 588.399 N
  • Rounded to one decimal place, the weight is about 588.4 N.

This result is a force, not 60 kg. Kilograms measure mass; newtons measure weight. Everyday scales often display a mass-equivalent in kilograms after sensing force and applying a calibration.

Mass and weight are different

Mass describes how much matter an object contains and remains the same when the object moves between planets. Weight is the gravitational force acting on that mass, so it changes when local gravity changes. For example, the same 60 kg mass weighs less on the Moon than it does on Earth because lunar gravity is weaker.

Common mistakes and limitations

  • Mixing mass and weight: do not label a result in newtons as kilograms.
  • Using the wrong gravity: 9.80665 m/s² is a conventional standard value near Earth's surface, not a universal constant for every location.
  • Entering incompatible units: convert grams to kilograms and kilonewtons to newtons before calculating.
  • Ignoring the physical situation: this simple equation calculates gravitational weight. It does not calculate apparent scale readings during acceleration, orbital free fall or other situations that require a more complete force model.

The tool accepts non-negative values and requires a non-zero divisor when solving for mass or gravity. Results are mathematical estimates based on the inputs; use the gravity value specified by your textbook, experiment or problem statement when it differs from the default.

Frequently asked questions

What is the weight formula?

Near a gravitational field, weight is calculated as W = mg: mass multiplied by gravitational acceleration.

What is the weight of 1 kg on Earth?

At standard gravity, 1 kg has a weight of about 9.80665 N, commonly rounded to 9.81 N.

Can mass change from Earth to the Moon?

No. The object's mass stays the same, but its weight changes because the Moon's gravitational acceleration is lower.

Why is weight measured in newtons?

Weight is a force. The SI unit of force is the newton, equal to one kilogram metre per second squared.

Can I use this for another planet?

Yes. Choose Calculate Weight and enter the local gravitational acceleration for that planet, in m/s². Confirm the value from a reliable science reference or the problem you are solving.
