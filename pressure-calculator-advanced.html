ADVANCED PRESSURE MODEL CHECK

Pressure describes force distributed over area

Pressure is scalar even though the underlying force has direction. The simple P = F/A model is useful when the relevant force is normal to the area being considered.

Absolute vs gauge

Gas-pressure calculations should state whether the value is absolute pressure or pressure relative to atmospheric pressure.

Area selection

Use the actual surface over which the normal force is distributed. Changing the area changes the calculated pressure even when the total force stays the same.

Pressure, force and area

Pressure is force distributed over an area: P = F/A. The SI unit is pascal, equal to one newton per square metre. The same force produces higher pressure when concentrated over a smaller area.

Worked example

A 600 N force acting over 0.30 m² produces pressure of 600 ÷ 0.30 = 2,000 Pa. If the same force is spread over 1.2 m², pressure falls to 500 Pa. This explains why wide foundations reduce ground pressure compared with narrow supports.

Practical context

Fluid pressure can depend on depth, density and gravity, while gas pressure can vary with temperature and volume. Atmospheric pressure and gauge pressure are also different reference conventions, so a reported pressure should specify its basis.

FAQs

Can pressure be negative? Gauge pressure can be negative relative to atmosphere; absolute pressure cannot be below zero. Is bar the same as pascal? No; 1 bar equals 100,000 Pa. Does area need square metres? Not necessarily, but units must be consistent.

Pressure from force and area

Pressure is P = F/A, measured in pascals when force is in newtons and area in square metres. The same force creates greater pressure over a smaller area.

Worked example

A 600 N force over 0.30 m² produces 2,000 Pa. Spread over 1.2 m², it produces 500 Pa. This principle helps explain foundations, hydraulic systems and contact pressure.

Practical context

Fluid pressure can depend on depth, density and gravity. Gas pressure can depend on temperature and volume. Always distinguish absolute pressure from gauge pressure when interpreting real measurements.

FAQs

Can gauge pressure be negative? Yes, relative to atmospheric pressure. Is bar a pascal? No; 1 bar is 100,000 Pa. Must area be m²? No, but units must be consistent.
