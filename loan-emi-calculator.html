How the reducing-balance EMI is calculated

For a standard amortizing loan, the monthly rate is the annual rate divided by 12 and the number of payments is the term in years multiplied by 12. The standard formula is P × r × (1+r)n ÷ ((1+r)n − 1), where P is the financed principal, r is the monthly rate and n is the number of payments. At a zero rate, the payment is simply principal divided by the number of payments.

Reducing balance vs flat rate

A reducing-balance calculation applies the rate to the outstanding principal as it declines. A flat-rate calculation applies the stated rate to the original principal for the agreed term. Because the balances are treated differently, the same percentage can produce very different payment and total-cost figures. Use the comparison below as an estimate only and check the lender's stated method.

Important: A flat-rate percentage should not be compared with a reducing-balance percentage as though they were the same type of rate. Ask the lender which method is used before comparing offers.

How to use this calculator

  1. Enter the amount you actually plan to finance.
  2. Subtract any down payment and enter the annual reducing-balance rate from the lender.
  3. Enter the full term in years and add known one-time fees.
  4. If you are comparing a benchmark such as KIBOR, enter it separately; do not replace the actual customer rate with the benchmark unless the product documents say so.
  5. Compare monthly EMI, total interest, fees and total repayment together.

Pakistan loan planning

For a Pakistan loan comparison, enter the rate and term from the actual offer rather than assuming a generic market rate. KIBOR is a benchmark and does not by itself tell you the consumer rate, because a product may use a spread, a fixed rate, a variable formula, or another pricing structure. If you enter a benchmark above, Calcora shows the mathematical difference between that benchmark and your entered loan rate; it does not treat the benchmark as a loan quote.

Islamic financing

Islamic financing products can use structures such as Murabaha, Ijarah or other Shariah-compliant arrangements rather than a conventional interest-bearing loan. Their payment schedules and contractual charges should be taken from the institution's actual agreement. This calculator can help with general repayment arithmetic, but it is not a Shariah ruling or a contract calculator.

What can change the final amount?

  • Rate type: fixed, variable and promotional rates behave differently.
  • Calculation method: reducing balance and flat-rate methods are not interchangeable.
  • Fees: processing, documentation and other charges can increase the cash cost.
  • Insurance or takaful: these may be separate from the mathematical EMI.
  • Payment timing and rounding: actual schedules can differ slightly from a monthly estimate.
  • Early settlement: the lender may apply its own settlement rules or charges.

Before accepting a loan offer

  1. Confirm whether the quoted rate is fixed or variable.
  2. Ask whether the calculation is reducing balance, flat rate or another method.
  3. Request the complete amortization or repayment schedule.
  4. Check processing fees, insurance/takaful and early-settlement charges.
  5. Compare total repayment and total cash cost, not just the monthly EMI.

Frequently asked questions

What does EMI mean?

EMI means equal monthly installment. In a standard amortizing loan, each payment contains a principal portion and an interest or markup portion.

Does a longer term lower the total cost?

A longer term often lowers the monthly payment but increases the total interest because the balance stays outstanding for more payments.

Does this include every bank charge?

No. The optional fee field includes only the amount you enter. Product-specific fees, taxes, insurance and other charges must be checked in the lender's documents.

Can I use KIBOR as my loan rate?

Only if your product documents explicitly use that benchmark in the pricing formula. Otherwise, enter the actual customer rate quoted by the lender and use the optional benchmark field only for comparison.

Is this an official bank quote?

No. It is a mathematical estimate based on the inputs and assumptions shown on this page.

Questions worth asking the lender

What rate am I actually paying?

Ask whether the quoted percentage is fixed, variable, benchmark-linked or promotional.

What is included in the payment?

Check processing charges, insurance or takaful, taxes and other costs separately.

How is early settlement handled?

Request the lender’s current settlement and rebate rules before signing.
