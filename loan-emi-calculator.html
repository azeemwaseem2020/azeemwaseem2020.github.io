How loan EMI is calculated

For a standard amortizing loan, the monthly rate is the annual rate divided by 12 and the number of payments is the term in years multiplied by 12. The standard EMI formula is P × r × (1+r)^n ÷ ((1+r)^n − 1), where P is financed principal, r is monthly rate and n is the number of payments. At a zero rate, EMI is simply principal divided by the number of payments.

Worked example

Suppose the financed amount is PKR 100,000, the annual rate is 10% and the term is 5 years. The calculator applies the reducing-balance formula to estimate the monthly payment and then multiplies that payment by the number of installments to estimate total repayment. The difference between total repayment and financed principal is the estimated interest under this simplified model. Your lender's official schedule can differ.

Loan amount, down payment and fees

A down payment reduces the principal being financed. A processing fee or other one-time charge can increase your overall cash cost without changing the mathematical EMI. For a meaningful comparison, keep the loan amount, term and rate clearly separated from additional fees and insurance.

Why a longer tenure can cost more

Extending the term usually spreads repayment across more periods. That can make the monthly installment easier to manage, but it often increases the total interest paid. Compare two offers using both monthly affordability and total cost; never select a loan from EMI alone.

What can change your real loan cost?

  • Interest or markup method: reducing-balance and other methods can produce different schedules.
  • Rate type: a fixed rate and a variable rate create different future-cost uncertainty.
  • Fees: processing, documentation and other charges can increase the cash cost.
  • Insurance: some products include or require insurance that is outside the simple formula.
  • Prepayment: early settlement may reduce future interest but can involve lender-specific charges or rules.
  • Rounding and payment dates: actual schedules may differ slightly from a monthly mathematical estimate.

Islamic financing note

Some Islamic financing products use structures that are not described completely by a conventional interest-rate formula. If you are evaluating Islamic financing, use the institution's actual agreement, profit/rent structure and official repayment schedule. This calculator should not be treated as a contract calculation.

Before accepting a lender quote

  1. Ask whether the quoted rate is fixed, variable or promotional.
  2. Request the full repayment schedule.
  3. Check processing, insurance and early-settlement charges.
  4. Confirm whether the lender uses reducing balance or another calculation method.
  5. Compare total repayment and total cash cost, not just EMI.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing the lowest EMI without checking total interest.
  • Ignoring fees and insurance.
  • Assuming a promotional rate lasts for the full term.
  • Using a generic calculator result instead of the lender's official schedule.
  • Comparing loans with different terms without normalizing the total cost.

Frequently asked questions

What is EMI?

EMI means equal monthly installment. In a standard amortizing loan, each payment contains a principal component and an interest or markup component.

Does a longer loan term reduce total cost?

Usually not. A longer term can reduce the monthly payment but often increases the total interest because the balance remains outstanding for longer.

Does this calculator include every bank fee?

No. It includes only the optional one-time fee you enter. Product-specific fees, insurance, taxes and other charges should be checked separately.

Is this an official bank quote?

No. It is an estimate based on the inputs and assumptions shown. Use the lender's official repayment schedule for a binding figure.
