Mosteller BSA formula

The Mosteller formula is BSA = √[(height in cm × weight in kg) ÷ 3600]. The result is expressed in square metres (m²).

Worked example

For 175 cm and 70 kg, BSA = √(175×70÷3600) ≈ 1.844 m².

Why BSA is used

BSA is used in some clinical and research calculations. The appropriate equation and interpretation depend on the application, and a prescribed clinical protocol should take priority.

Understanding body surface area

Body surface area (BSA) is an estimate of the body's external surface area expressed in square metres. A common clinical formula is the Mosteller equation: BSA = √[(height in cm × weight in kg) / 3600]. It is a calculation aid, not a direct measurement, and different clinical settings may use different equations.

Worked example

For an adult who is 170 cm tall and weighs 70 kg, BSA = √[(170 × 70) / 3600] = √3.3056 ≈ 1.82 m². The calculator should use the same units required by the formula; entering pounds or inches without conversion can produce a misleading result.

A practical Pakistan example

Suppose a hospital team in Lahore records a patient's height as 165 cm and weight as 60 kg. The Mosteller estimate is √[(165 × 60) / 3600] ≈ 1.66 m². This number can be documented alongside the patient's clinical measurements, but treatment decisions must follow the clinician's selected protocol rather than a calculator result alone.

Why BSA is used

BSA has historically been used in some clinical calculations, including certain medication-dosing approaches and physiological measurements. Its usefulness depends on the specific drug, guideline and patient population. It should never replace the prescribing information or a qualified clinician's judgment.

Common mistakes

Do not confuse BSA with BMI. BMI relates body mass to height squared, while BSA estimates surface area. Also check that height is in centimetres and weight is in kilograms before applying the Mosteller equation.

BSA FAQ

Which formula does this example use? The Mosteller formula, one of the commonly used BSA equations.

Can BSA determine a medication dose? It may be one input in some protocols, but dosing must follow the medicine's official guidance and clinician instructions.

Is BSA exact? No. It is an estimate derived from height and weight.

Is BSA the same as BMI? No. They measure different concepts and use different formulas.
