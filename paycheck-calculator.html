Understanding a paycheck estimate

A paycheck estimate starts with gross earnings for the pay period and then accounts for relevant deductions to estimate take-home pay. The exact result depends on pay frequency, overtime, allowances, taxes and other deductions.

Check the pay period

Make sure hourly, weekly, monthly and annual figures are not mixed. For an annual-to-monthly comparison, divide annual gross pay by 12 as a simple planning estimate; actual payroll can vary by pay schedule.

Gross pay vs take-home pay

Gross pay is earned before deductions. Take-home pay is what remains after applicable deductions. A calculator estimate should be treated as planning guidance unless it uses the exact rules and payroll data for the relevant employer and tax year.

Paycheck FAQ

Why can my paycheck differ from a calculator?

Actual payroll may include tax treatment, benefits, overtime, arrears or deductions that were not included in the estimate.
