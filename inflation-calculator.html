Inflation effect

Inflation formula

Future nominal amount = present amount × (1 + inflation rate)years. Estimated purchasing power is the present amount divided by the same inflation factor.

Worked example

For 100,000 at 5% annual inflation for 10 years, the nominal equivalent is about 162,889. The result is an assumption-based estimate, not a forecast.

Inflation formula and interpretation

Estimated future price level = current amount × (1 + annual inflation rate)years. To express a future amount in today's purchasing power, divide it by the same inflation factor. The calculator assumes one constant annual rate for the full period; real inflation rates change and vary by category.

Worked example

If an item costs 100,000 today and inflation averages 5% annually for 10 years, its equivalent future price is about 162,889. In the reverse direction, 162,889 received ten years later has purchasing power of about 100,000 in today's units under that same 5% assumption.

Use an appropriate inflation measure

General consumer inflation may not match your personal spending. Food, rent, energy, healthcare and transport can rise at different rates. For Pakistan-specific planning, choose a relevant published CPI measure and period when possible; this calculator does not fetch live or historical inflation data.

Frequently asked questions

How does the inflation calculator work?

It applies the assumed annual inflation rate over the selected number of years to estimate the future nominal amount and purchasing power.

Is the inflation rate guaranteed?

No. The rate is an assumption for the calculation. Actual inflation can vary substantially over time and by country.

What is purchasing power?

Purchasing power describes how much goods and services an amount of money can buy. Higher inflation generally reduces purchasing power over time.

Calculation context

Inflation calculations compare purchasing power or equivalent prices across time using an inflation rate or index assumption. Historical and future estimates depend on the rate and period selected.

Inflation example: future cost and purchasing power

If an item costs 10,000 today and inflation averages an assumed 5% a year for three years, its estimated future cost is 10,000 × 1.05³ = 11,576.25. Conversely, 10,000 received three years from now would have purchasing power equivalent to about 8,638.38 in today’s money under the same assumption. These figures illustrate the formula; they are not forecasts.

Inflation compounds

Annual rates apply to a changing price level, so they compound rather than simply add. A constant rate over several years can produce a different result from a path where inflation rises and falls. For historical comparisons, use a credible consumer price index for the relevant country and period when available.

What this estimate excludes

Inflation varies by category: food, housing, education and energy may not change at the same rate as an overall index. The result also excludes investment returns, taxes and wage growth. Treat the selected rate as an assumption and adjust it to explore different scenarios.
