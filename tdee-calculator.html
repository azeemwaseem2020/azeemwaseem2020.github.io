TDEE Calculator

Estimate total daily energy expenditure from BMR and your activity level using the Mifflin-St Jeor equation.

Formula

For adults, Mifflin-St Jeor BMR is approximately 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age + 5 for men, or the same expression −161 for women. TDEE is estimated as BMR × activity factor.

Important health note

TDEE is an estimate, not a diagnosis or a prescription. Individual energy needs vary. Consider professional advice for medical, pregnancy, eating-disorder or other health-specific situations.

What total daily energy expenditure means

TDEE is an estimate of daily energy expenditure built from a basal metabolic estimate and an activity assumption. It is useful for planning, but it is not a direct measurement of metabolism. Real energy needs change with body composition, activity, sleep, illness and other factors.

Calculation approach

A common approach estimates BMR first and then multiplies it by an activity factor. Different equations and activity categories produce different estimates, so the method used by the calculator should be kept consistent when comparing results.

Worked example

If a person's estimated BMR is 1,600 kcal/day and the selected activity factor is 1.5, the estimated TDEE is 1,600 × 1.5 = 2,400 kcal/day. This is a planning estimate, not a prescribed intake.

Pakistan-specific example

Food choices and portion sizes in Pakistan can vary widely across home cooking, restaurants and packaged foods. Someone using a TDEE estimate should compare it with real-world weight and activity trends rather than assuming the calculated number is exact.

Common questions

Is TDEE exact? No.

Does exercise change TDEE? It can, depending on activity level.

Can I use it for medical dieting? Medical nutrition decisions should be discussed with a qualified professional.

Why do two calculators differ? They may use different BMR equations or activity factors.
