Formula

TDEE Calculator: Formula, Example and Practical Interpretation

Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) estimates the energy a person uses across an average day. It includes energy used at rest, movement and exercise, and digestion. A calculator provides a starting estimate for nutrition planning; it does not measure metabolism directly and should not be treated as an exact calorie prescription.

Formula and components

Many calculators estimate basal or resting metabolic rate (BMR/RMR) and multiply it by an activity factor: TDEE ≈ BMR × activity factor. A commonly used Mifflin–St Jeor equation estimates resting energy expenditure. For men: 10 × weight in kg + 6.25 × height in cm − 5 × age + 5. For women: 10 × weight in kg + 6.25 × height in cm − 5 × age − 161. These population equations may not fit every person. Activity factors should reflect normal movement, work and exercise across a typical week, not one workout.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter age, height and weight using the units shown.
  2. Select the equation or profile option requested by the tool.
  3. Choose an activity level that reflects a typical week.
  4. Read the estimate in calories per day and treat it as a starting point for observation.

Worked example

If an illustrative BMR estimate is 1,600 kcal/day and the selected activity factor is 1.45, estimated TDEE is 1,600 × 1.45 = 2,320 kcal/day. This does not mean the person burns exactly 2,320 calories each day. Actual expenditure varies with movement, training, sleep, body composition, health and equation error. Choosing an activity factor that is too high overstates the result.

Using the result responsibly

Maintenance intake is often estimated near TDEE, but body-weight trends over several weeks provide more useful feedback than one calculation. Food labels, portions and activity estimates are imperfect. Avoid large dietary changes based solely on a single output. Adolescents who are still growing, pregnant or breastfeeding people, and people managing medical conditions or eating-disorder concerns may need individualized professional guidance. Calories are only one part of nutrition; food quality, protein, fiber, micronutrients, hydration and sustainable habits matter too.

Frequently asked questions

Is TDEE the same as BMR?

No. BMR estimates energy used at rest; TDEE includes activity and other daily energy costs.

Why can real results differ?

Equations, activity factors and tracking are estimates, and individual energy needs vary.

When should I recalculate?

Recalculate when weight, activity or other inputs change meaningfully, rather than reacting to daily fluctuations.
