How drawdown and recovery maths work

Drawdown (%) = (reference peak price − scenario price) ÷ reference peak price × 100. Value decline = (reference peak price − scenario price) × shares held. Gain required to recover (%) = (reference peak price ÷ scenario price − 1) × 100, when the scenario price is above zero.

A fall of 20% requires a gain of 25% from the lower price to return to the starting level. The percentages are not symmetrical because the recovery is calculated from a smaller base. This calculator does not predict whether or when recovery will happen.

What this calculator can and cannot tell you

The result is a mathematical comparison of user-entered prices and share quantity. It is not a live PSX quote, forecast, recommendation, stop-loss instruction or measure of the maximum possible loss. It does not account automatically for dividends, taxes, future transaction charges, liquidity, corporate actions or changes in holdings. The optional average-cost input provides a separate comparison against purchase cost; it should not be confused with drawdown from a market peak.

Before relying on a result

  • Confirm the peak and current price from dated, reliable records and ensure both are adjusted consistently for corporate actions.
  • Use actual holdings from your broker statement or PSX Trade Journal.
  • Check your total portfolio exposure using the PSX Portfolio Calculator and position exposure with the Position Size Calculator.
  • Verify fees and taxes separately with the Brokerage Commission Calculator and official guidance.

Official information

For investor education and market-process information, consult the Pakistan Stock Exchange, the Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan, and your broker's dated contract notes. This tool does not fetch current market data.

Frequently asked questions

Is drawdown the same as loss from my purchase price?

No. Drawdown compares the scenario price with a chosen peak. Unrealised gain or loss compares the price with your acquisition cost. The calculator shows these as separate comparisons when average cost is supplied.

Why does a 20% fall need a 25% recovery?

Because the recovery percentage is measured from the reduced value. For example, 100 falling to 80 is a loss of 20; returning from 80 to 100 requires 20 divided by 80, or 25%.

Does this predict a stock's recovery?

No. It only calculates the percentage change needed to revisit a selected reference price. It does not estimate probability, timing or future performance.

Does it use live PSX prices?

No. Prices and quantities are entered by the user. Verify market prices and corporate-action adjustments from reliable current records.
