TMR feed calculator vs ration formulation

A calculator can perform transparent arithmetic on the formulation you supply. Nutrient formulation is a different task because it depends on animal requirements and reliable feed analysis. University of Minnesota Extension explains that TMRs should supply balanced nutrients and that animal grouping, feed dry matter and actual intake matter in management.

Dry matter and as-fed weight

Rations are commonly expressed on a dry-matter basis while feeds are loaded as-fed. If feed moisture changes, the kilograms loaded can change even when the intended dry matter does not. Penn State Extension describes monitoring delivered feed, refusals, animal numbers and TMR DM to estimate actual DMI.

Batch scaling formula

Ingredient kg = batch kg × mix percentage ÷ 100. A 25% ingredient in a 1,000 kg batch is 250 kg. The percentage entries should total 100% before treating the batch as a complete formulation.

Actual dry matter intake

DMI/head/day = ((delivered kg − refused kg) × DM fraction) ÷ animals. Use representative current DM data, especially for wet feeds and silages.

Feed cost

Ingredient cost = ingredient kg × current price/kg. Total the ingredients for batch cost. Daily cost then depends on how much of the batch the group actually consumes. Feed cost is only one part of farm economics.

Why moisture testing matters on a dairy farm

Moisture variation is a practical reason to recalculate a ration: when a feed becomes wetter or drier, the same as-fed weight delivers a different amount of dry matter. Recheck wet forages when the material, storage conditions or harvest conditions change, and interpret the result alongside actual intake and refusals.

TMR quality-control checklist

A good TMR calculation is only useful when the numbers going into it reflect what is actually mixed and delivered. University extension guidance emphasizes regular monitoring of feed dry matter, ingredient weights, intake, refusals and particle size. In practice, a farm team can use this simple control loop:

  1. Check moisture: recheck wet forages and other high-moisture feeds when conditions or the material changes.
  2. Check the scale: compare the mixer reading with a known weight and keep weighing equipment maintained.
  3. Check the batch: confirm the ingredient weights and total batch weight against the intended formulation.
  4. Check the bunk: compare delivered feed with refusals and observe whether animals are sorting.
  5. Check consistency: compare results over several days rather than changing a ration from one unusual observation.
Why this matters: a TMR can be mathematically correct on paper but still differ at the feed bunk if moisture, weighing, mixing, particle size or sorting changes. Extension guidance recommends monitoring these management variables rather than relying on the formulated recipe alone.

Particle size and sorting: a practical check

TMR performance is not determined only by nutrient percentages. Particle size affects sorting and physically effective fiber, while over-mixing can reduce particle size. Penn State Extension describes the Penn State Particle Separator as a practical farm tool for measuring TMR particle distribution. Use a consistent sampling method and interpret the result in the context of the actual ration and herd.

What Calcora calculates—and what it does not

Useful for calculation

  • Batch scaling from known ingredient percentages
  • Dry-matter and as-fed arithmetic
  • Actual intake from delivered feed and refusals
  • Ingredient and batch feed-cost estimates
  • Weighted nutrient checks from supplied feed-analysis data

Needs professional judgment

  • Setting nutrient requirements for a particular animal or group
  • Diagnosing poor intake, milk changes or health problems
  • Choosing a complete ration without current feed analysis
  • Interpreting farm-specific nutrition, health and production data

Farmer checklist

  • Use current ingredient and forage information.
  • Keep as-fed and dry-matter units separate.
  • Check percentages total 100% for a batch formulation.
  • Record actual feed delivered and refusals.
  • Update wet-feed DM when moisture changes.
  • Use current local prices for cost estimates.
  • Do not treat an arithmetic calculator as a universal ration prescription.
