Find a value from a percentage: complete guide

This calculator finds a specified percentage of a number. It is useful for discounts, tax estimates, commissions, exam scores, budget allocations and percentage-based comparisons. The percentage tells you how many parts out of 100 to take from the original value.

Formula

Result = (percentage ÷ 100) × original value

For example, 15% is 15 ÷ 100 = 0.15. Multiplying the original amount by 0.15 gives 15 percent of that amount.

How to use it

  1. Enter the percentage in the percentage field.
  2. Enter the original number or amount in the value field.
  3. Select Calculate and read the result.
  4. Check whether the result should be interpreted as money, a count, a measurement or another unit.

Worked example

To find 15% of 240, calculate 15 ÷ 100 × 240 = 36. For a discount of 15% on a price of 2,000, the discount amount is 300; subtract it from the original price to get a discounted price of 1,700 before any other taxes or fees.

Common mistakes and related calculations

Finding a percentage of a value is not the same as finding what percentage one number is of another. To find the percentage, divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100. To increase an amount by a percentage, multiply by 1 + percentage/100; to reduce it, multiply by 1 − percentage/100. A negative percentage or value can be mathematically valid, but interpret it in context. The calculator performs the percentage multiplication only; it does not automatically add a tax, subtract a discount or determine a final invoice total.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find 15% of 240?

Convert 15% to 0.15 and multiply by 240: 0.15 × 240 = 36. The answer is 36. This calculator is useful whenever the percentage and the original whole are known.

How is this different from finding what percent one number is of another?

Here, you know the percentage and the whole and want the resulting part. In a “what percent” calculation, you know the part and whole and solve for the percentage. Check which value is missing before choosing a calculator.

Can the answer be greater than the original value?

Yes, if the percentage is greater than 100%. For example, 125% of 80 is 100. A percentage below 0% produces a negative result when the original value is positive, which may or may not make sense for your particular use case.
