Reducing Balance vs Flat Rate Loan Calculator Pakistan

Compare two common ways of expressing loan interest using the same principal, annual rate and term. The key question is not only the advertised percentage: it is how interest is applied to the balance and what you repay in total.

Maintained by: Abdul Qadir · Updated: 7 October 2026 · About Calcora

This is a mathematical comparison tool. Actual bank, vehicle-finance or consumer-finance products can include fees, insurance, taxes, minimum charges, different compounding conventions or product-specific definitions.

Reducing-balance method

Under the standard monthly reducing-balance model, the periodic interest calculation uses the outstanding principal. As scheduled principal is repaid, the balance falls, so the interest portion generally falls as well. The calculator uses the standard EMI formula for this illustration.

Monthly rate = annual rate ÷ 12. The EMI is then calculated from the principal, monthly rate and number of monthly payments.

Flat-rate method

For the flat-rate illustration, the stated annual rate is applied to the original principal for the full term. Total flat interest is therefore principal × annual rate × years, and the illustrative monthly payment is total repayment divided by the number of months.

This is a comparison model, not a claim that every lender uses exactly this formula. Always read the lender's repayment schedule and contract definitions.

Worked example: PKR 1,000,000 for 5 years at 12%

With a principal of PKR 1,000,000, a five-year term and a 12% annual rate, the reducing-balance calculation gives an estimated monthly payment of about PKR 22,244 and total interest of about PKR 334,651. The flat-rate illustration gives total interest of PKR 600,000, total repayment of PKR 1,600,000 and an illustrative monthly payment of about PKR 26,667.

The difference exists because the reducing-balance model charges interest against a declining balance, while the flat-rate illustration keeps the original principal as the interest base. These figures are illustrative and exclude lender-specific fees and charges.

What to compare before choosing a loan

  • Total repayment: the amount returned over the full term.
  • Total interest: the financing cost before additional product fees.
  • Monthly payment: important for affordability, but not enough by itself.
  • Fees and charges: processing fees, insurance, taxes and other costs can change the effective cost.
  • Repayment schedule: use the lender's actual schedule when making a borrowing decision.

Why the same percentage can be misleading

A 12% flat rate and a 12% reducing-balance rate are not economically equivalent simply because the headline percentage is the same. The interest base and repayment structure differ. When comparing Pakistani loan offers, ask for the total repayment, installment schedule and all mandatory charges rather than comparing only the advertised rate.

Frequently asked questions

Is reducing-balance interest always cheaper?

Not automatically. It depends on the actual rate, term, fees and product rules. But when the same nominal rate and term are modeled under the two formulas used here, the reducing-balance model generally produces less interest because the balance declines.

Does a flat rate mean the lender charges the same interest every month?

In this calculator's illustration, the total flat interest is spread evenly across the scheduled months. Real products can define flat-rate payments and fees differently, so check the lender's contract.

Should I compare monthly installments or total repayment?

Compare both. A lower monthly installment can result from a longer term, while a different interest method can change total repayment substantially.

Does this calculator include bank fees or insurance?

No. It compares the stated principal, rate and term using the two mathematical models. Add lender-specific costs separately when evaluating a real offer.
