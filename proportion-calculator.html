Checking a proportion

A proportion states that two ratios are equal. Cross-multiplication provides a simple way to verify a result when the quantities are non-zero and the ratio model is appropriate.

Worked check

For x/12 = 5/20, cross-multiplication gives 20x = 60, so x = 3. Substituting 3 into the original proportion confirms the equality.

Real-world use

Proportions can model rates and scaling relationships, but a real quantity does not automatically scale linearly just because two numbers can be written as a ratio.

Proportion Calculator: Ratios, Cross-Multiplication and Examples

A proportion states that two ratios are equal. It can help scale recipes, compare unit prices, convert map distances and solve algebra problems. A proportion is often written as a/b = c/d, where denominators are non-zero. A calculator can solve a missing fourth value, but the relationship must also make sense for the real situation before the result is used.

Formula

For a/b = c/d, cross-multiplication gives a × d = b × c. Rearrange this equation to solve the missing variable. For example, if a/b = c/d and d is unknown, then d = (b × c) ÷ a, provided a is not zero. The exact arrangement depends on which field is missing. A zero denominator makes a ratio undefined, and division by zero is not a valid solution.

How to use the calculator

  1. Identify the two quantities that should vary proportionally.
  2. Enter the three known values in their correct positions.
  3. Leave the unknown field empty or select the missing variable, according to the form.
  4. Calculate, then substitute the result into both ratios to check equality.

Worked examples

Recipe scaling: If 2 cups of rice serve 4 people, the proportional amount for 6 people is x = 2 × 6 ÷ 4 = 3 cups. Map scale: If 1 cm represents 5 km, then 4 cm represents 20 km. Unit price: If 3 identical notebooks cost 450 currency units, 5 at the same unit price cost 450 × 5 ÷ 3 = 750. These examples assume a constant rate and no fixed fees, discounts or other changes.

Common mistakes and limitations

Keep corresponding quantities in the same order. If the first ratio is people per serving, the second ratio must use the same orientation. Convert units before comparing centimetres with metres, or grams with kilograms. Proportionality also does not prove that a real-world relationship is linear: labour costs may include a fixed charge, bulk purchases may be discounted, and recipes may not scale perfectly. Treat the answer as an algebraic result only when the assumed relationship is appropriate.

Frequently asked questions

What is cross-multiplication?

It turns a/b = c/d into a × d = b × c, provided the denominators are non-zero.

Can a proportion contain zero?

Some numerators can be zero, but denominators cannot. The equation may also fail to determine a unique unknown.

How can I check the answer?

Substitute the missing value and verify that both ratios match, allowing for rounding.
