How Many Solar Panels Do You Need?

Panel count is not just daily kWh divided by panel watts. You also need an assumption for usable peak-sun hours and a system derating factor.

Start with daily energy

List appliances, their wattage and expected daily operating hours. Sum the resulting watt-hours and divide by 1,000 to get approximate daily kWh. This is a better starting point than sizing from the largest appliance alone.

Convert energy into PV capacity

A simplified model is PV capacity ≈ daily energy ÷ (peak-sun-hours × system efficiency factor). If daily demand is 10 kWh, usable sun is 5 hours and the assumed overall factor is 0.8, required PV capacity is about 2.5 kW.

Then calculate panel count

For 550 W modules, 2.5 kW ÷ 0.55 kW gives about 4.55, so the practical count rounds up to 5 panels in this simplified example. Real design may require additional capacity for seasonal conditions, shading and system constraints.

Energy size is not inverter size

Panels are sized around energy production. The inverter must also handle simultaneous running load and starting/surge behavior for applicable equipment. A system can have enough daily energy on paper while still having an undersized inverter.

Why local conditions matter

Solar output changes with weather, orientation, tilt, temperature, shading, dust and equipment losses. Use local production data or a qualified designer for a final installation.
