Percentage Increase vs Percentage Points: The Difference

A percentage change compares a new value with the old value. A percentage-point change subtracts two percentages directly. They answer different questions.

Percentage increase

The formula is (new − old) ÷ old × 100. If a price rises from 80 to 100, the increase is 25%, because 20 ÷ 80 = 0.25.

Percentage-point change

If an interest rate moves from 8% to 10%, the change is 2 percentage points. The relative increase in the rate itself is 25% because 2 ÷ 8 = 25%.

Why the distinction matters

News reports, financial documents and performance dashboards often use both forms. Saying a rate increased by 2% can be ambiguous: it might mean two percentage points or a relative 2% increase.

Percentage decrease

The same structure applies to decreases. A fall from 100 to 80 is a 20% decrease. The denominator is the original value, not the new value.

Reverse percentage problems

If a final value is 120 after a 20% increase, the original value is 120 ÷ 1.20 = 100. Subtracting 20% from 120 would give 96, which is not the original value.
