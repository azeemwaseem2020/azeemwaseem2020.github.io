WORKED EXAMPLE

Example: separate price gain from dividend income

Assume a holding cost Rs 100,000 and its reference market value is Rs 112,000. The price appreciation is Rs 12,000, or 12%, before considering transaction costs. If the investor also received Rs 3,000 in cash dividends, that income should be recorded separately rather than being added silently to the share-price gain.

This distinction makes portfolio reporting clearer: price appreciation, cash income and transaction costs can each be reviewed independently.

RECORDKEEPING & DATA LIMITS

Make your PSX portfolio result reproducible

Record the valuation date and price source alongside your holdings. Use executed quantities and acquisition costs from broker or custody records, and keep charges and dividend receipts separate when reconciling actual performance. A reference-price snapshot estimates current unrealised profit or loss; it is not the same as realised net profit after every fee and tax.

The holdings summary uses the shares and reference prices entered above. The dated-return tool separately uses the cash flows you record and adds the entered portfolio value on the valuation date. For accurate results, avoid mixing prices from different dates and reconcile corporate actions or changed quantities before calculating.

For market and investor information, consult the official PSX investor guide. For capital-gains-tax rules, verify the current guidance with NCCPL CGT resources; this portfolio snapshot does not calculate your final tax liability.

How to calculate PSX portfolio value

For each holding, current value equals shares multiplied by current price. Total portfolio value is the sum of all entered holdings. Invested cost is shares multiplied by average cost for each holding.

PSX portfolio profit or loss and return

Unrealised profit or loss = current portfolio value − total invested cost. Portfolio return = unrealised profit or loss ÷ total invested cost × 100. These are scenario calculations based on the figures you enter.

PSX investment portfolio allocation

Holding allocation = holding current value ÷ total portfolio value × 100. Allocation helps you see whether one PSX security represents a large share of the portfolio and could therefore have a larger effect on the overall result.

PSX dividend income and yield

Estimated annual dividend income = shares × dividend per share, summed across holdings. Dividend yield on cost = estimated dividend income ÷ total invested cost × 100. Check the dividend period and current company announcements before comparing yields.

Why PSX transaction costs and taxes matter

The portfolio calculator focuses on the arithmetic of the entered holdings. Brokerage, taxes, CDC-related charges and other costs can affect the actual realised return. Use your broker's current tariff and contract notes when calculating a completed transaction.

Current prices and data limitations

Calcora intentionally does not invent live market prices. Enter a verified current or valuation price from your broker or an authoritative market source. Corporate actions, dividend declarations, taxes and charges can change the final outcome.

Who should use a PSX portfolio calculator?

This is handy when you have several PSX holdings and want a quick snapshot without opening a spreadsheet. Enter the figures from your own records or broker statement, then compare the results with your actual account.

Common questions

Can I calculate more than one PSX holding?

Yes. Add your holdings one by one and the calculator combines their cost, current value, profit or loss and allocation.

Does it use live PSX prices?

No. Enter a current price you have verified yourself. This keeps the calculator transparent instead of presenting an unverified live price.

Does portfolio return include every trading cost?

No. Brokerage, taxes and other charges can change the final realised return, so use your broker's records for a completed trade.
