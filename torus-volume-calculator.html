Torus volume: formula and practical guide

A torus is a ring-shaped solid, like a doughnut or a circular tube bent into a loop. This calculator estimates the volume of an ideal ring torus using two radii: the major radius from the centre of the hole to the centreline of the tube, and the minor radius from the tube centreline to its outer surface.

Formula

Volume = 2 × π² × R × r². Here, R is the major radius and r is the minor (tube) radius. Both values must use the same length unit. The answer is in cubic units: centimetres produce cm³, and metres produce m³.

Worked example

If the major radius is 5 cm and the tube radius is 2 cm, volume = 2 × π² × 5 × 2² = 40π² ≈ 394.7842 cm³. Check that the tube radius is smaller than or equal to the major radius for a standard ring-shaped torus.

How to use the calculator

  1. Measure the major radius from the centre of the ring to the centre of the tube.
  2. Measure the tube radius from the tube’s centre to its outer edge.
  3. Enter both radii in the same unit and calculate.
  4. Report the result in cubic units and round it to match the precision of the measurements.

Common mistakes and limitations

Do not substitute the torus’s outer diameter for either radius. The major radius is not the outside radius of the whole object; it locates the tube centreline around the ring. The minor radius is squared, so errors in tube-radius measurement have a strong effect on the result. This formula assumes a uniform circular tube and a standard ring torus; it does not model irregular, hollow, compressed or variable-thickness shapes. For material estimates, also consider wall thickness, gaps and manufacturing tolerances.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between major and minor radius?

The major radius reaches from the centre of the hole to the centre of the tube. The minor radius reaches from the tube centre to its surface. They describe different parts of the shape.

Can I enter the diameter instead?

Convert each diameter to its corresponding radius by dividing it by two. Use the major diameter for the major radius and the tube diameter for the minor radius.

Does the result tell me the torus’s surface area?

No. This page calculates volume only. Surface area is a separate measurement and uses a different formula.
