Volume of a torus

V = 2π²Rr², where R is the major radius and r is the tube radius.

Worked example

For R = 5 cm and r = 2 cm, V = 2π²(5)(2²) ≈ 394.8 cm³.

A practical Pakistan example

This can estimate the volume of a ring-shaped manufactured part when its radii are measured consistently.

Important assumptions

The major radius is measured from the torus centre to the centre of the tube; it is not the outside radius.

Torus volume

For a ring torus, volume is V = 2π²Rr², where R is the major radius from the centre of the hole to the tube centre and r is the tube radius. The result is in cubic units.

Worked example

With R = 5 cm and r = 2 cm, volume is approximately 394.78 cm³. Because r is squared, measurement errors in tube radius can have a noticeable effect.

Practical use

The formula suits idealized rings, seals and geometric models. Manufactured objects with non-circular cross-sections may need CAD geometry or another model.

FAQs

Is R the outside radius? No. What units are returned? Cubic units. Does diameter go directly into the formula? No; convert diameter to radius first.
