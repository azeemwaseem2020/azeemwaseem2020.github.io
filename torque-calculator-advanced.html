Torque relationship

The magnitude of torque is τ = rF sinθ. The angle matters because only the perpendicular component of force produces the corresponding torque.

For a direct torque calculation, use the Torque Calculator.

Torque from force and lever arm

Torque magnitude is commonly calculated as τ = rF sin θ, where r is the distance from the pivot to the point of force application and θ is the angle between the lever arm and force. The SI unit is newton-metre.

Worked example

A 40 N force applied 0.5 m from a pivot at 90° produces τ = 0.5 × 40 × 1 = 20 N·m. At 30° with the same force and distance, the torque is 10 N·m because only the perpendicular component produces rotational effect.

Practical use

Torque is important for bolts, levers, shafts, motors and mechanical design. Direction matters for full rotational analysis, so clockwise and counterclockwise torques may need signed values and a consistent convention.

FAQs

Is torque the same as work? They share the unit N·m dimensionally but describe different physical quantities. What gives maximum torque? A 90° force angle. Can torque be negative? Yes, when direction is represented relative to a chosen convention.

Torque and lever arms

Torque magnitude is τ = rF sin θ, where r is the distance from the pivot and θ is the angle between the lever arm and force. The SI unit is newton-metre.

Worked example

A 40 N force applied 0.5 m from a pivot at 90° produces 20 N·m. At 30° with the same force and distance, torque is 10 N·m because only the perpendicular component produces rotational effect.

Practical use

Torque matters for bolts, levers, shafts and motors. Full rotational analysis may require signed clockwise and counterclockwise torques and several forces.

FAQs

When is torque maximum? At 90°. Can torque be negative? Yes, when direction is represented. Is torque work? No; they are different physical quantities.
