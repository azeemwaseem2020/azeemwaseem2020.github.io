Formula and assumptions

Torque magnitude is τ = rF sin θ, where r is the distance from the pivot to the force application point, F is force in newtons, and θ is the angle between the lever arm and force. Enter distance in metres to obtain N·m. This tool reports magnitude, not clockwise/counter-clockwise sign.

Use this page for rotational problems where torque depends on force, lever-arm distance and the angle between them.

Torque relationship

The magnitude of torque is τ = rF sinθ. The angle matters because only the perpendicular component of force produces the corresponding torque.

For a direct torque calculation, use the Torque Calculator.

Advanced torque analysis

Torque is a vector quantity. For complex systems, the point about which torque is calculated and the direction of each force both matter. Choosing a convenient reference point can simplify equilibrium analysis.

Equilibrium check

A rigid body in static rotational equilibrium has zero net torque about the chosen axis, while translational equilibrium separately requires zero net force. Both conditions may be needed.

Common error

Using the full force instead of its perpendicular component can overestimate torque when the force is not at a right angle to the lever arm.
