Frequently asked questions

What does the retirement calculator show?

It estimates the balance at retirement from current savings, monthly contributions, an assumed annual return and years remaining.

Does this predict my actual retirement income?

No. It estimates an account balance only. Actual retirement income depends on withdrawals, inflation, taxes, investment performance and other factors.

Can I change the assumptions?

Yes. Adjust the savings, contribution, return and time inputs to compare different retirement scenarios.

Planning a retirement target

Retirement projections are sensitive to time, contributions, investment returns, inflation and the point at which withdrawals begin. A calculator can illustrate a mathematical scenario, but the result is not a guaranteed future balance. Small changes in the assumed return or contribution period can materially change a long-term projection.

Formula

For a simplified regular-contribution model, future value combines the growth of the starting balance with the future value of recurring contributions. If compounding is monthly, the periodic rate is the annual rate divided by 12 and the number of periods is years × 12.

Worked example

If Rs 100,000 is invested initially and Rs 20,000 is added monthly, the final value depends on the assumed annual return and investment period. Compare scenarios rather than treating one return assumption as certain.

Pakistan-specific example

A Pakistani saver planning for retirement should consider inflation in rupees, pension or provident-fund benefits, expected housing costs, healthcare and whether contributions are made monthly or irregularly. A nominal future balance can look large while buying less than expected after inflation.

Common questions

Is the return guaranteed? No. It is an assumption.

Does this account for inflation? Only if the calculator explicitly includes it.

Should I increase contributions over time? A scenario with growing contributions can be more realistic for rising income.

Can I use it for a pension? It can illustrate savings growth, but pension rules require their own assumptions.
