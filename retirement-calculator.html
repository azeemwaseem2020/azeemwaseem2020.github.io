Retirement Calculator: Contributions, Compound Growth and Inflation

This retirement savings calculator estimates a future account balance from current savings, monthly contributions, an assumed annual return and the time remaining. It is a scenario tool, not a guarantee of investment performance or a complete pension-income plan. The estimate does not automatically include fees, tax, contribution increases or inflation.

Formula and assumptions

With monthly compounding, future value of current savings = P × (1 + i)n. For end-of-month contributions, future value of contributions = C × (((1 + i)n − 1) ÷ i), where P is current savings, C is monthly contribution, i is the monthly rate (annual nominal rate divided by 12), and n is the number of monthly periods. If i is zero, contributions simply total C × n. This calculator rounds years to a whole number of monthly periods.

How to use it

  1. Enter current retirement savings and the amount you plan to contribute each month.
  2. Enter an assumed annual return as a percentage; it may be negative, zero or positive within mathematically valid bounds.
  3. Enter the years remaining until retirement.
  4. Compare conservative, middle and optimistic scenarios instead of relying on one forecast.

Worked example

If current savings are PKR 25,000, monthly contributions are PKR 500, the assumed annual return is 7%, and the period is 20 years, the tool compounds the current balance and each monthly contribution using a monthly rate of 7% ÷ 12. The displayed balance is a nominal estimate in rupees, not a promise of future purchasing power.

Consider inflation and income needs

Inflation reduces the amount that a future balance can buy. Fees and taxes can lower returns, while irregular contributions or market losses can make actual outcomes differ from a smooth monthly-compounding model. To assess readiness, compare several return assumptions, consider the cost of healthcare and housing, and estimate the income you may need during retirement. For regulated pension benefits, use the relevant employer or government scheme rules separately.

Frequently asked questions

Does this show monthly retirement income?

No. It estimates a future savings balance; a withdrawal or annuity plan is a separate calculation.

Is the annual return guaranteed?

No. It is an assumption used to compare scenarios, not a forecast or guarantee.

Does the result account for inflation?

No. Compare the nominal balance with a separate inflation-adjusted estimate before planning spending.
