Electricity cost formula

Energy (kWh) = power (W) ÷ 1000 × hours used. Cost = energy consumed × electricity rate per kWh.

Worked example

A 1,000 W appliance used 5 hours per day consumes 5 kWh/day. At $0.20/kWh, the estimated daily cost is $1.00 and a 30-day estimate is $30.00.

Electricity cost from power and time

For a device with power in kilowatts, energy use is kWh = kW × hours. Estimated energy cost is then kWh multiplied by the applicable price per kWh. If power is given in watts, divide by 1,000 to obtain kilowatts.

Worked example

A 1,500 W heater uses 1.5 kW. Running it for 4 hours consumes 6 kWh. At an assumed PKR 50/kWh, the energy component is PKR 300 before other bill charges.

Pakistan billing context

Actual electricity bills can include tariff slabs, taxes, fixed charges, adjustments and other components. Appliance labels also show rated power, while real consumption may be lower when equipment cycles.

FAQs

Does 1,500 W mean 1,500 kWh? No; watts are power and kWh is energy. Can I estimate a monthly bill? Yes by modeling daily use over the billing period, but the tariff structure matters. Is the result exact? It is an estimate unless actual energy use is measured.
