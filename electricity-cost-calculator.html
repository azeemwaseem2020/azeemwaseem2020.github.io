Estimate electricity cost from power usage, hours and electricity price per kWh.

Electricity cost formula

Energy (kWh) = power (W) ÷ 1000 × hours used. Cost = energy consumed × electricity rate per kWh.

Worked example

A 1,000 W appliance used 5 hours per day consumes 5 kWh/day. At $0.20/kWh, the estimated daily cost is $1.00 and a 30-day estimate is $30.00.

Electricity cost calculator guide: estimate kWh and bill cost

Electrical energy consumption is commonly measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh). For an appliance with a power rating in watts, first divide watts by 1,000 to get kilowatts, then multiply by the number of hours it operates. Estimated energy use is therefore (watts ÷ 1,000) × hours. Multiply the resulting kWh by the rate per kWh to estimate the energy charge before other bill items.

Worked example

A 100-watt fan running for 8 hours uses 100 ÷ 1,000 × 8 = 0.8 kWh. At an illustrative rate of PKR 60 per kWh, the energy charge would be 0.8 × 60 = PKR 48. This is an example only, not a current electricity tariff or a prediction of a complete bill. If the appliance cycles on and off or does not draw its rated power continuously, actual consumption can be lower or otherwise different.

Estimate multiple appliances

For several devices, calculate each device's daily kWh and add the results. Multiply by the number of days in the billing period for a rough monthly estimate. A device used for 2 hours a day does not use the same energy as one left on continuously, even if their power ratings are identical. Standby consumption can also contribute when devices remain plugged in.

Why the final bill may differ

The entered rate may not include fixed charges, taxes, fuel or tariff adjustments, time-of-use pricing, slab rates, duties or other items on a utility bill. In Pakistan, the amount payable can depend on the applicable tariff category and billing rules. Use the rate and units from your own bill for a closer estimate, and compare the final calculation with the utility's published tariff. This calculator estimates energy cost; it does not replace an official bill or confirm a live tariff.

From appliance watts to monthly cost

Appliance labels usually show rated power, not the exact energy used over a billing period. Energy consumption is power in kilowatts multiplied by operating hours. For example, a 200 W fan running for 8 hours uses 0.2 kW × 8 = 1.6 kWh per day. Multiply daily consumption by the number of days to estimate usage over the selected period.

Use your actual electricity rate

Enter the price per kWh that is relevant to your usage. The amount on a real electricity bill may also include fixed charges, taxes, duties, fuel or tariff adjustments, and different rates across usage slabs. Those charges are not automatically included in a simple appliance energy estimate. For Pakistan household budgeting, check the current bill or applicable tariff schedule rather than relying on a generic example rate.

Rated power is not always continuous power

A refrigerator, air conditioner, pump or thermostat-controlled appliance may cycle on and off, so its average consumption can be lower or vary from the rated input. Inverters and variable-speed appliances can also change power draw with load. If possible, use measured kWh from a meter or the manufacturer's energy label for a more representative estimate.

Estimate savings from usage changes

To compare scenarios, keep the tariff constant and change only the operating hours or appliance power. Reducing daily use from 5 hours to 4 hours reduces the modeled running time by 20%, but actual bill savings can differ when fixed charges or tariff slabs apply. This calculator estimates energy-related cost, not a complete utility bill.

Estimate the energy charge, not the whole bill

For a device with rated power P watts running h hours each day, estimated daily energy is (P ÷ 1,000) × h kWh. Multiply by the tariff per kWh to estimate the energy charge. The 30-day figure assumes the same usage and rate every day, so it is a planning estimate rather than a bill forecast.

Practical checks

  • Use watts from the appliance label, and enter hours per day rather than hours per month.
  • Use the relevant rate from your current bill or tariff schedule; do not treat an illustrative rate as a live tariff.
  • For several appliances, calculate each one separately and add the kWh estimates.
  • Actual bills can include fixed charges, taxes, duties, fuel adjustments and slab or time-of-use pricing.
