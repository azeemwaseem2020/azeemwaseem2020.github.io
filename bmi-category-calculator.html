How BMI categories are interpreted

BMI is calculated from body mass and height and is commonly used as a screening measure. A category is a statistical classification, not a diagnosis and not a direct measurement of body fat.

Why age and context matter

The same BMI value can have different interpretations for children, teenagers, pregnancy and some athletic populations. This calculator is best used with the population assumptions shown on the page rather than as a complete health assessment.

BMI category FAQ

Does BMI measure body fat?

No. It uses height and weight only and cannot distinguish fat mass from muscle mass.

What should I do with a borderline result?

Use the result as a screening signal and consider other measures and professional guidance rather than treating the category as a diagnosis.

BMI formula and categories

For adults, BMI is calculated as weight in kilograms ÷ height in metres². The resulting number is then compared with the reference categories stated on the page. BMI is a screening measure, not a diagnosis.

Worked example

A person weighing 70 kg and measuring 1.75 m has BMI = 70 ÷ 1.75² ≈ 22.86 kg/m².

Important limitations

BMI does not distinguish muscle from fat and is not interpreted the same way for every age or population. Children, teenagers, pregnancy, and some athletic populations require appropriate reference standards.

Adult BMI categories: ranges and worked interpretation

For many adult screening references, BMI below 18.5 is classified as underweight, 18.5 to below 25 as the healthy-weight range, 25 to below 30 as overweight, and 30 or above as obesity. These are screening cut-offs, not a diagnosis, and some health systems use additional obesity classes or population-specific thresholds. Always use the reference standard named by the health service or study you are following.

Worked example

For a weight of 70 kg and height of 1.75 m, BMI = 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 70 ÷ 3.0625 ≈ 22.86 kg/m². Under the common adult ranges above, this falls in the 18.5–24.9 range. The category does not establish body-fat percentage, fitness or overall health.

Why age and context matter

Children and teenagers are assessed using age- and sex-specific growth charts rather than adult cut-offs. Pregnancy, fluid retention, high muscle mass, older age and some medical conditions can also affect interpretation. If the result is unexpected or relevant to a health decision, discuss it with a qualified clinician rather than making a diagnosis from BMI alone.

BMI category FAQs

Is 24.9 the same category as 25.0?

Under the common adult cut-offs stated here, 24.9 is below 25 and 25.0 reaches the overweight threshold. BMI is a continuous number, however, and a category boundary does not represent a sudden biological change.

Can BMI identify muscle mass?

No. BMI uses height and weight only, so it cannot distinguish muscle, fat, bone or fluid.

Should I use adult ranges for a child?

No. Use an age- and sex-appropriate growth reference and professional guidance.
