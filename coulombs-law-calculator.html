Coulomb's law formula

Coulomb's law describes the electrostatic force between two point charges: F = k|q₁q₂|/r² in vacuum or approximately in air, with direction along the line joining the charges.

What changes the force?

Increasing either charge increases the magnitude of the force. Increasing separation decreases the force according to the inverse-square relationship. The sign of the charge product determines whether the interaction is attractive or repulsive.

Worked example

For two charges of equal magnitude, doubling the separation makes the force one quarter as large because the distance is squared in the denominator.

Coulomb's law FAQ

What is k?

In vacuum, k is approximately 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C².

Does the formula apply to extended objects exactly?

The simple point-charge form applies directly to point charges. Extended charge distributions may require integration or another model.

Calculation context

Coulomb's law relates electric force to charge magnitudes and separation distance. The common point-charge model follows F = k|q₁q₂|/r², with units kept consistent.
