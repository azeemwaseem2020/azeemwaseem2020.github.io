Work Calculator — Force, Distance & Angle

Calculate mechanical work using W = Fd cos θ, including the direction of the applied force.

Calculate

Formula

W = Fd cos θ. When force and displacement point in the same direction, θ = 0° and W = Fd.

Angle changes the result

At 90°, the work from that force is zero. At angles greater than 90°, the work is negative because the force component opposes displacement.

Worked example

A 100 N force moves an object 5 m in the same direction: W = 100 × 5 × cos 0° = 500 J.

Common mistakes

  • Using the full force instead of its component along displacement.
  • Entering radians when the angle field expects degrees.
  • Confusing work with force or power.
  • Ignoring friction or other forces when the question asks for net work.

Work Calculator Guide

Use this calculator to estimate mechanical work when a force moves an object through a distance at a known angle.

Formula

W = Fd cos(θ), where W is work in joules, F is force in newtons, d is displacement in metres, and θ is the angle between force and displacement.

Worked example

A 50 N force moves an object 4 m in the same direction as the displacement. With θ = 0°, the work is 200 J.

Why the angle matters

A force perpendicular to the displacement does no mechanical work in this simple model because cos(90°) is zero. A force opposite the displacement produces negative work.

Common mistakes

Use displacement rather than an arbitrary path length, keep the angle relative to the displacement, and convert units before calculating.

Limits

This is a basic constant-force model. Variable forces, rotational work and more complex systems require an appropriate energy or calculus-based model.

How this calculation is checked

Keep units consistent, review the formula shown above, and check the result against the physical assumptions of the problem.

WORK MODEL CHECK

Mechanical work depends on the force component along displacement

For a constant force, W = Fd cos θ. The angle determines how much of the force contributes to displacement. A force perpendicular to the displacement does no mechanical work in this simplified relationship.

Direction matters

θ = 0° gives positive work, θ = 90° gives zero work, and θ = 180° gives negative work for the chosen displacement direction.

Force must be defined

If force changes with position, a simple Fd calculation may not be sufficient; variable-force work requires integrating force over displacement.

Energy connection

Net work is linked to change in kinetic energy through the work–energy theorem, making Work and Kinetic Energy useful companion tools.

Work calculation context

Mechanical work connects force, displacement and the angle between them. In the simplest aligned case W = Fd; direction and the physical model determine how the relationship should be applied.
