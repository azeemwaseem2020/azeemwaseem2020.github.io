Calculate mechanical work using W = Fd cos θ, including the direction of the applied force.

Work calculation context

Mechanical work connects force, displacement and the angle between them. In the simplest aligned case W = Fd; direction and the physical model determine how the relationship should be applied.

Mechanical work and force direction

Mechanical work measures energy transferred by a force through displacement. For a constant force, the component of force along the displacement determines the work.

Worked example

A 50 N force acting in the same direction as a 4 m displacement does 200 J of work. A force perpendicular to displacement contributes zero work in the simple constant-force model.

Why direction matters

Work can be positive, zero or negative depending on the angle between force and displacement. Friction, for example, commonly does negative work on a moving object.

Work FAQ

Is work the same as force?

No. Force is an interaction; work describes energy transfer associated with force acting through displacement.

Check the sign and units of work

Use force in newtons and displacement in metres to obtain work in joules. The angle is measured between the force vector and the displacement vector—not necessarily the angle the object makes with the floor.

Worked angle example

A 20 N force pulls an object 3 m at 60° to the direction of travel. Work is W = 20 × 3 × cos(60°) = 30 J. At 90°, the force contributes zero work in this constant-force model; at an angle greater than 90°, its work contribution is negative.

When this formula is not enough

The equation W = Fd cos θ assumes a constant force and a straight displacement for the calculation. If force changes along the path, work generally requires summing or integrating the force component along the path. Do not confuse work with power: work measures energy transferred, while power measures the rate of transfer.

More work questions

Is work zero when an object does not move?

For zero displacement, mechanical work by that force is zero, even if the force is non-zero.

Can work be negative?

Yes. A force opposing displacement, such as kinetic friction in a simple sliding example, does negative work on the object.

Work calculator guide: choose the force component that acts along displacement

Mechanical work is energy transferred by a force through displacement. For a constant force and straight displacement, use W = Fd cos θ, where F is force in newtons, d is displacement in metres and θ is the angle between the force vector and the displacement vector. The result is in joules (J), with 1 J equal to 1 N·m.

Worked example with a non-zero angle

A person pulls a trolley with a 40 N force at 30° to the direction it moves for 5 m. The force component along the path is 40 cos 30° ≈ 34.64 N. Work done by that applied force is therefore 34.64 × 5 ≈ 173.2 J. This is the work by the pulling force only; friction or other forces may do additional work on the trolley.

Interpret the sign, not just the magnitude

  • 0°: the force acts along displacement, so work is positive.
  • 90°: the force is perpendicular to displacement, so its work is zero in this constant-force model.
  • More than 90°: the force component opposes displacement, so its work is negative.

When to use a more complete model

If the force changes in magnitude or direction along the path, one value of F and θ may not describe the whole motion. Work is then calculated from the force component along the path over the displacement, often using an integral or the area under a force–displacement graph. For multiple forces, calculate each force's work or use the net force consistently; do not add magnitudes without considering direction.

Work calculator FAQ

Does holding a heavy object still count as mechanical work on it?

If the object has no displacement, the mechanical work done on the object by a stationary holding force is zero under the basic W = Fd cos θ model. The person may still use metabolic energy; that is a different meaning of work.

Is work always positive?

No. Work can be positive, zero or negative depending on the force component along displacement. Kinetic friction commonly does negative work on an object sliding across a surface.

Can I enter centimetres or minutes?

Use metres for displacement and newtons for force to obtain joules directly. Convert centimetres to metres before calculation; time is not an input to this work formula.

What is the difference between work and power?

Work measures energy transferred, in joules. Power measures the rate of energy transfer, in watts or joules per second. Use the Power Calculator when time is part of the question.
