US Electricity Bill Calculator

Estimate a monthly electricity cost from the kWh shown on your utility bill, the energy rate in cents per kWh and fixed monthly charges.

Enter your bill details

Enter your usage and rates, then calculate your estimate.

How to use your utility bill

  • Use measured kWh, not just the amount due.
  • Find your energy charge or price per kWh on the rate details.
  • Enter fixed charges separately; include other taxes or fees only if you know the amount.
  • For time-of-use or tiered plans, a single average rate is only an approximation.

This estimator does not use your location or assume a national average rate. Enter figures from your own bill.

Need help finding the right numbers on your statement? Read our guide to reading a US electricity bill, including kWh, supply and delivery charges, fixed fees and rate-plan limits.

Electricity bill formula

Energy charge = monthly kWh × energy rate (cents/kWh) ÷ 100. The estimated total adds the fixed monthly charges and any extra charges you enter. Effective cost per kWh divides that total by monthly usage; it is not necessarily the utility's advertised energy rate.

Example calculation

If a home uses 900 kWh, has an energy rate of 16 cents per kWh and $15 in fixed charges, the energy charge is $144 and the estimated total before other fees is $159. Your actual bill may differ due to rate tiers, time-of-use periods, taxes, credits, delivery charges and local utility rules.

How to find the right rate on your US utility bill

Start with the billing-period usage in kWh and the detailed charges section, not just the amount due. Some utilities show a supply or generation rate separately from delivery, distribution, customer charges and taxes. If you enter only the supply rate here, the estimate will omit charges you leave out; if your bill provides a total energy-related charge and matching kWh, dividing those amounts can produce a useful blended estimate, but do not add the same delivery charges twice.

For a quick reasonableness check, the US Energy Information Administration publishes annual average residential electricity prices and state-level comparisons. Its Electric Power Annual state price table is historical context, not a substitute for your current utility tariff. Prices vary by state, utility, plan and billing period. Use the rate and fees that match your own bill whenever possible.

When this simple estimate is not enough

This calculator applies one rate to all monthly kWh. It may not match a plan with different peak and off-peak prices, seasonal rates, demand charges, minimum bills, credits, or tier thresholds. For block-based usage prices, switch to the Tiered Electricity Rate Calculator; for general usage-to-cost calculations, see the Electricity Cost Calculator. To estimate one appliance’s energy cost, try the Air Conditioner Electricity Cost Calculator. Review the utility’s current rate schedule before relying on an estimate.

Have a tiered electricity rate?

If your utility changes the price per kWh after usage thresholds, use our Tiered Electricity Rate Calculator to estimate each block separately. It models block-based rates, not time-of-use pricing.

Does this calculate solar savings?

No. A bill estimate alone cannot predict solar savings. That requires a site-specific solar production estimate, utility export-credit rules, system costs, financing, degradation and tariff details. For a first solar-load estimate, use our Solar Load Calculator and read the US home solar load guide.

Frequently asked questions

What is a kWh?

A kilowatt-hour is a unit of electrical energy. It represents using 1,000 watts for one hour, or an equivalent combination of power and time.

Should I enter cents or dollars per kWh?

Enter cents. For example, a rate of 16 cents per kWh should be entered as 16, not 0.16.

Why is my actual bill different?

Your bill may include delivery charges, tiered pricing, time-of-use rates, taxes, credits, minimum charges or adjustments not captured by this simplified estimate.
