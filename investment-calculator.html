Investment Calculator

Estimate investment growth from an initial amount, regular contribution, annual return and time.

Investment growth

Investment growth formula

With monthly compounding, the future value of the initial amount is P(1+r)n. A regular monthly contribution is accumulated using C((1+r)n−1)/r, where r is the monthly return and n is the number of months.

Worked example

For an initial 10,000, monthly contribution of 300, 8% annual return and 10 years, the calculator estimates the future value from 120 monthly periods. The result is a mathematical scenario, not a guaranteed return.

How to use

  2. Enter regular monthly contributions.
  3. Enter an assumed annual return.
  4. Enter the investment period.
  5. Compare total contributions with estimated growth.

Frequently asked questions

What does this investment calculator estimate?

It estimates future value from an initial investment, monthly contributions, an assumed annual return and the investment period.

Does it include taxes and fees?

No. The result is a mathematical estimate and does not automatically account for taxes, management fees, inflation or investment losses.

How does compounding affect the result?

Returns are compounded monthly in the calculation, so the assumed return can produce growth on previously accumulated returns as well as contributions.

Where this calculator fits

Investment growth connects initial capital, recurring contributions, return assumptions and time. It complements compound-interest and ROI tools while focusing on future investment value.

How an investment projection works

An investment calculator illustrates how an initial amount, recurring contributions, assumed return and time can interact. It is a mathematical scenario, not a prediction. Market returns vary, and a constant annual return is a simplification that can hide volatility and losses.

Formula

For compound growth without contributions, future value = present value × (1 + r)^n. With regular contributions, each contribution grows for the remaining periods, so the final value is the sum of the grown initial balance and contribution stream.

Worked example

Suppose Rs 100,000 is invested for five years at an assumed 10% annual compound rate with no additional deposits. The simplified future value is 100,000 × 1.10⁵ ≈ Rs 161,051. Actual investments will not necessarily deliver a constant 10% every year.

Pakistan-specific example

A Pakistani investor comparing a savings product, mutual fund or other investment should consider fees, taxes, liquidity and risk alongside the projected value. For PSX investments, brokerage and transaction costs can affect the net result.
