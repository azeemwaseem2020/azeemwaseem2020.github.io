Core relationship

For motion near Earth's surface, PE = mgh is the standard model. For larger altitude changes or other gravitational systems, the appropriate physical model can differ.

For a straightforward calculation, use the Potential Energy Calculator.

Gravitational potential energy

Near Earth's surface, gravitational potential energy is commonly modeled as PE = mgh, where m is mass, g is gravitational acceleration and h is height relative to a chosen reference level. The result is measured in joules when mass is in kilograms, acceleration in m/s² and height in metres.

Worked example

A 20 kg object raised 3 m with g = 9.81 m/s² gains approximately 20 × 9.81 × 3 = 588.6 J of gravitational potential energy. The zero level is arbitrary; only changes in potential energy affect many practical calculations.

Limitations

This simple equation assumes a nearly uniform gravitational field and modest height changes relative to Earth's radius. For large astronomical distances, gravitational potential requires a different model. Also, potential energy is not “lost” when an object falls; it is converted into other forms such as kinetic energy and heat.

FAQs

Can height be negative? Yes, if the chosen reference makes it so. Does heavier always mean more PE at the same height? Yes under the same g. What if g changes? Use the appropriate local or specified gravitational acceleration.
