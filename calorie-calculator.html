Formula & assumptions

Mifflin–St Jeor: BMR = 10W + 6.25H − 5A + S, where S is +5 for men and −161 for women. Estimated maintenance calories = BMR × activity factor.

Worked example

Frequently asked questions

What does a calorie calculator estimate?

It estimates daily energy needs from personal inputs such as age, sex, height, weight and activity level.

Are calorie needs exact?

No. Energy needs vary between individuals and can change with activity, body composition, health and other factors.

Can I use this for medical treatment?

Use the result as a general estimate, not as a substitute for individualized advice from a qualified healthcare professional.

Before you use the result

How daily calorie needs are estimated

The calculator first estimates BMR with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation and then applies an activity factor to estimate daily energy needs. Because activity and energy expenditure vary between people, the result is an estimate rather than a fixed requirement.

Choosing an activity level

Select the level that most closely matches your normal routine rather than a single unusually active day. If your routine changes substantially, recalculate using the new activity pattern.

Use the result carefully

Calorie estimates can help with general planning, but they do not account for every health or dietary factor. For medical nutrition needs, pregnancy, adolescence or significant weight changes, use guidance from a qualified professional.

How calorie estimates are built

Daily calorie estimates generally start with an estimate of resting energy needs and then apply an activity factor or another method to approximate total daily energy expenditure. The result is an estimate because real energy needs vary between people and over time.

Activity level matters

Someone with a sedentary routine and someone doing regular strenuous exercise can have very different energy requirements even with the same height and weight. Choose the activity description that best matches a typical routine.

Calorie calculator FAQ

Is the result an exact calorie requirement?

No. It is an estimate based on the inputs and equation used. Appetite, activity, body composition and metabolic differences can change actual needs.

Should I use one number every day?

It can be a planning baseline, but actual energy expenditure varies by day and should be interpreted alongside longer-term weight and performance trends.
