PSX Calculator Guide: How to Calculate Profit, Loss, Return and Dividend Yield

By: Abdul Qadir · Updated: October 2, 2026

A share price alone does not tell you what happened to an investment. Your purchase price, number of shares, selling price, transaction costs, dividends and corporate actions all help complete the picture.

Quick idea: calculate the gross result first, then add realistic transaction costs and any tax assumption you have verified. Keeping the calculation transparent makes it easier to audit against your broker statement.

What is a PSX calculator?

A PSX calculator is a planning tool for Pakistan Stock Exchange investors. Depending on the tool, it can estimate share profit or loss, return percentage, dividend income, dividend yield, average cost or break-even price. Calcora keeps the main inputs manual so you can use figures from your broker statement or research instead of relying on an invented live price.

How to calculate share profit or loss

First calculate the invested amount: average buy price × number of shares. Then calculate the current or sale value: current price × number of shares. Gross profit or loss is the second number minus the first.

Example: Buy 1,000 shares at PKR 100 and the price later reaches PKR 120. The invested amount is PKR 100,000 and the market value is PKR 120,000, giving a gross gain of PKR 20,000 before costs.

Net profit is more useful than gross profit

A gross gain can overstate what you actually keep. If you enter PKR 1,500 of total transaction costs, the same example becomes PKR 18,500 before any separate tax assumption. The lesson is simple: use actual charges whenever you have them rather than assuming that the share-price difference is your final return.

Return percentage

Basic return percentage = profit or loss ÷ invested amount × 100. In the example above, PKR 20,000 ÷ PKR 100,000 × 100 = 20% before costs.

For a more realistic scenario, calculate the net result first and compare it with the amount actually invested. Remember that a simple holding-period return is not the same thing as an annualized return.

Break-even share price

If you know your total invested amount and additional costs, the break-even price is approximately (invested amount + costs + any applicable amount you want to recover) ÷ shares. It is useful for understanding how much price movement is required before a position covers its known costs.

Dividend income and yield

Dividend income can be estimated as dividend per share × number of shares. Dividend yield is commonly expressed as dividend per share ÷ reference share price × 100. When comparing yields, use the same price date and clearly identify whether you are using an annual dividend, a single announced dividend or another period.

Average share price after buying more

When adding shares at a different price, a weighted average helps describe the combined purchase cost: (existing shares × existing average price + new shares × new purchase price) ÷ total shares. This is a cost calculation, not a prediction of where the market will trade next.

Why corporate actions matter

Historical share prices can be affected by dividends, bonus shares and rights issues. When comparing a historical price with today's price, check whether a corporate action changed the basis of comparison. Otherwise, a simple price difference can give a misleading picture of performance.

PSX calculation checklist

1. Verify the price

Use the correct buy, current or sale price and record the relevant date.

2. Verify the quantity

Use the actual number of shares held, purchased or sold rather than a rounded estimate.

3. Add known costs

Enter brokerage and other transaction charges when you want a closer estimate.

4. Check corporate actions

Confirm whether dividends, bonuses or rights issues affect the comparison.

5. Verify tax assumptions

Tax treatment can depend on current rules and investor circumstances. Do not guess when the decision matters.

6. Reconcile the result

Compare the estimate with your broker contract note or statement before relying on it.

Common mistakes when calculating PSX returns

  • Using the current price as if it were the actual sale price.
  • Ignoring brokerage, taxes or other known transaction costs.
  • Comparing a single dividend with an annual dividend yield without checking the period.
  • Forgetting that bonus and rights issues can change share count and cost calculations.
  • Treating a calculator estimate as a prediction of future market performance.
  • Using stale prices or unverified tax rates for a current decision.

What this calculator does not do

It does not predict whether a stock will rise, choose a stock for you, fetch live prices or replace your broker's contract note. It also does not automatically apply every possible tax or regulatory rule. Those figures can change and may depend on the investor, security and transaction.

Frequently asked questions

Does the PSX calculator show live prices?

No. Keeping prices as user inputs avoids presenting stale or invented market data. Enter a verified price from your broker or an authoritative market source.

Does a 20% price gain mean I earned exactly 20%?

Not necessarily. Brokerage, taxes, fees, dividends and corporate actions can change the real outcome. The calculator helps you separate the gross price movement from the entered costs and assumptions.

Can I use this for dividend yield?

Yes. Enter the dividend per share and the reference share price. Make sure the dividend period and price date are appropriate for the comparison.

Is the result financial advice?

No. It is an arithmetic estimate for education and scenario planning. Important investment, tax and regulatory decisions should be checked against current authoritative information or qualified professional advice.
