What the calculator calculates

For z₁ = a + bi and z₂ = c + di, addition combines like components: (a + c) + (b + d)i. Subtraction is (a − c) + (b − d)i. Multiplication uses i² = −1, giving (ac − bd) + (ad + bc)i.

Worked example

Take z₁ = 2 + 3i and z₂ = 4 + i. The sum is 6 + 4i, the difference is −2 + 2i, and the product is 5 + 14i. The multiplication check is 8 + 2i + 12i + 3i² = 8 + 14i − 3 = 5 + 14i.

Magnitude of a complex number

The magnitude of z = a + bi is |z| = √(a² + b²). It is the distance from the origin to the point (a, b) in the complex plane. This calculator focuses on arithmetic operations; use the formula above when you need the magnitude.

How to avoid sign mistakes

  • Keep the real and imaginary coefficients separate.
  • When subtracting, subtract both components.
  • For multiplication, replace i² with −1 before simplifying.
  • Write negative imaginary coefficients explicitly, such as 4 − 2i.

Where complex numbers are used

Complex numbers are a standard mathematical tool in algebra, electrical engineering, signal processing, control systems and physics. In applications, the real and imaginary components represent model-specific quantities; they should not automatically be treated as two independent physical measurements.

FAQ

What is i?

The imaginary unit is defined by i² = −1.

Can complex numbers be compared like ordinary real numbers?

There is no standard total ordering of complex numbers that behaves like the ordinary ordering of real numbers.

Does the calculator support division?

This version focuses on addition, subtraction and multiplication so that each operation can be checked directly from the displayed formulas.
