Complex numbers in rectangular form

A complex number is commonly written as z = a + bi, where a is the real part, b is the imaginary-part coefficient and i² = −1. Addition and subtraction operate component by component.

Multiplication example

(2 + 3i)(4 + i) = 8 + 2i + 12i + 3i² = 5 + 14i because i² = −1.

Magnitude

The magnitude of a + bi is √(a² + b²). It represents the distance from the origin to the corresponding point in the complex plane.

Complex number FAQ

Can complex numbers be ordered like real numbers?

There is no standard total ordering of complex numbers compatible with ordinary real-number ordering.

Where are complex numbers used?

They are used in areas including electrical engineering, signal processing, control systems, mathematics and physics.

Understanding complex-number calculations

A complex number has the form z = a + bi, where a is the real part, b is the imaginary coefficient and i² = −1. Addition and subtraction work component by component. For multiplication, distribute the terms and replace i² with −1. The magnitude is |z| = √(a² + b²).

Worked example

Take z₁ = 3 + 2i and z₂ = 1 − 4i. Their sum is (3 + 1) + (2 − 4)i = 4 − 2i. Their product is 3(1) + 3(−4i) + 2i(1) + 2i(−4i), giving 3 − 12i + 2i − 8i² = 11 − 10i. For z = 3 + 2i, the magnitude is √(9 + 4) = √13 ≈ 3.606.

A practical Pakistan example

Complex numbers appear in electrical engineering when alternating-current quantities are represented with real and imaginary components. An engineering student in Pakistan might use a complex-number calculation while checking an impedance or phasor exercise. The calculator can verify the arithmetic, but the engineering interpretation still depends on the chosen sign convention and circuit model.

Common mistakes

The most frequent error is forgetting that i² = −1. Another is treating complex numbers as ordinary ordered numbers; there is no standard “greater than” comparison between arbitrary complex numbers. Keep real and imaginary parts separate until the final simplification.

Complex-number FAQ

What is i? It is the imaginary unit defined by i² = −1.

Can a complex number be purely real? Yes. If its imaginary coefficient is zero, it is simply a real number.

How is magnitude calculated? Use √(a² + b²), which is the distance from the origin on the complex plane.

Where are complex numbers used? They are common in algebra, signal processing, control systems and AC circuit analysis.
