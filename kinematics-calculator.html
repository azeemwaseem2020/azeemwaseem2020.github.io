Kinematics Calculator — Motion Variables

Solve common one-dimensional constant-acceleration problems from initial velocity, final velocity, acceleration, displacement and time, with equation selection and steps.

This tool solves common one-dimensional constant-acceleration relationships. It selects a direct equation from the supplied variables and shows the calculation.

Core equations

  • v = u + at
  • s = ut + ½at²
  • v² = u² + 2as
  • s = ½(u + v)t

Assumption

The equations assume constant acceleration along one dimension. They are not a general solver for changing acceleration or multidimensional motion.

Kinematics Calculator Guide

Use this calculator for one-dimensional motion when acceleration is constant. Choose the equation that matches the variables you already know, then check the units and direction of the result.

Which equation should you use?

  • v = u + at when initial velocity, acceleration and time are known.
  • s = ut + ½at² when initial velocity, acceleration and time determine displacement.
  • v² = u² + 2as when time is not needed and displacement is known.
  • s = ½(u+v)t when both velocities and time are known.

Worked example

If u = 5 m/s, a = 2 m/s² and t = 3 s, then v = 5 + (2×3) = 11 m/s and s = 5×3 + ½×2×3² = 24 m.

Important limits

These equations assume constant acceleration along one dimension. They do not directly model changing acceleration, air resistance, curved paths or multi-dimensional motion. Keep units consistent and treat velocity as signed when direction matters.
