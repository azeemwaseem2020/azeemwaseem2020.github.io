Why kg and lb are different

The kilogram is the SI base unit of mass, while the pound is commonly used as a mass unit in the US customary system. Weight is often used colloquially for mass, but the physical concepts are not identical.

Practical uses

This conversion is useful for body-weight records, shipping, recipes, fitness, luggage, product specifications, and international unit conversion.

Choose precision based on the source

Step-by-step conversion and reverse check

  1. Confirm the source reading is in kilograms, not grams.
  2. Multiply the kilogram value by 2.2046226218 to obtain pounds.
  3. Round to the precision needed for the task and keep the unrounded value for any subsequent arithmetic.
  4. For a reverse check, divide the pound result by the same conversion factor to recover approximately the original kilograms.

For example, 12.5 kg × 2.2046226218 ≈ 27.56 lb. Converting 27.56 lb back gives approximately 12.5 kg, with a small difference possible because 27.56 was rounded. A common error is dividing when converting kg to lb; because a pound is a smaller mass unit than a kilogram, the numerical value in pounds should be larger than the same mass expressed in kilograms.

For luggage, parcels or commercial goods, use the unit and rounding rule required by the carrier or scale. Never round a value down to appear under a limit. For medical or nutrition records, the conversion itself does not interpret the measurement or recommend a target. Kg to lb FAQs

How many pounds are in 1 kilogram?

One kilogram is approximately 2.20462 pounds.

Should I multiply or divide to convert kg to lb?

Multiply kilograms by approximately 2.20462. Divide pounds by that factor to return to kilograms.

Are pounds and kilograms both units of force?

In common conversion tables, lb usually refers to pound-mass. Pound-force is a different unit and should not be substituted without checking the context.
