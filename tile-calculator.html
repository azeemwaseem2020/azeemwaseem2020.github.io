Tile Calculator

Tile quantity formula

Tiles needed = (room length × room width) ÷ (tile length × tile width) × (1 + waste%). The final quantity is rounded up because tiles are purchased as whole units.

Worked example

A 20 × 15 ft room using 2 × 2 ft tiles has 300 ft² of floor area and 4 ft² per tile. With 10% waste, the estimate is 83 tiles.

How it works

Use the same unit for all dimensions. Tile count = room area ÷ tile area, increased by waste and rounded up.

Tile Calculator FAQ

How much waste should I allow? A common planning allowance is around 10%, but cuts, room shape, tile pattern and breakage can change the amount required.

Can I mix units? No. Convert dimensions to the same unit before calculating.

Why include waste? Cutting, breakage and layout can require additional tiles.

Tile quantity planning guide

The calculator estimates how many rectangular or square tiles are needed to cover a rectangular floor. It divides the room’s area by the area of one tile, adds the selected waste allowance and rounds up to a whole tile. It assumes the dimensions are measured consistently and that the tile layout can be planned without complex geometry.

Formula and worked example

Room area = length × width. Tile area = tile length × tile width. Estimated quantity = ceiling[(room area ÷ tile area) × (1 + waste percentage ÷ 100)]. For a 20 ft × 15 ft room, the floor area is 300 ft². A 2 ft × 2 ft tile covers 4 ft², so the basic count is 75 tiles. With 10% allowance, 75 × 1.10 = 82.5, which rounds up to 83 tiles.

How to measure and use the result

  1. Measure the usable room length and width.
  2. Enter tile dimensions in the same unit used for the room.
  3. Choose a waste percentage that reflects the layout and cutting requirements.
  4. Calculate and round up to whole tiles; check box quantities and coverage with the supplier.

Waste, patterns and irregular rooms

Waste depends on room shape, diagonal or staggered layouts, tile size, grout lines, breakage, cuts around doorways and future repair needs. A simple rectangular estimate may understate requirements for rooms with alcoves or many obstacles. Split an irregular floor into non-overlapping rectangles, calculate each area and add them before estimating tiles. The tool does not calculate adhesive, grout, levelling compound, skirting tiles or labour.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the calculator round up?

Tiles are bought as whole units. Even if the calculation gives a fraction, you cannot usually purchase a fraction of a tile, and cuts may require additional pieces.

How much waste should I enter?

Ten percent is a common planning example, not a universal rule. Intricate patterns, diagonal installation, fragile tiles or an unusual room shape may need a different allowance. Follow installer and manufacturer advice.

Can I enter centimetres for tiles and feet for the room?

Convert every dimension to the same unit first. Mixing feet and centimetres makes the area ratio incorrect.
