Tile Calculator

How to calculate tile quantity

First calculate the floor area by multiplying room length by room width. Then calculate the area covered by one tile by multiplying its length by its width. Divide the room area by the tile area, apply the waste allowance and round up: tiles needed = ceiling[(room area ÷ tile area) × (1 + waste percentage ÷ 100)]. Keep every dimension in the same unit. The result is a planning estimate for a rectangular area, not a cutting layout.

Worked example

A 20 ft × 15 ft room has 300 ft² of floor area. A 2 ft × 2 ft tile covers 4 ft², so the base quantity is 75 tiles. With a 10% allowance, 75 × 1.10 = 82.5; round up to 83 whole tiles. If each box contains 6 tiles, you need 14 boxes (84 tiles), leaving one tile above the calculated requirement.

Measure carefully before ordering

Measure the usable floor dimensions rather than relying only on a rough room plan. For an L-shaped or irregular room, divide the floor into non-overlapping rectangles, calculate each area and add them. Avoid counting the same space twice. Check whether the supplier's stated tile dimensions are nominal or actual, and confirm the number of tiles and coverage printed on each box.

Choose a sensible waste allowance

Waste depends on tile size, room shape, installation pattern, edge cuts, breakage and whether spare tiles are kept for future repairs. A straight-lay rectangular room may need less cutting than a diagonal or herringbone layout, but there is no universal percentage for every project. Treat 10% as an example only and follow your installer or supplier's advice. The calculator does not estimate grout, adhesive, levelling compound, labour, skirting tiles or the effect of grout-joint width on a detailed layout.

What this estimate does not include

This tool assumes a rectangular room and rectangular or square tiles, with dimensions entered in a consistent unit. It does not optimize tile orientation, calculate individual cut pieces, subtract doorways or built-in fixtures, or model complex patterns. For several rooms, calculate each room separately and consider ordering a small compatible reserve from the same production batch. Confirm package coverage, shade and calibration with the retailer before purchase.

Tile calculator FAQs

How much extra tile should I buy?

It depends on the layout, cuts, breakage and future repair needs. A 10% allowance is a common illustration, not a guaranteed recommendation for every room.

Why are tiles and boxes rounded up?

Tiles and retail boxes are normally sold as whole units. The calculator rounds the tile requirement up, then rounds boxes up to the next complete package.

Can I mix feet and centimetres?

No. Convert room and tile dimensions to one common unit before calculating, or the area ratio will be wrong.
