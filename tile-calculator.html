Tile quantity formula

Tiles needed = (room length × room width) ÷ (tile length × tile width) × (1 + waste%). The final quantity is rounded up because tiles are purchased as whole units.

Worked example

A 20 × 15 ft room using 2 × 2 ft tiles has 300 ft² of floor area and 4 ft² per tile. With 10% waste, the estimate is 83 tiles.

How it works

Use the same unit for all dimensions. Tile count = room area ÷ tile area, increased by waste and rounded up.

Tile Calculator FAQ

How much waste should I allow? A common planning allowance is around 10%, but cuts, room shape, tile pattern and breakage can change the amount required.

Can I mix units? No. Convert dimensions to the same unit before calculating.

Why include waste? Cutting, breakage and layout can require additional tiles.

Estimate tiles and material

Tile quantity starts with the surface area and tile coverage. For a rectangular floor, area = length × width. Divide the floor area by the area covered by one tile, then add a reasonable allowance for cuts, breakage and future replacement.

Worked example

A 5 m × 4 m room has 20 m² of floor area. If each tile is 0.4 m × 0.4 m, one tile covers 0.16 m², so the mathematical minimum is 20 ÷ 0.16 = 125 tiles. Actual ordering should include waste.

For homes, shops or farm buildings, check whether tiles are sold by piece or box and how many pieces each box contains. Keep spare tiles from the same batch when possible because shade can vary between production runs.

FAQs

Why add extra tiles? Cutting and breakage create waste. Can wall tiles be calculated the same way? Yes, using wall area, but subtract major openings. Should every room be measured separately? Yes for better estimates.

Tile quantity and waste

Tile planning starts with surface area divided by tile coverage, followed by a practical waste allowance. For a rectangular room, calculate floor area first and keep tile area in the same unit.

Worked example

A 4 m × 5 m room has 20 m². If each tile covers 0.25 m², the mathematical minimum is 80 tiles. With an 8% allowance, the planning quantity is 86.4, so at least 87 tiles are needed before considering box sizes.

Check whether suppliers quote per piece, box or square metre and whether batches can vary in shade. Diagonal layouts, patterned tiles and many corners can increase cutting waste.

FAQs

Should waste always be 10%? No; layout matters. Do wall tiles use the same method? Measure wall area separately. Why buy extra? To cover cuts, breakage and future repairs.
