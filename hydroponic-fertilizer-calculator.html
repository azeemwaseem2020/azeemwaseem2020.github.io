How the calculation works

For a dilute nutrient solution, 1 ppm is approximately 1 milligram of nutrient per litre. First subtract the nutrient already present in source water from the target. Multiply that deficit by the final solution volume to obtain milligrams of nutrient required. Then divide by the nutrient fraction in the fertilizer and convert milligrams to grams.

Formula: fertilizer grams = (target ppm − source-water ppm) × final litres ÷ (10 × effective nutrient percentage).

If a target is 100 ppm, source water contributes 20 ppm, the final volume is 100 L and the fertilizer contains 15.5% of that nutrient, the deficit is 80 ppm and the estimate is 80 × 100 ÷ (10 × 15.5) = 51.61 g. This is a worked arithmetic example, not a recommended crop recipe.

Scale a dose across multiple reservoirs

For identical reservoirs using the same target and source-water analysis, calculate the dose for one reservoir first, then scale it by the number of reservoirs. The optional extra batch allowance adds finished solution at the same target concentration; it is not a recommendation to overfill each tank.

Batch example: Three reservoirs × 100 L each, plus a 10% preparation allowance, requires 330 L of finished solution. Using the worked example above (51.61 g per 100 L), the estimated batch amount is about 170.32 g. If you do not need extra solution, leave the allowance at 0%.

This scaling is valid only when reservoir volumes, target concentration, source-water nutrient contribution and fertilizer analysis are the same. For different crops, growth stages, water reports or recipes, calculate each tank separately rather than averaging unlike requirements.

Important P₂O₅ and K₂O label conversion

Many fertilizer labels report phosphorus as P₂O₅ and potassium as K₂O, while hydroponic recipes may state elemental P and K. This calculator can convert those label percentages for a single-nutrient calculation: elemental P ≈ P₂O₅ × 0.4364 and elemental K ≈ K₂O × 0.8301. Do not convert if your target and label already use the same basis. Nitrogen percentage is normally entered as N.

What this calculator does—and does not—solve

  • Estimates grams of one fertilizer needed to meet one selected nutrient target in a known final volume.
  • Subtracts a user-entered source-water contribution for that nutrient.
  • Shows the nutrient deficit, effective label percentage, grams, grams per 10 litres and milligrams.
  • Does not solve interacting nutrients from multi-nutrient products. Calcium nitrate, potassium nitrate and many blends contribute more than one nutrient; a dose chosen for one element may oversupply another.
  • Does not convert EC directly into nutrient ppm. EC measures total ionic conductivity and cannot identify the concentration of each individual nutrient.
  • Does not prescribe target ppm, pH, EC, acid dosing or a crop-stage recipe.

Hydroponic mixing checklist

  1. Confirm crop, growth stage, system type and a trusted nutrient recipe.
  2. Check source-water quality; include existing nutrients only when reliable test data are available.
  3. Verify the product is suitable and water-soluble, and enter its exact guaranteed analysis.
  4. Weigh the calculated amount with a suitable scale. Very small micronutrient doses require accurate equipment.
  5. Follow the product and system instructions. Dissolve concentrated fertilizers separately where required; calcium-containing concentrates can react with phosphate or sulfate concentrates.
  6. After mixing, measure pH and EC with calibrated meters and follow crop-specific guidance. EC is a cross-check, not proof that every nutrient is balanced.

Frequently asked questions

Is ppm the same as mg/L?

For dilute water-based nutrient solutions, 1 ppm is commonly treated as approximately 1 mg/L. Use the convention and units specified by your laboratory or recipe.

Can I use this to calculate a complete A/B nutrient recipe?

No. This page calculates one nutrient contribution at a time. A complete recipe must account for every nutrient supplied by every product and avoid incompatible concentrates.

Can I calculate fertilizer from EC alone?

No. EC indicates the combined conductivity of dissolved ions and does not reveal each nutrient's individual ppm. Use an appropriate recipe and water analysis.

Does this tool recommend the correct ppm for lettuce or tomatoes?

No. Target concentrations vary by crop, growth stage, system and source-water chemistry. Enter a target from a reliable crop-specific source.
