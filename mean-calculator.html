Arithmetic mean = sum of values ÷ number of values.

How to find the arithmetic mean

Add all values together and divide the total by the number of values: mean = sum ÷ count. Enter the values carefully and keep their units consistent when a unit is meaningful.

Mean vs average

In everyday use, “average” often means arithmetic mean. Other averages and statistical measures can behave differently, so choose the measure that matches your data.

How to calculate the arithmetic mean

The arithmetic mean is found by adding all observations and dividing the total by the number of observations. It is useful when each observation should contribute equally to the summary.

Example

For 10, 15 and 20, the sum is 45 and there are three observations, so the mean is 15.

When the mean can be misleading

Extreme values can pull the mean upward or downward. For skewed data, compare the mean with the median and inspect the distribution before drawing conclusions.

Mean FAQ

Can the mean be used with negative numbers?

Yes. Negative and positive observations can be included normally; the arithmetic rules do not change.
