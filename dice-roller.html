Dice roller guide: outcomes, totals and probability

A virtual die models a die with numbered faces. With a standard six-sided die, each roll returns an integer from 1 to 6. When you roll several dice, the total is the sum of the individual results. For example, rolling two six-sided dice can produce totals from 2 through 12. The extremes are less common than middle totals because more combinations add up to values such as 6, 7 or 8 than to 2 or 12.

How many outcomes are possible?

If each die has the same number of sides and each face is equally likely, the number of ordered outcomes for n dice with s sides is s raised to the power n. Two six-sided dice have 6 × 6 = 36 ordered outcomes. Only one combination gives a total of 2 (1+1), while six ordered combinations give a total of 7. This explains why the sum is not uniformly distributed even though each individual die is fair.

Use the tool appropriately

Choose the number of dice and the number of sides to match the game or activity. A non-standard die with 20 sides is often used in tabletop games, while a custom-sided die can support classroom probability exercises. The roller is intended for casual simulations and learning, not as a certified source of randomness for security, gambling or high-stakes decisions.

Repeat trials to explore chance

A single roll can differ from the expected pattern by chance. Repeating many trials may help illustrate probability, but a short sample does not guarantee that each face appears equally often. For formal probability work, distinguish theoretical probability from observed frequency, and remember that each roll should be treated as a new trial when the dice are independent.

For example, a game rule that asks for “2d6” means roll two six-sided dice and add both values. A rule that asks for “1d20” means one twenty-sided die, so the possible total runs from 1 to 20.

Reading a roll correctly

Check whether the game asks for individual results, a sum, or a special rule such as keeping the highest die. This page reports the roll and total supported by its controls; it does not automatically apply every tabletop rule. For “advantage” or “disadvantage” mechanics, roll the required dice and apply the rule specified by the game rather than assuming the raw total is the final score.

Probability example: rolling at least one six

For one fair six-sided die, the probability of a six is 1/6, or about 16.7%. For two independent six-sided dice, the chance of at least one six is 1 − (5/6 × 5/6) = 11/36, or about 30.6%. This is different from the chance that the sum equals six: that event has 5 favourable ordered outcomes out of 36, or about 13.9%.

Dice roller FAQ

Are all totals equally likely?

No. A single fair die gives each face the same probability, but sums from multiple dice have different numbers of combinations. Middle totals are generally more common than extreme totals.

What does 3d6 mean?

It means roll three six-sided dice. If the rule says to add them, the total ranges from 3 to 18.

Can I use this for secure random selection?

No. This casual tool is not a cryptographically secure random-number source and should not be used for passwords, security tokens, regulated gambling or other high-stakes outcomes.
