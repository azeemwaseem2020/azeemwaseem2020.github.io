How dice totals and probability work

A fair six-sided die has six equally likely outcomes, numbered 1 through 6. The expected value of one roll is (1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6) ÷ 6 = 3.5. This is a long-run average, not a promise that any individual roll will be near 3.5.

Worked example: two dice

When two fair six-sided dice are rolled, the smallest total is 2 and the largest is 12. There are 36 equally likely ordered outcomes. A total of 7 can occur in six ways, so its probability is 6/36, or about 16.67%. Extreme totals such as 2 and 12 each have only one outcome and are less likely.

What a digital roll represents

This tool uses a computer-generated pseudo-random selection for each die. It is intended for casual games, classroom demonstrations and simulations, not cryptographic security, regulated gambling or audited random draws. For formal use, choose a process with independently verified randomness and rules.

Frequently asked questions

Can a die roll be predicted from the previous result? For a fair physical die, each roll is modelled as independent. Previous results do not make a particular next face due.

Why is the average not always a whole number? Expected value is a probability-weighted average across possible outcomes and can be fractional even when each roll is an integer.

Does rolling more dice guarantee a specific total? No. More dice change the distribution of totals but do not guarantee an outcome.
